Query: What evidence supports autophagy and lysosomal dysfunction as drivers of RPE degeneration in dry AMD, and what fluorescent reporters, biochemical assays, or imaging platforms are best suited to measure autophagic flux and lysosomal enzyme activity in RPE cells?

Evidence for Autophagy/Lysosomal Dysfunction Driving RPE Degeneration in Dry AMD  
1. Histopathology of human AMD eyes  
   • Accumulation of lipofuscin and undegraded photoreceptor outer segments in RPE cells.  
   • Increased numbers of dysfunctional lysosomes and autophagic vacuoles on electron microscopy.  

2. Genetic and transcriptomic studies  
   • Reduced expression of key autophagy genes (e.g., ATG5, ATG7, BECN1) in AMD donor RPE.  
   • Dysregulation of lysosomal genes (e.g., LAMP1/2, CTSB, CTSD) in single‐cell RNA-seq of RPE from dry AMD patients.  

3. Oxidative and mitochondrial stress models  
   • In vitro oxidative injury (e.g., H2O2 or blue‐light exposure) leads to accumulation of LC3‐II and p62/SQSTM1 in RPE, indicating stalled autophagic flux.  
   • Mitochondrial damage triggers impaired mitophagy, elevated ROS, and RPE cell death.  

4. Animal and organoid models  
   • RPE‐specific knockouts of autophagy regulators (Atg5, Atg7) develop AMD‐like features: drusen‐like deposits, RPE atrophy, photoreceptor loss.  
   • Lysosomal acidification inhibitors (e.g., chloroquine) in RPE/choroid explants induce drusen‐like extracellular deposits.  

5. Proteomic and lipidomic analyses  
   • Enrichment of undegraded lipoproteins, cholesterol esters, and bisretinoids (A2E) in AMD RPE, implicating lysosomal clearance failure.  
   • Altered cathepsin D activity correlates with drusen burden.  

Key Tools to Measure Autophagic Flux and Lysosomal Enzyme Activity in RPE Cells  
A. Fluorescent Reporters for Autophagy Flux  
   1. Tandem mCherry-GFP-LC3  
      – GFP signal quenches in low‐pH lysosomes; mCherry persists → distinguishes autophagosomes (GFP+/mCherry+) vs. autolysosomes (GFP–/mCherry+).  
   2. GFP-LC3 or RFP-LC3 puncta count  
      – Quantify induction of autophagosomes, especially combined with lysosomal inhibitors (bafilomycin A1) to assess flux.  
   3. Keima‐LC3 or mito-Keima  
      – pH‐sensitive spectral shift upon delivery to lysosome; suitable for live‐cell tracking of general autophagy or mitophagy.  

B. Fluorescent Probes for Lysosomal Function  
   1. LysoTracker and LysoSensor dyes  
      – Accumulate in acidic compartments; measure lysosomal pH changes.  
   2. Magic Red cathepsin substrates  
      – Cell‐permeant fluorogenic peptides (e.g., for cathepsin B or L) that fluoresce upon enzymatic cleavage.  
   3. DQ-BSA Green/Red  
      – Self-quenched BSA that fluoresces when degraded by lysosomal proteases; a live‐cell readout of global lysosomal proteolysis.  

C. Biochemical Assays  
   1. Western blot for LC3-I/II ratio and p62/SQSTM1 accumulation  
      – In combination with lysosomal blockade to calculate flux rate.  
   2. Cathepsin enzymatic activity assays (fluorogenic substrates, e.g., Z-RR-AMC for cathepsin B)  
      – Performed on whole-cell lysates or isolated lysosomal fractions.  
   3. Lysosomal pH measurement by ratiometric probes (e.g., FITC-dextran vs. Texas Red-dextran calibration).  
   4. β-Hexosaminidase release assay  
      – Quantifies lysosomal membrane permeability/dysfunction.  

D. Imaging Platforms  
   1. Live‐cell confocal or spinning‐disk microscopy  
      – High temporal resolution for tracking autophagosome–lysosome fusion events with fluorescent reporters.  
   2. High‐content imaging systems (e.g., Opera Phenix, CellInsight)  
      – Automated quantification of LC3 puncta, lysosomal volume, acidification across large RPE culture wells.  
   3. Super‐resolution (STED, SIM) or correlative light‐electron microscopy (CLEM)  
      – Detailed morphology of autophagic structures, lysosomal ultrastructure, and drusen‐like deposits in RPE monolayers or explants.  

Putting It All Together  
By combining inducible stressors (oxidative, lipid overload) in primary or iPSC‐derived human RPE with these reporters and assays, one can dissect:  
 • Basal vs. induced autophagic flux.  
 • Lysosomal acidification and protease functionality.  
 • Downstream effects on RPE survival, drusen‐like aggregate formation, and epithelial barrier integrity.  

This integrated toolkit allows mechanistic interrogation of how impaired autophagy–lysosome pathways drive RPE degeneration in dry AMD and offers readouts to test small molecules or genetic modulators that restore flux and enzymatic activity.
