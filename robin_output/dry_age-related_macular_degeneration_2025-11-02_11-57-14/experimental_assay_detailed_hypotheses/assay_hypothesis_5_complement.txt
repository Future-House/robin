Assay Overview  
• Rationale and disease aspect modeled: Dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is strongly linked to local overactivation of the alternative complement pathway at the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE)–Bruch’s membrane interface. This assay models sublytic complement attack on RPE monolayers and quantifies generation of the potent anaphylatoxins C3a and C5a as readouts of complement overactivation.  
• Cells/materials: Primary human RPE (hRPE) cells or the ARPE-19 line cultured to confluence on Transwell® inserts or standard tissue culture plates. Human serum (normal or complement-activated) or purified complement components (C3, factor B, factor D, properdin) added to initiate complement cascade. Optionally, drusen extracts or oxidized photoreceptor outer segments are co-administered to mimic local triggers.  
• Measurements:  
 – Secreted C3a and C5a levels in the culture supernatant, quantified by sandwich ELISA or multiplex bead-based immunoassay.  
 – Complement deposition on RPE (C3b/iC3b, membrane attack complex C5b-9) by immunocytochemistry or flow cytometry.  
 – RPE viability/cytotoxicity (LDH release, propidium iodide uptake) to correlate anaphylatoxin levels with sublytic damage.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Genetic and histopathological data: Genome-wide association studies have repeatedly implicated complement-related genes (CFH, C3, C2/CFB, CFI) in AMD susceptibility (Hageman et al., 2005; Fritsche et al., 2016). Post-mortem AMD eyes and drusen extracts contain activated C3 fragments, C5b-9, and anaphylatoxins (Johnson et al., 2001; Anderson et al., 2010).  
• Functional studies: In vitro and in vivo models show that excessive C3a/C5a promotes RPE cytokine release, inflammasome activation, barrier disruption, and extracellular matrix remodeling (Nozaki et al., 2006; Doyle et al., 2012). Sublytic C5b-9 formation on RPE induces chronic oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, phenotypes characteristic of early dry AMD (Sohn et al., 2018).  
• Clinical correlations: Aqueous humor levels of C3a and C5a are elevated in AMD patients compared to age-matched controls, and higher levels correlate with progression from geographic atrophy (GA)–sparing dry AMD to GA (Schick et al., 2016).  

Previous Use  
• Mechanistic insights: Lynn et al. (J. Immunol. 2015) used a very similar RPE-serum co-culture to show that C3a and C5a mediate upregulation of IL-1β and VEGF in RPE, laying groundwork for complement-centric intervention strategies. Nozaki et al. (Exp. Eye Res. 2006) demonstrated that RPE viability under complement attack could be rescued by addition of complement regulators (e.g., recombinant factor H).  
• Screening tool: Researchers have adapted this assay into 96-well format for medium-throughput screens of small molecules and biologics that reduce C3a/C5a release or block MAC deposition (Ricklin & Lambris, 2013). Hits from such screens include compstatin analogues that inhibit C3 activation in RPE cultures.  
• Validation of biomarkers: ELISA measurement of culture supernatants has been used to validate novel complement activation markers and to test their modulation by anti-inflammatory or antioxidant co-treatments.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
1. Pathway specificity: Directly measures key pathogenic mediators (C3a/C5a) implicated by genetics, histology, and clinical biomarker studies.  
2. Mechanistic relevance: Recapitulates sublytic complement attack, a critical driver of RPE dysfunction and drusen formation in dry AMD.  
3. Quantitative, scalable: ELISA/multiplex readouts enable dose-response curves, kinetic profiling and adaptation to medium-throughput formats.  
4. Modifiable triggers: Can incorporate drusen proteins, oxidized lipids or advanced glycation end products to better mimic the AMD microenvironment.  

Weaknesses  
1. Incomplete microenvironment: Lacks choroidal endothelial cells, macrophages/microglia and Bruch’s membrane components that modulate complement in vivo.  
2. Cell line limitations: ARPE-19 may not fully recapitulate primary RPE phenotype or barrier function; primary hRPE are donor-variable and less amenable to high throughput.  
3. Simplified complement source: Use of pooled serum or purified components may not reflect patient-to-patient variability or local complement regulator expression.  
4. Translational gap: In vitro modulation of C3a/C5a does not guarantee efficacy in the complex, chronic setting of geographic atrophy progression.  

Conclusion  
The complement activation assay—quantifying C3a/C5a release from RPE under sublytic complement stress—directly addresses a genetically and clinically validated mechanism in dry AMD, offers quantitative readouts suitable for screening, and has been used to discover and validate complement-targeting modalities. Its main limitations lie in the reductionist nature of the culture system and the absence of multicellular interactions that modulate complement in the retino-choroidal niche. When combined with co-culture or organotypic models, it represents a valuable component of a tiered in vitro strategy for early-stage therapeutic evaluation.
