Assay Overview  
• Disease aspect modeled: Impaired autophagic clearance in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), a core feature of dry AMD pathogenesis leading to lipofuscin/A2E accumulation, oxidative stress, and eventual RPE cell death.  
• Readouts: Transgenic RPE monolayers (primary human RPE or ARPE-19) stably expressing an mCherry-GFP-LC3 tandem reporter. Under baseline or drug-treated conditions, high-content fluorescence imaging yields:  
  – Number of yellow puncta (mCherry+GFP+; autophagosomes)  
  – Number of red puncta (mCherry+GFP–; autolysosomes)  
  – Autophagic flux score = ratio of red-only to total puncta, or changes in flux over time  
  – Secondary readouts (optional): p62/SQSTM1 immunostaining, lysosomal pH dyes, cell viability assays  
• Workflow: Plate RPE monolayers in 96- or 384-well plates, treat with candidate modulators of autophagy (e.g., small molecules), fix or live-image at defined time points, quantify puncta by automated image analysis.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Central role of autophagy in RPE homeostasis: Several groups (Mitter et al. Autophagy 2014; Krohne et al. Exp Eye Res 2010; Ferrington et al. Oxid Med Cell Longev 2016) have shown that chronic light/oxidative stress and bisretinoid buildup (A2E, lipofuscin) impair autophagosome formation and clearance in RPE.  
• Link to dry AMD: Post-mortem human AMD donor eyes exhibit reduced expression of ATG genes (ATG5, ATG7) and lysosomal enzymes (cathepsin D) in the RPE (Zhao et al. Cell Death Differ 2011). Genetic mouse models with RPE-specific ATG5/7 knockout develop drusen‐like deposits, RPE atrophy, and photoreceptor loss (Mukai et al. Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 2019).  
• Clinical correlations: Levels of autophagy markers (LC3-II, p62) in the aqueous humor of patients with early AMD differ from controls, suggesting autophagic dysfunction correlates with disease stage (Shi et al. Sci Rep 2015). Enhancing autophagy in RPE reduces lipofuscin accumulation in vitro and in vivo, improving visual function in rodent AMD models (Sinha et al. J Biol Chem 2018).  

Previous Use  
• Drug discovery: High-content screens using mCherry-GFP-LC3 RPE cells have identified rapamycin analogs, AMPK activators (AICAR), and transcription factor EB (TFEB) agonists as autophagy enhancers that reduce A2E-induced toxicity (Mitter et al. Autophagy 2014; Zhong et al. Nat Commun 2016).  
• Key findings:  
  – Demonstration that trehalose rescues dysfunctional autophagy in A2E-loaded RPE (Wang et al. Sci Rep 2019).  
  – Validation that small-molecule enhancers of lysosomal biogenesis (via TFEB) decrease lipofuscin in RPE and slow photoreceptor degeneration in murine AMD models (Xing et al. EMBO Mol Med 2019).  
• Adaptations: Some studies have combined the reporter assay with co-culture of RPE and macrophages to model drusen clearance and inflammatory crosstalk (Rohrer et al. Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 2013).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
1. Direct, quantitative measure of autophagic flux rather than static LC3 levels.  
2. Scalable to moderate-high throughput 96/384-well formats for screening libraries.  
3. Demonstrated correlation between restored flux and reduction of lipofuscin/A2E toxicity in RPE.  
4. Amenable to multiplexing with viability, reactive oxygen species, or lysosomal pH dyes.  

Weaknesses  
1. Overexpressed reporter may not fully recapitulate endogenous LC3 dynamics; risk of artifactual puncta.  
2. ARPE-19 cells can differ from primary human RPE in autophagic competence and polarization; primary cells are more physiologically relevant but less scalable.  
3. Autophagy modulation is pleiotropic; flux increase may not translate to clinical benefit if downstream lysosomal function remains impaired.  
4. Assay does not model subretinal environment, Bruch’s membrane changes, or immune/inflammatory contributions to dry AMD.  

Conclusion  
The mCherry-GFP-LC3 autophagic flux assay in RPE monolayers provides a robust, quantitative platform to screen for small molecules that restore autophagy—a mechanism strongly implicated in dry AMD pathogenesis. However, hits from this assay should be validated in more complex models (3D RPE–choroid organoids, in vivo rodent AMD models) and assessed for effects on lysosomal health, drusen‐like deposit formation, and photoreceptor support before clinical translation.
