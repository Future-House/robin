Assay Overview  
The Seahorse XF Metabolic Flux Assay measures real-time oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) to profile mitochondrial respiration and glycolysis in cultured cells. In the dry AMD context this assay models key aspects of retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) energy failure under chronic oxidative or lipofuscin‐mediated stress.  
•  Disease aspect modeled: mitochondrial dysfunction and compensatory glycolytic shift in RPE, central to RPE atrophy and photoreceptor degeneration in dry AMD.  
•  Measurements:  
   – Basal OCR (ATP‐linked respiration), maximal respiration (FCCP‐stimulated), proton leak, spare respiratory capacity.  
   – Basal ECAR and glycolytic capacity (glucose, oligomycin, 2-DG injections).  
   – Data acquired via fluorescent oxygen and pH sensors in the Seahorse XF cartridge.  
•  Biological material: primary human RPE (fetal or adult donor), iPSC-derived RPE, or ARPE-19 cells preconditioned with chronic oxidative stress (e.g., H₂O₂, A2E) or lipofuscin to mimic aged RPE.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1.  Mitochondrial dysfunction is an early and central event in dry AMD pathogenesis:  
    – Postmortem AMD RPE mitochondria show reduced complex I/III activities and altered morphology (Ferrington et al., Sci Rep. 2018; Ferrington et al., J Proteome Res. 2017).  
    – Genetic and transcriptomic studies link AMD risk alleles (CFH, ARMS2/HTRA1) to impaired RPE mitochondrial homeostasis and oxidative‐stress responses (Ratnapriya et al., Nature Genet. 2019).  
2.  Energy imbalance contributes to drusen formation and RPE atrophy:  
    – Under oxidative or A2E‐induced stress, RPE shifts toward glycolysis but fails to meet ATP demand, triggering inflammasome activation and cell death (Saini et al., Aging Cell. 2017; Estner et al., IOVS. 2019).  
    – Clinical imaging (OCT, FAF) correlates regions of metabolic stress with geographic atrophy progression (Smith et al., Prog Retin Eye Res. 2019).  
3.  Restoring mitochondrial function protects RPE:  
    – Activation of PGC-1α or treatments that boost NAD⁺ levels rescue mitochondrial respiration, reduce ROS, and delay RPE senescence (Zhang et al., Aging Cell. 2015; Terluk et al., Mol Vis. 2015).  

Previous Use  
•  Drug discovery:  
   – Seahorse profiling has been used to screen small molecules (PGC-1α agonists, NAD⁺ precursors) in oxidatively stressed ARPE-19 or iPSC-RPE to identify compounds that restore spare respiratory capacity (Fisher et al., Redox Biol. 2019).  
   – A2E-treated primary RPE were assayed to reveal that antioxidants (e.g., MitoQ) normalize ΔΨm and OCR, guiding preclinical validation (Chakrabarti et al., Free Radic Biol Med. 2016).  
•  Mechanistic insights:  
   – Demonstrated that RPE under lipofuscin‐mediated photooxidative stress exhibit a “metabolic bottleneck” at complex II, later linked to impaired mitophagy (Dunaief et al., Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci. 2016).  
   – Identified that ETC flux deficits precede lysosomal alkalinization and drusen-like deposit formation in 3D RPE cultures (Sokol et al., Autophagy. 2018).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths:  
•  Direct functional readout of two core energy pathways, highly relevant to dry AMD RPE pathology.  
•  Quantitative, kinetics‐based, amenable to medium-throughput screening of therapeutic libraries (96-well format).  
•  Sensitive to partial rescue of mitochondrial reserve, enabling early go/no‐go decisions in lead optimization.  

Weaknesses:  
•  Monoculture RPE lacks Bruch’s membrane, photoreceptors, immune components—limits modeling of drusen formation and para-inflammatory milieu.  
•  Seahorse assays require careful optimization of cell density, medium buffering, and stress paradigms; inter-batch variability can challenge reproducibility.  
•  OCR/ECAR changes may reflect general cytotoxicity or off-target metabolic effects, necessitating orthogonal validation (e.g., ATP assays, ROS measurements, mitophagy markers).  

Conclusion  
The Seahorse XF Metabolic Flux Assay provides a robust, disease-relevant readout of RPE energy dysfunction in dry AMD. Its ability to quantify mitochondrial and glycolytic parameters under controlled stress makes it a powerful tool for early-stage screening of compounds aimed at preserving RPE bioenergetics. However, hits must be validated in more complex co-culture or organoid models to ensure translation to in vivo AMD pathology.
