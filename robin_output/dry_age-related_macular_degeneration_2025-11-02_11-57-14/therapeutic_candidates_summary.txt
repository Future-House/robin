Therapeutic Candidate 1:
Candidate: AICAR (5-Aminoimidazole-4-carboxamide ribonucleotide; Sigma-Aldrich Cat. A9978)
Hypothesis: AICAR enters RPE cells, is converted to ZMP and allosterically activates AMPK, leading to phosphorylation of ACC and upregulation of tight junction proteins claudin-1 and occludin via FOXO and KLF2 transcription. This strengthens transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) and reduces paracellular permeability under oxidative/A2E stress in dry AMD models.
Reasoning: AMPK activation has been linked to enhanced barrier function in epithelial monolayers. AICAR’s ZMP mimicry of AMP triggers AMPK, driving transcription of junctional genes and supporting autophagy of damaged junction complexes. Preclinical data in endothelial cells show TEER improvement and cytoskeletal stabilization. AICAR is cell-permeable, has known pharmacokinetics in rodents (t½ ~30 min i.v.) and a favorable safety margin. Its mechanistic specificity for AMPK and evidence in A2E-laden RPE warrant testing in hESC-derived RPE and NaIO₃-injured mice.

Therapeutic Candidate 2:
Candidate: Metformin (AMPK activator; Sigma-Aldrich Cat. D150959)
Hypothesis: Metformin accumulates in RPE mitochondria, mildly inhibiting complex I to raise AMP:ATP ratio, activate AMPK, and induce expression of ZO-1 and occludin via PGC-1α–mediated mitochondrial biogenesis. This rescues TEER decline and junctional disassembly in A2E/photooxidative stress models.
Reasoning: Metformin’s clinical safety profile and extensive use provide strong translational potential. In epithelial barrier assays, metformin preserves tight junction integrity via AMPK-dependent pathways and attenuates ROS. Preliminary data in diabetic retinopathy models show protection of the inner blood–retinal barrier. Its oral bioavailability (F ~50%), long half-life (t½ ~4–6 h), and low cost support repurposing for dry AMD.

Therapeutic Candidate 3:
Candidate: Y-27632 (ROCK1/2 inhibitor; Selleckchem Cat. S1049)
Hypothesis: Y-27632 inhibits RhoA-activated ROCK1/2 in RPE, reducing myosin light chain phosphorylation, relaxing cortical actin tension, and promoting assembly of claudin-1/occludin tight junctions. This restores TEER and reduces FITC-dextran permeability under oxidative and inflammatory stress.
Reasoning: ROCK inhibition is well validated to enhance barrier function in vascular endothelium. In RPE, reduced actomyosin contractility favors junctional stability. Y-27632 has nanomolar potency (IC₅₀ ~140 nM) and good solubility, with published ocular safety in rabbit models. Its mechanism specificity, preclinical data in H₂O₂-challenged RPE, and ease of intravitreal or topical delivery make it an attractive candidate.

Hypothesis: SB203580 inhibits p38α/β in RPE, blocking phosphorylation of occludin residues that lead to its internalization under 7-ketocholesterol stress. Maintained occludin localization at tight junctions restores TEER in drusen lipid models.
Reasoning: p38 MAPK is implicated in oxysterol-induced junctional loss. SB203580’s established use in multiple stress paradigms (IC₅₀ ~100 nM) and cell permeability support its application. Its ability to rescue barrier integrity in endothelial cells guides translation to RPE.

Therapeutic Candidate 49:
Candidate: Anakinra (Kineret; IL-1R antagonist; Amgen)
Hypothesis: Anakinra competitively blocks IL-1R1 on RPE, preventing IL-1β-driven activation of MAPK/NF-κB that induces MMP-9 and junctional protein degradation. This preserves ZO-1/occludin and TEER under inflammasome-driven AMD stress.
Reasoning: Anakinra’s clinical use in rheumatoid arthritis and safety in ocular injections provide translational support. Blocking IL-1R prevents paracrine barrier damage from microglial or RPE inflammasomes. Its single-agent and receptor-specific mechanism align with precision therapy goals.

Therapeutic Candidate 50:
Candidate: Tocilizumab (Actemra; IL-6R monoclonal antibody; Roche)
Hypothesis: Tocilizumab binds IL-6R on RPE, blocking both classical and trans-signaling, reducing STAT3 activation and MMP expression. This limits cytokine-driven junctional breakdown and restores TEER under chronic parainflammatory conditions.
Reasoning: Tocilizumab’s ocular safety in uveitis trials and known inhibition of IL-6–mediated barrier disruption in endothelial cells support its use in dry AMD. By targeting a key cytokine amplifier of parainflammation, it may prevent RPE barrier decline.
