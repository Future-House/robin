Query: Research on PPARγ agonists or modulators that enhance RPE barrier function in TEER-based assays under oxidative stress conditions mimicking dry AMD, with mechanistic studies confirming PPRE activation, in vivo proof-of-concept in AMD animal models, and systemic/ocular pharmacokinetic and toxicity assessments

Below are 15 distinct mechanistic hypotheses or therapeutic‐strategy proposals, each amenable to cell‐culture readouts (e.g. TEER, reporter assays, biochemical end points) and follow‐up in vivo proof‐of-concept plus PK/Tox profiling.

1. PPARγ Agonists to Fortify RPE Barrier  
Hypothesis: Selective PPARγ agonists restore TEER in ARPE-19 under H₂O₂ stress by activating PPRE-driven transcription of tight‐junction genes (ZO-1, occludin).  
Cell assays: TEER, luciferase PPRE reporter, qPCR/Western for junctional proteins.  
In vivo: Oral/topical dosing in dry AMD mouse (e.g. Cfh⁻/⁻) → OCT/fundus, RPE flat mounts.  
PK/Tox: Ocular vs systemic exposure, retinal safety, systemic pathology.

2. Nrf2 Keap1-Disruptors to Boost Antioxidant Defenses  
Hypothesis: Small molecules that disrupt Keap1–Nrf2 interaction upregulate HO-1/NQO1, preserving TEER under oxidative insult.  
Cell assays: Nrf2 nuclear translocation (immunofluorescence), ARE-luciferase, ROS assays, TEER.  
In vivo: Oral gavage in NaIO₃-induced RPE degeneration model; ERG, RPE histology.  
PK/Tox: Brain/ocular penetration, liver toxicity panel.

3. SIRT1 Activators to Suppress RPE Senescence  
Hypothesis: SIRT1 agonists deacetylate p53/FOXO, reduce SA-βgal and p16 levels, and restore barrier function.  
Cell assays: SA-βgal staining, acetyl-p53 Western, TEER, mtDNA copy number.  
In vivo: Systemic dosing in aged mouse AMD model; fundus autofluorescence, RPE morphology.  
PK/Tox: Hematology, renal/hepatic enzymes, ocular histopath.

4. Mitochondria-Targeted Antioxidants (MitoQ Derivatives)  
Hypothesis: Lipophilic triphenylphosphonium-linked antioxidants prevent Δψm collapse and tight-junction loss under H₂O₂.  
Cell assays: JC-1 Δψm, ROS (MitoSOX), TEER, ATP levels.  
In vivo: Intravitreal vs systemic in NaIO₃ mouse; mitochondrial rescue in RPE by EM.  
PK/Tox: Mitochondrial accumulation, off-target cardiac toxicity.

5. Autophagy Enhancers (mTOR Inhibitors) to Clear Lipofuscin Precursors  
Hypothesis: Rapalogs stimulate LC3-II conversion, reduce A2E accumulation, and preserve TEER.  
Cell assays: LC3 puncta, p62 turnover, A2E fluorescence, TEER.  
In vivo: Rapalog injection in ABCA4⁻/⁻ mouse; measure drusen-like deposits, RPE integrity.  
PK/Tox: Immune profiling, retinal immune cell activation.

6. Factor D (FD) Inhibitors to Block Complement-Mediated RPE Injury  
Hypothesis: FD blockade prevents C3b accumulation on stressed RPE, reducing cell death and TEER decline.  
Cell assays: Incubate ARPE-19 with human serum + FD inhibitor; detect C3a/C3b ELISA, LDH release, TEER.  
In vivo: Systemic FD inhibitor in Cfh⁻/⁻ mice; assess complement deposition, RPE survival.  
PK/Tox: Complement profiling, animal immunocompetence.

7. NLRP3 Inflammasome Inhibitor (MCC950) in RPE  
Hypothesis: MCC950 blocks NLRP3-ASC speck formation, lowers IL-1β release, and improves barrier.  
Cell assays: ASC speck imaging, IL-1β ELISA, caspase-1 activity, TEER.  
In vivo: Intravitreal MCC950 in NaIO₃ mice; evaluate RPE inflammatory markers and morphology.  
PK/Tox: Local toxicity, systemic immunomodulation.

8. LOX-1 Antagonists to Prevent Oxidized‐Lipid Uptake  
Hypothesis: Small-molecule LOX-1 blockers reduce ox-LDL internalization, lipid peroxidation, and junctional breakdown.  
Cell assays: Dil-oxLDL uptake, malondialdehyde (MDA) assay, TEER.  
In vivo: Oral LOX-1 antagonist in AMD rat; fundus lipid staining, RPE ultrastructure.  
PK/Tox: Lipid panels, vascular function.

9. MerTK Agonists (Gas6 Mimetics) to Enhance RPE Phagocytosis  
Hypothesis: Gas6 analogs boost MerTK signaling, improving phagocytic clearance of photoreceptor outer segments and TEER.  
Cell assays: FITC-POS uptake assay, MerTK phosphorylation (WB), TEER.  
In vivo: Subconjunctival Gas6 mAb in Royal College of Surgeons (RCS) rat; photoreceptor counts, ERG.  
PK/Tox: Retinal inflammation, systemic coagulation.

10. miR-146a Mimics to Damp NF-κB–Driven Inflammation  
Hypothesis: miR-146a restoration suppresses TRAF6/IRAK1, lowering cytokines (TNFα, IL-6) and rescuing barrier.  
Cell assays: Transfect miR-146a mimic, NF-κB reporter, cytokine ELISA, TEER.  
In vivo: Intravitreal nanoparticle delivery in NaIO₃ or laser-injury model; cytokine profiling, RPE ultrastructure.  
PK/Tox: Off-target immune suppression, miRNA biodistribution.

11. Sphingosine-1-Phosphate Receptor Modulators to Tighten Junctions  
Hypothesis: S1PR2 or S1PR3 agonists enhance Rac1 signaling, promoting tight‐junction assembly and TEER recovery.  
Cell assays: GTP-Rac1 pull-down, ZO-1 immunostaining, TEER.  
In vivo: Topical S1PR agonist in PDT-induced RPE injury; barrier function by fluorescein leakage.  
PK/Tox: Vascular reactivity, systemic lymphocyte counts.

12. RAGE Antagonists (sRAGE) to Block AGE-Mediated Damage  
Hypothesis: Soluble RAGE decoy reduces AGEs-induced NF-κB activation, cytokine release, and TEER drop.  
Cell assays: RAGE reporter, IL-8 release, ROS, TEER.  
In vivo: Systemic sRAGE in diabetic-accelerated AMD model; OCT, neovascular markers.  
PK/Tox: Glucose homeostasis, renal clearance.

13. PGC-1α Inducers to Drive Mitochondrial Biogenesis  
Hypothesis: Small molecules that increase PGC-1α expression restore OXPHOS and barrier function under chronic stress.  
Cell assays: mtDNA copy number, oxygen consumption (Seahorse), TEER.  
In vivo: Oral PGC-1α inducer in aged mice; mitochondrial EM in RPE, visual behavior.  
PK/Tox: Muscle metabolism, weight change.

14. Erythropoietin (EPO) Analogs to Protect RPE from Hypoxic Injury  
Hypothesis: Non-erythropoietic EPO derivatives activate EPOR–Stat3 signaling, boosting survival and junction integrity.  
Cell assays: pStat3 WB, TUNEL assay under CoCl₂ hypoxia mimic, TEER.  
In vivo: Intravitreal EPO analog in laser-induced RPE injury; RPE barrier by fluorescein angiography.  
PK/Tox: Hematocrit, systemic EPO receptor engagement.

15. MSC-Derived Exosomes to Deliver Protective miRNAs  
Hypothesis: Exosomes from hypoxia-preconditioned MSCs carry miR-21/miR-222 to downregulate pro-apoptotic pathways in RPE, preserving TEER.  
Cell assays: Exosome uptake (PKH26), target gene knockdown, apoptosis assay, TEER.  
In vivo: Subconjunctival exosome injections in NaIO₃ model; retinal thickness, RPE integrity.  
PK/Tox: Exosome biodistribution, immunogenicity.
