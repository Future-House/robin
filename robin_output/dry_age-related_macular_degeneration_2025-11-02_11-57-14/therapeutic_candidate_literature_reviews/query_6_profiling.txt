Query: Profiling of hydrogen sulfide donors, nitric oxide modulators, and redox-active compounds in RPE barrier integrity assays measuring TEER and permeability, with studies confirming target engagement at molecular level, restoration of barrier function in dry AMD models, and pharmacokinetic/toxicity evaluations

Below are 15 distinct mechanistic‐hypothesis proposals, each paired with a cell‐culture assay plan (TEER/permeability), molecular target‐engagement readouts, proof‐of‐concept in dry AMD RPE models, and basic PK/tox studies.

1. Mitochondria-targeted H2S donor AP39  
   • Hypothesis: AP39 S-sulfhydrates mitochondrial respiratory complexes, lowers ROS and preserves RPE tight junctions.  
   • Assays: TEER and FITC-dextran permeability in human RPE monolayers ± AP39.  
   • Engagement: Biotin-switch assay for S-sulfhydration of complex I subunits; ROS (MitoSOX) reduction.  
   • AMD model: NaIO3-injured RPE with AP39 rescue, monitor ZO-1/occludin localization.  
   • PK/Tox: Single- and multiple-dose mouse PK, liver/kidney enzymes, mitochondrial toxicity panel.

2. Slow-release H2S donor GYY4137 via Nrf2 activation  
   • Hypothesis: GYY4137 liberates H2S that activates Nrf2, upregulates HO-1/GCLM, restores barrier.  
   • Assays: TEER/permeability; immunofluorescence for tight-junction proteins.  
   • Engagement: Nrf2 nuclear translocation (immunoblot), HO-1/GCLM mRNA by qPCR.  
   • AMD model: Dry AMD iPSC-RPE treated with GYY4137; measure barrier recovery.  
   • PK/Tox: Rat PK, glutathione levels, cytotoxicity in hepatocytes.

   • AMD model: Light-stressed RPE; NaHS restores TEER and junctional localization.
