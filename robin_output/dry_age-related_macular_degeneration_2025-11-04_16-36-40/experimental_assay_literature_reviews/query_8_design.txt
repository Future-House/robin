Query: Design co-culture assay systems combining RPE and choroidal endothelial cells to study paracrine signaling alterations in dry AMD, investigating VEGF/PDGF modulation, inflammatory mediators, endothelial barrier dysfunction, angiogenic sprouting assays, and multiplex cytokine profiling

Platform and architecture: Culture primary, polarized RPE on microporous transwells (laminin- or Matrigel-coated) for 4–5 weeks until honeycomb morphology and TER ≥250–400 Ω·cm2, then pair with choroidal endothelial cells either on the opposite side (contact) or in a non-contact compartment to isolate paracrine signaling; sample apical and basolateral compartments separately to capture polarized secretion (notably VEGF/PlGF) (fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 11-12, fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 12-13, fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 8-9, fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 16-16, ghareeb2020coculturetechniquesfor pages 9-10). As an alternative, use perfused microfluidic BRB chips with spatially separated RPE and endothelium, embedded TEER electrodes, and controlled gas/osmolarity to reduce ischemic artifacts (jahagirdar2021degenerativedisease‐on‐a‐chipdeveloping pages 16-19, ghareeb2020coculturetechniquesfor pages 9-10).

Perturbations to model dry AMD stress: Apply IL-1β or TLR3/NF-κB agonists to induce polarized cytokine release and barrier loss; use hypoxia-mimetics (CoCl2) or metabolic stress (low glucose) to upregulate RPE VEGF; control oxidative stress to avoid confounding cell-death–driven outputs (fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 16-16, jahagirdar2021degenerativedisease‐on‐a‐chipdeveloping pages 16-19, fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 8-9).

Barrier readouts: Quantify TEER (baseline primary RPE ~200–400 Ω·cm2), fluorescent dextran permeability, and junctional proteins (ZO‑1, claudin‑5), and challenge with EDTA or TNF‑α; assess VEGF-driven, NOS-dependent transcytosis as a mechanistic axis (fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 8-9, jahagirdar2021degenerativedisease‐on‐a‐chipdeveloping pages 16-19).

Angiogenic assays: Embed primary choroidal endothelial cells in type I collagen to form choriocapillaris-like tubes; co-culture overlying RPE from day 0 to promote sprouting or add later to test inhibition; neutralize VEGF and bFGF to dissect paracrine control (ghareeb2020coculturetechniquesfor pages 9-10).

Secretome profiling: Perform longitudinal, compartment-resolved multiplex of VEGF/PlGF/PDGF and chemokines, with media-only controls, technical duplicates, pooling for low-abundance factors, and attention to dynamic range and detection limits; analyze with two-way ANOVA and orthogonal validation (eyre2019developingahuman pages 123-126, fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 16-16).

Microenvironment and rigor: Tune Bruch’s-like ECM stiffness/composition to optimize RPE maturation, use human primary cells where possible, incorporate perfusion, and run long-term studies with standardized reporting (ghareeb2020coculturetechniquesfor pages 9-10, eyre2019developingahuman pages 51-53). Track viability/ATP and caspase-3/7 to interpret paracrine changes independent of cell death (fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 4-6, fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 8-9).

This co-culture suite uses polarized RPE with compartmental sampling, rigorous barrier metrics, and 3D endothelial sprouting to resolve VEGF/PDGF and inflammatory crosstalk relevant to dry AMD. Controlled inflammatory/hypoxic stimuli, perfusion, and ECM tuning increase physiological fidelity, while multiplex profiling and neutralization tests mechanistically attribute paracrine drivers (fietz2023cocultivationofprimary pages 16-16, ghareeb2020coculturetechniquesfor pages 9-10, jahagirdar2021degenerativedisease‐on‐a‐chipdeveloping pages 16-19, eyre2019developingahuman pages 123-126).
