Proposal for GSK3β inhibitor CHIR99021 (Tocris 4423)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
CHIR99021 is a fully synthetic, small-molecule aminopyrimidine that potently inhibits glycogen synthase kinase-3 (GSK3), with common use and profiling as a GSK3β-selective tool compound and canonical Wnt/β-catenin pathway agonist in cell systems (notably embryonic stem cell self-renewal and iPSC generation) (Rethineswaran et al., 2021; Lyssiotis et al., 2011). In ocular biology, CHIR99021 has been implemented as a defined, stage-specific Wnt activator to accelerate and improve directed differentiation of human ESC/iPSC to retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), increasing MITF/OTX2, pigmentation, and RPE functional readouts (apical PEDF, basal VEGF, integrin αVβ5–dependent phagocytosis) when applied at 3 μM during a defined mid-specification window (days 8–14) (Leach et al., 2015). Beyond development and differentiation protocols, CHIR99021 (as CHIR trihydrochloride) has been developed into an in vivo pharmacology tool with validated LC/MS assays and tissue PK in mice; after a 25 mg/kg intraperitoneal dose, it achieves rapid systemic distribution and measurable retinal exposure with prolonged ocular retention relative to plasma and liver (plasma Tmax ~30 min, liver Tmax ~30 min, retina Tmax ~30 min; terminal half-lives ~118, 120, and 405 min, respectively) (DiCesare et al., 2023).

As a GSK3 inhibitor, CHIR99021 belongs to a class leveraged widely across neuroscience, regenerative medicine, and retinal biology. In retinal preclinical systems this class has been used in models of retinitis pigmentosa, optic nerve injury/regeneration, and steroid-induced glaucoma; clinically, lithium chloride (LiCl) remains the only FDA-approved GSK3 inhibitor but with a narrow therapeutic window and mixed efficacy for neurodegenerative disease (DiCesare et al., 2023). In RPE disease–relevant preclinical work, low-dose CHIR99021 exposure in RPE-derived models reduces production/secretion of the EFEMP1/F3 glycoprotein and broadly represses extracellular matrix (ECM), complement, protease, and TGFβ-associated components linked to sub-RPE deposit biology; in vivo, systemic CHIR99021 prevents formation of basal laminar deposits (BLamD) in a homozygous EFEMP1 R345W knock-in mouse (ML/DHRD, an AMD-like pathology) without detectable retinal toxicity on scotopic ERG and H&E histology over one month (DiCesare et al., 2023).

Therapeutic History  
Class history in neural/retinal indications: GSK3 is a central kinase with broad substrate scope (>100 substrates) and regulates Wnt/β-catenin, PI3K/Akt, mTOR, NF-κB, and apoptosis pathways central to retinal homeostasis and degeneration (Hottin et al., 2022). Across retinal neurodegeneration models, GSK3 inhibitors have shown neuroprotection (e.g., in rd10 retinitis pigmentosa, NMDA excitotoxicity, and diabetic retinopathy), and GSK3 inhibition is mechanistically linked to CNS axon regeneration pathways (e.g., via CRMP2, mTOR), though effects are context-dependent and can diverge across cell types and disease stages (Hottin et al., 2022). The translational record for systemic GSK3 inhibition is mixed; lithium’s broad CNS history underscores both feasibility and risk of narrow windows and systemic effects, motivating localized ocular delivery or targeted strategies for retinal disease (Hottin et al., 2022; DiCesare et al., 2023).

CHIR99021 in ocular biology and disease: In vitro, CHIR99021 is a core Wnt agonist in hESC/hiPSC-to-RPE protocols where it improves RPE specification, pigmentation, and function when properly staged; its effects can be recapitulated by WNT3A (canonical) but not by WNT5A (non-canonical), consistent with a canonical Wnt readout (Leach et al., 2015). In applied disease models:

- Anti-deposit/ECM remodeling in EFEMP1-driven pathology: Using ARPE-19 edited lines expressing HiBiT-tagged F3 (WT and R345W), CHIR99021 reduced F3 transcript and protein in both intracellular and secreted compartments without overt cytotoxicity; genetic knockdown of GSK3α/β phenocopied and synergized, supporting an on-target mechanism (DiCesare et al., 2023). Low-dose, longer-term exposure (1 μM for 1 week) suppressed ECM, complement, metalloproteinase, and TGFβ-associated proteins in proteomics (≥2-fold reductions in multiple collagens, MMP2, SERPINE1, LTBP2, TGFβ1; decreases in HMGB1/2, C3; reductions in FBLN1/2) and produced parallel RNA-seq changes (downregulation of genes linked to sub-RPE deposits and ECM crosslinking; upregulation of TYR/TYRP1 and PEDF) without detectable activation of canonical TCF reporters, establishing a low-dose on-target biochemical window (DiCesare et al., 2023). Functionally, 1 μM CHIR99021 for 1 week normalized the R345W F3–linked elevation in MMP2 activity and reduced HiBiT F3 secretion apically and basally in transwell cultures (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- In vivo efficacy and PK: In EFEMP1 R345W+/+ mice (~8 months old), systemic CHIR trihydrochloride (25 mg/kg IP, weekdays for 1 month) reduced the fraction of retinal FOVs containing BLamD from 14.205% to 4.167% and halved the mean deposit area (0.817 to 0.416 μm²), with no detectable ERG or histological toxicity (DiCesare et al., 2023). LC/MS/MS PK in plasma, liver, and retina confirmed rapid tissue distribution at 30 min and prolonged retinal retention (retina terminal T½ ~405 min) with estimated intraocular concentrations on the order of the effective in vitro range over the first hours (e.g., ~1.3 μM at 30 min and ~0.36 μM at 6 h) (DiCesare et al., 2023). Analytical assay development and rigorous methodology are reported (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Regeneration/reprogramming contexts: CHIR99021 is a frequent component of small-molecule cocktails that reprogram Müller glia toward photoreceptor-like fates, induce Rho+ cells in vitro and in degenerate mouse retinas, and is used in multi-pathway modulation (Wnt activation combined with inhibitors of TGF-β, BMP, and Notch) to enhance MC-derived photoreceptor-like outputs; effects vary with species/model, and cocktails require careful balance (Fujii et al., 2021; Hottin et al., 2022). CHIR99021 also appears in chemical reprogramming schemes that convert adult human RPE to dopaminergic neurons and in multi-factor protocols generating photoreceptor progenitors from RPE or iPSCs, generally in combination with other pathway modulators and epigenetic agents (Rzhanova et al., 2024).

- Safety and adverse contexts: Despite benefits in differentiation, in a large-lesion, macula-sized RPE wound-repair model designed to mimic GA, CHIR99021 hindered lesion closure and yielded morphologically suboptimal infiltrating cells, even though pigmentation outside the lesion increased; EMT-suppressing agents (RepSox, Y-27632) performed better for wound closure under those conditions (Bailey-Steinitz, 2021; Hottin et al., 2022). Reviews caution that GSK3 inhibition can activate VEGF via canonical Wnt and could exacerbate angiogenesis, raising a risk signal for wet AMD contexts and arguing for careful selection of indication (dry vs wet AMD), timing, and combination strategies (e.g., with anti-VEGF) (Hottin et al., 2022).

To our knowledge, no human clinical studies have evaluated CHIR99021 for dry AMD; the most advanced ocular efficacy package is the EFEMP1 R345W mouse, an AMD-like BLamD model, where CHIR achieved both biochemical (ECM/complement/protease remodeling) and morphologic (deposit reduction) endpoints with ocular exposure in the in vitro effective range and no detectable electroretinal toxicity over one month (DiCesare et al., 2023).

Mechanism of Action  
Direct target and pathway entry points: GSK3 exists as α and β isoforms (85% identity), is constitutively active under basal conditions, and is negatively regulated by serine phosphorylation (S21 on α, S9 on β). It phosphorylates primed substrates on Ser/Thr motifs often culminating in β-TrCP recognition and proteasomal degradation of targets including β-catenin in the absence of Wnt; Wnt pathway activation sequesters/inhibits GSK3 within the destruction complex, stabilizing β-catenin and altering transcription via TCF/LEF (Hottin et al., 2022). CHIR99021 pharmacologically inhibits GSK3β and thereby is a canonical Wnt/β-catenin activator in many contexts, explaining its frequent use to bias fate decisions in pluripotent and somatic reprogramming paradigms (Lyssiotis et al., 2011; Leach et al., 2015).

Dose-dependent Wnt signaling in RPE systems: In RPE-derived ARPE-19 models, CHIR99021 shows a clear dose window: at 1 μM for one week it decreases EFEMP1/F3 transcripts/protein and globally represses ECM/complement/TGFβ signatures without detectable activation of TCF reporters; at ≥2.5 μM, TCF reporter activation becomes detectable, indicating canonical Wnt activation above a threshold (DiCesare et al., 2023). This low-dose window was sufficient to reduce MMP2 activity associated with the R345W F3 secretory stress pathophysiology, consistent with broad extracellular remodeling (DiCesare et al., 2023).

On-target genetic support: siRNA knockdown of GSK3α and GSK3β individually reduces F3 and combined knockdown synergizes, phenocopying CHIR’s effect on F3 production and secretion, supporting a GSK3-dependent mechanism for its anti-F3/anti-ECM actions (DiCesare et al., 2023). The effect generalizes across cell types (ARPE-19, human dermal fibroblasts, NIH-3T3), consistent with GSK3’s broad role in secretory/ECM regulation (DiCesare et al., 2023).

Secretory/ECM/complement/TGFβ axis remodeling: One-week, low-dose CHIR caused ≥2-fold reductions in multiple collagens (α1(V), α2(IV), α1(IV), α3(IV)), MMP2, complement C3, HMGB1/2, and TGFβ-related proteins such as SERPINE1/PAI-1 and LTBP2, with transcript-level suppression of ECM crosslinking and EMT-proximal genes (e.g., FN1, LOX, WNT ligands), while upregulating RPE differentiation/pigmentation (TYR, TYRP1) and increasing PEDF (SERPINF1) (DiCesare et al., 2023). These data point to a coordinated shift of the RPE secretome toward reduced pathogenic ECM/complement burden and increased pro-RPE homeostatic factors (e.g., PEDF), in line with the observed mitigation of BLamD in vivo (DiCesare et al., 2023).

mTOR–lysosome/autophagy coupling: Outside the retina, CHIR99021 has been shown to inhibit the Rheb/mTOR axis and increase lysosomal activation and autophagy in late endothelial progenitor cells, with evidence that CHIR reduces phosphorylated mTOR and Rheb independent of β-catenin, via effects attributed to TSC2 and lysosomal mTOR localization (Rethineswaran et al., 2021). These studies connect GSK3β inhibition to increased autophagic flux through mTORC1 suppression at the lysosome, a mechanistic route that in principle converges on transcriptional lysosome biogenesis programs (e.g., TFEB) though TFEB itself was not directly assayed in these reports (Rethineswaran et al., 2021). In retinal disease biology, GSK3 interdigitates with oxidative stress (NRF2 regulation), inflammatory NF-κB signaling, and mTOR pathways that are all implicated in AMD pathogenesis; pharmacologic GSK3β inhibition can increase NRF2 and reduce ROS in diabetic contexts, and reduced GSK3 activity in microglia dampens LPS-induced NF-κB signaling, underscoring additional protective axes that may be engaged in RPE or choroid (Hottin et al., 2022).

Angiogenic and repair liabilities: GSK3 inhibition/Wnt activation can increase VEGF and angiogenesis; reviews caution this may be beneficial in dry AMD (neuroprotection/anti-inflammatory) but harmful in wet AMD, suggesting indication-specific selection and potential need for combination with anti-VEGF or restricted/local delivery (Hottin et al., 2022). In a large RPE wound model mimicking GA-scale atrophy, continuous CHIR exposure hindered repair, in contrast to EMT-suppressing agents that improved closure, highlighting a context where Wnt activation can be counterproductive for lesion repopulation (Bailey-Steinitz, 2021).

Mechanistic net assessment for dry AMD: In RPE disease–proximal models, CHIR99021’s dominant effects at low dose are anti-secretory for F3/EFEMP1 and anti-ECM/complement/TGFβ, with reduced MMP2 and enhanced RPE differentiation markers, culminating in prevention of BLamD in vivo without electrophysiologic deficits over one month (DiCesare et al., 2023). Independent data show CHIR increases autophagy and lysosomal function via mTOR inhibition in other cell types, a mechanistic route that could contribute to proteostasis restoration in the RPE but remains to be demonstrated directly in RPE/AMD systems (Rethineswaran et al., 2021).

Expected Effect  
Assay-level predictions under the stated hypothesis: The team’s hypothesis posits that CHIR99021 will inhibit GSK3β in RPE, facilitate TFEB nuclear localization, enhance lysosomal acidification and cathepsin D (CTSD) maturation, increase autophagic flux, and thereby prevent RPE atrophy in dry AMD. Direct evidence for TFEB nuclear localization or CTSD maturation with CHIR99021 in RPE is not yet provided in the extant literature summarized here; however, two mechanistic pillars are established:

- In RPE disease models, low-dose CHIR99021 reduces a causal secretory burden (EFEMP1/F3) and pathogenic ECM/complement/protease output, normalizes MMP2 activity, increases homeostatic PEDF, and prevents BLamD formation in vivo, providing a deposit-centric, mechanistically credible route to slow early and mid-stage dry AMD pathology that is driven by sub-RPE matrix remodeling and complement activation (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- In non-ocular cells, CHIR99021 inhibits mTOR signaling at the lysosome (reduced Rheb/p-mTOR) and increases lysosomal activation and autophagy independent of β-catenin, consistent with a mechanism that would be predicted to favor TFEB nuclear translocation, transcription of lysosome/autophagy genes, and maturation of proteases such as CTSD; these precise endpoints have not been measured in RPE with CHIR99021 and thus remain to be validated (Rethineswaran et al., 2021).

Given GSK3 expression in ARPE-19 and its manipulation in the cited experiments (use of anti-GSK3α/β antibodies and on-target siRNA phenocopy), RPE is a relevant target cell for CHIR99021’s kinase inhibition (DiCesare et al., 2023). Under oxidative and secretory stress common to dry AMD, one would expect CHIR99021 at sub-TCF-activating exposure (≈1 μM) to:

- Reduce EFEMP1/F3 transcription/secretion and broadly depress ECM and complement cues that seed and stabilize BLamD/drusen, thereby lowering sub-RPE deposit biogenesis and associated pro-inflammatory signaling (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Normalize MMP2 activity and reduce matrix proteolysis-driven remodeling that contributes to basal laminar/basal linear deposit architecture (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Enhance RPE homeostatic phenotype, reflected by increased pigmentation gene expression and PEDF secretion, which supports photoreceptor survival and RPE trophic function (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Potentially increase autophagic flux and lysosomal competence via mTOR inhibition, improving clearance of aggregation-prone or secretory-stressed substrates; this inference is supported mechanistically outside the retina and should be formally confirmed in RPE by LC3 flux, p62/SQSTM1 turnover, LAMP1/2, lysotracker, and cathepsin maturation assays (Rethineswaran et al., 2021).

Boundary conditions and risks for the expected effect: The low-dose window is critical; at ≥2.5 μM, CHIR99021 activates canonical Wnt/TCF reporters in RPE-derived cells, and continuous Wnt activation can impair large-lesion RPE repair and increase angiogenic drive (VEGF), both of which are undesirable in late-stage GA or in patients at risk of conversion to exudative AMD (DiCesare et al., 2023; Bailey-Steinitz, 2021; Hottin et al., 2022). In vivo PK after 25 mg/kg IP yields retinal concentrations in the 0.15–1.3 μM range over the first 24 h, overlapping the RPE efficacy window for EFEMP1/ECM suppression and below the Wnt activation threshold in vitro; however, local ocular delivery and controlled-release formulations may be preferable to avoid systemic exposure and to maintain sub-TCF-activating intraocular concentrations (DiCesare et al., 2023; Hottin et al., 2022).

Overall Evaluation  

Strengths  
- Disease-proximal efficacy with mechanistic depth. CHIR99021 reduces EFEMP1/F3 production (transcript and protein), broadly suppresses ECM/complement/TGFβ secretome programs, decreases MMP2 activity, upregulates pigmentation genes and PEDF, and prevents BLamD in an EFEMP1 R345W knock-in model—an AMD-like pathology characterized by sub-RPE matrix deposits (DiCesare et al., 2023). The convergence of proteomics, transcriptomics, reporter assays, and genetic knockdown around GSK3 establishes on-target pathway control in RPE-derived systems (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Ocular exposure and safety. Tissue PK demonstrates retinal penetrance and prolonged retention (terminal T½ ≈ 6.75 h), with estimated intraocular levels overlapping the in vitro effective window; one month of systemic dosing at 25 mg/kg produced no detectable ERG or histological abnormalities in adult mice, supporting a tolerability window (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Plausible extension to proteostasis/autophagy. Independent mechanistic data show CHIR99021 inhibits mTORC1 signaling at the lysosome and increases autophagy/lysosomal activation via the TSC2–Rheb axis in non-ocular cells, a route that could bolster RPE lysosomal function under drusen/BLamD-associated stress even if TFEB was not yet directly measured in RPE (Rethineswaran et al., 2021).

- Modality versatility with established use. Extensive prior use of CHIR99021 as a defined Wnt agonist in RPE/retinal differentiation and reprogramming provides a deep operations base for dosing/timing and cell-specific responses, which can be leveraged to avoid undesired pathway activation during therapy (Leach et al., 2015; Fujii et al., 2021).

Weaknesses and risks  
- Translational maturity and source quality. The core anti-deposit efficacy and ocular PK/safety package resides in a bioRxiv preprint; while comprehensive and internally consistent, it lacks peer-reviewed confirmation and replication across independent labs and models (DiCesare et al., 2023). Moreover, the EFEMP1 R345W model (ML/DHRD) is a rare, early-onset macular dystrophy with AMD-like deposits; it does not recapitulate the full aging/inflammation/metabolic milieu of typical dry AMD (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Wnt/angiogenesis liability and wound repair. GSK3β inhibition is a canonical Wnt activator and can increase VEGF/angiogenesis, potentially contraindicating use in patients with neovascular risk; in RPE monolayers with macula-sized lesions, CHIR99021 impaired lesion repair despite increasing peripheral pigmentation, highlighting a deleterious effect in GA-like settings requiring repopulation (Hottin et al., 2022; Bailey-Steinitz, 2021).

- Dose/timing and cell-type dependency. Benefits of GSK3 modulation are highly context-dependent in retinal disease; the same pathway promotes different outcomes across disease stage and cell types (photoreceptors, Müller glia, RPE, endothelium). Reviews emphasize that treatment timing, duration, and targeted retinal layer will determine benefit vs harm (DiCesare et al., 2023; Hottin et al., 2022). The in vitro window where 1 μM avoids TCF activation is narrow; in vivo pharmacology must control ocular exposure carefully (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Mechanistic gap for the autophagy/TFEB claim in RPE. Although CHIR99021 increases autophagy through mTOR suppression in other cell types, direct evidence in human RPE or RPE-derived models for TFEB nuclear translocation, lysosomal acidification improvements, or CTSD maturation with CHIR99021 is currently absent from the surveyed literature; this is an experimental gap relative to the team’s stated hypothesis (Rethineswaran et al., 2021).

Development recommendations  
- Indication scoping to dry AMD segments with deposit-dominated pathology. Based on anti-ECM/complement and anti-BLamD efficacy, prioritize early/mid dry AMD segments characterized by drusen/sub-RPE deposits and low neovascular risk; de-prioritize active geographic atrophy where repopulation/repair of large RPE lesions is the therapeutic aim, given the CHIR-associated repair impairment in vitro (DiCesare et al., 2023; Bailey-Steinitz, 2021).

- Local ocular delivery to widen the therapeutic index. Intravitreal or suprachoroidal controlled-release systems should be evaluated to maintain intraocular concentrations in the ~0.1–1 μM range that achieve biochemical efficacy but remain below the canonical Wnt activation threshold, while minimizing systemic exposure and wet-AMD conversion risk; the reviews support local delivery as a strategy for GSK3-based ocular interventions (DiCesare et al., 2023; Hottin et al., 2022).

- Mechanistic validation in RPE for proteostasis endpoints. In primary hfRPE and iPSC-RPE, directly measure CHIR99021’s impact on autophagy and lysosome: LC3-II flux (±bafilomycin A1), p62/SQSTM1 turnover, TFEB nuclear localization (immunofluorescence/fractionation), lysosomal pH (LysoSensor), LAMP1/2 expression, and CTSD maturation/activity; determine whether these benefits occur at sub-TCF-activating exposures and under relevant stresses (oxidative, lipid, complement) (DiCesare et al., 2023; Rethineswaran et al., 2021).

- Angiogenic surveillance and combination strategies. Quantify VEGF and angiogenic markers (VEGFA mRNA/protein, HIF-1α targets) in RPE/choroid after CHIR, and consider anti-VEGF co-therapy if Wnt-VEGF coupling poses a risk; preclinical reviews support the rationale for combination or timing-restricted inhibition to avoid pro-angiogenic consequences (Hottin et al., 2022).

- Broaden efficacy across dry AMD models. Test CHIR in aging-related and inflammation-metabolism models (e.g., high-fat diet + complement stress; smoke exposure; ApoE variants) in addition to EFEMP1 R345W to ensure generalizability beyond monogenic ECM disease; define the minimal exposure and duration to alter drusen/BLamD dynamics and microglial/complement activation (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Dose-finding anchored to ocular PK/PD. Use the established LC/MS retinal assay to map intravitreal/subconjunctival/suprachoroidal dose to retinal/choroidal concentration–time profiles; overlay with PD markers (F3 secretion, ECM transcripts, complement, PEDF) to define a sub-TCF window in vivo; confirm absence of ERG deficits and retinal histopathology across multi-month dosing (DiCesare et al., 2023).

- Translational vigilance. Lithium’s status as the sole FDA-approved GSK3 inhibitor and mixed outcomes of GSK3 modulation in retinal disease underscore the need to target specific retinal layers or cell types and to avoid broad systemic inhibition; this aligns with adopting local ocular delivery and possibly RPE-targeted formulations (DiCesare et al., 2023; Hottin et al., 2022).

Conclusion  
On balance, CHIR99021 has a compelling, disease-proximal efficacy package for dry AMD centered on sub-RPE deposit biology. At low doses that do not detectably activate canonical Wnt, CHIR99021 reduces EFEMP1/F3 and globally attenuates ECM/complement/protease pathways in RPE-derived systems, increases PEDF and pigmentation gene expression, and prevents BLamD formation in an EFEMP1 R345W mouse model, with retinal exposures matching in vitro efficacy windows and month-long dosing without ERG/histology toxicity (DiCesare et al., 2023). Independent mechanistic data demonstrate CHIR-mediated inhibition of Rheb/mTOR and increased lysosomal/autophagic activity in non-ocular cells, consistent with the proteostasis-restoring arm of the team’s hypothesis, but direct evidence for TFEB nuclear translocation and CTSD maturation in RPE with CHIR is currently lacking and should be considered a key validation gap (Rethineswaran et al., 2021). The principal risks are Wnt-linked angiogenesis (potentially harmful in wet AMD) and impaired RPE wound repair in GA-like lesions when Wnt activation is sustained; these risks argue for a dry-AMD, early/mid-stage positioning, careful intraocular dose control to remain below the TCF activation threshold, and consideration of local delivery and/or anti-VEGF combinations in higher-risk patients (Hottin et al., 2022; Bailey-Steinitz, 2021).

Overall, CHIR99021 is a promising repurposed candidate for dry AMD if developed within a well-defined exposure window and indication niche. Its strengths are a clear kinase target with on-target cellular genetics, strong anti-deposit and secretome remodeling data in RPE and in vivo, ocular exposure that aligns with cellular pharmacology, and plausible lysosome/autophagy engagement. Its weaknesses are translational maturity (preprint stage; single primary in vivo model), Wnt-associated angiogenesis and repair liabilities at higher/continuous exposure, and incomplete validation of the autophagy/TFEB axis in RPE. A go decision for preclinical advancement is justified, contingent on rapid mechanistic confirmation in RPE for autophagy/lysosome endpoints, rigorous angiogenesis/wound-repair safety pharmacology, and PK/PD-guided local delivery strategies that maintain sub-TCF intraocular concentrations (DiCesare et al., 2023; Hottin et al., 2022; Rethineswaran et al., 2021).
