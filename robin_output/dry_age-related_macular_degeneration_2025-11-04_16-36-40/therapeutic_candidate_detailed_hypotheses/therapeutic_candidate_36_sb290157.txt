Proposal for SB290157 (Sigma SML0390)

Below is our comprehensive evaluation of SB290157 as a repurposed therapeutic candidate for dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD), with a focus on its chemical origin, its therapeutic history, the detailed mechanism of action at the molecular level, the expected pharmacological effects in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) models, and an overall assessment of strengths and weaknesses.

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
SB290157 is a fully synthetic, nonpeptidic small‐molecule agent originally discovered as a selective antagonist of the complement anaphylatoxin C3a receptor (C3aR). Chemically, it is defined as N2-[(2,2-diphenylethoxy)acetyl]-L-arginine and was identified via high-throughput screening of chemical libraries using membranes from cells stably expressing human C3aR, such as RBL-2H3 cells (Ames et al., 2001). The lead compound was chemically optimized from an initial hit (SKF 63649) to improve affinity and potency, and its synthesis has been well described using peptide–like solid‐phase synthetic strategies (e.g., Fmoc protecting group chemistry on Wang resin) thereby yielding a compound with high purity (≥95%) and reproducible physicochemical properties (Ames et al., 2001). SB290157 belongs to a class of compounds that modulate G protein–coupled receptors (GPCRs), in this case a complement receptor, and similar nonpeptidic small‐molecule ligands have been employed in preclinical studies to probe inflammatory pathways in various disease states (Iyer et al., 2017; Ames et al., 2001). Its discovery set an important precedent for the therapeutic targeting of complement receptors, especially given the historical absence of potent, selective C3aR antagonists, and it has been widely used as a research tool in animal models and cell-based assays to interrogate the physiological roles of C3a/C3aR signaling (Ames et al., 2001).

Therapeutic History:  
SB290157 has been extensively employed in preclinical models to examine the role of C3aR in inflammation and immune modulation. In early studies, it was shown to antagonize C3a-induced intracellular calcium mobilization and receptor internalization in RBL-2H3 cells and human neutrophils, with reported IC50 values in the low-nanomolar range (approximately 27–30 nM), suggesting robust antagonism under defined experimental conditions (Ames et al., 2001). In vivo, SB290157 demonstrated antiinflammatory efficacy by reducing neutrophil recruitment in guinea pig models of lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced airway neutrophilia and by decreasing paw edema in rat adjuvant-induced arthritis models through intraperitoneal dosing (Ames et al., 2001). Although valuable as a pharmacological probe, its history is mired by concerns as several subsequent investigations indicated that SB290157 can exhibit partial agonist activity—depending on the assay system and receptor expression context—and even engage an off-target complement receptor, C5aR2, to induce β-arrestin recruitment (Coulthard, 2015; Li et al., 2021; Mathieu et al., 2005). With more than 70 publications referencing its use, SB290157 remains one of the most commonly used C3aR inhibitors; however, it is essentially confined to the research arena and has never reached clinical development for AMD or other indications (Li et al., 2021).  
There is emerging evidence in ophthalmologic research relevant to dry AMD. For example, an in vitro human fetal RPE cell model has shown that complement fragment C3a can drive the formation of sub-retinal pigment epithelium deposits via dysregulation of the ubiquitin proteasome pathway; importantly, co-application of SB290157 with recombinant C3a in these cultures prevented the abnormal deposit formation, suggesting that C3aR blockade can modulate key pathological events relevant to AMD pathogenesis (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018). Despite this promising mechanistic link, SB290157 itself has not been advanced clinically, and its use in retinal models remains primarily exploratory. Moreover, the existence of antagonistic and agonistic properties in various assay systems necessitates careful interpretation of its efficacy when contemplating the translation to human ocular diseases (Iyer et al., 2017; Li et al., 2021).

Mechanism of Action:  
At the molecular level, SB290157 has been characterized as a competitive antagonist that binds to the orthosteric site of the C3a receptor, thereby inhibiting the native ligand, C3a, from eliciting its downstream signaling responses. In classic radioligand binding assays using 125I-labeled C3a, SB290157 displaces the radiolabeled peptide with an IC50 of ~200 nM, indicating it competes effectively with C3a (Ames et al., 2001). Functionally, this displacement correlates with inhibition of C3a-induced intracellular calcium mobilization, a key second messenger response mediated via G-protein coupling to pertussis-toxin–sensitive Gi/o proteins (Ames et al., 2001). Additionally, SB290157 has been shown to block C3a-mediated receptor internalization in immune cells, further supporting its role in antagonizing C3aR activation (Ames et al., 2001; Iyer et al., 2017).  
Beyond the canonical receptor blockade, several studies have pointed out that SB290157’s activity is highly context-dependent. In systems where C3aR is overexpressed—such as recombinant CHO or transfected RBL cells—SB290157 can paradoxically act as an agonist to trigger intracellular signaling cascades like ERK phosphorylation, potentially due to rapid receptor desensitization or kinetic differences in ligand-receptor interaction (Coulthard, 2015; Li et al., 2021; Mathieu et al., 2005). Moreover, its off-target partial agonism at the closely related receptor C5aR2 has been documented, wherein it mediates β-arrestin recruitment at higher concentrations, thereby dampening C5a-induced ERK signaling in primary macrophages (Li et al., 2021; Mathieu et al., 2005). Such dual or biased pharmacological properties underscore that SB290157’s molecular actions cannot be solely categorized as pure antagonism without considering assay conditions and cell contexts (Li et al., 2021; Mathieu et al., 2005).  
In the context of RPE cells, C3a binding to C3aR is hypothesized to disrupt intracellular calcium homeostasis, leading to lysosomal alkalinization—a key factor that impairs the maturation and proteolytic activity of lysosomal enzymes such as cathepsin D (CTSD). Cathepsin D is critical for proper turnover of extracellular matrix (ECM) components and degradation of proteinaceous aggregates; its dysfunction is implicated in drusen formation, a hallmark of dry AMD (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018). By competitively inhibiting C3a binding, SB290157 is expected to mitigate abnormal Ca2+ signaling, maintain lysosomal acidification, and therefore preserve CTSD processing and activity, which in turn would prevent or lessen the accumulation of drusen-like deposits (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018; Iyer et al., 2017). This proposed mechanism is supported by the observation that in hfRPE cultures, SB290157 co-application with C3a prevented both the upregulation of C3aR expression and the subsequent deposition of abnormal ECM structures (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018). Nonetheless, the precise molecular interactions at the receptor—the positioning of the diphenylethoxy moiety and arginine side chain—is critical for binding affinity and selectivity (Iyer et al., 2017; Mishra et al., 2025), and minor alterations in these interactions might contribute to the observed functional variability.

Expected Effect:  
Based on the hypothesis for dry AMD, SB290157 is expected to act on RPE cells by antagonizing the C3aR, thereby preventing the cascade of intracellular events that lead to lysosomal stress. In a typical RPE culture assay where C3a is exogenously added—thereby simulating enhanced complement activity as observed in AMD—SB290157 should block C3a binding, which would normally result in the perturbation of lysosomal pH homeostasis. The blockade of this receptor is envisaged to preserve the normally acidic lysosomal environment required for the optimal maturation and proteolytic function of cathepsin D (CTSD), an enzyme whose dysfunction is implicated in the formation of drusen and the progression of dry AMD (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018; Iyer et al., 2017).  
Furthermore, by preserving lysosomal function, SB290157 is expected to reduce subsequent activation of the inflammasome—a multi-protein complex that is triggered when lysosomal destabilization occurs—and thus attenuate the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β. This cascade is particularly relevant because chronic inflammation is thought to drive many of the degenerative changes associated with AMD (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018). In vitro, RPE cells pre-treated with SB290157 before C3a challenge would be anticipated to maintain normal lysosomal acidity (measured by appropriate pH-sensitive dyes), exhibit proper CTSD processing (as observed by Western blot analysis for mature cathepsin D forms), and display reduced markers of inflammasome activation such as caspase-1 cleavage and IL-1β release. Gene expression studies in these cells might also reveal normalized levels of key ECM components and proteases, ultimately leading to a reduction in sub-RPE deposit accumulation (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018).  
It is important to note that C3aR is expressed on RPE cells and its activation has been implicated in additional downstream effects—including modulation of the ubiquitin proteasome pathway and extracellular matrix turnover—which further contribute to cell dysfunction and deposit formation. Therefore, antagonism of C3aR by SB290157, if effective and selective, could theoretically interrupt this pathogenic cascade and offer a protective effect against drusen-mediated RPE degeneration in dry AMD (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018; Ames et al., 2001).

Overall Evaluation:  
SB290157 offers a compelling mechanistic rationale for repurposing in dry AMD given the central role of complement dysregulation in the pathogenesis of the disease. Among its strengths is a well-documented ability to block C3a-induced signaling events across multiple preclinical models. Early characterizations demonstrated that SB290157 effectively inhibited intracellular Ca2+ mobilization, receptor internalization, and other pro-inflammatory events in both immune cells and engineered cell systems (Ames et al., 2001). Its application in an hfRPE model demonstrated that C3a-induced sub-RPE deposit formation can be reversed upon treatment with SB290157, underscoring its potential utility in preventing one of the critical pathological features of dry AMD (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018). Furthermore, the known rodent tolerability and defined in vivo pharmacokinetics observed in other disease models—such as LPS-induced airway inflammation and adjuvant-induced arthritis—support the concept that SB290157 can be delivered systemically with demonstrable pharmacodynamic effects (Ames et al., 2001).  

However, several weaknesses temper enthusiasm for immediate clinical translation. A significant concern is the compound’s context-dependent pharmacology. Multiple studies have reported that SB290157 can function as a partial or even full agonist under certain conditions, particularly in overexpression systems, and paradoxically activate C3aR instead of inhibiting it (Coulthard, 2015; Li et al., 2021; Mathieu et al., 2005). Such functional divergence is critical when considering a therapy designed to block a deleterious signal in a delicate tissue such as the retina. Moreover, SB290157 has been shown to exhibit off-target effects, including partial agonism at C5aR2—a related complement receptor—which may confound downstream anti-inflammatory responses and produce unintended biological effects (Li et al., 2021; Mathieu et al., 2005).  
Additionally, while there is evidence of efficacy in non-ocular models and even in an hfRPE culture system, there is a paucity of robust in vivo data specifically demonstrating efficacy in ocular or AMD preclinical models. Modern translational approaches demand rigorous evaluation of pharmacokinetics, tissue penetration, ocular tolerability, and local off-target activity in the complex microenvironment of the eye—data that remain limited for SB290157 in the AMD setting (Iyer et al., 2017; Li et al., 2021). Furthermore, owing to its reputation as a “controversial” tool compound, some in the field recommend the use of complementary genetic models (e.g., C3aR knockout mice) to validate findings derived from its use (Coulthard, 2015; Li et al., 2021). This underscores the need for next-generation C3aR antagonists with improved selectivity and a more predictable pharmacodynamic profile if the goal is clinical translation for dry AMD.  

In summary, SB290157 has strong theoretical appeal as a means to interrupt a pathological complement cascade that leads to lysosomal dysfunction, impaired CTSD maturation, and ultimately drusen formation in dry AMD. Its prior use in various inflammatory and in vitro RPE models, along with the biochemistry linking C3aR activation to intracellular Ca2+ dysregulation and lysosomal stress, supports the hypothesis that C3aR blockade may be beneficial in preserving RPE function (Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018; Iyer et al., 2017). Nonetheless, the intrinsic limitations of SB290157—including its potential for agonistic behavior, off-target C5aR2 activity, and inconsistent effects across different cellular contexts—present significant challenges that must be addressed prior to advancing this candidate into clinical trials for AMD (Ames et al., 2001; Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018; Li et al., 2021).

Overall Evaluation:  
In conclusion, SB290157 is a well-characterized research tool with documented efficacy in blocking complement-mediated inflammatory responses in several animal models as well as in an hfRPE culture system, making it a promising candidate to test the hypothesis that C3aR blockade can prevent lysosomal alkalinization and CTSD dysfunction in dry AMD. Its strengths include a defined mechanism of competitive antagonism at C3aR, cross-species activity, and experimentally demonstrated protective effects against abnormal sub-RPE deposit formation. However, its context-dependent agonist behavior and off-target activation of C5aR2 raise significant concerns about its selectivity and reliability as a clinical therapeutic. Additionally, the limited data regarding ocular pharmacokinetics and direct efficacy in robust in vivo dry AMD models suggest that further validation is required. Thus, while the underlying rationale for targeting complement-mediated lysosomal dysfunction remains compelling, SB290157 is best viewed as a stepping stone toward the development of next-generation C3aR antagonists with improved pharmacological profiles for potential clinical application in dry AMD (Ames et al., 2001; Fernandez-Godino & Pierce, 2018; Li et al., 2021).
