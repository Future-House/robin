Proposal for Calpeptin (Sigma C1571)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
Calpeptin (Sigma C1571) is a synthetic, cell‐permeable peptidomimetic inhibitor originally developed to target calpain family proteases. It belongs to a class of small molecule calpain inhibitors that are designed to mimic specific peptide sequences found in the endogenous inhibitor calpastatin. These peptidomimetics have been refined chemically to enable cellular penetration and high-affinity binding to the calpain active site, thereby blocking its auto‐proteolytic activation process (Vu et al., 2022). Calpeptin was discovered through efforts aimed at identifying effective inhibitors of calcium‐dependent cysteine proteases that contribute to proteolytic events in pathogenic conditions. Its synthesis involves conventional organic synthesis techniques designed to yield a molecule with the critical chemical features—namely, an amide backbone that mimics peptide bonds and side chain substitutions that ensure selectivity and stability in biological systems. This compound has been widely adopted as a research tool to probe calpain‐dependent cellular processes, particularly in the context of neurodegeneration and ocular pathologies (Vu et al., 2022).

Therapeutic History:  
Calpeptin has a rich history as a biochemical tool in both in vitro and in vivo experimental paradigms. Within the realm of ocular disease research, calpain inhibitors similar in mechanism to calpeptin have been evaluated in multiple models of retinal degeneration. For example, studies using rodent models of inherited retinal degeneration, such as those employing the Rho P23H‐1 rat model, have demonstrated that calpeptin—alongside other calpain inhibitors—can reduce photoreceptor cell death and modulate apoptotic pathways (Azam & Jastrzebska, 2025). Although the majority of these studies have been conducted in the context of retinitis pigmentosa or other inherited forms of retinal degeneration, the underlying mechanism—namely, the inhibition of aberrant calpain activation leading to cell death—is also highly relevant to dry age‐related macular degeneration (Azam & Jastrzebska, 2025). In various experimental systems, such as in neuroblastoma cells and retinal cell cultures subject to stress (e.g., hypoxia or oxidative challenge), calpeptin has been shown to reduce calpain‐mediated cleavage of key substrates and inhibit caspase activation, which correlates with improved cell survival. Although direct clinical trials using calpeptin in dry AMD have not yet been reported, its use in ocular research, combined with evidence from preclinical models involving other calpain inhibitors (such as SNJ‐1945 and SJA6017), underscores the translational potential of this approach (Vu et al., 2022).

Mechanism of Action:  
Calpeptin functions by inhibiting the auto‐proteolytic activation of calpain isoforms, particularly cytosolic calpains such as CAPN1 and CAPN5. Under physiological conditions, calpains are maintained in an inactive state; however, under pathological conditions marked by calcium dysregulation—such as those encountered in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) during oxidative stress—calpains become activated and catalyze the proteolytic cleavage of vital cellular substrates. Calpeptin binds to the protease’s active site, thereby preventing the conformational changes necessary for auto‐activation and subsequent proteolytic activity (Vu et al., 2022).  
In the specific context of dry AMD, the hypothesis is that drusen accumulation leads to oxidative and metabolic stress in the RPE. This stress triggers calcium overload, which in turn activates calpains. One of the deleterious consequences of calpain activation is lysosomal membrane permeabilization (LMP). LMP destabilizes the lysosomal environment, leading to the dissipation of the proton gradient maintained by v-ATPase complexes and resulting in impaired acidification. This loss of lysosomal integrity negatively affects the processing and activity of lysosomal hydrolases, particularly cathepsin D (CTSD), which is essential for proper degradative and autophagic functions. By inhibiting calpain activity, calpeptin is expected to prevent the proteolytic degradation of components critical to lysosomal membrane stability, such as v-ATPase subunits, thereby preserving lysosomal pH and ensuring proper CTSD processing (Vu et al., 2022). The preservation of these lysosomal functions is crucial in the context of drusen-induced stress in dry AMD, as it directly addresses the underlying mechanism of lysosomal dysfunction and subsequent cell death.

Expected Effect:  
The proposed therapeutic application of calpeptin in dry AMD is based on its ability to mitigate calpain-mediated lysosomal damage in the retinal pigment epithelium. In an experimental setup where RPE cells are exposed to oxidative or drusen-like stress, one would expect calpeptin to reduce calpain activation, thereby decreasing lysosomal membrane permeabilization. This inhibition should lead to the stabilization of v-ATPase complexes, which are responsible for maintaining the acidic environment within lysosomes. Consequently, normal lysosomal acidification is preserved, allowing for proper activation and function of cathepsin D (CTSD), a critical enzyme in the degradation of photoreceptor outer segments and in cellular homeostasis (Azam & Jastrzebska, 2025; Vu et al., 2022).  
Furthermore, by blocking the calpain-driven cleavage events that would otherwise lead to cytoskeletal disorganization and cellular apoptosis, calpeptin is anticipated to reduce overall caspase activation and apoptotic signaling in RPE cells. Studies in other retinal disease models have demonstrated that calpain inhibition can restore cellular homeostasis by reducing markers of apoptosis and mitigating structural damage (Azam & Jastrzebska, 2025; Vu et al., 2022). Given that RPE cells are known to express significant levels of calpains and are highly susceptible to calcium dysregulation upon exposure to drusen and oxidative stress, the use of calpeptin is expected to yield improved cellular viability and function in these cells. This outcome would be assessed through indicators such as restored lysosomal pH, reduction in cytosolic release of lysosomal enzymes, and maintenance of normal CTSD processing, ultimately contributing to neuroprotection and preservation of retinal integrity in dry AMD (Vu et al., 2022).

Overall Evaluation:  
A comprehensive review of the literature suggests that calpeptin holds promise as a repurposed therapeutic candidate for the treatment of dry age-related macular degeneration. Its classification as a cell-permeable peptidomimetic inhibitor of calpain places it within a well-studied drug class known for modulating pathological proteolytic cascades initiated by calcium dysregulation. The strengths of calpeptin include its well-characterized mechanism of action—namely, the inhibition of calpain auto-proteolysis, which is pivotal for preventing the proteolytic breakdown of critical lysosomal components such as v-ATPase (Vu et al., 2022).  
Moreover, the therapeutic history of calpeptin and its analogues in various retinal degeneration models supports its potential applicability in dry AMD. Experimental evidence from studies in Rho P23H transgenic rat models, among others, demonstrates that calpain inhibitors can confer neuroprotection by reducing apoptotic pathways, preserving cellular integrity, and maintaining lysosomal function in stressed retinal cells (Azam & Jastrzebska, 2025). The existing knowledge regarding in vivo tolerability and the reversible nature of calpeptin’s inhibitory effects further bolster its candidacy for preclinical testing targeting dry AMD (Vu et al., 2022).

Despite these favorable attributes, several weaknesses and challenges must be acknowledged. First, while the mechanism underpinning calpeptin’s action—specifically, its inhibition of calpain-mediated lysosomal damage—is strongly supported by in vitro and retinal degeneration model data, direct evidence linking calpeptin to improved outcomes in dry AMD models remains limited (Azam & Jastrzebska, 2025; Vu et al., 2022). Furthermore, the hypothesized benefit of stabilizing v-ATPase complexes and preserving CTSD activity, although biologically plausible, requires further experimental validation in an AMD context to confirm that calpeptin’s inhibition of calpain is sufficient to counteract drusen-induced lysosomal stress.  
An additional concern relates to target selectivity. Calpain inhibitors, including calpeptin, can potentially exhibit off-target effects due to the conserved structure of the catalytic domains within the calpain family and related proteases. This lack of absolute specificity may lead to unintended inhibition of other proteases, such as certain cathepsins, which could compromise cellular function if not tightly regulated (Vu et al., 2022). Furthermore, chronic ocular delivery of small molecule inhibitors poses formulation challenges, as therapeutic concentrations must be maintained in the retina without eliciting local toxicity. The eye’s compartmentalized anatomy offers the advantage of localized delivery (e.g., via intravitreal injections or topical applications), yet achieving sustained and controlled drug release remains a technical hurdle that must be addressed in subsequent preclinical studies (Vu et al., 2022).  
Another potential weakness is the complexity of dry AMD pathophysiology itself. Dry AMD is characterized by a multifactorial etiology involving oxidative stress, complement dysregulation, and inflammatory processes, in addition to lysosomal dysfunction. While addressing calpain-mediated LMP directly targets one critical aspect of the disease mechanism, comprehensive protection of RPE cells may require combination approaches. Calpeptin’s role might be best viewed as a part of a broader therapeutic cocktail aimed at modulating multiple convergent pathways implicated in dry AMD progression (Vu et al., 2022).  
In summary, calpeptin shows considerable potential as a repurposed drug candidate for dry AMD based on its well-documented pharmacological profile and its mechanistic alignment with the proposed therapeutic strategy—namely, inhibiting calpain-mediated lysosomal destabilization in RPE cells. Its synthetic origin, cell-permeable nature, and established use in experimental models of retinal degeneration make it an attractive candidate for further preclinical evaluation. However, the absence of direct evidence in dry AMD models, in addition to concerns regarding specificity, delivery, and the need for combination intervention strategies, underscores the importance of rigorous preclinical studies to further validate calpeptin’s efficacy and safety in this indication (Vu et al., 2022; Azam & Jastrzebska, 2025). Overall, while promising, calpeptin will require targeted studies to evaluate its impact on lysosomal membrane permeabilization, v-ATPase stabilization, and cathepsin D activity in the context of drusen-induced oxidative stress, thereby confirming its potential to protect RPEs and mitigate the progression of dry age-related macular degeneration (Vu et al., 2022).
