Proposal for ZLN005 (Sigma SML1649)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
ZLN005 is a synthetic, small-molecule benzimidazole identified by high-throughput screening of ~48,000 compounds using a HEK293 cell line harboring a human PGC-1α promoter–driven luciferase reporter; it was selected for its ability to transcriptionally induce the Ppargc1a (PGC-1α) gene and validated in follow-up orthogonal assays and cell types (Zhang et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2021). Chemically, ZLN005 is 2-(4-tert-butylphenyl)-1H-benzimidazole with a reported molecular weight of 250.3; it is repeatedly described as a “small-molecule PGC-1α transcriptional activator/agonist” and a “specific and selective transcriptional inducer of PGC-1α expression” (Bleckwehl et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2013; Maurice et al., 2022). The initial discovery campaign also yielded a related backbone compound (ZLN027) that showed cytotoxicity, whereas ZLN005 did not under the reported in vitro conditions, and ZLN005 was later found to inhibit luciferases—an assay interference red flag that necessitates careful interpretation of luciferase-based readouts (Zhang et al., 2013).

Pharmacologic class and precedent: ZLN005 belongs to the class of tool/investigational small molecules that transcriptionally upregulate PGC-1α and thereby stimulate mitochondrial biogenesis, oxidative phosphorylation, and fatty-acid oxidation in a tissue-selective fashion (Zhang et al., 2013). This class has been deployed broadly in preclinical systems—skeletal muscle, cardiomyocytes, neurons, kidney, lung epithelium, vascular smooth muscle, and retinal pigment epithelium (RPE)—to probe whether enhancing PGC-1α can restore mitochondrial capacity, reduce oxidative stress, and improve cell/tissue resilience (Zhang et al., 2013; Liu et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2025; Shu et al., 2021). A subset of studies emphasizes that ZLN005 acts upstream of AMPK→PGC-1α/MEF2 transcriptional circuits and engages canonical mitochondrial biogenesis regulators (NRF1/2, TFAM, ERRs), while in some tissues it promotes downstream fatty-acid oxidation via CPT-1α, aligning with the recognized role of PGC-1α as a master coactivator of oxidative metabolism (Zhang et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2020).

Therapeutic History  
ZLN005 has no reported clinical use. Its portfolio of preclinical applications includes:

- Metabolic disease: In diabetic db/db mice, chronic oral ZLN005 (15 mg/kg/day for 6 weeks) improved glucose tolerance, insulin sensitivity, and dyslipidemia, with induction of PGC-1α and mitochondrial/FAO gene programs in skeletal muscle and reduced hepatic gluconeogenic transcripts; AMPK/ACC phosphorylation rose in muscle but not liver, consistent with tissue selectivity (Zhang et al., 2013). Basic single-dose oral pharmacokinetics in db/db mice showed rapid plasma absorption and high initial liver exposure, with relatively stable muscle exposure over 4 hours (Zhang et al., 2013). A short-term rat safety study (75 mg/kg/day, 14 days) reported survival with no obvious changes in body weight gain or gross metabolic parameters (Zhang et al., 2013).

- Skeletal muscle cells: In L6 myotubes, ZLN005 dose-dependently increased PGC-1α mRNA, glucose uptake, and palmitate oxidation; AMPK and ACC phosphorylation increased, PGC-1α induction was AMPK-dependent (Compound C–sensitive), and MEF2 promoter integrity was required, consistent with a transcriptional mechanism (Zhang et al., 2013).

- Kidney ischemia–reperfusion injury (AKI): In mouse I/R models and H/R-injured renal tubular cells, ZLN005 restored PGC-1α and CPT-1α expression, improved mitochondrial ultrastructure/membrane potential, reduced ROS and lipid peroxidation (H₂O₂, MDA), lessened apoptosis, and required CPT-1α activity for benefit; high concentrations were toxic to HK2 cells (Wang et al., 2021).

- Neuronal injury: ZLN005 improved viability in OGD/R-injured PC12 cells, induced PGC-1α and antioxidant genes (SOD1/HO-1), and post-ischemia administration reduced infarct volume and neurological deficit in rat tMCAO, indicating neuroprotection likely via PGC-1α-driven mitochondrial and antioxidant programs; in a separate study, ZLN005 crossed the blood–brain barrier and protected against PBDE-47 neurotoxicity via PGC-1α-dependent mitochondrial biogenesis (Xu et al., 2018; Tian et al., 2023).

- Cardiomyocytes: In hESC-derived cardiomyocytes, brief ZLN005 exposure (10 μM, 48 h) increased PGC-1α and ERRγ, upregulated mitochondrial biogenesis and OXPHOS/FAO genes (NRF1, TFAM, respiratory subunits, CPT1b), increased mtDNA copy number, basal OCR and ATP, and drove structural/electrophysiological maturation (longer sarcomeres, more negative RMP, enhanced Ca²⁺ handling, increased CX43) (Liu et al., 2020).

- Lung epithelium/infection: In AECs (A549 and primary AEC2), ZLN005 increased PGC-1α and NRF1, improved mitochondrial bioenergetics, membrane potential, ATP, decreased ROS and senescence markers (p21, SA-β-gal), and these effects required PGC-1α (shPGC-1α attenuated benefit); in a P. aeruginosa pneumonia model, ZLN005 pretreatment (15 mg/kg × 3) reduced bacterial load and IL-1β and rescued SIRT3 in lung, consistent with PGC-1α–SIRT3 axis activation (Ma et al., 2023; Maurice et al., 2022).

- Vascular smooth muscle cells: ZLN005 increased PGC-1α, ESRRA, NRF1, mtDNA, and ATP, shifted mitochondrial dynamics toward fusion (↑MFN2, ↓DRP1), and dispersed perinuclear aggregates, indicating enhanced mitochondrial function (Zou et al., 2025).

- Retinal and RPE models: In ARPE-19, ZLN005 robustly increased PGC-1α, upregulated transcription factors, antioxidant enzymes and mitochondrial genes, improved basal/maximal respiration and spare capacity, and protected against oxidative insults (H₂O₂, oxLDL, NaIO₃); protection was PGC-1α-dependent; in primary human RPE (H-RPE), 10 μM ZLN005 selectively induced PGC-1α and blocked TGFβ₂-induced EMT and migration, with smaller fold-change than in ARPE-19 due to higher baseline PGC-1α (Tong et al., 2022; Shu et al., 2021). In ex vivo rd1 retinal explants (inherited photoreceptor degeneration), ZLN005 reduced overall calpain activity, shifted calpain isoform balance toward neuroprotective calpain-1 and away from calpain-2, and enhanced photoreceptor viability, supporting retinal neuroprotection via PGC-1α activation (Wang et al., 2025).

There are no reports that ZLN005 has been tested in dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD) animal models, nor direct demonstrations that it improves RPE phagosome clearance, lysosomal acidification, cathepsin D activity, lipofuscin burden, or drusen in AMD contexts; TFEB-targeted rescue of lysosomal function and cathepsin activity in RPE has been achieved with other agents (e.g., trehalose) or genetic TFEB activation, but ZLN005 has not been evaluated in those axes in RPE (Ghosh et al., 2024; Ghosh et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2022; Shu et al., 2021).

Mechanism of Action  
Transcriptional induction of PGC-1α. ZLN005 increases PGC-1α transcription via the PGC-1α promoter, requiring an intact MEF2 binding site; it increases PGC-1α mRNA and protein across multiple cell types where responsive, including myotubes, cardiomyocytes, lung epithelium, VSMCs, and RPE; reliance on MEF2 sites suggests ZLN005 engages upstream signaling and chromatin/transcriptional complexes that drive Ppargc1a expression (Zhang et al., 2013; Liu et al., 2020; Ma et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2025; Shu et al., 2021).

AMPK activation via mild mitochondrial uncoupling. In L6 myotubes, ZLN005 increases the cellular ADP:ATP ratio and mildly increases mitochondrial oxygen consumption in isolated mitochondria, consistent with weak uncoupling that activates AMPK; AMPK and ACC phosphorylation increase, and pharmacologic AMPK inhibition (Compound C) blocks PGC-1α induction and glucose uptake; the pathway is further modeled as AMPK-phosphorylated PGC-1α engaging MEF2C to amplify its own gene expression via positive feedback (Zhang et al., 2013). AMPK-dependence extends functionally: glucose uptake and PGC-1α induction in muscle cells require AMPK activation (Wang et al., 2021).

Downstream PGC-1α programs. Across tissues, ZLN005 induces the canonical PGC-1α program: coactivation of ERRs/PPARs/NRF1/2, upregulation of TFAM, increases in mtDNA copy number, OXPHOS complex gene products, fatty-acid oxidation enzymes (e.g., CPT-1, MCAD), and functional increases in basal/maximal OCR, ATP production, and mitochondrial content/number—data are robust in cardiomyocytes and skeletal muscle, and consistent in lung epithelial cells and RPE (Liu et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2013; Ma et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2022). In kidney I/R, PGC-1α induction by ZLN005 increased CPT-1α, restored mitochondrial FAO, reduced ROS and MDA, and decreased apoptosis; genetic or pharmacologic CPT-1α inhibition abrogated ZLN005 benefit, placing FAO downstream of PGC-1α as a necessary mediator of cytoprotection in that model (Wang et al., 2021).

PGC-1α–SIRT axis and anti-inflammatory effects. In lung infection models, ZLN005 activated the PGC-1α–SIRT3 axis, rescued SIRT3 expression, and lowered IL-1β expression in infected lungs, aligning with the role of SIRT3 in mitochondrial antioxidant function (activation of SOD2, glutathione system) and with PGC-1α’s coactivation of oxidative metabolism and antioxidant defense (Maurice et al., 2022).

Cell-type specificity and paradoxical tissue effects. ZLN005 shows a cell-type- and tissue-selective response: skeletal muscle and diverse non-hepatic cells robustly induce PGC-1α, whereas primary hepatocytes do not; in db/db mice, liver PGC-1α was paradoxically downregulated, with concurrent suppression of gluconeogenic genes, possibly via indirect systemic mechanisms (Zhang et al., 2013). In RPE, primary cells (H-RPE) exhibited modest PGC-1α fold-induction relative to ARPE-19 due to higher baseline PGC-1α expression, yet ZLN005 still blocked TGFβ₂-induced EMT and migration in H-RPE (Shu et al., 2021).

Assay caveats. ZLN005 potently inhibits luciferases, necessitating caution and orthogonal validation for any conclusions drawn from luciferase reporters, including those targeting TFEB/TFE3 or PGC-1α target promoters (Zhang et al., 2013).

Linking to TFEB/lysosomal axis: rationale vs evidence. AMPK primes lysosomal biogenesis and function partly by inducing TFEB/TFE3, while simultaneously regulating mitochondrial biogenesis via PGC-1α; a combined AMPK activation and mTORC1 inhibition strategy has been proposed as superior for mitochondrial disease contexts (Boya et al., 2023). In RPE/retina, genetic and pharmacologic activation of TFEB restores lysosomal hydrolases (CTSD, CTSL), improves autophagosome–lysosome fusion and catabolism, and reduces disease-relevant lipid accumulation; these TFEB-centered effects are well documented in RPE but have not been demonstrated for ZLN005 (Ghosh et al., 2024; Ghosh et al., 2023). Some evidence in non-retinal systems places PGC-1α upstream of TFEB-regulated lysosomal activity (e.g., in Huntington disease models), supporting a conceptual link between mitochondrial biogenesis and lysosomal/autophagy competence; however, ZLN005-specific co-activation of TFEB or enhancement of lysosomal acidification/CTSD in RPE has not been shown (Blasiak et al., 2021).

Expected Effect  
Dry AMD pathobiology—RPE mitochondrial–lysosomal dysfunction. Dry AMD features RPE oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction (mtDNA damage, reduced OXPHOS, fewer/disorganized mitochondria), defective mitophagy, impaired proteostasis, and lysosomal/autophagy defects that promote lipofuscin and drusen biogenesis (Hyttinen et al., 2023; Luján et al., 2022; Felszeghy et al., 2019). PGC-1α is highly expressed in retina/RPE and is a potent regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis and antioxidant defenses in these cells; genetic deficiency of PGC-1α (especially combined with NRF2 loss) fosters RPE degeneration with proteostatic/lysosomal stress reminiscent of dry AMD (Felszeghy et al., 2019; Hyttinen et al., 2018; Hyttinen et al., 2023). Mechanistically, AMPK→PGC-1α signaling induces mitochondrial biogenesis and interacts with NRF2/ARE antioxidant programs; reciprocally, AMPK can promote TFEB/TFE3-driven lysosomal biogenesis, providing a bidirectional mito–lyso coupling framework relevant to RPE homeostasis (Nita & Grzybowski, 2023; Boya et al., 2023).

What ZLN005 is expected to do in RPE assays. In ARPE-19 and H-RPE, ZLN005 selectively induces PGC-1α, improves mitochondrial respiration, upregulates mitochondrial/antioxidant gene expression, and protects against oxidative insults; PGC-1α dependence of cytoprotection has been demonstrated in ARPE-19 (Tong et al., 2022; Shu et al., 2021). In H-RPE, ZLN005 prevents TGFβ₂-induced EMT and migration, linking PGC-1α induction to preservation of epithelial phenotype and metabolic competence under stress (Shu et al., 2021). Based on these data, in RPE assays ZLN005 is expected to:

- Increase PGC-1α transcript/protein and downstream mitochondrial biogenesis (e.g., NRF1/TFAM/OXPHOS genes), raising basal/maximal respiration and spare capacity, and boosting ATP (Tong et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2020).  
- Lower ROS and improve mitochondrial membrane potential and structure, thereby reducing oxidative injury susceptibility (Tong et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2023).  
- Preserve epithelial features and reduce EMT/migration under profibrotic stress (TGFβ₂), maintaining RPE barrier function (Shu et al., 2021).

Hypothesized effects on lysosomes/TFEB/CTSD and phagosome clearance. The proposal posits that ZLN005 will “cooperate with TFEB to enhance lysosomal acidification and cathepsin D activity, thereby improving phagosome clearance” and that improved mito–lyso coupling will reduce lipofuscin. Available evidence supports the general biological framework—AMPK can activate TFEB/TFE3 and PGC-1α in parallel, PGC-1α/NRF2 promote mitochondrial homeostasis and antioxidant capacity in RPE, and TFEB activation restores lysosomal hydrolases and autophagic flux in diseased RPE—but there are no direct data that ZLN005 activates TFEB, increases lysosomal acidification, elevates cathepsin D activity, or enhances phagosome clearance in RPE (Boya et al., 2023; Ghosh et al., 2024; Tong et al., 2022; Shu et al., 2021). In fact, the TFEB-centric rescue of CTSD/LAMP1 and lysosome function in AMD-model RPE has been demonstrated with trehalose or genetic TFEB activation, not with ZLN005; therefore, any expectation that ZLN005 alone will enhance lysosomal acidification/CTSD or phagosome clearance is a plausible but unproven hypothesis that requires explicit testing with lysosomal pH probes, CTSD activity assays, and POS phagocytosis/phagosome clearance metrics in primary human RPE (Ghosh et al., 2024).

Important caveats for experimental design:  
- Reporter interference: ZLN005 inhibits luciferases and may confound TFEB/E-box luciferase or PGC-1α reporter assays; use non-luciferase readouts (e.g., immunoblots, qPCR, ChIP-qPCR, RNA-seq, proteomics, TFEB nuclear translocation imaging, LysoTracker/DQ-BSA/CTSD activity assays) (Zhang et al., 2013).  
- Primary vs cell line responsiveness: Primary H-RPE shows smaller PGC-1α fold-induction than ARPE-19 due to higher baseline PGC-1α; dosing windows and exposure times must be optimized in H-RPE to achieve functional gains without undue mitochondrial uncoupling (Shu et al., 2021).  
- Cross-talk assessment: If synergy with TFEB is a key aim, include a TFEB activator arm (e.g., trehalose) as comparator/combination and measure canonical TFEB outputs (CTSD/L, LAMP1/2, CLEAR network gene expression), while maintaining AMPK/mTORC1 pathway controls to dissect mechanism (Ghosh et al., 2024; Boya et al., 2023).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Direct RPE activity. ZLN005 selectively upregulates PGC-1α in ARPE-19 and primary H-RPE, enhances mitochondrial respiration, and protects ARPE-19 from oxidative stress in a PGC-1α-dependent manner; it also suppresses TGFβ₂-induced EMT/migration in H-RPE, linking mitochondrial competence to epithelial stability—both highly relevant to dry AMD pathophysiology (Tong et al., 2022; Shu et al., 2021).  
- Robust mitochondrial biogenesis/transcriptional program. Across cardiomyocytes, muscle, and lung epithelium, ZLN005 reproducibly induces PGC-1α and downstream transcriptional programs (NRF1/TFAM/OXPHOS/FAO), increasing mtDNA, OCR, and ATP, establishing a mechanistic basis for improving RPE mitochondrial deficits observed in dry AMD (Liu et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2013; Ma et al., 2023; Hyttinen et al., 2023).  
- Systems-level cytoprotection. In AKI and neuronal ischemia/toxicity models, ZLN005 reduces ROS and apoptosis, improves mitochondrial integrity, and requires key downstream effectors (CPT-1α in kidney), demonstrating that PGC-1α activation can translate to organ protection, which conceptually extends to RPE under oxidative/energetic stress (Wang et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2018; Tian et al., 2023).  
- Disease relevance of the target. Extensive genetic/pathophysiologic evidence places PGC-1α at the center of RPE mitochondrial biogenesis, antioxidant defense, and senescence control; PGC-1α or PGC-1α/NRF2 loss causes AMD-like degeneration, and AMPK→PGC-1α and NRF2/ARE axes are validated protective mechanisms in RPE (Felszeghy et al., 2019; Hyttinen et al., 2018; Hyttinen et al., 2023; Nita & Grzybowski, 2023).  
- Translational feasibility. ZLN005 has oral bioactivity with favorable metabolic endpoints in mice, rudimentary short-term rat safety, and a breadth of preclinical efficacy across tissues; it penetrates the CNS, implying some potential for ocular exposure via circulation, though eye PK is unknown (Zhang et al., 2013; Tian et al., 2023).

Limitations and risks  
- No direct evidence for TFEB/lysosomal activation by ZLN005. There are no data showing that ZLN005 increases TFEB activity, lysosomal acidification, cathepsin D activity, or phagosome clearance in RPE; TFEB-dependent improvements in lysosome function and CTSD have been demonstrated with other interventions (trehalose or genetic TFEB activation), not with ZLN005 (Ghosh et al., 2024; Tong et al., 2022; Shu et al., 2021). The hypothesized mito–lyso crosstalk is biologically plausible via AMPK signaling but remains to be demonstrated for this compound (Boya et al., 2023).  
- Potential pro-angiogenic signaling. PGC-1α/ERRα activation in RPE has been linked to VEGF upregulation under certain conditions; in a chronic retinal setting, inadvertent promotion of angiogenic signaling could be undesirable, especially near neovascular AMD thresholds; careful assessment of VEGF/angiogenic gene expression in RPE and choroid is warranted (Blasiak et al., 2021).  
- Dose window and uncoupling toxicity. ZLN005 acts at least partly via mild mitochondrial uncoupling to activate AMPK; while beneficial in moderation, uncoupling can exacerbate ROS and bioenergetic stress if overdosed; high ZLN005 concentrations were toxic in HK2 cells, underscoring the need for careful titration in primary H-RPE (Zhang et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2021).  
- Cell-type and species specificity. ZLN005 does not induce PGC-1α in primary hepatocytes and downregulated hepatic PGC-1α in db/db mice; responsiveness varies by cell context (e.g., smaller fold-change in H-RPE than ARPE-19), complicating dose translation and efficacy prediction in human RPE in vivo (Zhang et al., 2013; Shu et al., 2021).  
- Reporter assay interference. The compound inhibits luciferases; any reliance on luciferase readouts for PGC-1α or TFEB activity risks artifact and demands orthogonal assay confirmation (Zhang et al., 2013).  
- Ocular pharmacokinetics unknown. Oral PK demonstrates high liver exposure and moderate muscle exposure; there are no data on ocular distribution, RPE exposure, or retinal bioavailability, making formulation and dosing strategies for eye delivery an open question (Zhang et al., 2013).

Decision-critical gaps and suggested de-risking experiments  
- TFEB/lysosomal axis: Directly test whether ZLN005 increases TFEB nuclear translocation, CLEAR network gene expression, lysosomal pH acidification (ratiometric probes), CTSD/CTSL protein/activity, and autophagosome flux (LC3B-II/p62 turnover) in primary H-RPE; benchmark versus trehalose and/or genetic TFEB activation and evaluate additivity or synergy in ZLN005+trehalose combination, with AMPK/mTORC1 pathway readouts to mechanistically link signaling (Ghosh et al., 2024; Boya et al., 2023).  
- Phagosome processing: Quantify photoreceptor outer segment (POS) uptake and phagosome clearance kinetics, including cathepsin-dependent proteolysis and lipofuscinogenic adduct accumulation, under oxidative/complement stress ± ZLN005; include PGC-1α knockdown to confirm dependence (Tong et al., 2022; Felszeghy et al., 2019).  
- In vivo efficacy in AMD-relevant models: Test in complement/CFH risk allele iPSC-RPE xenograft or sodium iodate/aging mouse models focusing on RPE structure/function, lipofuscin proxies, sub-RPE deposit formation, and ERG endpoints; incorporate TFEB activation arms where possible to validate the dual-axis hypothesis (Ghosh et al., 2024; Luján et al., 2022).  
- Safety pharmacology: Titrate ZLN005 dosing in ocular delivery paradigms (intravitreal, periocular, or systemic) with eye PK, retinal/RPE exposure, retinal function (ERG), and VEGF expression profiling to exclude pro-angiogenic shifts; assess off-target mitochondrial stress at higher exposures in H-RPE (Blasiak et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021).

Conclusion: fit for further evaluation with focused lysosome endpoints. ZLN005 has strong mechanistic and preclinical support as a PGC-1α activator that improves mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative resilience in RPE and retina-adjacent systems, and it produces protective effects across diverse tissues via PGC-1α-dependent programs (Tong et al., 2022; Shu et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2021). However, the core elements of the present hypothesis—enhancement of TFEB activity, lysosomal acidification, cathepsin D activity, and phagosome clearance, plus reduction of lipofuscin and drusen—are not yet evidenced for ZLN005 and remain inferential based on pathway crosstalk (AMPK→TFEB/TFE3, PGC-1α–NRF2) and TFEB-targeted rescue in RPE by other agents (Boya et al., 2023; Ghosh et al., 2024). Consequently, ZLN005 is a promising mitochondrial-centric candidate for dry AMD that merits accelerated, targeted validation on lysosomal/phagosome endpoints and ocular delivery feasibility. The compound’s strengths—a validated mitochondrial biogenesis mechanism in RPE, demonstrated oxidative stress protection in ARPE-19, primary H-RPE EMT suppression, and broad cross-tissue efficacy—justify this investment, provided that the risks of off-target uncoupling, reporter assay interference, cell-type specificity, and potential pro-angiogenic signaling are actively managed in the experimental plan (Tong et al., 2022; Shu et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2013; Blasiak et al., 2021).
