Proposal for AZD9056 (Axellus)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
AZD9056 is a small‐molecule, orally active P2X7 receptor antagonist developed by AstraZeneca that belongs to the class of synthetic adamantane‐based compounds. Although its precise chemical structure has not been fully disclosed in the public domain, multiple reports indicate that it emerges from a medicinal chemistry program focused on adamantyl benzamide scaffolds discovered through high‐throughput screening and subsequent structure–activity relationship (SAR) optimization (Chrovian et al., 2014; Gunosewoyo et al., 2007). The compound is understood to maintain the essential adamantane moiety linked through a methylene spacer to an aromatic benzamide, which is thought to force a twist in the benzamide conformation critical for high P2X7 antagonism (Chrovian et al., 2014; Gunosewoyo et al., 2007). In general, the adamantane class of compounds has been explored for its lipophilic and sterically constrained properties that can impart favorable bioavailability and receptor selectivity; indeed, the adamantane scaffold has also been exploited in a number of antiviral and anti-inflammatory agents. In the case of AZD9056, this design strategy was implemented to yield a molecule that potently blocks the ATP‐driven activity of the P2X7 receptor—a ligand‐gated ion channel implicated in inflammation and cell death—while offering the oral bioavailability and acceptable pharmacokinetic properties required for systemic application (Chrovian et al., 2014; Eser et al., 2015). The overall discovery process appears to have combined hit identification with parallel synthetic approaches in order to establish an SAR profile that ultimately led to a candidate with nanomolar potency in cell‐based assays of P2X7 function, such as inhibition of Bz-ATP–induced Ca2+ influx and dye uptake, although the specific synthetic steps remain proprietary (Chrovian et al., 2014; Gunosewoyo et al., 2007).  

Therapeutic History:  
AZD9056 has been evaluated extensively in non‐ocular inflammatory disease contexts. Early-phase clinical trials established its safety and tolerability in healthy volunteers through single- and multiple-dose studies that characterized its pharmacokinetic profile, with parameters such as a moderate oral bioavailability (~62%), a terminal half-life in the range of 16–18 hours, and the capacity for once-daily dosing (Arulkumaran et al., 2011). Its development program initially focused on rheumatoid arthritis (RA). In a 4-week phase IIa trial in RA patients, AZD9056 was found to be safe and well tolerated at the doses assessed, and target engagement was evidenced by significant inhibition of ex vivo ATP-induced monocyte IL-1β release (Chrovian et al., 2014). However, a subsequent larger phase IIb study in RA, enrolling up to 385 or more patients and including an open etanercept comparator arm, failed to demonstrate superior clinical efficacy over placebo across several objective endpoints, even though tolerability remained acceptable (Arulkumaran et al., 2011; Chrovian et al., 2014; Lepre et al., 2023). In parallel with its RA development, AZD9056 also underwent evaluation for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), specifically in patients with Crohn's disease, where a phase IIa trial using a 200 mg once-daily regimen showed nominal improvements in the Crohn’s Disease Activity Index (CDAI) but no accompanying improvement in inflammatory biomarkers (Arulkumaran et al., 2011; Rech et al., 2016). Aside from these clinical endeavors in systemic inflammatory disorders, a dedicated ocular safety study was conducted in healthy volunteers; in this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study, retinal function was directly assessed by measures including electroretinography and visual acuity—an indication that the eye was considered as a potential compartment of interest, even though no efficacy studies in ocular disease states such as dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD) have been reported (Arulkumaran et al., 2011). Overall, while the compound has been repurposed extensively for anti-inflammatory indications and has an established human safety profile, its clinical history thus far has not included a trial specifically targeting dry AMD or any direct veterinary employments for ocular diseases (Arulkumaran et al., 2011; Guile et al., 2009).  

Mechanism of Action:  
The P2X7 receptor (P2X7R) is an ATP-gated, nonselective cation channel predominantly expressed on immune cells and, importantly for the intended application, on the retinal pigmented epithelium (RPE) (Calzaferri et al., 2020). Under physiological conditions, P2X7R is quiescent; however, in the presence of elevated extracellular ATP—a phenomenon noted in inflammatory and degenerative conditions, including AMD—the receptor becomes activated, mediating several deleterious downstream events. Upon activation, P2X7R undergoes a conformational change that not only allows the influx of Ca2+ and Na+ but also promotes the formation of large membrane pores owing to its ability to transition into a state that permits the passage of molecules as large as 900 Da (Chrovian et al., 2014; Eser et al., 2015). This pore formation results in cytotoxic calcium overload and is directly linked to the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, a multiprotein complex responsible for the processing of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and IL-18 (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Gu & Wiley, 2018). In the retinal pigment epithelium, this ATP-driven activation has been shown to contribute to lysosomal alkalization—a process that impairs the optimal acidic environment required by lysosomal enzymes, including cathepsins—and ultimately leads to defective clearance of cellular debris (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Tempest-Roe, 2017). Specifically, experimental studies in ARPE-19 cells (a human RPE cell line) have demonstrated that stimulation of P2X7 induces lysosomal dysfunction; conversely, pharmacological inhibition of P2X7 using antagonists such as Brilliant Blue G (BBG) can reverse this lysosomal impairment (Tempest-Roe, 2017). Mechanistically, AZD9056 is designed to bind to the P2X7 receptor with high potency and selectivity, thereby preventing the receptor’s activation by ATP. By doing so, it is anticipated to block the cascade of events that lead from receptor activation to pore formation, inflammasome assembly, and subsequent IL-1β release (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Gu & Wiley, 2018; Lee et al., 2023). Although the exact molecular binding mode of AZD9056 has not been detailed in available literature, it is presumed to interact with key residues involved in channel gating and pore formation, thereby stabilizing the receptor in a closed, inactive conformation (Chrovian et al., 2014; Lee et al., 2023). Additionally, its ability to reduce ex vivo ATP-induced monocyte IL-1β release in healthy volunteers has served as a functional biomarker for target engagement, reinforcing the hypothesis that P2X7 antagonism can modulate inflammasome-driven inflammatory signaling (Chrovian et al., 2014; Lee et al., 2023).  

Expected Effect:  
In the context of dry age-related macular degeneration, the central hypothesis for repurposing AZD9056 is that it will antagonize P2X7 receptors on retinal pigmented epithelium cells, thereby preventing a cascade of ATP-driven pathological events. Elevated extracellular ATP levels have been documented in the vitreous of AMD patients, which suggests a pathological activation of purinergic signaling in the retina (Calzaferri et al., 2020). When ATP activates P2X7 receptors on RPE cells, it leads to two major deleterious outcomes. First, the receptor’s activation results in sustained Ca2+ influx and large pore formation, triggering cytotoxicity and subsequent cell death mechanisms, including apoptotic and inflammasome-dependent cell death pathways (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Gu & Wiley, 2018). Second, P2X7 activation has been mechanistically linked to lysosomal alkalization in RPE cells, which impairs the activity of lysosomal proteases such as cathepsin D; this enzyme is critical for the degradation of photoreceptor outer segments and other cellular debris (Gu & Wiley, 2018; Tempest-Roe, 2017). The hypothesis posits that by blocking P2X7 receptor activation with AZD9056, one would preserve the normal acidic environment of the lysosomes, thereby maintaining or even restoring optimal cathepsin D activity. In turn, these effects are anticipated to protect the RPE from inflammasome-mediated apoptosis and lysosomal dysfunction, which are key drivers of geographic atrophy, the advanced clinical manifestation of dry AMD (Gu & Wiley, 2018; Calzaferri et al., 2020). Preclinical studies support the role of P2X7 in mediating RPE degeneration; for example, genetic knockout of P2X7 in mouse models has been shown to protect against Alu RNA-induced RPE toxicity, an established model of dry AMD pathology (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Gu & Wiley, 2018). Furthermore, in vitro studies using human RPE cell cultures have indicated that P2X7 receptor blockade can reverse lysosomal impairment, suggesting that AZD9056 could produce measurable improvements in functional assays that evaluate lysosomal protease activity, such as cathepsin D activity assays, and overall RPE viability assays (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Tempest-Roe, 2017). Ultimately, if the protective effects seen in preclinical studies translate into the clinical setting, AZD9056 may reduce the accumulation of undigested cellular debris (drusen) and slow or halt the progression of geographic atrophy in patients with dry AMD by dampening the inflamed state and preserving RPE function (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Gu & Wiley, 2018).  

Overall Evaluation:  
From a drug development perspective, the repurposing of AZD9056 for dry AMD presents a compelling but challenging opportunity. One of the primary strengths of this therapeutic candidate lies in its robust mechanistic rationale. The P2X7 receptor plays a central role in mediating ATP-induced inflammasome activation, lysosomal dysfunction, and subsequent RPE cell death—pathophysiological processes that have been repeatedly implicated in dry AMD and geographic atrophy models (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Gu & Wiley, 2018; Tempest-Roe, 2017). The ability of P2X7 antagonists to block these processes has been demonstrated in various in vitro and in vivo models, where blockade of the receptor reduces IL-1β release, reverses lysosomal alkalization, and confers protection against RPE degeneration (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Gu & Wiley, 2018; Tempest-Roe, 2017). Furthermore, AZD9056 has gone through extensive clinical safety evaluations in multiple inflammatory disease indications; its pharmacokinetic profile—characterized by acceptable oral bioavailability and a half-life conducive to once-daily dosing—has been well established in early phase studies (Arulkumaran et al., 2011; Chrovian et al., 2014). This pre-existing clinical safety data is an asset in reducing the risk profile typically associated with novel drug candidates and may streamline early-stage clinical trial designs for an ocular indication. Additionally, preliminary ocular safety assessments in healthy volunteers, which included measures such as retinal electroretinography and visual acuity testing, suggest that systemic administration of AZD9056 does not produce overt ocular toxicity—an encouraging sign for its potential repurposing in dry AMD (Arulkumaran et al., 2011).  

Nevertheless, there are several notable limitations and challenges associated with using AZD9056 in the treatment of dry AMD. First, the prior clinical development history of AZD9056 in rheumatoid arthritis and Crohn’s disease revealed limited or inconsistent efficacy despite demonstrable target engagement. In the phase IIb studies for RA, the compound failed to outperform placebo across objective outcome measures even though safety endpoints were met (Arulkumaran et al., 2011; Chrovian et al., 2014). This raises concerns about whether P2X7 receptor blockade alone is sufficient to generate a meaningful clinical outcome, particularly in a complex and multifactorial disease process such as dry AMD. Moreover, the inflammatory milieu in rheumatoid arthritis or IBD differs significantly from that in the retina, and systemic inflammation may not accurately recapitulate the localized, chronic degenerative processes occurring in the RPE (Arulkumaran et al., 2011; Guile et al., 2009).  

Another concern relates to the dual and sometimes paradoxical roles of the P2X7 receptor. While its pathological activation is linked to pro-inflammatory signaling, P2X7 also appears to have a scavenger or phagocytic function under conditions of low ATP levels (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Newman, 2024). In the context of the RPE, which is specialized for high-volume phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments, complete blockade of P2X7 might inadvertently impair beneficial clearance processes that serve to prevent debris accumulation. In animal models, alternative data has shown that the loss of P2X7 function can result in changes reminiscent of early AMD, such as reduced phagocytosis, suggesting that finely balanced modulation rather than total inhibition may be necessary (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Gu & Wiley, 2018). Such duality underscores the potential risk that while AZD9056 may ameliorate inflammasome activation and lysosomal destabilization, it might simultaneously disrupt physiologic functions of RPE cells that are critical for maintaining retinal homeostasis.  

The lack of direct evidence for AZD9056’s efficacy in ocular models of dry AMD is another major gap. While the preclinical literature supports a role for P2X7 signaling in RPE dysfunction and geographic atrophy (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Tempest-Roe, 2017), the specific application of AZD9056 in these models remains largely inferential. There are no published studies documenting that AZD9056, when administered at doses achieving retinal penetration, effectively preserves cathepsin D activity or prevents RPE cell death in animal models of dry AMD. Furthermore, the translation of systemic pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic data to ocular compartments is nontrivial; disparities in drug distribution, blood–retinal barrier permeability, and local metabolism could limit effective target engagement in the retina even if systemic safety is acceptable (Arulkumaran et al., 2011). The undisclosed chemical structure of AZD9056 also complicates any efforts to optimize the compound specifically for ocular bioavailability or to better understand its off-target profile relative to other P2X7 antagonists that have been more thoroughly characterized in the literature (Chrovian et al., 2014; Gunosewoyo et al., 2007).  

In summary, the overall evaluation of AZD9056 for treatment of dry AMD is one of cautious enthusiasm tempered by several unresolved questions. On the strength side, the compound leverages a clear mechanistic rationale by antagonizing the P2X7 receptor—a key mediator of ATP-induced inflammasome activation and lysosomal dysfunction known to be active in the RPE of AMD patients—and it comes with an established clinical safety record in inflammatory disorders (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Chrovian et al., 2014; Gu & Wiley, 2018). Its ability to suppress IL-1β release in ex vivo assays supports its functional engagement with the P2X7/IL-1β pathway, and preclinical data suggest that P2X7 inhibition can protect against RPE degeneration (Gu & Wiley, 2018; Tempest-Roe, 2017). However, significant challenges persist: prior clinical trials in RA and IBD have yielded mixed efficacy results, casting doubt on whether P2X7 blockade alone can significantly modify disease progression in complex conditions; the potential for interference with beneficial receptor functions in the RPE may compromise its utility; and the lack of direct preclinical data in dry AMD models means that much of the benefit remains theoretical at this stage (Arulkumaran et al., 2011; Chrovian et al., 2014; Gu & Wiley, 2018; Territo & Zarrinmayeh, 2021). Additionally, uncertainties related to ocular pharmacokinetics and the inability to adjust the synthetic structure for improved retinal targeting (given the proprietary nature of the molecule’s chemistry) further complicate its repositioning for an ocular indication (Chrovian et al., 2014; Eser et al., 2015).  

Given the current state of the literature, it would be advisable to conduct targeted preclinical studies that specifically address the effects of AZD9056 on RPE cells and retinal tissue. These studies should include assays for lysosomal pH stability, cathepsin D activity, inflammasome assembly (e.g., NLRP3 activation and caspase-1 processing), and overall RPE cell viability under conditions of ATP stress. Furthermore, careful evaluation of retinal drug levels following systemic dosing—or the development of a topical formulation—might be necessary to ensure that sufficient drug concentrations can be achieved in the retina without systemic toxicity. A parallel investigation into the potential compensatory roles of P2X7 in RPE phagocytosis would also be warranted to minimize any risk of disrupting beneficial cellular functions (Calzaferri et al., 2020; Gu & Wiley, 2018; Arulkumaran et al., 2011).  

Overall, while AZD9056’s strong mechanistic basis and favorable early-phase clinical safety profile make it an attractive candidate for repurposing, the translational challenges and previous clinical setbacks in other indications necessitate rigorous preclinical validation in dry AMD models before further clinical development is considered. The compound’s capacity to specifically maintain lysosomal function and cathepsin D activity in the RPE represents a promising therapeutic opportunity if these effects can be reliably recapitulated in the ocular setting. However, caution is advised due to the dual roles of P2X7 signaling in both pathological and physiologic processes and the potential non-optimal receptor occupancy in the retina. In conclusion, the repurposing of AZD9056 for dry AMD holds potential but would require a focused, mechanistically guided preclinical program to address the unique challenges posed by the ocular environment and the complex biology of the P2X7 receptor (Arulkumaran et al., 2011; Chrovian et al., 2014; Calzaferri et al., 2020; Tempest-Roe, 2017).
