Proposal for SB216763 (Sigma S3442)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
SB216763 is a synthetic small‐molecule inhibitor originally discovered during GlaxoSmithKline’s dedicated program in developing GSK-3 inhibitors. It belongs to the bisarylmaleimide class of compounds, with its chemical structure based on a pyrrole-2,5-dione core that is further substituted by aryl and indole moieties. In its initial characterization, SB216763 was identified as an ATP-competitive inhibitor of glycogen synthase kinase 3 beta (GSK3β), a central serine/threonine protein kinase implicated in multiple intracellular signaling cascades. The chemical class to which SB216763 belongs – maleimides – has been widely exploited across medicinal chemistry due to their synthetic tractability and ability to serve as potent inhibitors of enzymes, particularly kinases. This group of compounds has previously been used in various preclinical studies targeting neurodegenerative diseases and cell proliferative disorders by modulating cell signaling pathways such as the Wnt/β-catenin cascade, which is ubiquitously expressed in many tissues including the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) and the central nervous system (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Ruiz & Eldar-Finkelman, 2022; Alhassan et al., 2025).

Therapeutic History:  
SB216763 has a well-established history in preclinical research, particularly in models of neurodegeneration. Several studies have deployed this compound in rodent models of Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, and other neurological insults to demonstrate its neuroprotective potential. In these models, SB216763 was shown to reduce tau hyperphosphorylation, protect neurons against both β-amyloid-mediated toxicity and radiation-induced apoptosis in hippocampal neurons, and promote beneficial modulation of signaling pathways linked to PI3K–Akt (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Ruiz & Eldar-Finkelman, 2022). Beyond its neuroprotective effects, SB216763 has been applied as a tool compound in cellular studies to activate canonical Wnt signaling through stabilization of beta-catenin by inhibiting GSK3β. Importantly, a radiolabeled derivative, [11C]SB216763, has been developed as a positron emission tomography (PET) probe, which confirmed its ability to cross the blood–brain barrier in rodent and nonhuman primate models, although significant selectivity issues have been noted with respect to off-target kinase inhibition (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Benn & Dawson, 2020). In addition, there is documented evidence of its application in ocular contexts; for instance, in an experimental ischemic retinal neuron model in rats, SB216763 was found to promote DNA repair by upregulating DNA ligase IV transcriptional activity. However, while it has been evaluated extensively in neurological and in vivo rodent models for central nervous system diseases, formal clinical deployment or veterinary studies specifically targeting dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD) remain sparse, with no documented clinical trials registered on major platforms dedicated to AMD indications (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Ruiz & Eldar-Finkelman, 2022; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).

Mechanism of Action:  
The primary mechanism of action of SB216763 is centered on its potent inhibition of GSK3β. Under normal physiological conditions, GSK3β is a constitutively active kinase that phosphorylates a wide array of substrates, including beta-catenin, tau, and proteins critical to cellular metabolism and survival. One key substrate relevant to our hypothesis is the transcription factor EB (TFEB), a master regulator of the Coordinated Lysosomal Expression and Regulation (CLEAR) gene network. TFEB’s activity is tightly regulated by its phosphorylation state; when phosphorylated by kinases such as mTORC1 and possibly GSK3β, TFEB binds to cytosolic 14-3-3 proteins and is retained in the cytoplasm. Inhibition of GSK3β by SB216763 has been implicated in the dephosphorylation of TFEB, thereby allowing its translocation to the nucleus where it drives the transcription of CLEAR network genes. These genes are essential for lysosomal biogenesis, autophagic flux, and the expression of lysosomal proteases such as cathepsin D (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023). Detailed molecular studies in neuronal stem cells and N2a cells have demonstrated that SB216763 can inactivate GSK3β and thereby stimulate the nuclear accumulation of TFEB. In addition, the compound’s ATP-competitive mode of inhibition suggests it directly competes with ATP in the GSK3β active site, leading to rapid modulation of downstream substrates implicated in metabolic and autophagic processes (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023; Djajadikerta et al., 2020). From a biochemical standpoint, SB216763’s action not only stabilizes beta-catenin – thereby modulating Wnt signaling – but also appears to influence key regulatory nodes in autophagy. Activation of TFEB results in upregulation of lysosomal proteins, enhancement of lysosomal acidification via increased expression of components like the vacuolar-type ATPase (V-ATPase), and improved cathepsin D maturation and activity, all of which collectively promote the efficient clearance of intracellular aggregates and damaged organelles (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Ruiz & Eldar-Finkelman, 2022; Pan & Valapala, 2022).

Expected Effect in Dry AMD Context:  
The central hypothesis behind repurposing SB216763 for dry AMD rests on its ability to correct the cellular deficits observed in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) associated with aging and disease. Dry AMD is characterized by the accumulation of lipofuscin and other aggregate-prone materials within the RPE, coupled with impaired autophagic and lysosomal clearance mechanisms. The proposed pharmacological intervention with SB216763 is expected to inhibit GSK3β in RPE cells, leading to dephosphorylation and nuclear translocation of TFEB. Once in the nucleus, TFEB can activate the CLEAR network, thereby augmenting lysosome biogenesis and catalytic function. Enhanced lysosomal acidification, as mediated by the V-ATPase and corroborated by increased expression of lysosomal enzymes such as cathepsin D, should facilitate the degradation of accumulated lipofuscin, oxidized proteins, and lipid aggregates. In preclinical RPE models under oxidative stress, similar pharmacological modulation of TFEB (for example, via trehalose treatment or genetic induction) has been shown to improve lysosomal function and reduce the burden of autofluorescent granules analogous to lipofuscin (Falcão et al., 2025; Falcão et al., 2024). Therefore, SB216763 is hypothesized to recapitulate these beneficial effects by leveraging its upstream modulation of GSK3β. Given that RPE cells naturally express both GSK3β and TFEB, and considering the documented mechanistic role of GSK3β in regulating TFEB nucleo-cytoplasmic shuttling, the expected outcome is an enhancement of autophagic clearance and restoration of RPE homeostasis. This would theoretically slow or potentially reverse early degenerative changes in dry AMD by mitigating the cytotoxic accumulation of undegraded material (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Ruiz & Eldar-Finkelman, 2022; Yang et al., 2023).

Overall Evaluation:  
SB216763 exhibits several promising attributes as a candidate for repurposing in the treatment of dry AMD. One major strength lies in its well-characterized and potent inhibition of GSK3β – a kinase that plays a central role in various signaling pathways implicated in both neurodegeneration and retinal pathology. The mechanistic rationale is underpinned by evidence that GSK3β inhibition facilitates the nuclear translocation of TFEB, the master regulator of lysosomal function. Through activation of TFEB, SB216763 can potentially stimulate the expression of the CLEAR gene network, leading to heightened lysosomal acidification and increased cathepsin D activity. These molecular events are directly relevant to mitigating the defective autophagic clearance that is a hallmark of dry AMD, and they align well with preclinical observations in other cell types where TFEB activation has been associated with improved degradation of accumulated toxic material (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023; Djajadikerta et al., 2020).

Further strengths include the extensive use of SB216763 in various preclinical models of neurodegeneration, wherein its ability to modulate key pathways such as beta-catenin/Wnt signaling has been repeatedly demonstrated. Its blood–brain barrier penetrance, as seen in rodent and nonhuman primate studies involving a radiolabeled derivative, suggests favorable pharmacokinetics that might also translate to ocular tissues with appropriate formulation strategies. The documented ocular application in rodent ischemic retinal neuron models – where SB216763 was shown to upregulate DNA repair mechanisms – provides additional indirect support for its potential in the ocular setting (Gianferrara et al., 2022).

Notwithstanding these promising aspects, there are notable weaknesses and gaps in the literature that warrant cautious interpretation. First, while preclinical evidence in neural and other cellular models supports the concept that GSK3β inhibition can drive TFEB-dependent transcriptional activation, direct evidence of SB216763’s efficacy in RPE cells under conditions of oxidative stress or in established dry AMD models is sparse. There is a lack of comprehensive studies that definitively link SB216763 treatment in RPE cells to enhanced lysosomal acidification, increased cathepsin D activity, and consequent reduction in lipofuscin accumulation. For example, while related studies using trehalose or mTOR inhibitors have shown clear benefits on TFEB activation and autophagic clearance in RPE models (Falcão et al., 2025; Falcão et al., 2024), SB216763-specific data in this context remain limited. 

Furthermore, concerns regarding kinase selectivity must be acknowledged. SB216763, being an ATP-competitive inhibitor, may exhibit off-target effects by inhibiting structurally related kinases, as indicated by the limited selectivity noted in studies involving its radiolabeled derivative [11C]SB216763 (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Benn & Dawson, 2020; Pan & Valapala, 2021). Off-target kinase inhibition could potentially disrupt other essential signaling pathways in the RPE and might lead to unforeseen adverse events upon clinical translation. This issue of specificity is particularly important in the context of chronic diseases like dry AMD, where long-term safety is paramount. 

Additionally, while its use in central nervous system models is encouraging, dry AMD pathophysiology has distinct features that may not be fully addressed by a therapeutic designed for neurodegeneration. The multifactorial etiology of dry AMD, which encompasses oxidative stress, chronic inflammation, and impaired autophagic degradation specific to the high metabolic and phagocytic demands of RPE cells, underscores the need for targeted evidence that SB216763 exerts its effects in these specialized cells. Moreover, the absence of any registered clinical trials on major platforms for SB216763 in the AMD space (as revealed by comprehensive searches on ClinicalTrials.gov) suggests that the translational path for this compound remains uncharted in the context of retinal disease (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Ruiz & Eldar-Finkelman, 2022).

Another consideration is the potential for conflicting actions of GSK3β inhibition. While inhibiting GSK3β does promote TFEB activation and lysosomal biogenesis, GSK3β also phosphorylates other substrates that could have deleterious effects if dysregulated in the RPE. For example, uncontrolled activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway may have proliferative effects or contribute to unwanted cell signaling changes. Thus, a delicate balance must be maintained in modulating GSK3β activity to elicit the desired autophagic responses without incurring off-target toxicity.

From a developmental perspective, the availability of SB216763 as a commercially sourced compound (Sigma S3442) is an advantage. Its established synthesis, reproducibility, and well-documented use in numerous preclinical studies allow for rapid deployment in early-stage in vitro and in vivo assays. However, the lack of formulation studies specifically for ocular delivery, and potential challenges in achieving therapeutic concentrations in the retina while minimizing systemic exposure, remain hurdles to overcome in advancing SB216763 as an AMD therapy.

In summary, SB216763 is a compelling candidate from a mechanistic standpoint, with a robust preclinical pedigree as a GSK3β inhibitor that can modulate TFEB and downstream lysosomal functions. Its synthetic origin as a bisarylmaleimide and proven in vitro potency provide a strong rationale for its repurposing. The proposed mechanism—wherein inhibition of GSK3β leads to enhanced nuclear localization of TFEB, activation of CLEAR genes, improved lysosomal acidification, and increased cathepsin D activity—directly addresses key pathogenic features of dry AMD, including the accumulation of lipofuscin and defective autophagy in RPE cells. This mechanistic alignment, supported by studies across neurodegenerative disease models and early ocular research, indicates that SB216763 has the potential to restore defective autophagic clearance in the RPE and slow AMD progression (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023; Djajadikerta et al., 2020).

Nonetheless, the overall success of SB216763 in the context of dry AMD will depend on addressing several critical issues. First, direct experimental validation in RPE cells exposed to oxidative stress is needed to confirm that SB216763 can indeed induce TFEB nuclear translocation and activate the CLEAR network in this specific cell type. Second, detailed pharmacokinetic and safety studies tailored for ocular administration are essential to determine whether the compound can reach effective concentrations in the retina without eliciting systemic toxicity or off-target kinase inhibition. Third, comprehensive in vivo studies in relevant animal models of dry AMD must be conducted to assess whether the biochemical improvements (e.g., enhanced lysosomal acidification and cathepsin D activity) translate into clinically meaningful outcomes, such as reduction in lipofuscin accumulation and preservation of RPE morphology and function.

Finally, while the theoretical framework is strong, the translational gap remains significant. The absence of clinical trials or robust preclinical data directly linking SB216763 to improvements in dry AMD phenotypes suggests that careful, incremental studies are required. These studies should ideally include dose–response evaluations, biomarker analyses of autophagic flux, and long-term safety assessments to establish the therapeutic window for SB216763 in the context of retinal disease (Ruiz & Eldar-Finkelman, 2022; Gianferrara et al., 2022; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).

Overall, SB216763 represents a promising mechanistic candidate whose ability to modulate GSK3β and, consequently, regulate TFEB activity offers a novel angle for addressing the autophagic deficits observed in dry AMD. Its well-defined synthetic origin, established preclinical usage in neurodegeneration, and documented biochemical effects in activating lysosomal and autophagic pathways suggest that it could, in principle, improve RPE function through enhanced clearance of toxic intracellular aggregates. However, the limitations regarding its selectivity, the need for targeted ocular studies, and the paucity of direct evidence in AMD models require that further rigorous investigations be conducted. A stepwise preclinical evaluation, starting with in vitro RPE models and advancing to in vivo AMD models, would be necessary to validate its efficacy and safety profile before considering clinical translation. In this regard, SB216763 should be advanced as a high-priority candidate for additional mechanistic studies and exploratory preclinical evaluations, albeit with cautious optimism given the potential for off-target effects and formulation challenges (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Ruiz & Eldar-Finkelman, 2022; Yang et al., 2023; Djajadikerta et al., 2020; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).

In conclusion, while SB216763 offers a robust mechanistic rationale for its repurposing in dry AMD based on its known capacity to inhibit GSK3β, promote TFEB-mediated lysosomal biogenesis, and potentially enhance autophagic clearance, careful attention must be paid to validating these effects directly in RPE cells under oxidative stress. The strengths of its preclinical performance and synthetic availability must be balanced against the challenges of kinase selectivity, ocular pharmacokinetics, and the absence of direct AMD-specific data. A systematic, comprehensive series of studies is warranted to firmly establish whether SB216763 can serve as a viable therapeutic candidate for dry AMD. The outcomes of these studies will significantly influence the strategic direction of the drug development program and may open the door to novel, mechanism-based interventions for a disease with substantial unmet clinical need (Gianferrara et al., 2022; Ruiz & Eldar-Finkelman, 2022; Yang et al., 2023; Djajadikerta et al., 2020; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).
