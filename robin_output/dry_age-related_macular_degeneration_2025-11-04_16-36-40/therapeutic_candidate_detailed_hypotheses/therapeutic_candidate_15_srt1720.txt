Proposal for SRT1720 (Sigma SML0271)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
SRT1720 is a first-generation synthetic small-molecule sirtuin 1 (SIRT1)–activating compound (STAC) originally discovered by high-throughput screening at Sirtris Pharmaceuticals and optimized around an imidazo[1,2-b]thiazole chemotype to yield higher apparent potency and selectivity than the natural product resveratrol in early assay systems (Chang et al., 2024; Mazzone, 2018). Subsequent mechanistic work across the STAC class resolved that many apparent activation signals were substrate/context dependent and, in some assay configurations, artifactual; nevertheless, later kinetic/biophysical and structural studies established that STACs can directly engage SIRT1 via an assisted allosteric mechanism that stabilizes the enzyme–substrate complex, with mutational data showing that E230K/A SIRT1 variants abolish both binding and activation by STACs (Lee et al., 2021; Mazzone, 2018). While most STACs are SIRT1-selective, SRT1720 exhibits notable polypharmacology, including reported inhibitory activity against Sirt3, highlighting potential off-target risks intrinsic to this first-generation class (Mazzone, 2018). As a class, STACs have been widely evaluated for metabolic, inflammatory, neurodegenerative, and aging phenotypes predicated on SIRT1’s broad substrate repertoire (for example, PGC-1α, NF-κB p65/RelA, FOXO family, p53), which link to mitochondrial biogenesis, anti-inflammatory signaling, oxidative stress responses, and cell survival programs (Chang et al., 2024; Mazzone, 2018).

Therapeutic History  
SRT1720 has extensive preclinical use, but there is no evidence in the provided corpus that it has been tested in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), in the eye, or in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) models (cellular or in vivo) (Ji et al., 2021; Ding et al., 2017; Bononi et al., 2022). In non-ocular systems, SRT1720 increased mitochondrial biogenesis and rescued mitochondrial function after oxidant injury in renal proximal tubule cells, providing proof-of-concept that STAC activation can drive PGC-1α–linked mitochondrial programs in mammalian cells under oxidative stress (Ji et al., 2021). In metabolic disease models, SRT1720 is reported to improve hepatic lipid metabolism via SIRT1-dependent pathways regulating PGC-1α, AMPK, PPARα, FOXO1, SREBP-1c and FGF21, with consistent decreases in lipogenesis and inflammation and increases in fatty-acid β-oxidation in NAFLD models (Ding et al., 2017). Multiple lifespan/healthspan studies reported benefits in mice—improved survival and healthspan in diet-induced obese animals and lifespan extension on standard diet—again consistent with systemic SIRT1-axis engagement (Mazzone, 2018). At the same time, substantial controversy surrounds both mechanism and safety. Pacholec et al. argued SRT1720 (and related first-generation STACs) are not direct SIRT1 activators under specific biochemical conditions, although later work provided structural and kinetic support for allosteric activation with specific substrates (Lee et al., 2021; Bononi et al., 2022). Importantly for repurposing risk, SRT1720 promoted tumor cell migration and lung metastasis in a breast cancer model, and SRT1720 can trigger SIRT1-independent cytotoxicity in some hematologic malignancy cells, underscoring unresolved off-target liabilities (Bononi et al., 2022; Chang et al., 2024). No pharmacokinetic (PK), ADME, or clinical tolerability data for SRT1720 are supplied in the present corpus; by contrast, the second-generation STAC SRT2104 has human PK/tolerability and multiple preclinical and clinical signals across diseases, including ocular/neurologic models (but those data cannot be extrapolated to SRT1720 without qualification) (Bononi et al., 2022; Chang et al., 2024). Collectively, the historical record supports that SRT1720 is a pharmacologically active first-generation STAC with broad preclinical use, mixed mechanistic interpretations, and safety concerns; critically, there is no published use in AMD or RPE models in the material provided here (Ji et al., 2021; Bononi et al., 2022).

Mechanism of Action  
- Biochemical pharmacology and target engagement: SRT1720 belongs to the STAC class that can directly activate SIRT1 through an assisted allosteric mechanism requiring specific substrate motifs; mutating SIRT1 E230 disrupts both activator binding and activation, and co-crystal structures of SIRT1 with SRT-like activators support an allosteric binding pocket that enhances deacetylation efficiency in a substrate-dependent manner (Lee et al., 2021; Mazzone, 2018). Early reports of direct activation were contested due to labeled peptide assay artifacts, but subsequent kinetic and structural data reconciled these findings with an allosteric model; nonetheless, the field recognizes pharmacological caveats including context-dependent activation and off-targets, such as reported Sirt3 inhibition by SRT1720 (Mazzone, 2018; Lee et al., 2021).  
- Canonical SIRT1 substrates and pathway biology: SIRT1 deacetylates transcriptional regulators and autophagy/mitophagy machinery that converge on mitochondrial quality control, oxidative stress defense, and inflammatory tone—each relevant to AMD pathogenesis. These include PGC-1α (mitochondrial biogenesis/oxidative metabolism), FOXO1/FOXO3a (antioxidant/autophagy transcription), NF-κB p65/RelA (inflammatory gene programs), p53 (apoptosis/senescence), and Nrf2 (antioxidant transcription), as well as autophagy proteins (e.g., ATG family members and LC3) and trafficking nodes such as Rab7 that promote autophagosome–lysosome fusion (Chang et al., 2024; Luján et al., 2022; Luo et al., 2025). In multiple cell types, SIRT1 activation or NAD⁺ elevation increases PGC-1α activity and mitochondrial biogenesis and can suppress NF-κB signaling via p65 deacetylation, consistent with anti-oxidative and anti-inflammatory effects that are therapeutically desirable in RPE (Ding et al., 2017; Luo et al., 2025).  
- RPE-specific SIRT1/autophagy linkage: In ARPE-19 cells under oxidative stress, PARP1 activation depletes NAD⁺, inhibits SIRT1 (elevated p53 acetylation), and selectively impairs late-stage autophagy by blocking autolysosome formation without preventing autophagosome formation; restoring NAD⁺ or inhibiting PARP1 preserves SIRT1 activity and rescues late autophagic flux, whereas PARP1 inhibition fails to rescue autophagy when SIRT1 is depleted—placing SIRT1 as the essential downstream effector for recovering autophagy in oxidatively stressed RPE (Jang et al., 2020). In vivo, PARP1 inhibition protected the retina in sodium iodate (SI) injury and the benefit was autophagy-dependent, reinforcing the PARP1→NAD⁺→SIRT1→autophagy axis as a mechanistic driver of RPE/retinal resilience (Jang et al., 2020). AMD donor RPE exhibits decreased NAD⁺, reduced SIRT1 levels, increased acetylated (inactive) PGC-1α, impaired AMPK signaling, and hyperactive mTOR—all changes expected to depress SIRT1-dependent autophagy and mitochondrial biogenesis and to contribute to lysosomal/autophagic abnormalities (Zhang et al., 2020).  
- TFEB/CLEAR and lysosomal acidification context: TFEB is the master transcription factor for lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy gene networks (CLEAR), whose nuclear translocation is negatively regulated by mTORC1; CLEAR target upregulation increases lysosomal capacity and flux, while lysosomal ion/pH homeostasis (v-ATPase–dependent proton pumping) determines enzyme activation, including cathepsins such as CTSD (Boya et al., 2023; Falcão et al., 2025; Kaarniranta et al., 2023). In AMD/RPE-relevant literature, TFEB/CLEAR is positioned as a principal lever for lysosomal rejuvenation, and lysosomal acidity and calcium handling are identified as key determinants of autophagic flux and RPE health (Boya et al., 2023). Although sirtuin family members can intersect TFEB signaling (e.g., AKT2/SIRT5/TFEB axis in non-neovascular AMD), direct evidence for SIRT1-driven TFEB deacetylation/activation is not provided in the present corpus; however, PGC-1α has been placed upstream of TFEB in other disease contexts, linking SIRT1→PGC-1α activity to potential TFEB–CLEAR induction by inference (not by direct RPE data) (Falcão et al., 2025).  
- Critical gaps relevant to the working hypothesis: Within the provided literature, there is no direct evidence that SRT1720 activates SIRT1 in RPE, no demonstration that SRT1720 promotes TFEB deacetylation or nuclear activation, and no reports that SRT1720 enhances lysosomal acidification or cathepsin D (CTSD) activity in RPE or AMD models; any such assertions remain unsubstantiated by the references supplied here (Ji et al., 2021; Mazzone, 2018; Kaarniranta et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2020). Therefore, while the SIRT1→autophagy/mitophagy rationale in RPE is strong, the specific mechanistic chain “SRT1720→SIRT1 activation in RPE→TFEB deacetylation→CLEAR upregulation→lysosomal acidification/CTSD enhancement” lacks direct experimental support in RPE/AMD contexts in these sources and should be treated as a testable, not established, hypothesis (Falcão et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2020).

Therapeutic biology of the disease target supports SIRT1 activation in RPE. Multiple AMD-relevant datasets show NAD⁺ depletion and SIRT1 downregulation in AMD RPE (primary donor-derived cultures and iPSC-RPE), accompanied by impaired PGC-1α activity, AMPK suppression, and mTOR hyperactivation, together with autophagy/lysosome structural and functional abnormalities; the PARP1-dependent late-autophagy block under oxidative stress in RPE is SIRT1-dependent for rescue, further bolstering SIRT1 as a target for restoring autophagic flux and organelle quality control (Zhang et al., 2020; Jang et al., 2020). However, these observations do not establish which pharmacologic SIRT1 activator is optimal for RPE/AMD, and they do not establish TFEB deacetylation as a SIRT1-dependent event in RPE (Jang et al., 2020; Falcão et al., 2025).

Therapeutic History in Related Ocular Systems (Class Precedent)  
Although direct SRT1720 ocular data are absent here, SRT2104—a second-generation STAC with improved specificity and exposure—shows SIRT1-linked protection in retinal ischemia/reperfusion and TNF-induced optic nerve injury, including deacetylation of canonical SIRT1 substrates (p53, NF-κB p65, STAT3), reduced apoptosis/inflammation, and induction of autophagy in optic nerve, which provides class-level ocular precedent that SIRT1 activation can be neuroprotective and autophagy-enhancing in the visual system (Chang et al., 2024). These results should not be extrapolated to SRT1720 without direct data, but they reinforce the biological plausibility that a STAC could benefit retinal tissues if target engagement and safety can be achieved (Chang et al., 2024).

Expected Effect  
The proposed assay hypothesis is that SRT1720 will activate SIRT1 in RPE, leading to TFEB deacetylation/activation, increased lysosomal acidification and CTSD activity, and improved clearance of photoreceptor outer segment (POS) material. Based on the literature provided here, the following expectations are reasonable and aligned with evidence, and several components are speculative:

- Supported expectations tied to SIRT1 biology in RPE:
  - If SRT1720 achieves bona fide SIRT1 activation and target engagement in RPE, it should decrease acetylation of canonical SIRT1 substrates (for example, p53 K382ac, p65 K310ac, PGC-1α), restore late-stage autophagic flux under oxidative stress, and enhance autophagosome–lysosome fusion, given the demonstrated requirement for SIRT1 to rescue late-stage autophagy in H₂O₂-stressed ARPE-19 cells (Jang et al., 2020; Luo et al., 2025).  
  - Biomarkers: recovery of p62 turnover, normalization of LC3-II/LC3-I dynamics in flux assays, increased autolysosome signal in tandem RFP-GFP-LC3 imaging, and potentially increased Rab7 activity/localization to autophagosomes are anticipated if SRT1720 strengthens late-stage autophagy via SIRT1 (Jang et al., 2020; Luján et al., 2022).  
  - Mitochondrial endpoints: increased PGC-1α deacetylation/activity and improved mitophagy markers (PINK1, Parkin induction) would be consistent with SIRT1 activation; SRT1720 has driven PGC-1α–dependent mitochondrial biogenesis after oxidant injury in renal cells, supporting feasibility of this axis in oxidatively stressed epithelia (Ji et al., 2021; Luján et al., 2022).  

- Plausible, but not demonstrated within the provided literature:
  - TFEB deacetylation/activation: the present corpus does not document SIRT1-dependent TFEB deacetylation or STAC-induced TFEB activation in RPE. TFEB/CLEAR is a validated master lysosomal program, and PGC-1α can lie upstream of TFEB in other disease contexts, so an indirect route “SIRT1→PGC-1α→TFEB→CLEAR” is biologically plausible but remains unproven in RPE here and should be tested directly (Falcão et al., 2025).  
  - Lysosomal acidification and CTSD activity: lysosomal pH and hydrolase activation are central to RPE proteostasis and AMD; however, no experiment in the supplied sources demonstrates that SRT1720 or any STAC increases lysosomal acidification or CTSD activity in RPE. These readouts (e.g., LysoTracker/LysoSensor ratio, DQ-BSA, cathepsin activity assays) should be considered exploratory endpoints for SRT1720, not expected outcomes (Boya et al., 2023; Kaarniranta et al., 2023).  
  - LAMP2 expression: likewise, no provided reference shows SRT1720 increasing LAMP2 in RPE. While CLEAR activation generally elevates lysosomal genes (e.g., LAMP family, cathepsins), this requires evidence of TFEB activation in the same system and has not been shown for SRT1720 in RPE in the available literature (Boya et al., 2023; Falcão et al., 2025).  

- Target presence/assay feasibility:
  - SIRT1 is expressed in human RPE and is reduced in AMD donor RPE and AMD iPSC-RPE models, with associated increases in acetylated PGC-1α and deficits in mitochondrial/autophagic programs, confirming the presence of the pharmacological target and a disease-relevant deficit that SIRT1 activation could address (Golestaneh et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2020).  
  - The disease cell biology is well-aligned with a SIRT1-centric intervention: in RPE, autophagy and phagocytic digestion converge on lysosomes to clear POS; impaired lysosomal enzyme activity and acidification reduce flux and increase oxidative stress and protein/organelle damage—phenotypes linked to AMD and to which SIRT1’s autophagy/mitophagy-promoting actions are mechanistically relevant (Kaarniranta et al., 2023; Boya et al., 2023).

Overall Evaluation  
On balance, the therapeutic rationale “activate SIRT1 to restore autophagy/mitophagy and lysosomal competence in RPE for dry AMD” is well-supported by AMD-relevant cell biology and human donor data; however, the specific choice of SRT1720 has substantial evidentiary and safety gaps relative to alternatives in the STAC space, and the asserted TFEB/lysosome mechanistic specifics are not demonstrated in the provided literature.

Strengths  
- Strong target biology and disease alignment in RPE and AMD: Multiple independent datasets demonstrate that AMD RPE exhibit decreased NAD⁺, reduced SIRT1, increased acetylated PGC-1α, impaired AMPK signaling, and mTOR hyperactivity concurrent with autophagy/lysosomal abnormalities, which together point to an actionable SIRT1 deficit in AMD pathology (Zhang et al., 2020; Jang et al., 2020). In oxidatively stressed ARPE-19 cells and an SI mouse model, preserving NAD⁺ and thereby SIRT1 activity was necessary and sufficient to rescue late-stage autophagy and protect retina, placing SIRT1 centrally in autophagy restoration and retinal resilience (Jang et al., 2020).  
- Class-level ocular precedent for SIRT1 activation: SRT2104, a later-generation STAC with better drug-like properties, increases SIRT1 activity in retina/optic nerve, promotes SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of p53, NF-κB p65, and STAT3, reduces apoptosis/inflammation, and upregulates autophagy in optic nerve, establishing that STAC-mediated SIRT1 activation can be protective and autophagy-enhancing in ocular tissues (Chang et al., 2024).  
- Mitochondrial stress/biogenesis axis engagement by SRT1720: In non-ocular epithelia, SRT1720 induces PGC-1α–dependent mitochondrial biogenesis and rescues function after oxidant injury, a desirable capability for stressed RPE; SRT1720 also modulates metabolic signaling via SIRT1–PGC-1α/AMPK/PPARα/FOXO1 pathways, consistent with correcting mitochondrial oxidative metabolism defects that characterize AMD RPE (Ji et al., 2021; Ding et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2020).  
- Therapeutic tractability of lysosome/autophagy endpoints: TFEB/CLEAR biology, lysosomal acidification, and cathepsin function are well-defined levers in RPE; measuring autophagic flux (LC3, p62, RFP-GFP-LC3), lysosomal pH (LysoSensor), and hydrolase activity (e.g., CTSD) is straightforward and interpretable in RPE assays, enabling rapid validation of the hypothesis in vitro (Boya et al., 2023; Kaarniranta et al., 2023).

Weaknesses and Risks  
- Absence of direct RPE/AMD data for SRT1720: No provided reference shows SRT1720 engaging SIRT1 in RPE, improving autophagic flux in RPE, activating TFEB/CLEAR in RPE, or enhancing lysosomal acidification/CTSD activity in RPE or AMD-like models. The core elements of the hypothesis (TFEB deacetylation and CLEAR induction by SRT1720 in RPE) are not documented here and require de novo demonstration (Ji et al., 2021; Falcão et al., 2025; Kaarniranta et al., 2023).  
- Mechanistic controversy and off-targets: The STAC field’s historical assay artifacts, substrate dependence, and the assisted allosteric mechanism place a premium on rigorous target engagement readouts in the specific cellular context; SRT1720 additionally shows Sirt3 inhibitory activity and has been associated with SIRT1-independent cytotoxicity in certain cells, complicating mechanistic attribution (Mazzone, 2018; Chang et al., 2024).  
- Safety signal: SRT1720 increased tumor cell migration and metastasis in a breast cancer mouse model; while AMD is not a malignancy, a pro-migratory/pro-metastatic signal raises a red flag for systemic exposure and suggests careful ocular delivery strategies and off-target profiling if pursued (Bononi et al., 2022; Mazzone, 2018).  
- Lack of PK/ADME and clinical data for SRT1720 in this corpus: Unlike SRT2104, which has human PK/tolerability data and CNS/retinal preclinical activity, SRT1720 lacks disclosed PK/ADME and clinical reports here, hindering translational assessment and dose/exposure design for ocular studies (Bononi et al., 2022; Chang et al., 2024).  
- TFEB/lysosome-specific uncertainty: While CLEAR network activation and lysosomal acidification are compelling RPE targets, the present sources do not show SIRT1-dependent TFEB deacetylation in RPE, nor do they link STACs to lysosomal pH regulation or CTSD activation in RPE; asserting this mechanism is premature without experimental support (Falcão et al., 2025; Boya et al., 2023; Kaarniranta et al., 2023).

Recommendation and Development Path  
- Scientific merit: Pursuing SIRT1 activation to normalize late-stage autophagy and mitochondrial quality control in RPE is well-justified by the PARP1→NAD⁺→SIRT1 axis in RPE and AMD donor biology; however, SRT1720 is not, on current evidence, the optimal first-in-class molecule to test this hypothesis in RPE (Jang et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020).  
- Prioritization: If the team’s objective is target validation, consider tool-compound triage: compare SRT1720 with a second-generation clinical-stage STAC (SRT2104) and with an orthogonal approach that is known to restore SIRT1 activity in RPE (e.g., NAD⁺ boosting) in parallel RPE assays, using target-engagement and autophagy/lysosome endpoints defined a priori (Chang et al., 2024; Jang et al., 2020).  
- Minimal dataset to proceed (go/no-go):  
  1) Demonstrate SIRT1 activation in human RPE by SRT1720 at non-toxic concentrations via decreased acetyl-p53 (K382), decreased acetyl-PGC-1α, and decreased acetyl-p65 (K310), with rescue by SIRT1 knockdown or a SIRT1-dead mutant to confirm on-target action (Luo et al., 2025; Jang et al., 2020).  
  2) Show restoration of late-stage autophagy under H₂O₂ or A2E stress: increased autolysosome formation in tandem RFP-GFP-LC3, decreased p62, and restored LC3-II flux in the presence/absence of lysosomal inhibitors; confirm loss of effect with SIRT1 knockdown (Jang et al., 2020).  
  3) Test TFEB/CLEAR explicitly: TFEB nuclear translocation, TFEB transcriptional reporter, and CLEAR target induction (e.g., LAMP2 and CTSD mRNA/protein) with and without mTORC1 modulation; test necessity of SIRT1 by knockdown and sufficiency by PGC-1α gain-of-function to probe the SIRT1→PGC-1α→TFEB chain (Boya et al., 2023; Falcão et al., 2025).  
  4) Quantify lysosomal acidification and hydrolase activity: LysoSensor/LysoTracker measurements and CTSD activity assays; relate outcomes to TFEB activation and to autophagic flux to determine whether SRT1720 enhances lysosomal function or acts predominantly on pre-lysosomal steps (Kaarniranta et al., 2023; Boya et al., 2023).  
  5) Evaluate mitophagy and mitochondrial function: PINK1/Parkin recruitment, mt-Keima flux, mitochondrial respiration/ATP, and ROS after SRT1720 in stressed RPE to connect SIRT1–PGC-1α engagement to mitochondrial quality control (Kaarniranta et al., 2023; Luján et al., 2022).  
  6) Safety pharmacology in RPE: assess proliferation/migration, EMT markers, and off-target sirtuin activity (Sirt3) to de-risk pro-migratory liabilities observed in cancer models (Bononi et al., 2022; Mazzone, 2018).  

- If SRT1720 fails to deliver on TFEB/CLEAR and lysosomal pH/hydrolase endpoints but still rescues late-stage autophagic flux, the program can pivot to an autophagy-centric mechanism of action (MoA) without TFEB claims; conversely, if TFEB/CLEAR induction is robust, the candidate may differentiate from mTOR inhibitors via epigenetic/cofactor-driven CLEAR activation, contingent on confirming SIRT1 dependency (Boya et al., 2023; Falcão et al., 2025).  
- For in vivo progression, the SI model used to establish PARP1→SIRT1/autophagy dependency provides a tractable retinal degeneration context, but ocular delivery and safety (especially with first-generation STACs) will require localized dosing strategies (intravitreal/subretinal) and careful dosing to minimize systemic exposure (Jang et al., 2020).

Bottom Line  
- The hypothesis that SRT1720 can correct RPE proteostasis in dry AMD via SIRT1 activation is mechanistically credible with respect to autophagy/mitophagy restoration, but the specific claims of TFEB deacetylation, CLEAR network activation, and enhancement of lysosomal acidification/CTSD activity by SRT1720 in RPE are not supported by the present literature and must be treated as untested (Jang et al., 2020; Falcão et al., 2025; Boya et al., 2023).  
- Given the safety concerns and off-target profile of SRT1720, and the existence of a later-generation STAC (SRT2104) with human PK and ocular neuroprotection, SRT1720 should not be advanced beyond in vitro RPE validation until it demonstrates clear, on-target superiority in RPE assays and acceptable safety margins; otherwise, the team should prioritize either SRT2104 or NAD⁺ augmentation/PARP1 modulation for translational efforts (Chang et al., 2024; Bononi et al., 2022; Jang et al., 2020).  

Summary assessment  
- Pros: Strong disease–target alignment in RPE; robust class biology for SIRT1-driven autophagy/mitophagy; SRT1720 can activate SIRT1 pathways in other oxidative-stress contexts; feasible, discriminating assays exist to test the MoA (Zhang et al., 2020; Jang et al., 2020; Ji et al., 2021; Boya et al., 2023).  
- Cons: No direct evidence of efficacy/mechanism in RPE; TFEB/lysosome mechanistic claims unsupported; safety/off-target risks; no PK/clinical data in this corpus; better-characterized STAC alternatives exist (Falcão et al., 2025; Bononi et al., 2022; Chang et al., 2024).  

Decision guidance: Proceed with a narrowly scoped, mechanism-first in vitro validation of SRT1720 in human RPE focused on SIRT1 target engagement and late-stage autophagy rescue under oxidative/phagolysosomal stress, with explicit tests for TFEB/CLEAR activation and lysosomal acidification/CTSD activity; in parallel, benchmark against SRT2104 and NAD⁺ repletion, and use pre-specified go/no-go criteria tied to on-target mechanistic readouts and safety signals to decide whether SRT1720 merits in vivo exploration in retinal degeneration models (Jang et al., 2020; Chang et al., 2024; Boya et al., 2023).
