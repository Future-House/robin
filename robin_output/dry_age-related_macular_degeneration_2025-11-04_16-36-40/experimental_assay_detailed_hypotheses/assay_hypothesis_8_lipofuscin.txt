Assay Overview:  
The proposed assay measures endogenous lipofuscin-like autofluorescence in RPE cell cultures to model bisretinoid accumulation—a process central to AMD pathogenesis. High-content imaging methods (e.g., confocal microscopy and FACS) are employed to quantify changes in fluorescence intensity at defined spectral windows, reflecting accumulation or clearance of lipofuscin components such as A2E. The assay uses RPE cells, typically ARPE-19 or primary human RPE cells, and can incorporate photoreceptor outer segments (native or oxidized) to stimulate physiological bisretinoid formation (garciagarcia2022pathophysiologyofagerelated pages 5-6, lei2017inhibitionorstimulation pages 14-16).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Lipofuscin accumulation in RPE is a hallmark of aging and dry AMD, where bisretinoid compounds exert photo-oxidative stress, trigger inflammatory cascades, and impair lysosomal degradation. This cascade contributes directly to RPE dysfunction and subsequent photoreceptor loss, with clinical imaging of autofluorescence already used for AMD detection. The assay’s focus on autofluorescence ties directly to these pathological alterations and provides a surrogate measure of the oxidative and degradative stress experienced by RPE cells (garciagarcia2022pathophysiologyofagerelated pages 5-6, inana2018rpephagocyticfunction pages 1-2).

Previous Use:  
In vitro studies have successfully employed LLAF assays to monitor the modulation of lipofuscin accumulation by manipulating autophagy and lysosomal pathways. For example, rapamycin-induced autophagy has been shown to reduce LLAF, while inhibition of lysosomal function increases it. Such work has provided proof-of-principle for using autofluorescence as a readout in drug screening for retinal degenerative conditions (lei2017inhibitionorstimulation pages 1-3, lei2017inhibitionorstimulation pages 5-8).

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths of this assay include its direct link to a clinically relevant pathogenic process and the ability to obtain high-resolution, quantitative imaging data. However, weaknesses include technical challenges such as spectral heterogeneity, photobleaching, and the potential discrepancy between acute in vitro accumulation versus chronic in vivo conditions. Complementary functional endpoints may be required to validate that reductions in autofluorescence translate into meaningful RPE rescue (rozanowska2022photodegradationoflipofuscin pages 11-12, rozanowska2022photodegradationoflipofuscin pages 1-2).

Lipofuscin autofluorescence is a promising biomarker for early dry AMD drug screening. It directly reflects bisretinoid accumulation and associated oxidative stress, although careful assay calibration and complementary validations are necessary for robust therapeutic evaluation.
