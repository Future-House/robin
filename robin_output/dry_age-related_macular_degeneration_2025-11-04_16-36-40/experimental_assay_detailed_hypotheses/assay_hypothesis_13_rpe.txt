Assay Overview: This assay utilizes either a contact or non‐contact transwell coculture system in which retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells are paired with choroidal (or surrogate) endothelial cells to mimic paracrine signaling. It models the transition from dry AMD to neovascular AMD by replicating the increased secretion of pro‐angiogenic factors such as VEGF and PDGF by stressed RPE. Quantitative endpoints include endothelial sprouting and tube formation measurements—assessed via image analysis of tube length and node numbers—using conditioned media from the basal (choroidal) compartment (farjood2019effectofphysical pages 109-115, farjood2019effectofphysical pages 78-84).

Biomedical Evidence: Clinical and experimental data indicate that mechanical stress and loss of junctional integrity in RPE cells trigger upregulation of VEGF and other mediators that drive endothelial activation and subsequent neovascularization (farjood2019effectofphysical pages 19-23, farjood2019effectofphysical pages 52-57). Since the paracrine crosstalk between RPE and endothelial cells is central to CNV development in AMD, modeling this interaction is highly relevant for testing drugs aimed at preventing the dry-to-wet transition (helotera2022alinkagebetween pages 2-4, farjood2019effectofphysical pages 109-115).

Previous Use: Prior studies have demonstrated that mechanical perturbation of RPE leads to increased VEGF secretion and enhanced tube formation in coculture assays, providing key insights into anti-VEGF and potential anti-PDGF therapeutic strategies (farjood2019effectofphysical pages 109-115, farjood2019effectofphysical pages 84-88, wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 7-9).

Overall Evaluation: This assay’s strength lies in its ability to capture directional paracrine signaling via transwell setups and to quantitatively assess endothelial angiogenic responses. However, it may oversimplify the multifactorial microenvironment of AMD, as it does not fully recapitulate chronic RPE atrophy, ECM changes, or immune cell interactions (farjood2019effectofphysical pages 12-19, farjood2019effectofphysical pages 19-23). It remains a valuable early-stage screening tool, but complementary models may be necessary to cover additional aspects of dry AMD pathology.

This coculture angiogenesis assay is a suitable platform for evaluating therapeutic modulation of RPE-derived angiogenic signaling in dry AMD. It provides quantifiable endpoints relevant to the disease progression while maintaining a degree of experimental simplicity.
