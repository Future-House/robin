Assay Overview:  
This assay employs a tandem mCherry-GFP-LC3 construct in live RPE cells to dynamically monitor autophagic flux. It models a key pathologic feature of dry AMD—impaired autophagosome clearance due to lysosomal dysfunction and p62 accumulation—and distinguishes between increased autophagosome formation versus blocked autophagosome–lysosome fusion. Fluorescence microscopy quantifies the GFP (acid-sensitive) and mCherry (acid-stable) signal to track autophagosomes (yellow puncta) and autolysosomes (red puncta) in primary or well‐characterized RPE cell culture models (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Autophagy dysfunction in RPE cells is central to dry AMD pathogenesis. Impaired autophagic flux contributes to the accumulation of toxic debris, lipofuscin, and drusen formation, all hallmarks of AMD. Clinical and biochemical studies demonstrate that reduced clearance of damaged proteins and organelles due to lysosomal deficits directly correlates with disease progression (kaarniranta2017autophagyregulatesdeath pages 4-7, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 10-11). This assay is thus well suited to evaluate interventions targeting restoration of autophagy in the context of AMD pathology.

Previous Use:  
Prior studies have validated the tandem reporter in primary RPE cells and in models of light-induced degeneration to quantify autophagic flux. Interventions such as trehalose administration have shown increased autolysosomal maturation using this reporter, leading to improved cellular clearance (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8, si2023theroleof pages 5-7). These findings underscore its utility in early-stage drug discovery targeting RPE autophagy.

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths of this assay include its real-time, dynamic measurement of autophagic flux, which directly addresses a central defect in dry AMD. It allows for sensitive discrimination between altered autophagosome production and lysosomal degradation deficits. However, potential limitations include inter-assay variability due to diurnal effects on autophagy in RPE cells and the need for complementary controls to distinguish flux alterations from changes in autophagosome synthesis (kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3, cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 1-2).

This tandem mCherry-GFP-LC3 assay is a robust and mechanistically relevant method for evaluating therapeutics aimed at restoring autophagic flux in dry AMD. It directly addresses disease-specific autophagy defects, making it a valuable tool in early-stage drug discovery.
