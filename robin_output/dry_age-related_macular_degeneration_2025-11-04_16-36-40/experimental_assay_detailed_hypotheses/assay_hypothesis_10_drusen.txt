Assay Overview: Differentiated RPE grown on porous transwell inserts spontaneously deposit sub‑RPE (drusen‑like) material that can be quantified longitudinally by imaging. Endpoints include deposit burden (number, area, size distributions) from whole‑well tiling and semi‑automated image analysis after vital lipid staining (e.g., Nile red) and/or confocal immunofluorescence; composition can be assessed by IHC (APOE, CFH, C9, vitronectin, TIMP3, collagen V), and by IMS/SIMS and synchrotron XRF/XRD to resolve lipids, proteins, hydroxyapatite, and trace metals. Complement activation can be measured by C5b‑9 immunoreactivity with pathway dissection via serum depletion/reconstitution (e.g., C1q, factor B). Primary porcine RPE, human fetal RPE, and hiPSC‑RPE have all generated deposits on PET/polyester inserts over 12–26+ weeks, enabling imaging‑based quantification and compositional readouts suitable for pharmacologic perturbation studies (including apical vs basal compartment manipulations) (shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 3-6, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 6-7, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 6-7, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 1-2, johnson2011cellculturemodel pages 1-2, johnson2011cellculturemodel pages 5-5, chen2020unstimulatedserumfreecultures pages 1-3).

Biomedical Evidence: Soft drusen are central to early/intermediate AMD biology and correlate with progression; RPE‑derived lipoproteins (apoB/E) accumulate basally, and drusen volume is a clinically measurable endpoint by OCT. Drusen comprise 40–60% lipids with proteins (apolipoproteins, complement) and hydroxyapatite; CFH, C9, APOE, and vitronectin localize to deposits in culture and human tissue. In vitro, APOE‑rich deposits bind C1q and trigger classical pathway MAC formation, mirroring genetic and biochemical complement involvement in AMD. These culture deposits reproduce key constituents and complement activation mechanisms implicated in disease, providing pathobiologically relevant efficacy readouts (curcio2018softdrusenin pages 11-12, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 6-7, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 1-2, johnson2011cellculturemodel pages 1-2, johnson2011cellculturemodel pages 5-5).

Previous Use: Multiple groups quantified deposit dynamics over months and proposed these platforms for high‑throughput, cost‑scalable screening. Functional serum manipulations (C1q depletion/reconstitution) modulated MAC deposition, demonstrating tractability for mechanism‑based interventions. Imaging pipelines quantify treatment responses (e.g., lipid‑reducing strategies such as APOA‑I mimetics have been proposed), and related human RPE systems have identified candidate agents modulating drusen‑related phenotypes (shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 3-6, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 6-7, johnson2011cellculturemodel pages 1-2, johnson2011cellculturemodel pages 5-5, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 11-12, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—disease‑relevant biology (APOE/CFH/complement, lipid/mineral matrix), human‑translatable deposit metrics, multidimensional composition analyses (IHC, IMS/SIMS, XRF/XRD), polarity/barrier control, and complement readouts enabling MoA testing. Weaknesses—long culture times and variable viability, insert‑dependent entrapment, incomplete or model‑dependent lipid/oxidized lipid content (some hiPSC‑RPE lack neutral lipids), inconsistent detection of certain biomarkers (e.g., APOE variance), and serum conditions that may bias complement pathways (high factor H suppressing alternative pathway), all of which can affect assay sensitivity and external validity. Nile red detects only lipid‑rich deposits and limits re‑imaging frequency, necessitating multimodal endpoints and standardized culture conditions for robust screening (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 6-7, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 3-6, chen2020unstimulatedserumfreecultures pages 1-3, chen2020unstimulatedserumfreecultures pages 5-6, johnson2011cellculturemodel pages 5-5, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 6-7, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 11-12).

This transwell RPE drusen‑like deposit assay is well‑justified biologically and methodologically suited for therapeutic testing against dry AMD, especially for agents targeting lipoprotein deposition and complement activation. Adoption should include multiplexed readouts (deposit burden, composition, C5b‑9), defined serum/complement conditions, and standardized imaging analytics to mitigate model heterogeneity and timeline constraints (shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 3-6, johnson2011cellculturemodel pages 1-2, johnson2011cellculturemodel pages 5-5, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 1-2).
