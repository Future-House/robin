Assay Overview: The proposed assay combines pH‐sensitive LysoSensor probes with fluorogenic cathepsin D substrates to quantify both lysosomal acidification and protease function in cultured retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells. It models a critical aspect of dry AMD pathogenesis—impaired lysosomal clearance that leads to lipofuscin buildup—by measuring the intracellular lysosomal pH and the functional activity of cathepsin D via fluorescence detection. The assay can be implemented in RPE models such as patient-derived hiPSC‐RPE or ARPE‐19 cells (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 4-7, voisin2019cathepsinbphdependent pages 3-4).

Biomedical Evidence: Dry AMD pathogenesis is closely associated with lysosomal dysfunction, where lysosomal alkalization and diminished cathepsin D activity correlate with the accumulation of undigested photoreceptor outer segments and drusen formation. Clinical and experimental data demonstrate that restoring lysosomal acidification improves cathepsin function, thereby enhancing degradative capacity and reducing lipofuscin accumulation (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 4-7, ng2024impairedcathepsind pages 13-15).

Previous Use: Similar lysosomal pH and cathepsin activity assays have been employed in drug discovery to validate compounds that reacidify lysosomes and normalize proteolysis. These studies yielded key insights into the reversal of lysosomal defects using therapeutic agents, including interventions that modulate autophagy or complement pathways (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 4-7, ng2024impairedcathepsind pages 13-15).

Overall Evaluation: The assay offers robust, quantitative endpoints that directly reflect a central pathological mechanism in dry AMD and is well suited for high-throughput screening. However, variability in cell culture models and the need to correlate in vitro findings with in vivo outcomes warrant additional validation (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 2-4, voisin2019cathepsinbphdependent pages 3-4).

This assay provides a strong platform for early drug screening in dry AMD by directly linking lysosomal function restoration to therapeutic efficacy. It is a valuable, mechanistically informed tool for evaluating novel compounds in a disease-relevant context.
