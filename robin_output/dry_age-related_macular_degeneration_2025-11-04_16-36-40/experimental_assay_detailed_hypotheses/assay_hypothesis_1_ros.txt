Assay Overview: A ROS accumulation assay quantifies cytosolic and mitochondrial oxidant burden in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) under AMD‑relevant stress using CM‑H2DCFDA (total/cytosolic ROS) and MitoSOX Red (mitochondrial superoxide), read by plate reader and/or live imaging prior to overt cell death (e.g., 2 h post‑insult) (dorion2021thecd36ligandpromoted pages 1-2, ortiz2024themicrornalet7f pages 4-5). Typical implementations use ARPE‑19, iPSC‑derived RPE, or primary RPE; oxidants include NaIO3, H2O2, TBHP, paraquat, or disease mimetics (Aβ), with probe conditions such as CM‑H2DCFDA 5–10 µM 30 min or MitoSOX 2–2.5 µM 10–15 min and standard excitation/emission settings (kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 13-14, dorion2021thecd36ligandpromoted pages 1-2, ortiz2024themicrornalet7f pages 4-5, sun2020rosproductionand pages 1-2).

Biomedical Evidence: Oxidative stress is a central driver of RPE injury and dry AMD pathogenesis in human, animal, and cellular studies, with mitochondria and NADPH oxidases as major sources; mtDNA damage, antioxidant enzyme dysregulation, and redox biomarkers are consistently linked to disease, and mtROS elevation in RPE is sufficient to disrupt retinal metabolism and structure in vivo (chan2019reactiveoxygenspeciesdependent pages 1-2, brown2019mitochondrialoxidativestress pages 1-2, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 8-10, hernandez2025roleofoxidative pages 4-6, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 1-3). Clinically, antioxidant supplementation slows progression in subsets of patients, supporting therapeutic relevance of redox modulation while underscoring complexity and stage dependence (kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 13-14).

Previous Use: ROS probes are widely deployed to evaluate candidate protectants in RPE models: NaIO3 or H2O2 elevates ROS and triggers mitochondrion‑dependent apoptosis, which is mitigated by test agents that reduce DCF/MitoSOX signals alongside improved Δψm, Bax/Bcl‑2 balance, caspase activation, and histology in vitro/in vivo (chang2021protectiveeffectof pages 1-2, park2019protectiveeffectof pages 1-3, hu2024ginsenosiderg3improved pages 1-2). Workflows combining H2DCFDA/MitoSOX with lipid peroxidation (BODIPY‑C11) and iron (FerroOrange) readouts have identified ferroptosis‑targeted interventions; NaIO3 can preferentially raise cytosolic over mitochondrial ROS, highlighting compartmental specificity (lee20225lipoxygenaseinhibitionprotects pages 2-3, chan2019reactiveoxygenspeciesdependent pages 1-2). High‑throughput stress‑based screens have found RPE‑protective compounds and validated improvements in mitochondrial respiration, even when ROS probes were not primary endpoints (cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 1-2). Caveats include paradoxical outcomes where ROS scavengers worsen NaIO3 toxicity and probe discordance under chronic oxidative adaptation (chan2019reactiveoxygenspeciesdependent pages 1-2, macchioni2020crosstalkbetweenlongterm pages 13-15).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: mechanistically proximal to dry AMD; fast, sensitive, and HTS‑compatible; distinguishes compartmental ROS; integrates well with Δψm, apoptosis, respiration, lipid peroxidation, and iron assays to build causal chains from oxidant burden to cell fate (dorion2021thecd36ligandpromoted pages 1-2, ortiz2024themicrornalet7f pages 4-5, lee20225lipoxygenaseinhibitionprotects pages 2-3, cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 1-2). Weaknesses: probe artifacts and antioxidant adaptation can mask or mislead; ROS are pleiotropic (pro‑death and pro‑survival/autophagic), so ROS reduction alone is not a reliable efficacy surrogate; outcomes are oxidant- and model‑dependent and require orthogonal functional endpoints and in vivo confirmation (chan2019reactiveoxygenspeciesdependent pages 1-2, macchioni2020crosstalkbetweenlongterm pages 13-15, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 13-14).

A ROS accumulation assay in RPE is mechanistically relevant, operationally tractable, and informative for triaging redox‑targeted therapeutics for dry AMD. It should not be used in isolation; pairing with mitochondrial function, apoptosis/ferroptosis markers, and in vivo validation is necessary to avoid false leads and capture disease‑relevant benefit (lee20225lipoxygenaseinhibitionprotects pages 2-3, cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 1-2, park2019protectiveeffectof pages 1-3).
