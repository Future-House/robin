Assay Overview: This assay quantifies mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψm) in RPE using potentiometric dyes (e.g., JC-1, TMRE) to model mitochondrial dysfunction central to dry AMD. JC-1 reports high Δψm via red J-aggregate fluorescence and low Δψm via green monomer fluorescence; the red/green ratio is read by plate reader, flow cytometry, or confocal microscopy in ARPE-19 or primary human RPE under oxidative or pharmacologic perturbation (e.g., H2O2, FCCP) (excitation/emission ≈485/535 nm for green and 560–540/595–590 nm for red) (mitter2014dysregulatedautophagyin pages 15-16, bardak2018protectiveeffectsof pages 6-10, he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 4-7, guerra2021relativecontributionof pages 2-3). Typical conditions include 1 μg/ml JC-1 for 20 min at 37°C in primary RPE or 1 μM for 15 min in ARPE-19, followed by washing and ratiometric readout; orthogonal endpoints (ATP, cytochrome c release, ROS, LDH/PI, EM) are assessed in parallel (he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 4-7, bardak2018protectiveeffectsof pages 6-10).

Biomedical Evidence: In primary human RPE, decreased Δψm correlates with aging, reduced ATP, altered Ca2+ handling, ultrastructural mitochondrial abnormalities, and heightened susceptibility to oxidative stress, aligning Δψm loss with key pathologic features of AMD (he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 1-2). Δψm dissipation is an upstream trigger for mitophagy via PINK1–Parkin, linking the readout to mitochondrial quality control failures implicated in RPE degeneration (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 2-3). Diverse AMD-relevant insults (e.g., NaIO3, menadione, H2O2, tBHP) cause mitochondrial depolarization, increased ROS, and downstream apoptotic/necrotic signaling in RPE, supporting Δψm as a central functional biomarker (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 11-13).

Previous Use: JC-1 Δψm assays have been widely implemented in RPE drug studies. In ARPE-19, JC-1 red/green ratios quantified mitochondrial depolarization and were combined with mitochondrial mass (MitoTracker Green) and ROS/protein carbonyl assays to interpret therapeutic effects under oxidative stress (mitter2014dysregulatedautophagyin pages 15-16). In ARPE-19 exposed to 2-ethylpyridine, JC-1 detected Δψm preservation by candidate interventions, with rigorous plate-reader settings and replication, alongside ROS, lipid peroxidation, glutathione, VEGF, Ca2+, and apoptosis assays (bardak2018protectiveeffectsof pages 6-10). In primary human RPE, JC-1 was integrated with ATP, cytochrome c, EM, and viability to relate Δψm changes to bioenergetic failure and apoptosis during aging and H2O2 stress (he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 4-7, he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—Δψm is an early, sensitive, and scalable live-cell readout of mitochondrial injury; ratiometric JC-1 enables rapid screening in 96-well plates or flow cytometry and integrates well with ATP, respiration, ROS, cytochrome c, EM, and functional barrier assays; FCCP and electron transport inhibitors provide robust positive controls (he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 4-7, mitter2014dysregulatedautophagyin pages 15-16, bardak2018protectiveeffectsof pages 6-10, guerra2021relativecontributionof pages 2-3, guerra2021relativecontributionof pages 1-2). Weaknesses—JC-1 signals are relative and influenced by mitochondrial mass and fixation; dye loading/aggregation can artifactually shift ratios; Δψm alone is not proof of apoptosis or functional rescue and may miss disease-relevant differences depending on model (primary haRPE vs iPSC-RPE) and timing, necessitating orthogonal endpoints and normalization (mitter2014dysregulatedautophagyin pages 15-16, he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 4-7, fisher2022humanipscand pages 11-12, fisher2022humanipscand pages 1-2, guerra2021relativecontributionof pages 13-15).

Mitochondrial membrane potential assays are directly relevant and practical for screening RPE-targeted therapeutics in dry AMD, detecting early mitochondrial injury and rescue. Their value is maximized by pairing with ATP/respiration, ROS, cytochrome c, ultrastructure, and barrier-function assays, and by using appropriate positive controls and mass normalization. Model selection (primary vs iPSC-RPE) and assay design materially influence sensitivity and interpretability.
