Assay Overview: This assay utilizes Seahorse extracellular flux analysis to measure oxygen consumption rate (OCR) parameters—including basal respiration, ATP‐linked respiration, spare respiratory capacity, and maximal respiration—in RPE monolayers. It models the bioenergetic and oxidative stress deficits seen in dry AMD by challenging RPE cells (ARPE-19, primary human RPE, or iPSC-derived RPE) with stressors such as TBHP or UV-B and then assessing recovery with candidate drugs (e.g., ciclopirox olamine, metformin, trehalose) (cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 9-11, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15).

Biomedical Evidence: Mitochondrial dysfunction is a central pathogenic factor in dry AMD, as RPE cells depend on efficient oxidative phosphorylation for retinal homeostasis. Declines in mitochondrial respiration, ATP production, and reserve capacity are linked to increased oxidative stress, mtDNA damage, and eventual RPE cell death observed clinically (ferrington2017alteredbioenergeticsand pages 2-3, ferrington2017alteredbioenergeticsand pages 7-7). Thus, therapeutics that restore these bioenergetic parameters could directly counteract AMD-associated cellular deficits (bhattacharya2022modelingofmitochondrial pages 1-2, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 15-17).

Previous Use: Seahorse-based mitochondrial profiling has been employed extensively in RPE models for drug discovery, demonstrating that improved OCR metrics correlate with enhanced cell viability and reduced stress responses. Key discoveries using this approach include the identification of compounds such as ciclopirox olamine and AMPK activators that rescue mitochondrial function in both primary RPE and iPSC-derived RPE models (cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 9-11, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6).

Overall Evaluation: This assay’s strengths are its quantitative, real-time measurements of mitochondrial function and its applicability across different RPE sources, making it highly relevant for addressing dry AMD’s bioenergetic deficits. However, cell source variability and the specific culture conditions needed for differentiation and maturation of RPE may pose challenges for reproducibility (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 9-11, fisher2022humanipscand pages 11-12).

Mitochondrial respiration profiling via Seahorse provides critical insights into RPE metabolic dysfunction in dry AMD. It is a robust and translationally relevant assay for early-stage therapeutic screening.
