Assay Overview:  
This assay quantifies complement-mediated injury in dry AMD by measuring the deposition of the membrane attack complex (MAC, C5b-9) on retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cell monolayers. RPE cells (e.g., ARPE-19 or primary human fetal RPE) are cultured on transwell inserts, incubated with human serum to trigger complement activation, and optionally stimulated with agents like CEP to promote alternative pathway activation. Following incubation, immunostaining with an anti-C5b-9 antibody and fluorescent secondary detection are performed to assess MAC deposition, serving as a direct readout of terminal complement activity (mulfaul2021invitromeasurement pages 7-11, armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 1-2).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Multiple studies demonstrate that complement dysregulation is central to dry AMD pathogenesis. Genetic variants in regulators such as CFH (e.g., the Y402H substitution) are strongly associated with increased MAC deposition on RPE and subsequent cellular injury. Clinical and experimental data link elevated MAC levels to drusen formation and RPE dysfunction, thereby implicating terminal complement activation as a key contributor to geographic atrophy (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 1-2, hammadi2023bruch’smembranea pages 12-14, kassa2019complementinhibitionas pages 1-7).

Previous Use:  
Prior studies have employed MAC deposition assays on RPE cells to screen complement inhibitors, revealing that reduced MAC formation correlates with improved RPE function. These assays have provided insights into sub-lytic MAC effects, such as altered VEGF secretion and barrier disruption, thus proving useful in early-stage drug discovery for complement-targeted interventions (mulfaul2021invitromeasurement pages 7-11, ng2022membraneattackcomplex pages 13-15).

Overall Evaluation:  
The strengths of this assay include its mechanistic relevance, quantifiable endpoint, and ease of integration into early-stage screening pipelines. It directly models clinically implicated complement overactivation and RPE injury. However, limitations include its inability to fully recapitulate the complex tissue architecture of the retina and the chronic, low-grade complement activation seen in vivo. Despite these weaknesses, the assay remains a robust platform for initial therapeutic evaluation in dry AMD (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 4-5, lynch2020complementfactorsand pages 2-2).

This assay is a valuable tool for predicting the efficacy of complement-targeted therapies in dry AMD. Its ability to directly measure MAC deposition on RPE cells provides a mechanistically relevant and quantifiable endpoint for screening potential therapeutics.
