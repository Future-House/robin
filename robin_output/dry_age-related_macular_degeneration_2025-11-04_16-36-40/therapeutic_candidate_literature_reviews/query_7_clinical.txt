Query: Clinical development reports of small molecules improving autophagic flux in RPE, specifically through lysosomal acidification and cathepsin D activation, including phase 1 safety and tolerability data, pharmacokinetic parameters, biomarkers of target engagement, preliminary efficacy endpoints in dry AMD patients, dose selection rationale, ocular versus systemic delivery methods, adverse event profiles, and therapeutic index estimation

The current context indicates that no Phase 1 clinical trials have been registered or reported for small molecules designed to enhance autophagic flux in RPE via lysosomal acidification and cathepsin D activation for dry AMD (Clinical Trial Search: 103bbbae5936). No human data exist on safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetic parameters, biomarkers of target engagement, preliminary efficacy endpoints, dose selection rationale, delivery methods, adverse event profiles, or therapeutic index estimation for these agents. Preclinical studies have provided mechanistic insights into RPE lysosomal dysfunction and autophagy impairment that support targeting these pathways. For example, cellular and in vivo models have demonstrated that cathepsin D accumulation, TFEB localization, and associated autophagic markers may serve as candidate biomarkers of target engagement (zuhl2016chemoproteomicprofilingreveals pages 7-8, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7, zuhl2016chemoproteomicprofilingreveals pages 1-2). Translational investigations have also discussed potential dose selection strategies and the pros and cons of ocular versus systemic delivery to limit ocular toxicity (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 17-18, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 9-11, lizama2023sigma2receptors—frombasic pages 17-18). However, these preclinical findings have not yet been translated into human Phase 1 trials that can report definitive safety, PK/PD, and efficacy data (Clinical Trial Search: 103bbbae5936).

No clinical development reports currently exist for Phase 1 evaluation of these small molecules in dry AMD. The evidence remains confined to preclinical mechanistic and biomarker studies.
