Query: Autophagy and mitophagy pathway crosstalk with lysosomal acidification in RPE cells: evidence for defective cargo degradation and contribution to geographic atrophy lesion expansion in dry AMD, including mechanistic studies of Beclin1, LC3-II turnover, PINK1/Parkin signaling, lysosomal calcium regulation, and therapeutic rescue by pathway-specific modulators in in vitro and animal models

Autophagy–mitophagy is tightly coupled to lysosomal acidification in RPE via V‑ATPase–mTORC1 signaling: V‑ATPase integrity is required for mTORC1 nutrient sensing and thus for autophagy control, and age‑related declines in lysosomal enzyme activity/acidification inhibit autophagic flux and waste clearance in RPE (kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 10-11, blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 13-14). Human AMD RPE show biochemical and structural signatures of a degradative block: increased autophagosomes with a reduced LC3‑II/LC3‑I ratio, failure to clear SQSTM1 during starvation, and enlarged ring‑shaped LAMP1 compartments, consistent with stalled LC3 turnover downstream of lysosomal dysfunction and diversion to secretory autophagy when fusion is impaired (blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 11-13). In AMD‑like models, impaired mitophagy is evidenced by upregulated PINK1/PRKN alongside persistent mitochondrial damage and absent LAMP2–mitochondrial marker colocalization, indicating a block at the autophagosome–lysosome fusion/clearance step (brooks2023roleofmitophagy pages 6-7, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 3-5). Genetic loss of core autophagy genes (ATG5/ATG7) produces an AMD‑like RPE phenotype with oxidized protein accumulation, underscoring the causal role of defective autophagic clearance (blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 13-14).

Mechanistically, Beclin‑1 within the ULK1–PI3KC3 complex drives initiation/nucleation, and LC3/ATG8 lipidation supports phagophore expansion; in RPE, LAP for photoreceptor outer segment processing also requires ATG5 and Beclin‑1, linking Beclin‑1 to heterophagic and canonical autophagic clearance (blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 11-13). AMBRA1 connects Beclin‑1–regulated initiation to LC3 engagement and can promote Parkin‑independent mitophagy, emphasizing convergence of multiple entry routes on lysosome‑dependent clearance (kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 5-8). Canonical PINK1/Parkin signaling is perturbed in AMD contexts: early AMD perifoveal RPE exhibit reduced PINK1, pink1−/− mice show impaired mitophagic flux after mitochondrial damage, and ocular models document Parkin overexpression improving mitochondrial quality and neuronal survival, supporting targetability of this axis despite clearance blocks downstream (brooks2023roleofmitophagy pages 6-7, brooks2023roleofmitophagy pages 5-6). Lysosomal compromise is reinforced by lipofuscin‑driven membrane permeabilization and the central role of V‑ATPase–mTORC1 in autophagy control, providing a mechanistic bridge from acidification failure to stalled mitophagy (kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 10-11). The excerpts do not provide direct experimental evidence on lysosomal Ca2+ regulation in RPE, nor quantitative Beclin‑1 activity or LC3‑II turnover kinetics beyond LC3‑II/LC3‑I and SQSTM1 readouts (blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 13-14, nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 27-29, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 10-11).

Defective degradative flux activates secretory autophagy and SASP, and can drive autophagic secretion of mitochondria, lipofuscin/drusen accrual, and inflammation—processes that plausibly fuel geographic atrophy lesion expansion (blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 13-14, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 5-8). Pathway‑specific rescue is demonstrated in vitro and in preclinical models: the CD36 ligand MPE‑001 restores LC3–lysosome colocalization, increases mature STX17+ autophagosomes, reduces ROS, and improves NaIO3‑injured RPE survival in an autophagy‑dependent manner, directly correcting fusion/clearance steps (dorion2019leffetduligand pages 65-68). Activation of TFEB‑centered programs (PPARα–TFEB/CD36; AKT2/SIRT5/TFEB) improves RPE survival, energy metabolism, and reduces drusen, while mitochondrial ferritin overexpression elevates mitophagy and is cytoprotective; a mitochondrial‑targeted drug also improved visual function preclinically, though direct GA‑slowing is not shown here (blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 13-14, brooks2023roleofmitophagy pages 6-7). Collectively, these data position lysosomal acidification and autophagosome–lysosome fusion as critical bottlenecks for LC3‑marked cargo and mitophagosome turnover in AMD‑relevant RPE. However, definitive evidence for lysosomal Ca2+‑targeted rescue or in vivo GA‑slowing by specific Beclin‑1/LC3/PINK1–Parkin modulators is not provided in these excerpts (blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 13-14, brooks2023roleofmitophagy pages 6-7).

Autophagy–mitophagy in RPE is constrained by lysosomal acidification via V‑ATPase–mTORC1, and AMD is marked by stalled LC3 flux, blocked mitophagosome–lysosome fusion, and lysosomal remodeling, consistent with defective cargo degradation that promotes oxidative/inflammatory remodeling and drusen supporting GA growth (kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 10-11, blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 11-13, brooks2023roleofmitophagy pages 6-7, blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 13-14). Several pathway‑specific interventions restore autophagosome–lysosome fusion and improve RPE phenotypes in vitro and reduce risk markers in models, but direct evidence for lysosomal Ca2+ control and GA‑slowing in vivo is not available here (dorion2019leffetduligand pages 65-68, blasiak2024potentialofautophagy pages 13-14, brooks2023roleofmitophagy pages 6-7).
