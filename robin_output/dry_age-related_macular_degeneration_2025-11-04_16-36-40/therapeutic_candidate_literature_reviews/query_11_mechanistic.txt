Query: Mechanistic insights into lysosomal dysfunction in retinal pigment epithelium in dry age-related macular degeneration, including genetic associations of cathepsin D and v-ATPase subunit variants, autophagy impairment, drusen component accumulation, with data from GWAS, proteomics, lysosomal functional assays, drusen proteome characterization, and RPE degeneration imaging in human donor eyes and animal models

Recent studies using human iPSC‐derived RPE, animal models, and proteomic/functional assays have established that lysosomal dysfunction in dry AMD arises from impaired autophagy and disrupted CLEAR network signaling. In RPE cells from CFH Y402H donors, elevated AKT2 activity drives inhibitory phosphorylation of TFEB/TFE3, leading to reduced transcription of lysosomal genes and subsequent decreases in cathepsin D and cathepsin L levels and activities, as confirmed by enzymatic assays and proteomic analysis (ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 10-11, ghosh2023theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 30-34). Moreover, autophagy impairment is manifest as defective autophagosome–lysosome fusion and enhanced secretory autophagy, whereby RPE cells release extracellular vesicles containing drusen-associated proteins that contribute to deposit accumulation (ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 10-11, ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 6-6). Although direct GWAS hits for cathepsin D variants are lacking, Open Targets queries suggest a modest genetic association for the v-ATPase subunit ATP6V1B2, while other subunits like ATP6V0A1 are implicated through downregulation in Akt2-driven models, albeit with limited genetic data (OpenTargets Search: age-related macular degeneration-cathepsin D,ATP6V0A1,ATP6V1B2,ATP6V1C1,ATP6V0C,ATP6V1H). Imaging studies in human donor eyes and Akt2 knock-in mice further reveal RPE degeneration, including basal laminar deposit formation and loss of apical microvilli (ghosh2023theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 30-34, ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 4-6, nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 3-5).

Lysosomal dysfunction in RPE in dry AMD results from autophagy defects leading to reduced cathepsin activity and altered v-ATPase function. These integrated findings support targeting TFEB activation and restoration of lysosomal function as promising therapeutic strategies.
