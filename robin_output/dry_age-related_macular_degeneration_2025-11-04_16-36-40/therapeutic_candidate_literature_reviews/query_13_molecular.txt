Query: Molecular pathways linking impaired lysosomal acidification to chronic inflammation, inflammasome activation, complement dysregulation, and RPE cell death in dry AMD, including mechanistic dissection of NLRP3 inflammasome activation by lysosomal damage, complement C3/C5 cleavage product accumulation, cytokine profiling, and pharmacological inhibition experiments in geographic atrophy models

Impaired lysosomal acidification and lysosomal membrane permeabilization (LMP) in RPE provide the canonical “signal 2” for NLRP3 inflammasome assembly, mechanistically via lysosomal destabilization and cathepsin release after uptake of extracellular aggregates such as drusen and complement components (notably C1q), leading to caspase‑1 activation and maturation of IL‑1β/IL‑18 (with NF‑κB–dependent priming by TLR/IL‑1R/TNFR or CEP as “signal 1”) (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84, ortega2024geneticremovalof pages 1-4, nita2020interplaybetweenreactive pages 11-13, yamamotorodriguez2020newfrontiersand pages 4-5). Photo‑oxidative and lipid aldehyde stressors (A2E, HNE) and pharmacologic perturbations of proteostasis (MG‑132, bafilomycin A1) drive LMP‑NLRP3 activation in RPE; Alu RNA/DICER1 loss prime and activate NLRP3 with P2X7R dependence (P2X7R deficiency is protective) (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 84-86). Functionally, LL‑OMe–induced LMP triggers caspase‑1–dependent IL‑1β release and pyroptotic RPE death, preventable by lysosomal conversion blockade or caspase‑1 inhibition; selective NLRP3 inhibitors (CRID3, IFM‑632/514) suppress IL‑1β and cytotoxicity across LMP, oxidative, photo‑oxidative, and P2X7R models, and RPE injury is absent in Abca4−/−/Nlrp3−/− tissue (nita2020interplaybetweenreactive pages 11-13, wang2019efficacyofnovel pages 4-6). In vivo, genetic deletion of Nlrp3 or Casp1 reduces basal laminar deposits in EFEMP1‑driven RPE pathology, linking inflammasome activity to GA‑like deposit formation (ortega2024geneticremovalof pages 1-4).

Cytokine profiling shows robust IL‑1β with more variable IL‑18 (constitutive IL‑18 in RPE, preferential IL‑18 induction by 7‑ketocholesterol); C5a signaling augments TNF‑α and IL‑6, aligning complement signaling with proinflammatory cytokine networks (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 84-86, karemajokinen2023crosstalkofprotein pages 1-2, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 9-10). The secretion mechanism in human RPE is unresolved: cleaved GSDMD is often not detected, whereas GSDME cleavage is observed with atRAL, despite clear caspase‑1–dependent cytokine maturation (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84).

Complement dysregulation interlocks with lysosomal–inflammasome pathology. CFH Y402H risk RPE exhibit increased C3 turnover, cathepsin D leakage into drusen‑like deposits, and lysosomal dysfunction that is restored by C3 inhibition; MAC (C5b‑9) accumulation can overload lysosomes and promote proinflammatory release, providing a terminal‑pathway route to lysosomal injury and RPE death (mastellos2024aguideto pages 15-16, danzig2024c5inhibitoravacincaptad pages 2-3, yamamotorodriguez2020newfrontiersand pages 4-5). While detailed quantification of intraocular C3/C5 fragment accumulation is not provided here, multiple sources document complement within drusen and MAC‑linked lysosomal stress (danzig2024c5inhibitoravacincaptad pages 2-3, yamamotorodriguez2020newfrontiersand pages 4-5, ortega2024geneticremovalof pages 1-4).

Pharmacologically, complement inhibition slows GA: intravitreal C3 (pegcetacoplan) and C5 (avacincaptad pegol) inhibitors reduce GA growth in Phase III, whereas factor D blockade with lampalizumab failed; factor I augmentation (GT005) remains in Phase II (borchert2023theroleof pages 6-7, danzig2024c5inhibitoravacincaptad pages 2-3). Preclinically, C3 inhibition restores lysosomal function and suppresses drusen/sub‑RPE deposits and inflammasome priming; C5 inhibition can suppress priming, but isolated C5a blockade may be insufficient and C5aR deficiency can worsen neovascularization, highlighting target‑selection risks (mastellos2024aguideto pages 15-16, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 10-11, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 9-10). Anti‑IL‑1 biologics do not block caspase‑1 or IL‑18 and have shown limited retinal efficacy, emphasizing the need to target the lysosome→NLRP3→caspase‑1 axis or complement upstream of terminal cytokines (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84).

Impaired lysosomal acidification/LMP in RPE activates NLRP3 via cathepsin-dependent mechanisms after NF‑κB priming, producing caspase‑1–matured IL‑1β/IL‑18, pyroptosis, and chronic inflammation. Complement contributes both upstream (C1q‑driven lysosomal injury) and downstream (MAC‑induced lysosomal overload), and C3 inhibition can restore lysosomal function in risk RPE. Clinically, C3 and C5 inhibitors slow GA, while selective NLRP3 inhibition is effective preclinically across RPE LMP models, supporting dual targeting of complement and inflammasome pathways in dry AMD (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84, nita2020interplaybetweenreactive pages 11-13, mastellos2024aguideto pages 15-16, danzig2024c5inhibitoravacincaptad pages 2-3, borchert2023theroleof pages 6-7, wang2019efficacyofnovel pages 4-6).
