Query: Pharmacological studies on compounds with proven blood-retinal barrier penetration and lysosomal targeting properties, assessing capacity to restore acidic pH and enhance cathepsin D enzymatic activity in RPE, prevent RPE cell loss in animal models of geographic atrophy, including in vivo biodistribution, retinal drug concentration measurements, dosing regimens, systemic and ocular toxicity assessments, histological lesion quantification, and functional vision outcome measures

Several studies report that compounds activating NRF2/TFEB pathways—such as sulforaphane, dimethyl fumarate, and certain curcumin analogues—exhibit lysosomal targeting properties and can improve aspects of retinal function in oxidative stress models (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 20-20). For example, sulforaphane was shown to recover cone function in mice under RPE oxidative stress, implying improvements in visual outcomes; however, these studies did not include detailed histological lesion quantification or direct measurements of RPE cell loss associated with geographic atrophy (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 20-20, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 11-13). Similarly, remofuscin was reported to reverse lipofuscin accumulation and rescue retinal degeneration in a Stargardt disease model, yet quantitative histological assessments were not provided (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 4-5, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 15-17). Although preclinical work using CPPs, nanoparticles, and hydrogels has yielded promising in vivo biodistribution, retinal concentration data, and favorable toxicity profiles, explicit pharmacological studies demonstrating restoration of acidic lysosomal pH or enhanced cathepsin D enzymatic activity in RPE are not detailed (boddu2023anupdateon pages 17-18, parsons2021peptidomimeticstherapeuticsfor pages 23-25). In addition, while various animal models and dosing regimen designs have been described, integrated studies comprehensively addressing systemic/ocular toxicity, lesion counts, and functional vision outcomes remain incomplete (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 17-18, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7).

This body of work indicates promising mechanistic rationale but falls short of providing a complete pharmacological evaluation of blood–retinal barrier penetrant, lysosomal-targeting compounds in geographic atrophy models. Further dedicated studies are required to validate these endpoints with quantified histology, dosing regimens, and robust functional assessments.
