Query: Impact of age-related post-translational modifications, such as glycation, oxidation, or carbonylation, on cathepsin D stability and function in RPE lysosomes during dry AMD progression: mass spectrometry mapping of modification sites, enzyme kinetics assays under modified conditions, structural modeling of conformational changes, and correlation with enzymatic activity in patient-derived RPE cell extracts

Evidence in dry AMD RPE points to a lysosomal/autophagy-stressed, oxidizing milieu that plausibly favors non-enzymatic PTMs on lysosomal proteases. In this setting, cathepsin D (CTSD) processing is impaired: AMD RPE–derived EVs reduce CTSD maturation (50% lower pro-CTSD and 30% lower mature heavy chain), coincident with reduced autophagic flux, swollen/ruptured lysosomes, and oxidative/metabolic stress, all compatible with PTM-driven destabilization or altered trafficking of CTSD in RPE lysosomes (kurzawa‐akanbi2022retinalpigmentepithelium pages 17-18, bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 23-24). AGE-modified substrates alter lysosomal protease profiles in RPE, with reports of decreased CTSD activity and discordant increases in active CTSD protein without mRNA change, indicating post-transcriptional modulation consistent with PTMs affecting maturation/stability (sharif2019advancedglycationend pages 2-3, sharif2019advancedglycationend pages 3-5, sharif2019advancedglycationend pages 10-11, sharif2019advancedglycationend pages 1-2).

Direct MS maps of glycation/oxidation/carbonylation on CTSD are not reported in these sources, but the JEV study deposits proteomics data (PXD031135) that can be mined for CTSD peptides/modified masses; targeted CTSD IP from RPE lysosomes followed by PTM-aware searches would fill this gap (kurzawa‐akanbi2022retinalpigmentepithelium pages 19-20, kurzawa‐akanbi2022retinalpigmentepithelium pages 20-21). Enzyme kinetics under modified conditions can leverage the fluorometric CTSD assays already used in RPE studies, applying controlled glycation/oxidation to recombinant CTSD and measuring kcat/Km at lysosomal pH to quantify effects on catalysis and stability (sharif2019advancedglycationend pages 2-3, bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 29-32). Structural modeling/MD focused on lysine/arginine (glycation) and oxidation-sensitive residues proximal to the active site and propeptide–mature interfaces would mechanistically link identified PTMs to altered folding, processing, and activity (riccio2017intracellulartraffickingand pages 204-207).

Correlation in human material is feasible using rapid postmortem workflows and MACS-based enrichment to obtain viable RPE for paired MS-PTM quantification and CTSD activity, enabling linkage of modification burden to enzymatic deficits in donor eyes (biber2023thecomplementsystem pages 36-37, bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 23-24).

In dry AMD RPE, oxidative and glycation stressors coincide with reduced CTSD maturation and altered lysosomal function, making PTM-driven CTSD instability/function loss plausible (kurzawa‐akanbi2022retinalpigmentepithelium pages 17-18, sharif2019advancedglycationend pages 2-3). The cited studies lack direct PTM maps; integrated MS mapping, recombinant-enzyme kinetics under defined modifications, structural modeling, and activity measurements in patient-derived RPE are the next steps, supported by available datasets and workflows (kurzawa‐akanbi2022retinalpigmentepithelium pages 19-20, biber2023thecomplementsystem pages 36-37).
