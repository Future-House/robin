Query: Inflammation-mediated impairment of lysosomal function in RPE: studies on cytokine-induced v-ATPase downregulation, cathepsin D secretion alterations, and complement activation loops relevant to dry AMD pathology, including IL-1β and TNF-α effects on autophagic flux, inflammasome cross-talk, RPE cell death assays, and rescue by anti-inflammatory agents

Inflammation in the RPE impairs lysosomal function through interrelated mechanisms that disrupt acidification, protease handling, and autophagic flux. Although direct experimental evidence for cytokine‐induced v‑ATPase downregulation is limited, inflammatory cues are proposed to disturb mTOR/TFEB signaling and thereby reduce transcription of v‑ATPase subunits and associated lysosomal enzymes, including cathepsin D (boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 13-14, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3). Alterations in cathepsin D handling—manifested as abnormal, exosomal secretion or leakage—undermine intracellular proteolysis and may contribute to extracellular drusen formation (pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 9-11, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 2-4). 

Inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and TNF-α elevate NF-κB signaling and prime the NLRP3 inflammasome, leading to caspase-1–mediated maturation of IL-1β and pyroptotic RPE cell death (nita2020interplaybetweenreactive pages 11-13). In this context, compromised autophagic flux—exacerbated by oxidative stress and complement activation loops—further amplifies inflammasome activity and cellular injury (kaarniranta2018mechanisticalretinaldrug pages 14-16, liton2023autophagyinthe pages 23-24). Complement activation may also establish feed-forward loops, as complement fragments and MAC deposition worsen lysosomal destabilization and impair proteasomal as well as autophagic clearance (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 4-5, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 3-4). 

Rescue strategies with anti-inflammatory agents, mTOR inhibitors, and autophagy activators have demonstrated the ability to restore autophagic balance, dampen inflammasome activation, and reduce RPE cell death in experimental settings (kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 12-13, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 5-8).

Inflammation compromises lysosomal acidification and protease regulation in the RPE via disrupted v‑ATPase function, aberrant cathepsin D handling, and complement-mediated feedback. Anti-inflammatory treatments that restore autophagic flux offer promising therapeutic avenues for dry AMD.
