Query: Cross-species comparison of lysosomal pH dynamics and cathepsin D activity in primate versus rodent RPE, assessing relevance to human dry AMD pathophysiology and translational significance of preclinical model findings, including in vivo pH imaging, ex vivo lysosomal activity assays, genetic and pharmacological v-ATPase manipulation, and alignment of transcriptomic lysosomal pathway profiles

Direct, quantitative primate-versus-rodent comparisons of RPE lysosomal pH dynamics and cathepsin D (CatD) enzymatic activity are not provided in the cited materials, and in vivo lysosomal pH imaging or ex vivo cross-species CatD activity assays are absent; moreover, lysosomal pH is technically difficult to measure in living cells and commonly used probes (e.g., Lysotracker) have significant interpretive caveats that complicate cross-model comparisons (e.g., time-dependent accumulation, partial pH independence) (cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 164-167). 

Rodent genetic evidence supports a causal axis linking v-ATPase inhibition to elevated lysosomal pH, reduced CatD activity, and defective degradative flux in RPE: RPE-specific loss of βA3/A1-crystallin (Cryba1 cKO) impairs v-ATPase activity (βA3/A1-crystallin binds ATP6V0A1), raises lysosomal pH, diminishes CatD activity, and causes phagosome/autophagosome accumulation with retinal dysfunction; pharmacologic restoration of lysosomal pH rescued function in related RPE/mouse contexts, underscoring translational potential of targeting acidification (valapala2016modulationofvatpase pages 4-6). 

Human RPE models perturbed with lysosomotropic agents or v-ATPase inhibitors recapitulate lysosomal dysfunction features relevant to AMD, including CatD misprocessing, TFEB nuclear translocation, increased pigmentation, lysosomal exocytosis, and basal extracellular deposit formation; however, reporter-based pH readouts show paradoxes (e.g., prolonged chloroquine increases Lysotracker/Magic Red signals), emphasizing assay limitations for inferring absolute pH (cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 164-167, cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 97-101). 

Cross-species transcriptomics align on aging: human macular RPE/choroid and mouse RPE show predominant downregulation of autophagy/mitophagy and lysosome pathways, with stage-dependent modulation across AMD, supporting conserved lysosomal vulnerability but not supplying functional pH or CatD metrics (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 2-3, cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8). 

Model-system strengths and gaps for translation include: robust mouse genetics and ex vivo RPE culture platforms but species gene-content differences (e.g., ARMS2, CETP, APOE isoforms) and absence of in vivo RPE pH/CatD comparisons; nonetheless, multiple lysosome/autophagy-defective mouse models develop AMD-like phenotypes, and disease-relevant stressors such as A2E can inhibit v-ATPase and proteases, linking acidification to proteolysis failure (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 6-8, cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 58-61). 

In sum, available evidence supports a conserved mechanistic link between v-ATPase-dependent acidification, CatD competence, and RPE clearance across rodent and human models, but definitive cross-species pH and CatD activity comparisons in vivo/ex vivo are missing (valapala2016modulationofvatpase pages 4-6, cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 164-167, cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 2-3). Translationally, targeting lysosomal acidification remains plausible, yet validation will require species-bridging pH imaging and CatD activity assays to de-risk extrapolation to dry AMD (valapala2016modulationofvatpase pages 4-6, cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 2-3, cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 164-167).

There are no direct primate-versus-rodent measurements of RPE lysosomal pH or CatD activity, nor in vivo pH imaging or ex vivo cross-species CatD assays, in the cited sources. Rodent genetics and human RPE pharmacology converge on a model where v-ATPase dysfunction elevates lysosomal pH, compromises CatD, and impairs clearance, and aging transcriptomes are aligned across species. Translational relevance is strong but requires cross-species functional validation of lysosomal pH and CatD readouts for dry AMD.
