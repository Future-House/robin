Assay Overview  
• Rationale and disease connection  
  – In dry AMD, retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells chronically phagocytose photoreceptor outer segments (POS) and with age accumulate partially degraded lipids and lipofuscin, leading to drusen formation, RPE dysfunction and geographic atrophy.1,2  
  – This assay models two central, interlinked pathomechanisms of dry AMD: (a) lipid/drusen-like deposit accumulation in RPE, and (b) impaired autophagic/lysosomal clearance.3,4  
• Cell types and culture  
  – Human primary RPE or iPSC-derived RPE are grown to maturity (pigmented, polarized monolayers).5,6  
  – For autophagy readout, RPE are transduced with an mCherry-GFP-LC3 reporter.7  
• Induction of pathology  
  – RPE monolayers are fed daily or intermittently with photo-oxidized POS fragments (isolated from bovine or porcine retina and subjected to controlled blue-light/UV oxidation) for 1–4 weeks.3,8  
  – This chronic POS challenge drives intracellular neutral lipid (drusen-like) and lipofuscin accumulation.9  
• Readouts  
  1. Lipid/drusen-like deposits: Quantified by high-content confocal imaging of neutral lipid dyes (Nile Red or BODIPY 493/503).10,11 Reduction in lipid-positive puncta or overall fluorescence indicates enhanced clearance.  
  2. Autophagy flux: Monitored in mCherry-GFP-LC3 RPE; yellow (GFP + mCherry) puncta = autophagosomes, red-only puncta = autolysosomes. Automated image analysis yields autophagosome/autolysosome counts per cell and ratio of autolysosomes:autophagosomes.12  
• Intervention  
  – Candidate compounds (e.g., TFEB activators, lysosomal acidification enhancers, mTOR inhibitors) are added throughout POS feeding. A desirable hit reduces Nile Red/BODIPY signal and shifts LC3 reporter signal toward autolysosome formation.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Drusen and lipid accumulation as early AMD hallmarks  
   – Drusen are extracellular lipid-rich deposits between RPE and Bruch’s membrane; their size and confluence correlate with progression risk to geographic atrophy.1,2  
   – Curcio et al. (2010) established that drusen lipids derive both from photoreceptor outer segment constituents and impaired RPE handling.3  
2. Autophagy-lysosome dysfunction in AMD  
   – Autophagy is required for POS turnover and for general cellular proteostasis in RPE.13  
   – Several lines of evidence link impaired autophagy or lysosomal pH dysregulation to RPE aging and AMD pathogenesis:  
     • Kaarniranta et al. (2013) and Mitter et al. (2014) reported accumulation of autophagosomes and diminished autophagic flux in RPE from AMD donors or aged animal models.4,7  
     • Genetic association studies implicate regulators of complement, lipid metabolism and lysosomal function (e.g., C3, ABCA1, LIPC, TIMP3) in AMD risk, underscoring the role of lipid handling and clearance.14  
3. TFEB and lysosomal biogenesis as therapeutic targets  
   – TFEB is a master transcriptional regulator of lysosomal/autophagy genes. Overexpression or pharmacologic activation enhances lipofuscin clearance in RPE and ameliorates retinal pathology in animal AMD models.15,16  
   – Small-molecule TFEB activators (e.g., mTORC1 inhibitors, AKT inhibitors) restore lysosomal acidity and degradative capacity in RPE challenged with POS or A2E (a major lipofuscin fluorophore).17,18  

Previous Use  
• Mitter et al. (2014) used ARPE-19 cells stably expressing mCherry-GFP-LC3 to demonstrate that oxidative stress impairs autophagy flux and that rapamycin partially restores clearance of POS-derived material.7  
• Sparrow’s group employed oxidized POS feeding of primary human RPE to study A2E formation and lipofuscin phototoxicity; they showed that enhancing lysosomal degradation limits lipofuscin accumulation.9,17  
• Lakkaraju et al. (2012) used bovine POS–fed iPSC-derived RPE to elucidate the biogenesis of photoreceptor outer segment phagosomes and their maturation defects in disease models.6  
• High-content screening applications: Although no large-scale screens in primary/iPSC RPE have yet been published, proof-of-principle studies in ARPE-19 demonstrated feasibility of using BODIPY/Nile Red and LC3 reporters to identify autophagy modulators.11,12  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
1. Disease relevance: Recapitulates two core dry AMD processes—drusen/lipofuscin accumulation and autophagic impairment—in human RPE.  
2. Functional, multiplexed readouts: Enables simultaneous quantification of lipid clearance and autophagic flux, reducing false positives.  
3. Scalability: Compatible with 96- to 384-well formats and automated confocal imaging for moderate-throughput screening.  
4. Mechanistic insight: Hits can be triaged by whether they act on early autophagosome formation (GFP + mCherry puncta) versus autolysosome maturation (mCherry-only puncta) or directly on lipid handling.  

Weaknesses  
1. Incomplete microenvironment: Lacks Bruch’s membrane, choriocapillaris endothelial cells and immune components (e.g., complement, microglia), which contribute to drusen biology and RPE atrophy in vivo.  
2. Model simplification: Oxidized POS do not capture full complexity of drusen composition (complement proteins, apatite nodules, bisretinoids).  
3. Cell variability: Primary vs. iPSC-RPE differentiation protocols yield heterogeneous maturation states; ARPE-19 is easier to culture but less physiologically faithful.  
4. Reporter caveats: Overexpression of LC3 reporters can itself perturb autophagy dynamics; careful controls are required.  
5. Time and resources: Chronic POS feeding takes weeks to elicit robust phenotypes; this may limit throughput and increase assay variability.  

Conclusion  
This lipid accumulation and autophagy flux assay directly addresses central RPE dysfunction mechanisms in dry AMD and provides a practical, functional platform for screening modulators of lysosomal and autophagic pathways. While it cannot recapitulate all facets of drusen biogenesis or the inflammatory milieu, it offers a strong first-pass system for identifying compounds that restore RPE homeostasis and clear toxic lipid/lipofuscin deposits—key objectives in early-stage dry AMD drug discovery.
