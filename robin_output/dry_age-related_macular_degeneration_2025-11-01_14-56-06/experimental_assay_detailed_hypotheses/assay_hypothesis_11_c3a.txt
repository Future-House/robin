Assay Overview: The assay quantifies soluble anaphylatoxins C3a and C5a released from retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cultures using ELISAs on conditioned media collected from apical and basal compartments of polarized transwell monolayers (primary human fetal RPE, mature iPSC-RPE, ARPE-19, and disease‑relevant EFEMP1R345W lines) with molecular‑weight fractionation (3–10 kDa) to enrich analytes and standardized sampling under serum‑free or complement‑controlled conditions; parallel readouts can include SC5b‑9, C3, IL‑6/IL‑1β, intracellular Ca2+, kinase activation, and inflammasome priming after lipofuscin/A2E photooxidation for orthogonal pathway confirmation (e.g., VEGF, IL‑1β) (fernandezgodino2015alocalcomplement pages 10-11, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 32-35, busch2017anaphylatoxinsactivateca2+ pages 1-2, brandstetter2015complementcomponentc5a pages 1-2).

Biomedical Evidence: Complement is genetically and histologically implicated in AMD, and anaphylatoxins are potent pro‑inflammatory mediators detected in drusen that signal via RPE‑relevant GPCRs (C3aR1/C5aR1) to activate ERK/PI3K/Akt and Ca2+ pathways, linking fragment generation to RPE and immune responses (rohrer2018anaphylatoxinsignalingin pages 1-4, behnke2022ifnβimmunomodulationand pages 31-33, behnke2022ifnβimmunomodulationandb pages 31-33). C3a directly triggers sub‑RPE deposit formation in human RPE and reduces proteasome activity, while C5a primes RPE for NLRP3‑dependent IL‑1β release after lipofuscin photooxidation, anchoring these analytes to early dry‑AMD biology (fernandezgodino2018c3atriggersformation pages 2-4, fernandezgodino2018c3atriggersformation pages 11-12, brandstetter2015complementcomponentc5a pages 1-2). Mature iPSC‑RPE produce and secrete active C3 forms including C3a, indicating the RPE can be a local source of anaphylatoxins and that maturation state dictates assay sensitivity (schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 1-6, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 32-35).

Previous Use: Polarized RPE transwell cultures have been used to measure C3a and C5a by ELISA after 3–10 kDa fractionation, with defined vendors and complementary measurements (C3, SC5b‑9, cytokines), and with pharmacologic perturbations (recombinant proteins, neutralizing antibodies, inhibitors) over multi‑week protocols (fernandezgodino2015alocalcomplement pages 10-11). Co‑culture systems detected secreted C3a increases, demonstrating feasibility in multicellular contexts (mohlin2018amodelto pages 1-2). In vivo ocular studies quantified C5a by ELISA in RPE/choroid homogenates to track complement engagement and modulation, supporting translational relevance of the analyte (coughlin2016connectingtheinnate pages 9-10). Anaphylatoxin‑driven RPE signaling has been profiled with multiplex secretomes and kinase/Ca2+ readouts, revealing non‑linear C3a/C5a interactions that motivate multiplexed endpoints alongside ELISA (busch2017anaphylatoxinsactivateca2+ pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—Biological centrality to AMD; quantitative, polarizable readout in human RPE; prior methodological precedent (fractionation, apical/basal sampling); and rich orthogonal endpoints (Ca2+, Akt, VEGF/IL‑1β, SC5b‑9) for mechanism and target‑engagement mapping (behnke2022ifnβimmunomodulationand pages 31-33, fernandezgodino2015alocalcomplement pages 10-11, busch2017anaphylatoxinsactivateca2+ pages 1-2). Weaknesses—Context dependence and non‑linearity of C3a/C5a signaling; receptor heterogeneity (including C5aR2); potential low endogenous fragment production in monocultures without complement sources; maturation dependence of RPE complement output; need to control serum/complement artifacts and distinguish active vs desArg forms; and C3‑independent C5a generation (e.g., thrombin), arguing to measure both analytes and include functional orthogonals (busch2017anaphylatoxinsactivateca2+ pages 1-2, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 29-32, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 32-35, coughlin2016connectingtheinnate pages 4-7, rohrer2018anaphylatoxinsignalingin pages 1-4, gurubaran2021oxidativestressand pages 2-4). With careful design—mature iPSC‑RPE, polarity control, complement‑defined media, time‑stamped collection, and multiplexed downstream readouts—C3a/C5a ELISAs are well‑justified as screening endpoints for dry‑AMD therapeutics (schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 32-35, fernandezgodino2015alocalcomplement pages 10-11).

Measuring C3a/C5a release from RPE cultures is mechanistically grounded and methodologically feasible for dry‑AMD drug screening when paired with polarity control, complement‑defined conditions, and orthogonal signaling readouts. Model choice and assay controls are critical given maturation dependence, signaling non‑linearity, and alternative C5a generation pathways. This assay can support target engagement and pathway modulation decisions but should be embedded in a multiplexed workflow for robust interpretation.
