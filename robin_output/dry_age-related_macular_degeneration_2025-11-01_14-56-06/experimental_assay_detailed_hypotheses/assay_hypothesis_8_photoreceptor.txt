Assay Overview: The proposed photoreceptor outer segment (POS) phagocytosis assay employs fluorescently labeled POS to quantify retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cell functions that are critical for maintaining retinal homeostasis. This assay models the essential process of POS recognition, internalization, and degradation via lysosomal processing, mediated by surface receptors such as αvβ5 integrin and MerTK. Measurements are derived by tracking fluorescence intensity using microscopy, flow cytometry, or FRET-based techniques, while RPE cells—either primary or iPSC-derived—serve as the biological substrate (lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 30-35, liang2023comprehensiveanalysisof pages 1-2).

Biomedical Evidence: Dysregulation of POS clearance is central to dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) pathogenesis. Inefficient phagocytosis leads to lipofuscin accumulation, oxidative stress, and subsequent RPE dysfunction, all of which are well documented in both biomedical and clinical literature. The assay directly targets these pathogenic steps, offering a physiologically relevant readout that aligns with observed clinical features of AMD (egbejimi2022decipheringunderlyingmechanisms pages 21-26, lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 4-6).

Previous Use: Similar assays have been utilized to dissect phagocytic pathways in RPE cells. They have elucidated key receptor interactions and lysosomal deficits in AMD models and have been adapted to high-throughput formats for drug screening, thereby providing critical insights for early-stage drug discovery (lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 9-11, zhang2023improvedlipofuscinmodels pages 14-16).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths of the assay include its direct measurement of receptor-mediated uptake and lysosomal degradation, its adaptability to high-throughput screening, and its strong mechanistic relevance to AMD pathology. Weaknesses include potential donor-to-donor variability and challenges in fully recapitulating the complex in vivo microenvironment of the retina. This assay is a valuable tool for evaluating therapeutics aimed at restoring RPE phagocytic function and slowing dry AMD progression.

This assay directly quantifies critical RPE functions disrupted in dry AMD, making it highly relevant for early drug discovery. Its adaptability and mechanistic insights support its utility in screening potential therapeutics.
