Assay Overview: The proposed assay employs LysoTracker dyes to monitor lysosomal acidification and fluorogenic substrates to quantify cathepsin proteolytic activity (e.g., cathepsin D) in cultured retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells such as ARPE-19, hTERT RPE-1, or primary RPE. It models a key aspect of dry AMD pathogenesis by capturing lysosomal dysfunction, which is implicated in the accumulation of undigested material and subsequent RPE degeneration (mesquita2021purificationandcharacterizationa pages 27-30, sharif2015expressionandregulationa pages 189-192). Measurements include changes in lysosomal pH using fluorescence intensity shifts and cathepsin activity via substrate cleavage, both of which can be quantified using standard fluorescence microscopy and plate-reader formats (boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 3-4, ng2024impairedcathepsind pages 6-8).

Biomedical Evidence: Lysosomal dysfunction is central to dry AMD pathogenesis, as impaired autophagic clearance leads to lipofuscin buildup and drusen formation in RPE cells (mesquita2021purificationandcharacterizationa pages 27-30, sharif2015expressionandregulationa pages 68-72). Alterations in lysosomal pH directly affect enzyme maturation and activity, which in turn compromises proteolytic processing of photoreceptor outer segments (sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 227-230, sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 68-72). Clinical and biochemical studies have linked these dysfunctions to RPE degeneration, making lysosome-targeted assays highly relevant for therapeutic evaluation (mesquita2021purificationandcharacterization pages 69-72, sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 189-192).

Previous Use: This assay has been successfully applied in RPE models, where both pharmacologically induced lysosomal inhibition and CRISPR-mediated gene disruptions (e.g., CTSD knockout) have demonstrated its utility in replicating AMD-like phenotypes (mesquita2021purificationandcharacterizationa pages 69-72, mesquita2021purificationandcharacterizationa pages 30-33). Its use in drug discovery has enabled the identification of lysosomal modulators capable of restoring enzyme activity and acidification (cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 164-167, mesquita2021purificationandcharacterization pages 27-30).

Overall Evaluation: The strengths of this assay lie in its specificity for monitoring lysosomal dysfunction—a key driver of RPE pathology in dry AMD—and its compatibility with high-throughput screening methods (sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 195-199, sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 213-216). However, reliance on in vitro models may not capture the full complexity of the in vivo milieu, and cell line variability remains a concern (sharif2015expressionandregulationa pages 227-230, sharif2015expressionandregulationa pages 77-81).

This lysosomal pH and cathepsin activity assay is well supported by biomedical evidence and has a proven track record in modeling AMD-relevant dysfunctions. It represents a promising platform for the early-stage evaluation of therapeutic candidates targeting lysosomal pathways in dry AMD.
