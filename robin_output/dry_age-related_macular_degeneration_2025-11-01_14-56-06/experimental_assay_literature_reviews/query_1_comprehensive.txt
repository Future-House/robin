Query: Comprehensive reviews of molecular pathways driving RPE oxidative stress and lipofuscin accumulation in dry AMD, including roles of mitochondrial dysfunction, NADPH oxidase, A2E phototoxicity, and antioxidant defense assays in RPE cell cultures

Oxidative stress in the RPE arises from a pro-oxidative milieu (high O2, light, PUFA-rich POS) and multiple endogenous ROS sources—mitochondria, lysosomes/phagosomes, peroxisomes, melanosomes, and NADPH oxidases—compounded by the respiratory burst during daily POS phagocytosis (markitantova2025retinalpigmentepithelium pages 2-4, basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 5-7). Lipofuscin (LF) accumulates in RPE lysosomes with age; under visible light LF acts as a photosensitizer that generates superoxide, damaging melanosomes and weakening their antioxidant/screening capacity (markitantova2025retinalpigmentepithelium pages 2-4, abokyi2020centralroleof pages 2-3).

Mitochondrial dysfunction is a dominant driver and target of injury: RPE from AMD donors show elevated ROS and impaired mitochondrial function, and mtDNA damage establishes a ROS–mitochondria positive feedback loop that amplifies oxidative burden and inflammatory signaling (abokyi2020centralroleof pages 2-3, terao2022oxidativestressinducedcellular pages 2-4). Human and experimental data demonstrate reduced glutathione/ATP and compensatory PGC-1α upregulation in AMD RPE, consistent with disrupted bioenergetics and redox buffering (kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 7-8).

Proteostasis failure reinforces this cycle: excessive ROS induces protein unfolding/aggregation, ER stress, lysosomal damage, and inflammation; LF formation itself inhibits the proteasome and depresses autophagy, while autophagy acts as a key adaptive defense that declines with aging (markitantova2025retinalpigmentepithelium pages 2-4). NADPH oxidases contribute to the oxidative load in retinal cells, and pharmacologic NOX inhibition suppresses pathological ROS signaling in vivo, supporting NOX as a modifiable ROS source relevant to RPE stress (markitantova2025retinalpigmentepithelium pages 2-4, terao2022oxidativestressinducedcellular pages 2-4).

A2E, a lipofuscin bisretinoid fluorophore, accumulates in RPE and is phototoxic; its biogenesis depends on the visual cycle (RPE65), motivating strategies that modulate the visual cycle or restore RPE65 to limit A2E burden and downstream oxidative injury (kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 8-10, si2023theroleof pages 9-10). Experimental assays used to quantify oxidative injury/defense in RPE models include ROS measurements, lipid peroxidation (MDA), protein carbonyls, and DNA oxidation (8‑OHdG); in vitro stressors (H2O2, light, cigarette-smoke extract) elevate these readouts and perturb mitochondria (abokyi2020centralroleof pages 2-3, terao2022oxidativestressinducedcellular pages 2-4). Functional assays in cultured RPE commonly include mitochondrial/glycolytic flux, glutathione and ATP levels, antioxidant enzyme protein/activity (SOD, catalase, GPx, GR), and ELISA for lipid peroxidation adducts such as CEP (kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 7-8, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 8-10).

RPE oxidative stress in dry AMD is driven by mitochondrial dysfunction, NOX-derived and phagocytic ROS, and phototoxic lipofuscin/A2E, with proteostasis–autophagy failure accelerating damage (markitantova2025retinalpigmentepithelium pages 2-4, terao2022oxidativestressinducedcellular pages 2-4, si2023theroleof pages 9-10). In vitro RPE assays quantify ROS and oxidation biomarkers, mitochondrial function, cellular antioxidants, and antioxidant enzyme activities under defined oxidative challenges (abokyi2020centralroleof pages 2-3, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 7-8, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 8-10). Targeting mitochondrial quality control, NOX activity, visual-cycle–driven A2E, and NRF2/autophagy pathways represents a coherent therapeutic strategy space (terao2022oxidativestressinducedcellular pages 2-4, si2023theroleof pages 9-10, markitantova2025retinalpigmentepithelium pages 2-4).
