Query: Mechanisms of drusen formation on Bruch’s membrane, including extracellular lipid and protein aggregation, complement deposition, development of high-content imaging and fluorescent reporter assays for drusen-like material quantification in vitro in RPE and choroidal fibroblast co-cultures

Drusen formation on Bruch’s membrane arises from a multifactorial interplay of extracellular lipid and protein aggregation coupled with compromised clearance mechanisms by the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE). The RPE contributes by secreting lipoprotein particles—including those containing apoB—via cytoplasmic budding, leading to deposits rich in esterified and unesterified cholesterol, phospholipids, and ceramides that constitute at least 40% of hard drusen and an even greater percentage in soft drusen (curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 10-11, pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 1-3). Concurrently, aggregated extracellular proteins such as vitronectin, apolipoprotein E, and complement factor H accumulate, with oxidative modifications triggering inflammatory responses and complement deposition (curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 13-14, curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 11-13). Age-related changes in Bruch’s membrane, notably the buildup of esterified cholesterol, further hinder lipid clearance and promote the retention of these aggregates (curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 3-4, curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 9-10). Recent advances in high-content imaging and fluorescent reporter assays—employing markers like filipin—have been developed to quantitatively assess drusen-like material formation in vitro using co-cultures of RPE and choroidal fibroblasts, thereby elucidating the precise intercellular interactions and matrix alterations underlying drusen biogenesis (curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 11-13, curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 3-4, kliffen1996agerelatedmaculopathya pages 26-31).

Drusen formation involves complex interactions between lipid/protein aggregation, complement activation, and age-related alterations of Bruch’s membrane. High-content imaging in RPE and choroidal fibroblast co-cultures offers a robust platform for quantitatively analyzing these underlying pathogenic processes.
