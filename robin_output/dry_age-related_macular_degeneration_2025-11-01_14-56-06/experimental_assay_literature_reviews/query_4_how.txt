Query: How does autophagy and lysosomal degradation become compromised in RPE cells during dry AMD progression, which autophagy-related genes and pathways (e.g., LC3, p62) are dysregulated, and what robust cell culture assays can monitor rescue of autophagic flux by candidate drugs?

Compromise of the RPE autophagy–lysosome system in dry AMD is driven by chronic oxidative stress and the buildup of undegraded cargo (lipofuscin, drusen, Aβ) that depress lysosomal enzyme activity, elevate lysosomal pH, and cause lysosomal swelling, collectively blocking autophagosome–lysosome fusion/clearance and mitophagy. Human AMD RPE exhibit increased autophagosomes, swollen LAMP-1–positive organelles, lipid/glycogen accumulation, mitochondrial disintegration, higher ROS under stress, and impaired autophagic flux evidenced by failure to increase LC3-II/LC3-I and failure to downregulate p62 during starvation, indicating a block at late degradative steps. A feed-forward loop exists: inhibiting autophagy increases lipofuscin, and lipofuscin further inhibits autophagy; Aβ similarly overloads and swells lysosomes, reducing cargo-carrying capacity for POS turnover (si2023theroleof pages 5-7, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3, wang2023potentialregulatoryroles pages 7-9, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 5-9, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 10-11).

Dysregulated genes and pathways include reduced LC3 processing/levels, increased p62/SQSTM1, enlarged LAMP-1 puncta, and downregulation of ATG7 and ATG9; ATG5 can be enriched in drusen but does not equate to increased flux. Genetic RPE deletion of RB1CC1/FIP200 or Atg5/Atg7 causes p62 accumulation, LC3 reduction, RPE atrophy, lipofuscin/oxidized cargo buildup, and retinal degeneration, implicating defective induction and autophagosome biogenesis. Upstream regulation involves AMPK–mTOR–ULK1 and Beclin1–VPS34 complexes, with p62 impacting mTOR activity; lysosomal acidification via V-ATPase (regulated by CRYBA1) is critical, and selective mitophagy components (PINK1/PRKN/OPTN; BNIP3/NIX; FUNDC1) are engaged but functionally insufficient when lysosomes are compromised. Diurnal variation of ATG7, ATG9, LC3, and BECN1 warrants experimental timing control (wang2023potentialregulatoryroles pages 37-38, abokyi2021antioxidativeroleofa pages 23-27, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 5-9, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 10-11, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 3-5, wang2023potentialregulatoryroles pages 7-9, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 1-2).

Robust cell-culture assays to monitor rescue of autophagic flux include:
- LC3 immunoblotting to quantify LC3-II/LC3-I with and without lysosomal protease inhibitors (E64d/pepstatin A) to derive flux, plus starvation ± trophic cues (e.g., IGF-1) (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 5-9).
- p62/SQSTM1 turnover during starvation as a degradative readout; failure to reduce p62 indicates blocked flux (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 5-9).
- Tandem RFP–GFP–LC3B reporter imaging to quantify autophagosomes (GFP+RFP+) versus autolysosomes (GFP−RFP+), validated by LysoTracker; chloroquine as a fusion-block control; dose–response and toxicity by LDH; mitochondrial OCR/mito stress test for functional rescue (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8).
- LAMP-1 immunostaining to quantify lysosomal size/morphology (swollen/annular vesicles) and EM/fluorescence scoring of autophagosomes and mitochondria (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 10-11).
- Functional endpoints linked to autophagy outcomes: cellular ROS under oxidative stress, ATP partitioning or OCR to gauge mitophagy restoration; lipofuscin quantification where relevant (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 5-9, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2).
- Pharmacologic controls: autophagy inhibitors (wortmannin, bafilomycin A1) and inducers (metformin, trehalose) to benchmark assay dynamic range; trehalose increases LC3 turnover and p62 degradation and restores RPE mitochondrial respiration in vitro (abokyi2021antioxidativeroleofa pages 23-27, cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 1-2, cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8).

Dry AMD RPE show a lysosome-centered block in autophagic flux driven by oxidative damage and waste accrual, with swollen LAMP-1+ lysosomes, impaired LC3 processing, and p62 accumulation. Core autophagy genes (ATG7/ATG9, RB1CC1) and mTOR–AMPK–ULK1–Beclin1 pathways are dysregulated, and gene deletions recapitulate AMD-like pathology. Flux rescue can be rigorously monitored by LC3-II/I immunoblotting with lysosomal inhibitors, p62 turnover, tandem RFP–GFP–LC3B imaging with LysoTracker and CQ controls, LAMP-1 morphology, and ROS/mitochondrial function assays, using metformin/trehalose and wortmannin/bafilomycin as pharmacologic benchmarks (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 5-9, wang2023potentialregulatoryroles pages 37-38, abokyi2021antioxidativeroleofa pages 23-27, cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8, cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 1-2, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 10-11, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3, si2023theroleof pages 5-7, wang2023potentialregulatoryroles pages 7-9).
