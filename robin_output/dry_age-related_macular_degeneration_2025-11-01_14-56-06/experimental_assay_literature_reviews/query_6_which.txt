Query: Which pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and inflammasome components are elevated in dry AMD patients and in stressed RPE cultures, how do they mediate paracrine signaling that propagates retinal damage, and what multiplexed assays can simultaneously quantify inflammatory mediators in cell culture supernatants?

Elevated mediators in patients and models:
- Cytokines: IL-18 (increased in dry AMD serum), IL-1β (increased in vitreous; higher plasma in CFH Y402H CC subgroup), TNF-α and IL-6 (systemically elevated), IFN-γ (reported in AMD contexts). In stressed RPE, A2E and other AMD-relevant stimuli increase IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and can also raise IL-2 and VEGF-A; AGE stimulation increases IFN-γ and VEGF and prominently GM-CSF (plus interferon‑inducible chemokines). Chemokines: IL-8 and MCP‑1/CCL2 are robustly induced in RPE; additional chemokines reported in AMD contexts include IP‑10/CXCL10, eotaxin, MIP‑1α and MIP‑3α, and CXCL11/CXCL12 in AGE‑RPE models (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 79-82, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 84-86, kauppinen2016inflammationandits pages 11-12, gao2017nlrp3inflammasomeactivity pages 187-190, anderson2013a2einducesil1ss pages 1-2, gao2017nlrp3inflammasomeactivity pages 90-95, lin2013parainflammationassociatedwith pages 11-13, lin2013parainflammationassociatedwith pages 8-10).  
- Inflammasome components: NLRP3, ASC, active caspase‑1, caspase‑4 (noncanonical), AIM2, cGAS; full‑length GSDMD is elevated in GA and GSDME cleavage is observed under atRAL; IL‑18 is constitutive in RPE, whereas pro‑IL‑1β induction requires priming (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 79-82, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 76-79, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 86-87).

Paracrine propagation:
- IL‑1β from RPE drives VEGF‑A/C, induces IL‑6/IL‑8, and amplifies MCP‑1 autocrine/paracrine loops that recruit microglia/macrophages; inflammasome‑activated RPE media trigger microglial migration. Aβ‑induced IL‑1β initiates an autocrine program in RPE that elevates TNF‑α and IL‑6, sustaining local inflammation. Complement C5a and IL‑1α prime neighboring cells; exosomes and CD40L further amplify inflammasome signaling. IL‑18 contributes to RPE death in GA, while IL‑1β has proangiogenic effects (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 79-82, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 84-86, gao2017nlrp3inflammasomeactivity pages 90-95, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 86-87, gao2017nlrp3inflammasomeactivity pages 30-35, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 76-79).

Multiplexed quantification in supernatants:
- Suspension array–based multiplex immunoassays (e.g., Bio‑Plex cytokine assays) and 30‑plex cytokine/growth factor panels have been used; studies also report “multiplex assays” alongside ELISA for secreted mediators (lin2013parainflammationassociatedwith pages 11-13, lin2013parainflammationassociatedwith pages 8-10, anderson2013a2einducesil1ss pages 1-2, gao2017nlrp3inflammasomeactivity pages 187-190).

Dry AMD and stressed RPE show elevated IL-18, IL‑1β, IL‑6, TNF‑α, IFN‑γ, VEGF, and chemokines such as IL‑8, MCP‑1, IP‑10, eotaxin, alongside NLRP3–ASC–caspase‑1/4, AIM2, and cGAS pathway activation (with gasdermin changes). These mediators drive paracrine amplification by inducing VEGF, IL‑6/IL‑8, MCP‑1 loops, complement priming, microglial recruitment, and RPE death. Multiplex suspension array assays (Bio‑Plex and related panels) enable simultaneous quantification of these factors in culture supernatants.
