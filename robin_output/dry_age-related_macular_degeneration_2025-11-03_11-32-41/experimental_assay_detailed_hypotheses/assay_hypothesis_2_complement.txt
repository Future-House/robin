Assay Overview: This assay uses a co‐culture of ARPE-19 cells and THP-1-derived macrophages, exposing them to CEP- or MDA/4-HNE-modified proteins to mimic drusen‐mediated complement activation. Complement activation is quantified by measuring C3b/C5b-9 deposition on RPE via high-content immunofluorescence, while downstream inflammatory and pro‐angiogenic responses are assessed by ELISA for C3a/C5a, VEGF, and TGF-β2 (chirco2017mechanismsofpathophysiology pages 54-60, jiao2018theroleof pages 35-40).

Biomedical Evidence: Complement dysregulation and deposition of MAC in the choriocapillaris are central to dry AMD pathogenesis, with oxidation-specific epitopes in drusen acting as potent activators of the complement system (chirco2017mechanismsofpathophysiology pages 54-60, jiao2018theroleof pages 40-46). Genetic risk factors such as CFH variants exacerbate local complement activation, contributing to RPE damage and macrophage recruitment. These processes result in the generation of inflammatory and angiogenic mediators that drive disease progression, affirming the assay’s relevance (chirco2017mechanismsofpathophysiology pages 41-46, chirco2017mechanismsofpathophysiology pages 102-107).

Previous Use: In vitro models employing ARPE-19 cells, including co-culture systems with immune cells, have been used to elucidate the role of complement activation in AMD and to evaluate complement-targeted therapeutics (rastoin2020experimentalmodelsin pages 5-7, mohlin2018amodelto pages 2-3). These systems have yielded insights into complement-mediated cell damage and cytokine induction, supporting their application in early drug screening (lloriansalvador2022complementactivationcontributes pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: This assay’s strengths lie in its integration of multiple relevant endpoints—MAC deposition and soluble mediator release—capturing key aspects of complement-driven pathology in dry AMD. Limitations include the use of the ARPE-19 cell line, which may not fully recapitulate primary RPE physiology, and the acute nature of in vitro responses compared to the chronic progression of AMD. Further validation with primary cells and chronic exposure conditions may enhance its translational utility (mohlin2018amodelto pages 7-9, chirco2017mechanismsofpathophysiology pages 41-46).

This assay provides a robust platform to model innate-immune crosstalk via complement activation in a simplified, yet physiologically relevant, in vitro system for dry AMD. It offers measurable endpoints to assess therapeutic interventions targeting complement components.
