Query: Comprehensive mechanistic overview of molecular and cellular pathways driving dry age-related macular degeneration, focusing on RPE cell degeneration, drusen formation, complement system dysregulation, oxidative stress, lipid metabolism disturbances, mitochondrial impairment, autophagy and inflammasome activation, and correlate clinical biomarkers with preclinical targets for drug development

Dry AMD is initiated by age- and environment-driven stress in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), where incessant phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments, high oxygen/light exposure, and declining antioxidant capacity elevate reactive oxygen species (ROS) and overwhelm degradative systems, producing lysosomal lipofuscin, extracellular drusen, and progressive RPE/photoreceptor loss (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 72-76, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 1-2). Lipofuscin is polymeric and undegradable; its redox-active cargo permeabilizes lysosomes, inhibits proton pumps/hydrolases, spills toxic species to cytoplasm/organelles, and drives exocytosis of autophagic/exosomal material that seeds drusen, directly linking lysosomal/autophagy failure to deposit composition; lysophagy is therefore a tractable node (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 2-4, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 4-5, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 15-17). Proteostasis failure integrates UPS and autophagy defects with accumulation of p62/ubiquitin-positive aggregates also found in drusen, amplifying oxidative vulnerability (piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 59-62).

Mitochondrial impairment is central: AMD RPE exhibit mtDNA damage, altered bioenergetics, reduced ATP/cristae, dysregulated fission–fusion/mitophagy (PINK1, PGAM5/Drp1), and persistent ROS, all exacerbated by reduced NRF2/SOD signaling and photo-oxidative/A2E stress (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 168-171, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3, shu2023roleofoxidative pages 13-14, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12). Oxidized lipids and advanced glycation end-products accumulate; DHA-derived CEP is a robust oxidative biomarker tied to AMD, and systemic lipid imbalances correlate with risk and drusen biology (shu2023roleofoxidative pages 13-14, carozza2024anoverviewof pages 10-11, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 59-62). Chronic innate immune activation intersects these nodes: oxidative stress primes NLRP3 (canonical and cGAS-driven noncanonical) with context-dependent IL‑1β/IL‑18 effects, and pyroptotic signaling is detectable/targetable in RPE (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 168-171, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 21-22). Complement dysregulation—anchored by CFH risk alleles—drives systemic/ocular activation; C5b‑9 accumulates in RPE lysosomes and complement modulation restores lysosomal function, with complement inhibitors now slowing GA growth clinically (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 168-171, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 72-76, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 4-5, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 21-22). Multiple programmed cell deaths (apoptosis, necroptosis, ferroptosis) operate; iron-driven lipid peroxidation and HO‑1–linked ferroptosis are actionable (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 4-5, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 21-22, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12). Senescent RPE/microenvironmental SASP further reinforce inflammation, complement activity, lipid/protein deposition, and barrier failure (lee2021cellularsenescencein pages 1-2).

Clinical biomarkers map to these targets: fundus autofluorescence for lipofuscin; drusen size/composition (p62/ubiquitin, oxidized lipids); pigmentary changes; circulating/vitreous cytokines (e.g., IL‑1β), systemic complement activation, CEP and serum oxidative markers (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 2-4, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 59-62, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 168-171, carozza2024anoverviewof pages 10-11, shu2023roleofoxidative pages 13-14, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 4-5). Therapeutic strategies include NRF2 activation/NOX inhibition; lysosomal repair/lysophagy and autophagy enhancement (AMPK–mTOR, CIB2); mitochondrial protectants (NMN, elamipretide); inflammasome/NLRP3 modulators; ferroptosis inhibitors; complement regulators; and lipofuscin-removal approaches, each with translatable pharmacodynamic readouts in the listed biomarkers (shu2023roleofoxidative pages 13-14, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 1-2, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 21-22, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 4-5, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 15-17).

Dry AMD arises from convergent oxidative, proteostatic, mitochondrial, lipid, complement, and inflammasome dysfunction centered in RPE that produce lipofuscin, drusen, and cell death (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 2-4, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 168-171). Imaging and fluid biomarkers (autofluorescence, drusen metrics, cytokines, complement, CEP) align with preclinical targets spanning NRF2/antioxidant, autophagy–lysosome, mitochondrial, inflammasome/ferroptosis, and complement pathways, enabling mechanism-based drug development (carozza2024anoverviewof pages 10-11, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 4-5). Complement inhibition already validates this translational axis in GA, with additional nodes advancing toward clinical testing (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 21-22).
