Query: comparative analyses of screening libraries using high-content imaging or Seahorse mitochondrial stress assays in primary or iPSC-derived RPE cells from dry AMD patients, focusing on identification of novel small-molecule modulators of mitochondrial redox balance

Below are 15 distinct mechanistic hypotheses and associated cell-culture–based screening strategies in primary or iPSC-derived RPE cells from dry AMD donors, each aimed at discovering small molecules that rebalance mitochondrial redox homeostasis. Each entry specifies the target pathway, the hypothesis, the proposed small-molecule screen, and the key readouts (high-content imaging and Seahorse).

1. Nrf2–ARE Activation  
   • Hypothesis: Pharmacological Nrf2 activation up-regulates mitochondrial antioxidant enzymes (e.g. SOD2, PRDX3) and reduces mtROS.  
   • Screen: Library of known electrophiles and novel Nrf2 activators.  
   • Readouts: High-content imaging of mitochondrial ROS (MitoSOX), SOD2 immunofluorescence; Seahorse measurement of basal and maximal OCR.

2. SIRT3 Deacetylase Enhancement  
   • Hypothesis: Small-molecule SIRT3 agonists deacetylate and activate complex I subunits, restoring electron transport and lowering ROS.  
   • Screen: Focused library of sirtuin modulators.  
   • Readouts: Imaging of acetyl-lysine on mitochondrial proteins; Seahorse assays for spare respiratory capacity and proton leak.

3. NOX4 Inhibition  
   • Hypothesis: Targeting mitochondrial‐associated NADPH oxidase 4 diminishes ROS spillover into mitochondria and preserves membrane potential.  
   • Screen: Known NOX inhibitors plus chemical diversity set.  
   • Readouts: High-content imaging of mitochondrial membrane potential (TMRM) and mtROS; Seahorse for non-mitochondrial OCR fraction.

4. PGC-1α Agonism  
   • Hypothesis: Small molecules that induce PGC-1α drive mitochondrial biogenesis, increase antioxidant capacity, and improve redox buffering.  
   • Screen: Natural products and synthetic PGC-1α coactivator modulators.  
   • Readouts: mtDNA copy number by high-content FISH; Seahorse for maximal respiration and ATP production rate.

5. Mfn2–Mediated Fusion Promotion  
   • Hypothesis: Activators of mitofusin-2 foster mitochondrial network fusion, reducing fragmentation-associated ROS.  
   • Screen: Phenotypic library optimized for mitochondrial morphology modulators.  
   • Readouts: Automated image analysis of mitochondrial network morphology; Seahorse for coupling efficiency.

6. UCP2/3 Mild Uncoupling  
   • Hypothesis: Low-level activation of uncoupling proteins dissipates proton gradient enough to limit superoxide formation without collapsing ATP synthesis.  
   • Screen: Lipophilic weak acids and novel small-molecule UCP2/3 agonists.  
   • Readouts: Proton leak measurement by Seahorse; MitoSOX imaging for ROS.

7. NAD+ Salvage Pathway Activation  
   • Hypothesis: Boosting NAMPT or NMNAT elevates NAD+ pools, enhances sirtuin activity, and decreases oxidative damage.  
   • Screen: Libraries of NAD+ precursors, small-molecule NAMPT activators.  
   • Readouts: Immunofluorescence for NAD+ (via biosensor); Seahorse for OCR/ECAR ratio.

8. PARP1 Inhibition  
   • Hypothesis: Blocking PARP1 prevents NAD+ depletion under oxidative stress, preserving mitochondrial respiration.  
   • Screen: FDA-approved PARP inhibitors and analogs.  
   • Readouts: NAD+ immunostaining; Seahorse for basal respiration under H₂O₂ challenge.

9. DJ-1 Stabilization  
   • Hypothesis: Small molecules that stabilize DJ-1 enhance mitochondrial antioxidant responses and complex I assembly.  
   • Screen: Library targeting cysteine-reactive pockets on DJ-1.  
   • Readouts: DJ-1 mitochondrial translocation (imaging); complex I activity assays; Seahorse for OCR.

10. Drp1-Mediated Fission Inhibition  
    • Hypothesis: Inhibiting DRP1-dependent fission prevents excessive fragmentation, reducing ROS hotspots.  
    • Screen: Chemical inhibitors of Drp1-GTPase.  
    • Readouts: Mitochondrial length/fragmentation by high-content morphology; Seahorse for respiratory control ratio.

11. Mitochondrially Targeted Catalase Induction  
    • Hypothesis: Small molecules that up-regulate a mito-targeted catalase transgene or endogenous peroxidases decrease H₂O₂ load.  
    • Screen: Epigenetic modulators (e.g., HDACi) and transcription-factor activators.  
    • Readouts: Catalase immunofluorescence colocalized with mitochondrial marker; Seahorse under oxidative challenge.

12. CoQ10 Biosynthesis Enhancement  
    • Hypothesis: Agonists of COQ2 or other CoQ biosynthetic enzymes increase CoQ pools, facilitating electron flux and reducing ROS.  
    • Screen: Substrate analogs and enzyme-activating small molecules.  
    • Readouts: CoQ10 levels by fluorescent probe; Seahorse for complex II–linked respiration.

13. HIF-1α Stabilization (Normoxic)  
    • Hypothesis: Mild HIF-1α activation (via PHD inhibition) shifts metabolism toward glycolysis, reducing mitochondrial ROS burden.  
    • Screen: Prolyl hydroxylase inhibitors (PHDi) library.  
    • Readouts: HIF target gene fluorescence reporters; Seahorse for OCR/ECAR shift.

14. ATF4-Mediated Integrated Stress Response (ISR) Modulation  
    • Hypothesis: Pharmacological ISR activators up-regulate mitochondrial quality control genes (e.g. CHOP, GADD34) to clear damaged mitochondria.  
    • Screen: eIF2α-kinase activators and small molecules known to trigger mild ISR.  
    • Readouts: ATF4 nuclear translocation imaging; Seahorse for improvements in ATP‐linked respiration.

15. Parkin-Dependent Mitophagy Enhancement  
    • Hypothesis: Small molecules that promote Parkin recruitment to depolarized mitochondria accelerate removal of ROS-generating organelles.  
    • Screen: Ubiquitin ligase modulators and mitochondrial depolarization sensitizers.  
    • Readouts: Co-localization of Parkin and Tom20 by high-content imaging; Seahorse for recovery of spare respiratory capacity after mitophagy induction.

Each of these strategies can be executed in parallel 384- or 1,536-well formats, pairing automated high-content microscopy for redox and morphological endpoints with Seahorse XF analyses for functional bioenergetics profiling, to pinpoint lead compounds that restore mitochondrial redox balance in patient-derived RPE models of dry AMD.
