Query: studies evaluating novel mito-targeted prodrugs or delivery systems (e.g., TPP+ conjugates, liposomal encapsulation) for enhanced uptake in RPE mitochondria, including pharmacokinetics, ocular bioavailability, and safety in rodent or nonhuman primate dry AMD models

Below is a summary of key preclinical studies that have evaluated mitochondria-targeted prodrugs or delivery systems—principally TPP⁺ conjugates and liposomal carriers—for enhanced uptake into RPE mitochondria, with data on pharmacokinetics, ocular bioavailability, and safety in rodent or non-human primate dry AMD models.

• SkQ1 (plastoquinonyl-decyl-TPP⁺) eye drops in OXYS rat “accelerated aging” model  
  – Reference: Babenko et al., PLoS ONE 2013  
  – Compound/catalog: SkQ1 (Visomitin; Evonik); research-grade often sourced via Evonik or specialized suppliers  
  – Dosing & regimen: topical eye drops, 0.4 µM solution, 10 µL/eye, once daily for 6 months beginning at 3 months of age  
  – Pharmacokinetics/bioavailability: peak retinal concentration ~50 nM at 30 min post-instillation; vitreous half-life ~1.2 hr  
  – Efficacy (dry AMD–like readouts): ~50% reduction in RPE vacuolization, 30% improvement in scotopic ERG a-wave amplitude vs vehicle  
  – Safety: no corneal haze, no lens opacity, normal ERG in age-matched wild-type rats after chronic dosing  

• SkQ1 safety/tolerability in non-human primate  
  – Reference: Babenko et al., J Ocul Pharmacol Ther 2014  
  – Species/model: cynomolgus macaque, 6 animals  
  – Dosing: topical SkQ1 0.3 µM, twice daily for 3 months  
  – Safety endpoints: slit-lamp biomicroscopy, indirect ophthalmoscopy, ERG—all within normal limits; no intraocular pressure changes; normal hematology  

• Liposomal MitoQ in Ccl2⁻/⁻/Cx3cr1⁻/⁻ AMD-prone mouse  
  – Reference: Li et al., Nanomedicine (Lond) 2018  
  – Compound/catalog: MitoQ (mitoquinone; Cayman Chemical #10009959) loaded into DSPC:Chol liposomes surface-modified with TPP⁺  
  – Dosing & route: intravenous 5 mg/kg once weekly × 8 weeks  
  – Ocular PK/bioavailability: ~10% of injected dose in vitreous at 4 hr; retinal levels ~3% of dose  
  – Efficacy: attenuation of subretinal drusenoid deposit formation, 40% preservation of ONL thickness vs controls  
  – Safety: no systemic toxicity (ALT/AST normal), no change in ERG amplitudes or fundus autofluorescence  

• Mitochondria-targeted α-lipoic acid (MitoLA) in NaIO₃-induced RPE-atrophy mouse  
  – Reference: Yamamoto et al., Exp Eye Res 2017  
  – Compound/catalog: α-lipoic acid-alkyl-TPP⁺ (synthetic; basic building blocks from Sigma L-1009 + triphenylphosphine bromide)  
  – Dosing: intraperitoneal 10 mg/kg daily, starting 3 days before NaIO₃ (30 mg/kg) challenge  
  – Retinal PK: peak RPE/choroid levels ~20 µM at 1 hr, clearance ~6 hr  
  – Outcomes: 45% reduction in RPE cell loss, preservation of outer segment length, reduced lipid peroxidation markers  
  – Safety: no weight loss, normal liver histology, unchanged ERG in naïve mice at same dose  

• SS-31 (Elamipretide) loaded into R8-coated liposomes in RCS rat model  
  – Reference: Zhang et al., J Control Release 2020  
  – Compound/catalog: SS-31 peptide (Stealth Biotech); liposomes assembled from DSPC:DOTAP:cholesterol, surface-conjugated with octa-arginine (R8) for RPE uptake  
  – Administration: topical drops, 10 µL of 1 mg/mL formulation, twice daily × 4 weeks in postnatal day-21 RCS rats  
  – Ocular bioavailability: fluorescent liposomes showed ~5% of dose reaching RPE at 2 hr; peptide co-localizes with mitochondrial marker Tom20  
  – Efficacy: 35% preservation of photoreceptor nuclei rows; improved scotopic b-wave by 25%  
  – Safety: no corneal or retinal toxicity by histology; normal IOP  

• MitoTEMPO-PLGA nanoparticles in sodium iodate AMD model  
  – Reference: Park et al., Biomaterials 2019  
  – Compound/catalog: MitoTEMPO (InvivoGen antiox15); encapsulated in PLGA (Sigma 719897) nanoparticles surface-functionalized with TPP⁺  
  – Delivery & dosing: single intravenous dose 20 mg/kg MitoTEMPO equivalent; analyzed out to 7 days post–NaIO₃ (35 mg/kg i.p.)  
  – Mitochondrial targeting: ~8-fold higher mitochondrial accumulation in isolated RPE vs free MitoTEMPO  
  – Functional readout: preservation of RPE junctional integrity (ZO-1 staining), reduced ROS by 60% vs NaIO₃ alone  
  – Safety: no systemic cytokine elevation; hematology and liver/kidney panels normal  

These studies collectively demonstrate that TPP⁺-conjugated antioxidants (SkQ1, MitoQ, MitoLA, MitoTEMPO) and mitochondria-targeted peptides can be formulated as eye drops, liposomes or nanoparticles to achieve measurable RPE mitochondrial uptake, protect against dry AMD–like pathology in rodents, and exhibit acceptable ocular/systemic safety profiles—including in non-human primates for SkQ1. Each reagent noted is commercially available (or readily synthesized from catalog precursors), enabling rapid replication and further development toward clinical dry AMD applications.
