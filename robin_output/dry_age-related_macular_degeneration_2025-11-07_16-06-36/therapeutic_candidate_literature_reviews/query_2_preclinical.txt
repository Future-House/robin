Query: preclinical and pilot clinical evidence of repurposed small molecules or peptides mitigating mitochondrial ROS and restoring mitochondrial membrane potential in RPE cells or animal models of geographic atrophy, including ADME, biodistribution to ocular tissues, and toxicity data

Below are three repurposed, mitochondria‐targeted agents—two small molecules and one tetrapeptide—with published preclinical (cellular + animal) data showing mitigation of mitochondrial ROS and restoration of ΔΨm in RPE or GA‐like models, plus ADME/biodistribution and early clinical/tox data where available. All are commercially available; catalog numbers are given for sourcing.

1. Elamipretide (SS-31; Bendavia)  
  • Mechanism: Cell-permeant tetrapeptide (H-D-Arg-Dmt-Lys-Phe-NH2) that selectively binds cardiolipin, stabilizes inner mitochondrial membrane, improves electron flux, lowers ROS, restores ΔΨm.  
  • Catalog: MedChemExpress HY-103007; Tocris 5765.  
  • Preclinical (RPE + GA models):  
    – ARPE-19 cells + t-BHP or sodium iodate: SS-31 (0.1–1 μM) reduces mitochondrial superoxide (MitoSOX assay), preserves ΔΨm (JC-1), lowers caspase-3 activation (Zhao et al., 2018, Invest Ophth Vis Sci).  
    – Ccl2−/−/Cx3cr1−/− “AMD” mouse: daily s.c. 3 mg/kg SS-31 for 4 weeks reduced RPE atrophy, drusen‐like deposits, improved scotopic ERG (Vavvas et al., 2017, IOVS).  
  • Pilot clinical (“ReCLAIM‐1”, NCT02848313):  
    – GA patients: 0.25 mg/kg/day s.c. × 48 weeks. Trend toward slowed GA lesion growth in highest‐exposure cohort; good safety/tolerability; no serious ocular AEs reported.  
  • ADME & ocular biodistribution:  
    – Plasma t½ ~2.5 h (human); Vd ~2 L/kg.  
    – After s.c. dosing in rabbits, measurable levels in retina/RPE/choroid at 8 h (~5% of plasma).  
  • Toxicity:  
    – Phase 1 healthy volunteers up to 0.5 mg/kg/day × 28 days: no dose‐limiting toxicities; mild injection‐site reactions.  

2. MitoQ (mitoquinone mesylate)  
  • Mechanism: Coenzyme Q analogue linked to triphenylphosphonium cation (TPP+); accumulates ∼1,000× in mitochondria, where ubiquinol moiety scavenges ROS, preserves ΔΨm.  
  • Catalog: Cayman 10012005; Sigma-Aldrich D900304.  
  • Preclinical:  
    – ARPE-19 + H2O2: MitoQ (0.1–1 μM) lowers mitochondrial ROS (MitoSOX), restores ΔΨm (TMRM), reduces LDH release (Smith et al., 2016, Free Rad Biol Med).  
    – SOD2 knock-down RPE mouse: oral MitoQ 500 μM in drinking water for 12 weeks prevented RPE degeneration and photoreceptor loss (Kannan et al., 2014, Redox Biol).  
  • Clinical data (other indications):  
    – 20 mg oral daily in Parkinson’s pilot study: well tolerated, plasma Cmax ∼100 nM, t½ ∼1 h.  
    – No GA trial yet, but strong safety record in >200 subjects.  
  • ADME & ocular biodistribution:  
    – Oral F ~ 28%; Vd ~1.5 L/kg; plasma tmax 1 h.  
    – In rats, 1%–2% of plasma levels detected in retina and RPE/choroid.  
  • Toxicity:  
    – Up to 80 mg/day × 8 weeks: no serious AEs; mild gastrointestinal upset at highest doses only.  

3. SkQ1 (10-(6-Plastoquinonyl)-decyl­triphenyl­phosphonium) – “Visomitin”  
  • Mechanism: Plastoquinol antioxidant conjugated to TPP+; accumulates in mitochondria, quenches ROS, prevents ΔΨm collapse.  
  • Catalog: Sigma-Aldrich SML0967; Cayman 16387.  
  • Preclinical:  
    – ARPE-19 + H2O2: SkQ1 at 10–100 nM restores ΔΨm (JC-1), lowers ROS (DCFDA) and apoptotic markers (Levichev et al., 2015, Biochemistry (Moscow)).  
    – OXYS rat (spontaneous AMD‐like): topical SkQ1 eye drops (7 μM, 1 drop/day × 6 weeks) reduced RPE vacuolization, lipofuscin, preserved outer nuclear layer thickness (Nikonov et al., 2014, Graefes Arch Clin Exp Ophthalmol).  
  • Clinical (dry-eye indication):  
    – Russia: SkQ1 1.5 μM eye drops TID × 6 weeks improved tear stability; no ocular irritation (Univ. of Moscow data, 2013).  
    – No formal GA trial reported.  
  • ADME & ocular biodistribution:  
    – Topical absorption: corneal uptake ~80%, conjunctiva ~10%; vitreous levels ∼1–5 nM after 1 week.  
    – Minimal systemic exposure.  
  • Toxicity:  
    – Rabbit ocular safety: no corneal opacity or irritation at doses up to 100× therapeutic.  
    – Human dry-eye study: transient mild burning in ~5% of subjects; otherwise well tolerated.  

Summary  
– All three agents demonstrate robust target validation (mitochondrial ROS/ΔΨm), relevant RPE/animal data in GA-like contexts, clear mechanistic specificity (mitochondrial targeting), and have either pilot human data (Elamipretide) or excellent safety/ADME records (MitoQ, SkQ1).  
– They can be sourced easily for further in vitro, ex vivo or in vivo AMD/GA studies.  
– Given their distinct chemotypes (peptide vs. ubiquinone vs. plastoquinol), each offers a novel avenue for therapeutic development in geographic atrophy.
