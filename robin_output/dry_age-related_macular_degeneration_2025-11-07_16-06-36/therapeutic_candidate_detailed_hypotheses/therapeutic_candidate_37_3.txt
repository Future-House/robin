Proposal for 3-Mercaptopicolinic Acid (3-MPA; Tocris 2608)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
3-Mercaptopicolinic acid (3-MPA; also written 3-mercaptopicolinate/3-MP) is a small, thiol-containing picolinic-acid derivative historically identified as a biochemical inhibitor of gluconeogenesis through inhibition of phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PEPCK) and, at higher concentrations, glucose-6-phosphatase (G-6-Pase) in hepatic systems (Westergaard & Madsen, 2001). Early reports in the 1970s established 3-MPA as a preferential inhibitor of the cytosolic PEPCK isoform (PCK1), and the compound has since been used widely as a tool in mechanistic enzymology and physiology of gluconeogenesis across species and contexts (e.g., mammalian liver, pig, and protozoan PEPCKs) (Hidalgo et al., 2016; McLeod et al., 2019; Brearley et al., 2020). While the original discovery route is not detailed in the provided sources, a “convenient synthesis” was reported historically, and numerous structure–mechanism studies have refined the understanding of its binding behavior and chemical liabilities; by contrast, no data in the provided material indicate a natural origin (Westergaard & Madsen, 2001).

Chemically, 3-MPA belongs to the broader class of picolinic-acid–based, anionic, metal-coordinating inhibitors that engage the PEPCK active site by chelating the catalytically essential M1 divalent metal (typically Mn2+) via the ring nitrogen and exocyclic carboxylate; 3-MPA also contains a reactive free sulfhydryl group that drives both potency and time-dependent covalent reactivity with protein cysteines (McLeod et al., 2019). The picolinic-acid scaffold’s anionic, metal-binding character is a recognized design principle for PEPCK inhibitors and has been leveraged to build next-generation derivatives (e.g., 3-((carboxymethyl)thio)-picolinic acid, CMP) that seek to occupy both inner and outer subsites of the OAA/PEP pocket while reducing thiol-mediated liabilities (McLeod et al., 2019).

As a class, such PEPCK-directed small-molecule inhibitors have been applied as biochemical probes of gluconeogenic flux, as antihyperglycemic tools in preclinical metabolic studies, and as chemical probes to dissect PEPCK function in cancer and immunometabolism models; however, the field has wrestled with issues of thiol reactivity, complex multi-site binding kinetics, ADME/PAINS flags, and limited isoform selectivity, all of which complicate translation (Westergaard & Madsen, 2001; McLeod et al., 2019; Baptista et al., 2019; Hidalgo et al., 2016).

Therapeutic History  
3-MPA has a long track record as a research tool in hepatic metabolism. In perfused rat liver and in vivo rat studies, 3-MPA was used to blunt gluconeogenesis and lower blood glucose, with dosing paradigms including intraperitoneal administration (e.g., 100 mg/kg followed by 25 mg/kg three hours later) and 1 mM concentrations in perfusate to suppress fructose- or lactate/pyruvate-stimulated hepatic glucose production (Herling et al., 1998). More recently, 3-MPA has served as a comparator and positive control in modern PEPCK-targeted discovery programs, including enzyme assays against recombinant human cytosolic and mitochondrial PEPCK isoforms (PCK1/PCK2), CETSA target-engagement studies, cell-based functional assays (e.g., insulin secretion), and acute in vivo pharmacodynamic readouts (e.g., fasting glycemia five hours after 50 mg/kg IP administration) (Aragó et al., 2020). In several settings, 3-MPA reduces oncogenic phenotypes: it impairs anchorage-independent growth in MCF7 cells yet shows limited effects on glucose-stimulated insulin secretion in INS-1 cells, suggesting variable target engagement and a modest potency ceiling relative to newer chemotypes (Aragó et al., 2020).

3-MPA has been used to inhibit the mitochondrial isoform PEPCK-M (PCK2) in cancer models. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cell lines cultured under glucose deprivation, inhibition of human mitochondrial PEPCK by 3-MPA or genetic knockdown induced apoptosis, consistent with a reliance on PEPCK-mediated anaplerotic/cataplerotic cycling for survival under nutrient stress (Baptista et al., 2019). Computational docking studies against human PEPCK-M have used 3-MPA as a benchmark ligand to set score cutoffs and to elucidate canonical binding interactions (Mn2+ coordination; Arg/Lys salt-bridge network), aligning with structural insights from cytosolic isoforms (Baptista et al., 2019).

In melanoma models, 3-MPA (applied as a PCK1 inhibitor) sensitized vemurafenib-resistant tumors in vitro and in vivo by depressing pentose phosphate pathway–derived NADPH production and elevating ROS through KEAP1/Nrf2 axis modulation, thereby reversing resistance; here, 3-MPA increased oxidative stress rather than alleviating it (Ren et al., 2022). Across immunometabolism, 3-MPA (100 μM) reduced cytotoxic T lymphocyte effector functions and decreased maximal oxygen consumption rate (OCR) without altering basal glycolysis, indicating impaired mitochondrial respiratory capacity in activated T cells in which PEPCK-M predominates (Brownlie et al., 2025). In skeletal muscle myoblasts (C2C12), 3-MPA (0.25–1 mM) reduced proliferation and induced myogenic differentiation, with Pck2 transcripts predominant over Pck1; however, the compound interfered with enzymatic assays at ≥0.25–0.5 mM, complicating attribution to on-target inhibition (Brearley et al., 2020). Against porcine cytosolic PEPCK isoenzymes, 3-MPA exhibits mid-micromolar IC₅₀ (~65 μM) under saturating substrates and mechanistically mixed/complex kinetics (mixed non-competitive vs PEP; uncompetitive vs GDP/GTP), supporting dual-site binding behavior (Hidalgo et al., 2016).

No study in the provided literature assesses 3-MPA in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), retina, or dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD); a comprehensive mechanistic and structural study of related PEPCK inhibitors explicitly contains no mention of eye/RPE/AMD systems, emphasizing that the extant knowledge base for 3-MPA is outside ophthalmology (McLeod et al., 2019). Absent any reported ocular or RPE use, 3-MPA should be regarded as a systemic/metabolic tool compound with evolving applications in cancer and immunometabolism rather than as a precedent therapeutic in retinal degenerations (McLeod et al., 2019).

Mechanism of Action  
Mechanistic biochemistry and structural work converge on a dual-mode model for 3-MPA action on PEPCK:
- Active-site (inner-subsite) binding and Mn²⁺ coordination: The picolinic-acid moiety of 3-MPA coordinates the catalytic M1 Mn²⁺ ion via the ring nitrogen and exocyclic carboxylate oxygen(s), occupying the “inner” OAA/PEP subsite in a manner analogous to oxalate and other carboxylate inhibitors; co-crystal analyses and docking corroborate metal coordination along with salt-bridge/ion-pairing to conserved basic residues (e.g., Arg104/Arg423/Lys261/Lys262 in vertebrate enzymes) (McLeod et al., 2019; Baptista et al., 2019).
- Secondary/allosteric site binding: 3-MPA can occupy a second site near/behind the P-loop, with two molecules bound simultaneously (n≈2 by ITC), producing complex kinetic signatures (mixed non-competitive behavior with respect to PEP/OAA and uncompetitive inhibition versus GDP/GTP), and allowing allosteric influence on substrate/inhibitor binding at the active site (Hidalgo et al., 2016).

Kinetically, these binding modes yield mixed patterns that depend on the variable substrate: mixed non-competitive versus PEP/OAA; uncompetitive versus GDP/GTP, consistent with inhibitor binding to enzyme–nucleotide complexes and allosteric communication to the catalytic pocket (Hidalgo et al., 2016). Across isoforms, micromolar potency is typical: for rat cytosolic PEPCK (rcPEPCK) Kᵢ ~8 μM, for human mitochondrial PEPCK (hmPEPCK) Kᵢ ~3 μM in some determinations, though reported IC₅₀/Kᵢ values vary with assay format and metal/substrate conditions (McLeod et al., 2019). Variability is expected given that picolinic-acid derivatives chelate divalent metal cations; changes in available Mn²⁺ can partially masquerade as noncompetitive inhibition (i.e., by depleting active holoenzyme) in kinetic plots if not tightly controlled (McLeod et al., 2019).

A distinguishing chemical feature of 3-MPA is the free thiol. In vitro, the thiol can form mixed disulfide adducts with reactive cysteines on PEPCK (e.g., C288 on the P-loop in rat cytosolic PEPCK), producing time-dependent, covalent inactivation that contributes to potency but also to off-target reactivity and interpretational complexity; this chemistry is mitigated but not eliminated by inclusion of reducing agents in assays (McLeod et al., 2019). The thiol’s promiscuity likely explains reports of irreversible, time-dependent inhibition of G-6-Pase at millimolar concentrations, a phenomenon exploited historically to dissect gluconeogenic regulation but pharmacologically undesirable for selective PEPCK targeting (Westergaard & Madsen, 2001; McLeod et al., 2019). Because the M1 metal and a basic electrostatic microenvironment (e.g., Arg104/Arg423/Lys261/Lys262) are conserved in PEPCK-M and PEPCK-C, inhibitors that rely on metal chelation and anionic interactions, like 3-MPA, are predicted to show cross-isoform activity; docking against the human mitochondrial isoform reproduces these hallmarks (Baptista et al., 2019).

Several functional studies corroborate that 3-MPA perturbs cellular bioenergetics consistent with PEPCK inhibition but in cell-context-dependent ways. In activated effector T cells (PCK2-predominant), 100 μM 3-MPA reduced maximal OCR without changing basal ECAR, indicating compromised oxidative phosphorylation capacity while leaving basal glycolysis intact (Brownlie et al., 2025). In lung cancer cell lines, PEPCK-M inhibition by 3-MPA induced apoptosis only under glucose deprivation, highlighting the role of PCK2 in enabling biosynthesis from non-glucose substrates under stress (Baptista et al., 2019). Conversely, in BRAFi-resistant melanoma, PCK1 inhibition by 3-MPA depressed PPP-mediated NADPH synthesis, increased ROS by weakening antioxidant buffering (via KEAP1/Nrf2), and sensitized cells to targeted therapy, illustrating that PEPCK inhibition can amplify oxidative stress depending on metabolic wiring (Ren et al., 2022).

Finally, medicinal-chemistry and ADME flags apply. 3-MPA triggers PAINS warnings and violates the Pfizer 3/75 heuristic on polar surface area margins in in silico filtering, and its free thiol confers chemical reactivity that can perturb off-target proteins and auxiliary assay enzymes; analogs that remove the free thiol (e.g., thioethers like CMP) avoid mixed-disulfide inactivation at the cost of reduced potency (order-of-magnitude weaker Kᵢ) (Baptista et al., 2019; McLeod et al., 2019; Hidalgo et al., 2016).

Expected Effect  
The working hypothesis proposes that 3-MPA will inhibit mitochondrial PCK2 in RPE, redirecting flux toward TCA cycle maintenance, enhancing NADH generation, and reducing ROS during bioenergetic stress. Evidence from the supplied literature constrains and complicates each element of this chain:

- Direct engagement of PCK2 by 3-MPA. Biochemical and docking data support that 3-MPA inhibits human PEPCK-M with micromolar potency and establishes the binding physics (Mn²⁺ coordination; cationic pocket interactions), making PCK2 engagement in principle plausible in RPE if the protein is expressed and accessible (McLeod et al., 2019; Baptista et al., 2019). However, the provided sources do not document PCK2 expression in RPE (neither transcript nor protein), so expression in the proposed cell type cannot be confirmed from this corpus (McLeod et al., 2019). The supplied general isoform summary indicates that in humans both isoforms can be approximately equally abundant at the organismal level, underscoring that mitochondrial PEPCK may be physiologically relevant in human tissues, but this does not substitute for RPE-specific evidence (Yun, 2017).

- Direction of flux and impact on TCA maintenance/NADH. In activated T cells (PCK2-predominant), 3-MPA reduced maximal OCR, implying diminished electron transport chain capacity and, by extension, a reduction—not an enhancement—of NADH-supported oxidative phosphorylation reserve; this observation argues that PCK2 inhibition can constrain mitochondrial respiratory capacity in at least some mammalian cell types (Brownlie et al., 2025; Baptista et al., 2019). In NSCLC, PEPCK-M inhibition precipitated apoptosis during glucose deprivation, consistent with failure of metabolic adaptation when cataplerotic/anaplerotic cycling is blocked; this is contrary to the expectation that PCK2 inhibition would “rescue” TCA integrity and support survival under stress (Baptista et al., 2019). Together these data suggest that in cells relying on PEPCK-mediated cycling to support survival or effector function under nutrient/oxidative load, 3-MPA tends to reduce OXPHOS capacity and viability rather than bolster NADH generation and TCA pool maintenance.

- Effect on ROS. In BRAFi-resistant melanoma, 3-MPA decreased NADPH production (via PPP) and increased ROS by attenuating the Nrf2 antioxidant axis (increased KEAP1), thereby sensitizing cells to oxidative injury; this is the opposite sign from the hypothesis that 3-MPA would reduce ROS (Ren et al., 2022). Whether the same redox trajectory would manifest in RPE is uncertain, but the burden of evidence indicates that PEPCK inhibition can aggravate oxidative stress in at least some proliferative/stressed cells.

- Off-target and concentration-dependent effects relevant to RPE. At high exposure (hundreds of micromolar to millimolar), 3-MPA exhibits assay interference and off-target enzyme inhibition (e.g., G-6-P translocase and/or G-6-Pase, time-dependent irreversibility), and has been used at 1 mM in perfused livers, underscoring a risk window where non-PEPCK targets and chemical reactivity dominate (Westergaard & Madsen, 2001; Brearley et al., 2020; Herling et al., 1998). Its free thiol can form mixed-disulfide adducts with reactive cysteines, a liability in redox-sensitive tissues like the RPE where protein thiol chemistry is critical to homeostasis (McLeod et al., 2019). In silico PAINS flags and polarity metrics further caution against promiscuity and suboptimal drug-likeness (Baptista et al., 2019).

In summary, while 3-MPA can inhibit PEPCK-M and therefore could, in principle, modulate RPE mitochondrial metabolism, the best available functional evidence in related mammalian cell systems predicts reduced maximal OXPHOS capacity and, in certain contexts, increased ROS when PEPCK activity is blocked; this contradicts the proposed directionality of benefit for dry AMD. The provided sources do not document PCK2 expression in RPE, so the target linkage in the specified cell type remains unsubstantiated within this corpus (Brownlie et al., 2025; Baptista et al., 2019; Ren et al., 2022; McLeod et al., 2019).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Extensive mechanistic characterization and proven PEPCK engagement. 3-MPA’s dual-site binding, active-site metal coordination, and kinetic behavior are well described, with micromolar potency against both cytosolic and mitochondrial PEPCK isoforms under defined conditions, enabling precise hypothesis testing of PEPCK-dependent biology (McLeod et al., 2019; Hidalgo et al., 2016).  
- Demonstrated modulation of mitochondrial bioenergetics in mammalian cells. The compound reduces maximal OCR in activated T cells (PCK2-predominant), confirming that pharmacologic inhibition of PEPCK-M can reprogram OXPHOS in a predictable direction, albeit one that appears detrimental to respiratory capacity (Brownlie et al., 2025).  
- Tool-compound utility across contexts relevant to stress metabolism. In NSCLC under glucose limitation and in BRAFi-resistant melanoma, 3-MPA perturbs survival pathways and redox control, reinforcing that PEPCK is a nodal metabolic regulator in stress adaptation—an axis of interest in RPE pathobiology in AMD (Baptista et al., 2019; Ren et al., 2022).  

Weaknesses and Risks  
- Directionality of effect conflicts with the AMD hypothesis. Available cell-based data indicate 3-MPA reduces maximal mitochondrial respiratory capacity and can increase ROS in specific contexts by depleting PPP-derived NADPH, the opposite of the hypothesized benefit for RPE under bioenergetic/oxidative stress (Brownlie et al., 2025; Ren et al., 2022).  
- Chemical liabilities. The free thiol drives time-dependent covalent reactivity (mixed disulfides) with PEPCK and likely other proteins; at millimolar exposure, 3-MPA causes irreversible, time-dependent inhibition of G-6-Pase, underscoring a broad risk of off-target covalent modification that is unacceptable for ocular therapy without significant medicinal-chemistry mitigation (McLeod et al., 2019; Westergaard & Madsen, 2001). PAINS flags and polarity metrics caution against promiscuity and poor drug-likeness (Baptista et al., 2019).  
- Inconsistent target engagement in cells and modest potency relative to alternatives. Compared with newer PEPCK inhibitors (e.g., iPEPCK-2), 3-MPA shows two orders of magnitude weaker IC₅₀s in some cellular contexts and variable engagement (e.g., limited effect on insulin secretion), suggesting that achieving effective concentrations in relevant compartments may be difficult or that off-target liabilities confound outcomes (Aragó et al., 2020).  
- No precedent in retinal/RPE systems. The provided literature contains no data on 3-MPA in the eye, retina, RPE, or AMD models, and offers no RPE expression evidence for PCK2, making the translational leap speculative from the present corpus (McLeod et al., 2019; Yun, 2017).  
- Concentration-dependent assay interference and off-target effects. In C2C12 cells, 3-MPA interfered with PEPCK assays at ≥0.25–0.5 mM; perfused liver studies used 1 mM; such concentrations raise the likelihood of nonspecific effects in RPE (Brearley et al., 2020; Herling et al., 1998).  

Decision-Critical Gaps for AMD  
- Target validation in RPE. There is no evidence in the supplied material confirming PCK2 expression and activity in RPE, nor any demonstration that PEPCK-M–dependent cycling is maladaptive in dry AMD; this gap precludes mechanistic confidence (McLeod et al., 2019).  
- Directionality of benefit. The compound’s demonstrated tendency to reduce maximal OXPHOS capacity and, in some contexts, to enhance oxidative stress directly contradicts the intended pharmacology (increase NADH/OXPHOS; decrease ROS) in stressed RPE (Brownlie et al., 2025; Ren et al., 2022).  
- Ocular safety. Free-thiol reactivity, metal chelation, and PAINS liabilities make 3-MPA a risky starting point for ocular delivery; no ocular PK/safety data are available in the provided sources (Baptista et al., 2019; McLeod et al., 2019; Westergaard & Madsen, 2001).  

If the project’s strategic objective is to probe whether inhibiting PEPCK-M benefits RPE bioenergetic stability, a safer and more specific chemical tool would be required. Notably, thioether analogs like CMP remove the free thiol and still engage the active site competitively (albeit with ~10× weaker potency), and crystallography confirms occupancy of both inner and outer subsites; such molecules may offer cleaner on-target pharmacology for in vitro RPE studies while avoiding mixed-disulfide covalency (McLeod et al., 2019). However, even for such analogs, the directionality risk remains given that PEPCK-M inhibition has reduced mitochondrial respiratory capacity in mammalian cells that rely on this enzyme under activation stress (Brownlie et al., 2025).

Overall, based on the provided literature, I do not recommend prioritizing 3-MPA as a repurposed therapeutic candidate for dry AMD. The compound’s mechanistic actions and cell-level consequences in related systems predict outcomes counter to the proposed benefit in RPE, and its chemical liabilities are incompatible with ocular drug development without substantial medicinal-chemistry redesign (Brownlie et al., 2025; Ren et al., 2022; McLeod et al., 2019; Baptista et al., 2019).
