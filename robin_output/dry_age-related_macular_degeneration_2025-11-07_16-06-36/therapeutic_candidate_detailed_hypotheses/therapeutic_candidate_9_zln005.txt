Proposal for ZLN005 (MedChemExpress HY-100726)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
ZLN005 (MedChemExpress HY-100726) is a synthetic small-molecule compound that belongs to the benzimidazole class. Benzimidazole derivatives have a long history in medicinal chemistry for their wide-ranging biological activities, including antihelminthic, anti-inflammatory, and metabolic modulating effects. In the case of ZLN005, it has been identified specifically as a selective transcriptional activator of peroxisome proliferator‐activated receptor gamma coactivator‐1 alpha (PGC-1α), a master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis and antioxidant defense. The synthesis and chemical derivation of ZLN005, although not detailed exhaustively in the provided literature, follow typical synthetic protocols for benzimidazole compounds, which often involve the condensation of o-phenylenediamine with carboxylic acid derivatives to yield a benzimidazole core. This structural class is known not only for its high affinity to various protein targets but also for favorable pharmacokinetic properties that have motivated its use in drug development efforts. Several studies have demonstrated that ZLN005 effectively induces PGC-1α expression in various cell types, including skeletal muscle and particularly the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells, which are critical for maintaining retinal homeostasis (Satish et al., 2018). Its assignment under the benzimidazole class further suggests that ZLN005 may share common metabolic and pharmacological features with other compounds in the same class, which have been employed in different therapeutic areas, supporting its potential for repurposing in ocular disorders such as dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) (Sun et al., 2018; Bleckwehl et al., 2025).

Therapeutic History:  
Initially, ZLN005 emerged from screening studies designed to identify small molecules that could stimulate PGC-1α expression. Early research in cellular models demonstrated that ZLN005 enhances mitochondrial biogenesis and upregulates the expression of downstream oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) and antioxidant genes, which in turn improves mitochondrial health and overall cellular energy metabolism (Satish et al., 2018). In skeletal muscle and hepatic cells, for example, treatment with ZLN005 was associated with increased basal and maximal oxygen consumption rates, an increase in spare respiratory capacity, and a concomitant improvement in glucose uptake and cellular ATP levels (Satish et al., 2018). Such characteristics have spurred interest in its therapeutic application beyond metabolic diseases to conditions where mitochondrial dysfunction is a central pathogenic factor.

Notably, several groups have investigated ZLN005 in the context of retinal diseases. Preclinical studies using ARPE-19 cells—a well-accepted human RPE cell line—demonstrated that ZLN005 markedly upregulates PGC-1α expression and its associated transcription factors and antioxidant enzymes. These investigations have shown that ZLN005 can confer significant protection against oxidative stress-induced cell death when cells are exposed to prooxidative agents such as hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) and sodium iodate (NaIO₃) (Satish et al., 2018). Although ZLN005 has not yet undergone extensive clinical evaluation specifically for dry AMD, its robust activity in stimulating mitochondrial function and providing cytoprotection in RPE models forms a compelling rationale for its repurposing. Studies in other systems, including cardiomyocytes and skeletal muscle, support its therapeutic use in settings of bioenergetic decline and oxidative damage, which are hallmarks of many age-related degenerative diseases (Ma et al., 2023; Moradi, 2022). However, it should be noted that many of the studies exploring ZLN005 for ocular purposes have been in vitro, and while they present an optimistic scenario, further in vivo animal studies and careful pharmacokinetic analyses in ocular tissues are needed before clinical application in AMD can be considered (Satish et al., 2018).

Mechanism of Action:  
The pharmacological efficacy of ZLN005 is tightly linked to its mechanism of action as an activator of PGC-1α. PGC-1α is a transcriptional coactivator that regulates a myriad of genes implicated in energy metabolism, mitochondrial biogenesis, fatty acid oxidation, and antioxidant protection. In RPE cells—where the maintenance of a high mitochondrial integrity is crucial for visual function—the expression of PGC-1α is fundamental to mitigating oxidative stress and maintaining metabolic homeostasis (Felszeghy et al., 2019; Iacovelli et al., 2016). ZLN005 increases the expression levels of PGC-1α by enhancing its transcription, culminating in the upregulation of a host of downstream target genes. This includes key mitochondrial genes such as ATP50, COX4, COX5b, and NDUFB5, all of which are involved in oxidative phosphorylation and energy production (Satish et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2025).

At the molecular level, ZLN005 promotes the transcriptional program by potentially modulating AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) activity, a well-known regulator of PGC-1α function. The compound’s facilitation of PGC-1α nuclear translocation and its catalytic coactivation leads to the stimulation of mitochondrial replication and repair processes, thereby increasing mitochondrial mass and mtDNA copy number (Satish et al., 2018). In parallel, the upregulation of PGC-1α by ZLN005 induces the expression of several endogenous antioxidant enzymes, including superoxide dismutases (SOD1 and SOD2) and mitochondrial thioredoxin 2 (TXN2). This cascade of events enhances the cellular capacity to neutralize reactive oxygen species (ROS), thus lowering mitochondrial superoxide production and reducing the levels of oxidative stress markers such as 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE), a product of lipid peroxidation frequently associated with degenerative retinal changes (Satish et al., 2018).

Furthermore, ZLN005 appears to improve overall mitochondrial bioenergetics. It has been observed to increase both basal and maximal oxygen consumption rates (OCR) in RPE cells, indicating an enhanced capacity for ATP synthesis under both steady-state and stress conditions (Satish et al., 2018). The compound also augments the spare respiratory capacity, which is critical for cellular survival when faced with acute energy demands during oxidative stress. Such improvements in mitochondrial function are directly relevant to the pathogenesis of dry AMD, where RPE cells experience an energy deficit due to progressive mitochondrial dysfunction and chronic oxidative injury (Felszeghy et al., 2019). Additionally, by upregulating PGC-1α, ZLN005 may indirectly influence other cellular processes, including autophagy and lipid metabolism, both of which are altered in the aging RPE and contribute to the formation of drusen—the hallmark deposits seen in dry AMD (Zhou et al., 2024).

It is important to emphasize that the protective effects of ZLN005 are markedly dependent on the presence of functional PGC-1α. Experiments in which PGC-1α expression was silenced in RPE cells resulted in a significant loss of the cytoprotective benefits conferred by ZLN005, underscoring the compound’s mechanism-specific action (Satish et al., 2018). Parallel studies in non-ocular cell types, such as skeletal muscle myotubes and alveolar epithelial cells, have further supported its role in enhancing mitochondrial function and regulating oxidative stress through similar molecular pathways (Zhang et al., 2013; Bleckwehl et al., 2025). Thus, the molecular interactions initiated by ZLN005—ranging from the activation of PGC-1α to the subsequent stimulation of antioxidant enzymes and mitochondrial biogenesis—form the crux of its therapeutic utility, specifically in countering the bioenergetic decline characteristic of dry AMD.

Expected Effect:  
Given the central involvement of mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress in the pathogenesis of dry AMD, the therapeutic hypothesis for ZLN005 is that its activation of PGC-1α in retinal pigment epithelial cells will restore mitochondrial homeostasis and augment cellular antioxidant defenses. In the proposed assays, treatment with ZLN005 is expected to upregulate PGC-1α expression in RPE cells, thereby triggering a cascade of downstream events that lead to enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis. This includes an increase in mtDNA copy number and the expression of mitochondrial genes that are essential for optimal oxidative phosphorylation performance (Satish et al., 2018). As mitochondrial function improves, RPE cells should exhibit higher basal and maximal oxygen consumption rates, indicative of an improved metabolic profile and increased capacity to generate ATP under both normal and stress conditions (Satish et al., 2018).

The antioxidative effects of ZLN005 are also expected to manifest in augmented levels of enzymes such as SOD1, SOD2, and TXN2, which collectively enhance the cell’s capability to scavenge reactive oxygen species. These biochemical events are critical because the accumulation of oxidative stress in RPE cells, driven by chronic exposure to high levels of ROS, is a significant contributor to cell degeneration in dry AMD (Felszeghy et al., 2019; Blasiak et al., 2021). With improved mitochondrial function and reduced oxidative stress, it is anticipated that ZLN005 treatment will lead to a decrease in the accumulation of oxidative damage markers like 4-HNE, thereby mitigating processes that lead to RPE cell apoptosis and the subsequent photoreceptor degeneration observed in dry AMD (Blasiak et al., 2021; Iacovelli et al., 2016).

Moreover, in vitro studies have demonstrated that under conditions of experimentally induced oxidative stress with agents such as H₂O₂, ox-LDL, or NaIO₃, ZLN005 significantly reduces cell death in ARPE-19 cells (Satish et al., 2018). Therefore, the expected effect of ZLN005 in the proposed assay is a robust cytoprotective response in RPE cells. This would be characterized by improved cell viability, restoration of mitochondrial respiratory function, and a reduction in stress-induced apoptotic markers—a constellation of effects that directly addresses the bioenergetic and oxidative challenges underlying dry AMD (Satish et al., 2018). Additionally, since PGC-1α is known to regulate lipid metabolism and prevent lipid droplet accumulation—a process that may be linked to the formation of drusen in the AMD retina—the activation of PGC-1α by ZLN005 could also have beneficial effects on lipid handling within the RPE (Zhou et al., 2024; Iacovelli et al., 2016). Thus, through these concerted mechanisms, ZLN005 is anticipated to stabilize or even improve RPE function, thereby halting or reversing aspects of the degenerative cascade that leads to vision loss in dry AMD.

Overall Evaluation:  
A comprehensive evaluation of ZLN005 reveals several compelling strengths as well as notable weaknesses that should be weighed in the context of repurposing it for the treatment of dry AMD. One of the principal strengths of ZLN005 lies in its well-defined mechanism of action. By selectively activating PGC-1α, ZLN005 directly targets the mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress that are central to the pathogenesis of dry AMD. The upregulation of PGC-1α leads to improvements in mitochondrial biogenesis, an increase in the expression of mitochondrial OXPHOS genes, and the enhancement of antioxidant defenses—all factors that are critically compromised in the diseased RPE (Felszeghy et al., 2019; Blasiak et al., 2021). This mechanistic rationale is further supported by in vitro studies showing that ZLN005 can effectively reduce prooxidant-induced cell death and improve mitochondrial respiratory parameters such as basal respiration, maximal respiration, and spare respiratory capacity (Satish et al., 2018).

Another strength is the translational potential implied by the compound’s efficacy in multiple cell types. ZLN005 has not only demonstrated protective effects in RPE cells but has also been shown to modulate mitochondrial activity in skeletal muscle and hepatic cells. This broad-spectrum improvement in energy metabolism indicates that its bioenergetic benefits may be harnessed in the complex metabolic environment of the RPE, where energy deficits contribute to dry AMD pathology (Satish et al., 2018). Achieving a robust activation of PGC-1α is particularly attractive because it addresses both the cellular energy production and ROS management simultaneously, which are two critical determinants of RPE cell survival and function (Iacovelli et al., 2016; Rosales et al., 2019).

Furthermore, the specificity of ZLN005’s action has been validated by studies where the loss of PGC-1α abrogated its cytoprotective effects, underscoring that its efficacy is tightly linked to the activation of this pivotal regulatory pathway (Satish et al., 2018). This is an important consideration because it suggests that the therapeutic effects can be fine-tuned by modulating the expression or activity of PGC-1α. In the context of dry AMD, where PGC-1α levels are often diminished or dysregulated, the targeted upregulation provided by ZLN005 offers a focused therapeutic intervention.

Despite these strengths, several weaknesses and challenges remain. Foremost, the majority of the supportive evidence for ZLN005’s efficacy in protecting RPE cells has been generated through in vitro studies using cell lines such as ARPE-19. While these models are indispensable for preliminary evaluation, they do not fully recapitulate the complex in vivo environment of the human retina, and the translatability of these effects into clinical benefit remains to be demonstrated (Satish et al., 2018). Additionally, the current body of literature shows that some reports have emerged from journals of variable quality; this introduces a degree of uncertainty regarding the reproducibility of the findings and necessitates rigorous validation of these results in higher-order in vivo models and, eventually, clinical trials (Satish et al., 2018).

Another pertinent concern is the pharmacokinetic profile of ZLN005 in the ocular environment. While its favorable metabolic effects are evident in cell culture and certain systemic models, its ability to reach and maintain effective concentrations in the RPE following systemic or local administration is not well established in the available reports (Bleckwehl et al., 2025; Miller et al., 2019). In addition, the potential off-target effects of ZLN005, particularly in tissues where mitochondrial modulation may have unintended consequences, have not been fully explored. This includes the risk that enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis in non-target cells could lead to dysregulated energy metabolism or contribute to unwanted proliferative responses (Satish et al., 2018; Bleckwehl et al., 2025).

Moreover, the clinical development pathway for repurposing ZLN005 for ocular indications such as dry AMD will require comprehensive toxicological studies, detailed pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic analyses in ocular tissues, and the establishment of dosing regimens that ensure both efficacy and safety over chronic treatment periods. Given the slow progression of dry AMD and the need for long-term treatment, any adverse effects or suboptimal pharmacokinetic properties could significantly limit the compound’s clinical use (Satish et al., 2018; Iacovelli et al., 2016).

Additionally, while the activation of PGC-1α is a promising strategy for mitigating mitochondrial dysfunction, the role of PGC-1α in the retina is complex. Overexpression of PGC-1α has been linked in some studies to increased susceptibility to light-induced retinal damage or other forms of stress, suggesting that a delicate balance must be maintained to achieve therapeutic benefit without tipping the scales towards potential toxicity or dysregulation of cellular homeostasis (Iacovelli et al., 2016; Blasiak et al., 2021). This dual nature of PGC-1α activity means that careful titration of ZLN005 dosing—and possibly the development of combination therapies—may be necessary to harness its protective effects while avoiding exacerbation of retinal stress under certain conditions.

In summary, ZLN005 emerges as a promising candidate for repurposing in dry AMD based on its well-characterized mechanism of stimulating PGC-1α expression, its ability to enhance mitochondrial biogenesis and antioxidant defenses, and the encouraging results obtained from in vitro studies in ARPE-19 cells. Its molecular action aligns closely with the pathophysiological profiles observed in dry AMD, where mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress contribute to RPE degeneration. However, while the preclinical evidence is robust in terms of establishing a mechanistic framework, significant challenges remain in translating these findings to an in vivo and eventually a clinical setting. Key areas that require further investigation include detailed pharmacokinetic studies in ocular models, confirmation of efficacy in animal models of dry AMD, and a full safety profile with chronic dosing regimens (Felszeghy et al., 2019; Blasiak et al., 2021; Nashine et al., 2019).

Moreover, the integration of ZLN005 with current understanding of AMD pathogenesis suggests that the compound could potentially be part of combination therapies that target several pathogenic pathways simultaneously—such as inflammation, lipid dysregulation, and complement activation—in addition to mitochondrial dysfunction. Indeed, the multifactorial nature of AMD means that a monotherapy approach may be insufficient, and ZLN005’s role could be most valuable when combined with other agents that complement its effects on mitochondrial and oxidative stress parameters (Rosales et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2025).

From a strategic drug development perspective, ZLN005 offers several attractive points for further exploration. Its synthetic origin as a benzimidazole derivative means that established methods exist for its production and modification, allowing for potential structure-activity relationship (SAR) studies aimed at enhancing its potency, selectivity, and ocular bioavailability. Such modifications could lead to derivatives with improved pharmacokinetic profiles tailored for retinal delivery. Moreover, leveraging advanced drug delivery systems, such as nanoparticle carriers or sustained-release formulations, could further enhance the therapeutic window of ZLN005 in the eye while minimizing systemic exposure and associated off-target effects (Bleckwehl et al., 2025; Miller et al., 2019).

In conclusion, the repurposing of ZLN005 for the treatment of dry AMD is supported by a compelling mechanistic rationale and a growing body of preclinical evidence that underscores its potential to restore mitochondrial dynamics and counteract oxidative stress in retinal pigment epithelial cells. The strengths of this candidate include its specificity for PGC-1α activation, its demonstrated ability to enhance mitochondrial function and energy metabolism, and its protective effects against oxidative injury in vitro. On the other hand, the limitations primarily stem from the current reliance on in vitro data, the need for more rigorous in vivo validation, and uncertainties regarding its pharmacokinetic behavior in ocular tissues. Therefore, while ZLN005 stands out as a scientifically promising candidate, advancing it through the drug development pipeline will require concerted efforts to address these gaps through detailed preclinical studies, formulation optimization, and eventually, well-designed clinical trials to determine its efficacy and safety in patients with dry AMD (Felszeghy et al., 2019; Rosales et al., 2019).

Overall, the data presented through multiple independent studies provide a strong foundation for considering ZLN005 as a viable therapeutic candidate in the repurposing paradigm for dry age-related macular degeneration. With further validation and refinement, itself or an optimized derivative could potentially offer a new strategy to preserve RPE integrity and maintain visual function in AMD patients, addressing a significant unmet medical need in the realm of retinal degenerative diseases (Rosales et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2025). Continued research efforts should focus on delineating the precise molecular signatures of ZLN005’s effects in RPE cells, assessing its long-term impact on retinal morphology and function in vivo, and investigating its interactions with other signaling cascades implicated in AMD progression (Satish et al., 2018). Only through such thorough and multidisciplinary evaluations can the true therapeutic potential of ZLN005 for dry AMD be accurately determined and translated into clinical benefit.

In summary, while challenges remain in translating the promising in vitro results to clinical outcomes, the accumulated evidence suggests that ZLN005’s activation of PGC-1α represents a credible and innovative strategy to counteract the bioenergetic decline in RPE cells—a central feature in the pathogenesis of dry AMD. For these reasons, further preclinical evaluation and careful optimization of dosing and delivery are warranted to fully ascertain the viability of ZLN005 as a therapeutic option in the ongoing battle against dry age-related macular degeneration (Satish et al., 2018).
