Proposal for Bardoxolone methyl (CDDO-Me; MedChemExpress HY-15151)

Below is a comprehensive evaluation of Bardoxolone methyl (CDDO‐Me) as a repurposed therapeutic candidate for dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD), structured in sections that address its origins, therapeutic history, known mechanisms of action, expected effects in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), and an overall assessment of its strengths and challenges.

• Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Bardoxolone methyl is a semisynthetic derivative belonging to the synthetic oleanane triterpenoid class. Its origin stems from oleanolic acid—a naturally occurring pentacyclic triterpenoid abundantly found in various medicinal plants. Early medicinal chemistry programs sought to overcome the modest anti‐inflammatory activity and poor bioavailability of oleanolic acid by introducing targeted modifications. Through chemical derivatization, these programs generated CDDO (2‐cyano‐3,12‐dioxooleana‐1,9‐dien‐28‐oic acid) and subsequently its C‐28 methyl ester, known as Bardoxolone methyl (CDDO‐Me) (Borella et al., 2019). The synthetic process involves the installation of electrophilic Michael acceptor groups at strategic positions on the oleanane scaffold, such as the addition of an α,β‐unsaturated carbonyl in the A‐ring and further incorporation of a cyano group that substantially increases the compound’s potency relative to its natural precursor (Wong et al., 2016; Zhang & Chapman, 2020). These chemical modifications not only enhance the anti‐inflammatory and anticancer properties but also improve chemical stability and oral bioavailability. The structural design, which incorporates a reversible electrophilic reactivity, underpins the compound’s ability to engage diverse cellular targets (Thomas, 2012; Zhou et al., 2014). Bardoxolone methyl thus represents a chemically advanced, highly potent agent from a class of molecules originally designed to trigger cytoprotective responses via direct electrophilic modulation.

• Therapeutic History  
Bardoxolone methyl has undergone extensive preclinical and early clinical evaluation primarily in fields as disparate as oncology and chronic kidney disease (CKD). Initial studies established that synthetic oleanane triterpenoids, including CDDO and its variants, exert dramatic anti‐inflammatory effects by suppressing inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) and cyclooxygenase (COX‐2) expression in activated macrophages (Gatbonton‐Schwager, 2014). Preclinical studies in multiple mouse cancer models and inflammatory conditions confirmed its potent anticancer activity and cytoprotective properties, which catalyzed its progression into human clinical trials (Borella et al., 2019; Nakagami, 2016).  
In the clinical arena, Bardoxolone methyl was advanced through Phase I cancer trials, where its once‐daily oral dosing regimen and pharmacokinetic profile—with a terminal half‐life of approximately 39 hours—were characterized (Thomas, 2012; Nakagami, 2016). More notably, its unexpected ability to improve estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) in patients with advanced renal disease, particularly in diabetic nephropathy, led to its evaluation in large Phase II and Phase III trials, such as the BEAM and BEACON studies (Yagishita et al., 2020; Tanase et al., 2022). However, the BEACON trial was terminated prematurely due to an increased incidence of cardiovascular events, including heart failure, highlighting concerns about the compound’s systemic safety profile when used at higher doses or in populations with comorbidities (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Thomas, 2012).  
Despite its promising preclinical pharmacology and the breadth of clinical data available in non‐ocular indications, to date there is no direct record in the literature of Bardoxolone methyl being used specifically for the treatment of dry AMD or similar ocular degenerative conditions (Schiavoni et al., 2025). Nonetheless, the overall safety and pharmacokinetic properties observed in CKD and oncology trials lend valuable insights for considering drug repurposing across therapeutic areas.

• Mechanism of Action  
Bardoxolone methyl is best known for its ability to activate the nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2 (Nrf2) pathway—a master regulator of cellular defense mechanisms against oxidative and electrophilic stress. Under basal conditions, Nrf2 is tightly regulated by Kelch‐like ECH‐associated protein 1 (Keap1), which serves as an adaptor for ubiquitin ligases targeting Nrf2 for proteasomal degradation. Bardoxolone methyl functions as a potent electrophile and interacts with sulfhydryl groups on critical cysteine residues within Keap1. This covalent or reversible Michael addition reaction disrupts the Keap1–Nrf2 complex, thereby stabilizing and allowing Nrf2 to translocate into the nucleus (Borella et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2014).  
Once in the nucleus, Nrf2 heterodimerizes with small Maf proteins and binds to the antioxidant response element (ARE) in the promoter regions of a host of cytoprotective genes. This transcriptional activation results in the upregulation of a battery of antioxidant and phase II detoxifying enzymes, including but not limited to heme oxygenase‐1 (HO‐1), NAD(P)H dehydrogenase (quinone 1; NQO1), various glutathione transferases, and glutamate–cysteine ligase subunits (Thomas, 2012; Wong et al., 2016). Studies in non‐ocular tissues have shown that at very low (nanomolar) doses, Bardoxolone methyl robustly engages this cytoprotective program while also exerting dose‐dependent biology wherein intermediate and high doses trigger effects such as modulation of cell differentiation and direct anticancer activity, respectively (Borella et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2014).  
It is important to note that while the canonical targets of Nrf2 activation are well characterized, Bardoxolone methyl’s electrophilic character enables it to interact with multiple regulatory proteins. For example, it can inhibit IκB kinase β (IKKβ), leading to suppression of nuclear factor κB (NF‐κB) signaling and subsequent reduction in proinflammatory cytokine production (Zhou et al., 2014; Cuadrado et al., 2019). Although the literature does not provide direct experimental evidence that Bardoxolone methyl specifically upregulates mitochondrial antioxidants such as superoxide dismutase 2 (SOD2) and peroxiredoxin 3 (PRDX3) in RPE cells, the robust, broad‐spectrum transcriptional response induced by Nrf2 activation strongly suggests that mitochondrial and cytoplasmic components of the antioxidant defense system may also be upregulated (Zhou et al., 2014; Nakagami et al., 2015). Such upregulation would be consistent with previous findings of increased expression of antioxidant genes and improved cellular redox status upon treatment with similar synthetic triterpenoids (Schiavoni et al., 2025; Sporn et al., 2011).

• Expected Effect in Dry AMD  
The pathogenesis of dry age‐related macular degeneration is multifactorial, with chronic oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction playing a central role in the degeneration of the RPE—a critical monolayer that supports photoreceptor health. RPE cells are particularly vulnerable to oxidative damage due to high metabolic activity, exposure to light, and the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). In this context, the proposed mechanism of Bardoxolone methyl, namely activation of the Nrf2/ARE pathway, provides a biologically tenable strategy for neuroprotection and cellular preservation.  
When administered at low doses, Bardoxolone methyl is expected to trigger a cytoprotective transcriptional program in RPE cells. Activation of Nrf2 should lead to the induction of a range of antioxidant enzymes and detoxifying proteins which together help re-establish redox balance in the face of chronic oxidative insult (Borella et al., 2019; Thomas, 2012). One key hypothesis is that, in addition to the already well‐documented induction of cytosolic antioxidants such as HO‐1 and NQO1, Bardoxolone methyl may also promote the expression of mitochondrial antioxidant enzymes such as SOD2 and PRDX3. SOD2 is the primary defense against superoxide radicals within the mitochondria, and PRDX3 plays a critical role in detoxifying hydrogen peroxide produced by mitochondrial respiration. Although direct data in RPE cells are currently lacking, studies in other cellular contexts have demonstrated that activation of Nrf2 results in the upregulation of a broad spectrum of antioxidant genes, and it is plausible that under the oxidative duress typical of dry AMD, similar effects would be observed in RPE cells (Zhou et al., 2014; Nakagami et al., 2015).  
In vitro models simulating oxidative stress—such as exposure of RPE cells to hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)—have shown that compounds with similar electrophilic properties, and indeed other members of the CDDO family like CDDO-Im, reduce the intracellular MitoSOX fluorescence signal, indicative of a reduction in mitochondrial ROS (mtROS) (Cheng et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2024). Moreover, improvements in mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψm) have been correlated with such treatments, suggesting that these molecules can help maintain mitochondrial function under stressful conditions (Nakagami, 2016; Zhou et al., 2014). Such outcomes are highly relevant in dry AMD, where mitochondrial dysfunction in the RPE has been implicated in disease progression. The anticipated outcomes, therefore, include a reduction in mtROS, restoration of Δψm, and subsequent preservation of RPE cellular integrity and function.  
Another positive aspect is the compound’s previously established oral bioavailability and pharmacokinetic profile. Clinical studies in CKD patients have demonstrated that therapeutic plasma concentrations can be achieved with once-daily dosing. Although systemic administration in ocular diseases is not ideal, these data provide a strong pharmacological starting point for repurposing. In addition, if systemic toxicity remains a concern—as suggested by the cardiovascular signals in CKD trials—an alternative approach might involve localized ocular delivery (for instance, via intravitreal injection or by developing an ocular formulation) to achieve high retinal bioavailability while minimizing systemic exposure (Nakagami, 2016; Thomas, 2012).

• Overall Evaluation  
In assessing Bardoxolone methyl for repurposing as a therapeutic for dry AMD, several critical points emerge that underline both its promise and the challenges that must be overcome.

Strengths:  
1. Strong Chemical Rationale and Potency:  
Bardoxolone methyl is a product of sophisticated medicinal chemistry, designed to overcome the limitations of natural triterpenoids like oleanolic acid by installing reactive electrophilic groups that significantly enhance its potency. This design specifically enables the activation of the Nrf2 pathway—a mechanism central to antioxidant defense—at very low concentrations (Borella et al., 2019; Wong et al., 2016). The high potency of Bardoxolone methyl as an Nrf2 activator is a significant advantage, particularly when considering that even subtle improvements in cellular antioxidant capacity can have a profound impact on chronic degenerative diseases like dry AMD.

2. Broad Cytoprotective Mechanism:  
The mechanism of Bardoxolone methyl involves the disruption of the Keap1-Nrf2 complex via covalent modification of Keap1 cysteine residues, facilitating the nuclear translocation of Nrf2 and the subsequent transcriptional activation of a broad range of antioxidant and detoxifying enzymes (Zhou et al., 2014; Thomas, 2012). This broad cytoprotective response is particularly relevant to the RPE, where chronic oxidative stress has been implicated in cellular dysfunction and death. The possibility that Bardoxolone methyl may also induce mitochondrial antioxidant enzymes such as SOD2 and PRDX3, although not yet directly confirmed in RPE, is an attractive hypothesis; such an effect would directly address the mitochondrial dysfunction that is a hallmark of dry AMD (Zhou et al., 2014; Nakagami et al., 2015).

3. Extensive Clinical Experience and Pharmacokinetic Data:  
Bardoxolone methyl has been evaluated in several clinical trials—most notably in the context of CKD and oncology—yielding a wealth of pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, and tolerability data. Its oral bioavailability, with a terminal half-life that supports once-daily dosing, is well documented (Thomas, 2012; Nakagami, 2016). Although the systemic adverse events observed in these trials (such as cardiovascular-related events) present challenges for broad systemic administration, these data nonetheless offer a useful starting point for repurposing. The established clinical profile can inform dose-escalation studies and safety monitoring protocols should Bardoxolone methyl be advanced into ocular trials.

4. Support from Analogous Preclinical Models:  
Several studies in related models have demonstrated that synthetic triterpenoids can protect retinal tissue from oxidative damage. For example, experiments with CDDO-Im, a closely related analogue, have shown protective effects in retinal ischemia–reperfusion injury, including the attenuation of inflammatory responses and preservation of retinal structure (Cheng et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2024). Although these studies are not directly with Bardoxolone methyl, the mechanistic commonalities within this compound class support the notion that the same protective pathways can be harnessed in the context of dry AMD.

Weaknesses and Challenges:  
1. Lack of Direct Evidence in RPE/Dry AMD Models:  
Despite the strong theoretical rationale, direct evidence that Bardoxolone methyl upregulates mitochondrial antioxidants (SOD2, PRDX3) in RPE cells is currently lacking. Most published data are derived from non-ocular cell types or from studies using other analogues such as CDDO-Im (Cai et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2014). It is therefore essential to perform specific in vitro studies in RPE cell lines under oxidative stress conditions to measure the expression of mitochondrial antioxidant enzymes and the functional status of mitochondria (e.g., Δψm, mtROS levels) following treatment with Bardoxolone methyl.

2. Safety Concerns from Prior Clinical Experience:  
A significant challenge in repurposing Bardoxolone methyl stems from its safety profile in systemic indications. The Phase III BEACON trial in CKD patients was halted due to an increased incidence of serious cardiovascular events, including heart failure and increased mortality (Tanase et al., 2022; Cuadrado et al., 2019). Although the mechanisms underlying these adverse effects may be related to systemic fluid retention, off‐target reactivity, or other metabolic perturbations, they raise concerns about the safety of systemic administration. For ocular application, it may be necessary to consider alternative dosing strategies or localized delivery methods to avoid systemic exposure while still achieving therapeutic concentrations in the retina.

3. Translational and Formulation Challenges:  
The unique anatomy and physiology of the eye introduce additional obstacles in drug repurposing. RPE cells reside in a relatively immune-privileged, isolated microenvironment behind the blood–retinal barrier. The pharmacokinetics and tissue penetration profiles of Bardoxolone methyl observed in systemic circulation may not directly translate to effective intraocular concentrations. Development of specialized ocular formulations—such as sustained-release intravitreal injections or topical applications that can traverse ocular barriers—will be critical for successful translation (Nakagami, 2016; Thomas, 2012).

4. Multifactorial Nature of AMD Pathogenesis:  
Dry AMD is a complex, multifactorial disease where oxidative stress is only one component of a broader pathogenic network that includes genetic predisposition, inflammation, lipid dysregulation, and complement system activation. While activation of the Nrf2 pathway can counteract oxidative stress and may have secondary anti-inflammatory effects, it is uncertain whether this single mechanism will be sufficient to alter the course of the disease. A combinatorial therapeutic strategy incorporating Nrf2 activation with other modalities might eventually be required to address the full spectrum of dry AMD pathology (Cheng et al., 2021; Thomas, 2012).

5. Dose-Dependent Effects and Narrow Therapeutic Window:  
Bardoxolone methyl exhibits highly dose-dependent pharmacology. At low doses, it predominantly activates Nrf2 and induces cytoprotective genes; however, as the dose increases, additional actions such as pro-apoptotic and cytotoxic effects emerge (Borella et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2014). Determining the precise dose that is efficacious in RPE cells without triggering deleterious off-target effects will be crucial. This necessitates rigorous dose-ranging studies in preclinical models to define a therapeutic window that maximizes retinal protection while minimizing risks.

• Overall Evaluation and Recommendation  
In summary, Bardoxolone methyl presents a compelling candidate for repurposing in the treatment of dry AMD thanks to its well-characterized chemical design, potent induction of the Nrf2 pathway, and a broad cytoprotective mechanism that addresses many aspects of the oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction implicated in RPE degeneration. The extensive clinical data from studies in CKD and oncology provide a valuable foundation for understanding its pharmacokinetics and systemic effects, while the underlying chemistry ensures high potency at low concentrations—features that are highly desirable when targeting a chronic, degenerative condition such as dry AMD (Borella et al., 2019; Thomas, 2012).  
Nonetheless, significant challenges remain. There is a conspicuous gap in the literature regarding the direct evaluation of Bardoxolone methyl in RPE cells and dry AMD models, particularly concerning its ability to upregulate mitochondrial antioxidant enzymes like SOD2 and PRDX3 and thereby mitigate mtROS damage. This gap must be addressed through targeted in vitro assays and in vivo animal studies that simulate the retinal environment and its chronic oxidative stress. Safety concerns, as evidenced by the cardiovascular events observed in CKD trials, underscore the need for caution; these risks may be minimized through localized ocular administration, but careful formulation and dosing studies will be required to establish an acceptable safety profile in the ocular context (Tanase et al., 2022; Cuadrado et al., 2019).  
Given the current state of evidence, it is recommended that Bardoxolone methyl be subjected to further preclinical evaluation in the context of dry AMD. Specifically, studies should focus on:  
 – Conducting detailed in vitro experiments in human RPE cell lines to assess whether exposure to Bardoxolone methyl under oxidative stress conditions leads to the upregulation of mitochondrial antioxidants (e.g., SOD2 and PRDX3) and an associated decrease in mtROS (Zhou et al., 2014; Nakagami et al., 2015).  
 – Evaluating the effects of Bardoxolone methyl on mitochondrial function (including measurements of Δψm, oxygen consumption rate, and mitochondrial integrity) in these cells.  
 – Adapting ocular-specific formulations that allow high local retinal concentrations with minimal systemic exposure, potentially using intravitreal or topical delivery methods, and conducting pharmacokinetic studies that establish retinal tissue exposure (Nakagami, 2016; Thomas, 2012).  
 – Testing the compound in animal models of dry AMD to determine whether the in vitro antioxidant and mitochondrial protective effects translate into preservation of RPE and photoreceptor structure and function over time (Cheng et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2024).  
If these studies yield positive results that confirm the hypothesized upregulation of mitochondrial antioxidants and improved RPE function, Bardoxolone methyl could then be advanced to early-phase clinical trials in patients with dry AMD. Such trials would need to incorporate rigorous safety monitoring protocols to mitigate the known systemic adverse effects. A potential strategy may involve administering the drug in a localized manner or at doses that are effective in the eye but low enough to avoid triggering systemic electrophilic toxicity.

Overall, Bardoxolone methyl holds promise as a repurposed therapeutic for dry AMD based on its potent Nrf2-activating properties and the central role of oxidative stress in the disease’s pathogenesis. However, its development for an ocular indication will hinge on its ability to selectively and safely modulate the redox status of RPE cells while avoiding systemic side effects. A stepwise, targeted preclinical program that directly addresses these RPE-specific endpoints is therefore critical before consideration of clinical trials in the dry AMD patient population (Nakagami, 2016).

In conclusion, while Bardoxolone methyl offers a strong mechanistic rationale as an Nrf2 activator with the theoretical capacity to upregulate mitochondrial defenses in RPE cells, further targeted studies are essential. The compound’s repurposing for dry AMD is scientifically attractive given the unmet need for effective therapies in this condition; however, the translational path is complex due to safety concerns observed in systemic trials and the lack of direct ocular data. A rigorous preclinical research plan—encompassing in vitro RPE studies, ocular pharmacokinetic assessments, and animal model validation—will be required to determine whether Bardoxolone methyl can be safely and effectively repurposed to preserve RPE function and slow the progression of dry AMD.

• Summary of Key Supporting Points  
– Bardoxolone methyl is a chemically engineered derivative of oleanolic acid that exploits electrophilic modifications to robustly activate the Nrf2 pathway (Borella et al., 2019; Wong et al., 2016).  
– Its extensive preclinical and clinical history in oncology and CKD, though not in ocular disease, provides important pharmacokinetic and biological safety data that may be leveraged, with modifications, for ocular use (Nakagami, 2016; Tanase et al., 2022).  
– Its mechanism—covalent modification of Keap1 and subsequent nuclear translocation of Nrf2—leads to the transcriptional upregulation of cytoprotective genes, and it is hypothesized that this would include mitochondrial antioxidants (e.g., SOD2, PRDX3), which are critical for mitigating oxidative damage in RPE cells (Zhou et al., 2014; Nakagami et al., 2015).  
– Preclinical data with related triterpenoids in retinal models support the concept that Nrf2 activation can ameliorate oxidative stress and preserve retinal integrity, although direct data for Bardoxolone methyl in dry AMD are presently lacking (Cheng et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2024).  
– Key challenges include the translation of systemic dosing to ocular delivery, the need to specifically validate mitochondrial protective effects in RPE cells, and the resolution of safety issues exemplified by prior cardiovascular signals in systemic trials (Tanase et al., 2022; Cuadrado et al., 2019).

• Final Recommendation  
Based on the current literature review, Bardoxolone methyl emerges as a promising therapeutic candidate for dry AMD due to its potent Nrf2 activation, broad cytoprotective potential, and a well-documented clinical PK profile. However, its repurposing for ocular application must be preceded by targeted preclinical studies that corroborate its ability to upregulate mitochondrial antioxidants in RPE cells and to reduce mtROS while preserving mitochondrial function. These studies should also explore ocular-specific formulations and dosing regimens that minimize systemic exposure. Only after thorough validation in these preliminary investigations can Bardoxolone methyl be considered a credible candidate for clinical trials in dry AMD patients. Given these considerations, the candidate warrants further investigation despite the existing translational and safety challenges inherent in repurposing a systemically active electrophilic compound for an ocular indication (Borella et al., 2019; Yagishita et al., 2020; Thomas, 2012).
