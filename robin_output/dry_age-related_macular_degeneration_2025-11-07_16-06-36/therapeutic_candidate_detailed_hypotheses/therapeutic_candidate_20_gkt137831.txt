Proposal for GKT137831 (Setanaxib; MedChemExpress HY-108921)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
GKT137831, also known as Setanaxib, is a synthetic small‐molecule drug originally discovered and optimized for its ability to inhibit NADPH oxidase activity, with specificity primarily toward the NOX1 and NOX4 isoforms. It belongs to the dihydropyrazolone chemical class, sharing structural similarity with the free radical scavenger edaravone, and has been further refined from earlier analogues such as GKT136901 through structure–activity relationship studies to improve its potency, selectivity, and oral bioavailability (Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 26–30; Cai et al., 2024, pp. 8–10). The compound was originally developed by Genkyotex as a dual NOX1/NOX4 inhibitor; however, it possesses additional but lower potency activity against NOX2 and even shows some partial inhibition of NOX5 in certain settings (Elbatreek et al., 2020, pp. 12–14; Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 26–30). The initial discovery was based on high-throughput screening methods that targeted NADPH oxidase-mediated reactive oxygen species (ROS) production—a process that has been linked to multiple pathological conditions including fibrotic, vascular, and inflammatory diseases. As a representative of its class, GKT137831 exemplifies a modern approach to drug design wherein inhibition of the primary enzymatic source of ROS is favored over conventional antioxidant strategies, which often suffer from non-specificity and poor clinical translation (Cai et al., 2024, pp. 8–10; Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 21–26).

Therapeutic History:  
GKT137831 has a distinguished preclinical and clinical history rooted in its application to diseases characterized by oxidative stress and inflammation. In preclinical models, it has been shown to significantly reduce the generation of ROS and the downstream activation of pro-inflammatory signaling. In primary cultured human retinal endothelial cells, for instance, treatment with GKT137831 reduced both ROS generation and VEGFA expression, indicating its potential utility in limiting vascular dysfunction in ocular diseases (Cai et al., 2024, pp. 8–10). Beyond ocular applications, the compound has been evaluated in several preclinical models of fibrotic and vascular pathologies including diabetic kidney disease, hypertensive cardiac remodeling, and liver fibrosis, wherein its administration lowered the levels of ROS, inflammatory cytokines, and markers of tissue injury (Elbatreek et al., 2020, pp. 12–14; Cipriano et al., 2023, pp. 22–23). Clinically, GKT137831 has advanced to Phase II trials in diabetic nephropathy and primary biliary cholangitis, demonstrating both safety and acceptable pharmacokinetic properties in human subjects (Elbatreek et al., 2020, pp. 12–14; Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 21–26; Buglak et al., 2018, Art. 2468457). Although it has not been tested directly for dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) in clinical trials, several preclinical studies using models of ischemic retinopathy and diabetic retinopathy have provided supportive evidence that NOX inhibition may attenuate oxidative stress and inflammation in retinal tissues (Cai et al., 2024, pp. 8–10; Gaskin et al., 2021, pp. 15–17). The repurposing rationale for dry AMD hinges on the observation that oxidative stress plays a central role in the degeneration of retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells and the subsequent progression of AMD—pathophysiological processes that have been effectively modulated by GKT137831 in other experimental disease models (Ruan et al., 2021, pp. 10–11; Kim et al., 2021, pp. 1–7).

Mechanism of Action:  
From a molecular perspective, GKT137831 has been characterized as a dual inhibitor of NOX1 and NOX4, enzymes that catalyze the transfer of electrons from NADPH to molecular oxygen and thereby generate superoxide or hydrogen peroxide, respectively (Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 26–30; Elbatreek et al., 2020, pp. 4–6). Unlike classical antioxidants that scavenge ROS after they are generated, GKT137831 aims to prevent ROS formation at its source by directly inhibiting NOX enzyme activity. One study demonstrates that it can bind to the extracellular region of the catalytic subunit of NOX enzymes in an allosteric manner, distinct from the NADPH binding site, which leads to a decrease in NADPH-dependent superoxide generation and hydrogen peroxide production (Gorin et al., 2015, pp. 16–20). In addition to its enzyme inhibitory actions, the dihydropyrazolone scaffold confers an inherent ROS scavenging property, enabling the compound to neutralize peroxynitrite and hydrogen peroxide through redox-mediated chemical mechanisms (Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 26–30; Gaskin et al., 2021, pp. 8–9). This dual property—direct enzyme inhibition coupled with ROS scavenging—is theorized to offer a more comprehensive approach to modulating oxidative stress in target tissues. However, there are conflicting reports in the literature regarding the specificity of its NOX inhibition, with some studies suggesting that measured low Ki values may be artifactual due to interference with assay components such as horseradish peroxidase, raising questions about whether the observed effects are entirely attributable to NOX inhibition or, in part, to non-specific redox modulation (Vincent, 2021, pp. 79–80; Demircan et al., 2022, pp. 12–13). Notwithstanding these concerns, the prevailing hypothesis remains that by dampening NOX activity, GKT137831 reduces both cytosolic and mitochondrial ROS. In the context of RPE cells, which are rich in mitochondria and subject to high oxidative loads due to constant light exposure and high metabolic activity, the inhibition of NOX4 is expected to be particularly beneficial for mitigating oxidative damage to the electron transport chain (ETC) complexes and preserving the mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψm) (Cai et al., 2024, pp. 8–10; Kim et al., 2021, pp. 24–28).

Expected Effect:  
The central hypothesis underpinning the repurposing of GKT137831 for the treatment of dry AMD is that its inhibition of NOX4—and, by extension, NOX1—leads to a substantial reduction in the generation of reactive oxygen species within the retinal pigment epithelium. Oxidative stress in RPE cells is a key driver of mitochondrial dysfunction, particularly impairment of electron transport chain complexes, which in turn leads to decreased cellular energy production and the eventual onset of apoptosis. By pharmacologically reducing the burden of ROS, GKT137831 is expected to prevent the oxidative damage to mitochondrial ETC components, thereby preserving the mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψm) and ensuring continued ATP production and cellular viability under conditions of oxidative stress (Cai et al., 2024, pp. 8–10; Kim et al., 2021, pp. 24–28). In vitro assays utilizing RPE cells exposed to high-glucose or other oxidative stress conditions have shown that treatment with GKT137831 can dampen ROS levels, reduce caspase-3 activity (a marker of apoptosis), and decrease the expression of inflammatory molecules (Cai et al., 2024, pp. 8–10). Given that the pathogenesis of dry AMD involves chronic, low-grade oxidative damage and inflammation leading to RPE degeneration, the expected therapeutic effect of GKT137831 would be to attenuate these processes at a cellular level, thereby slowing or halting disease progression. Moreover, the expression profile of NOX enzymes—particularly NOX4 in RPE cells—provides a sound molecular basis for this approach, with several studies documenting increased NOX activity under conditions that mimic AMD pathogenesis (Ruan et al., 2021, pp. 10–11; Kulbay et al., 2024, pp. 2–5). Furthermore, the oral formulation of GKT137831, validated in other disease contexts, offers a practical advantage because it facilitates long-term administration, which is essential for a chronic condition like dry AMD where sustained drug exposure is necessary to counteract cumulative oxidative damage (Elbatreek et al., 2020, pp. 12–14; Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 21–26).

Overall Evaluation:  
In evaluating the potential of GKT137831 as a repurposed therapeutic for dry AMD, several strengths and weaknesses emerge. Among its major strengths is the robust preclinical evidence that supports the concept of targeting NOX enzymes to mitigate oxidative stress. The dual mode of action—combining direct enzyme inhibition with ROS scavenging—provides a theoretically advantageous approach to protecting RPE cells from both cytosolic and mitochondrial oxidative insults (Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 26–30; Gaskin et al., 2021, pp. 8–9). This is particularly pertinent in dry AMD, a disease where oxidative stress is a driving force behind RPE dysfunction, drusen formation, and subsequent photoreceptor degeneration (Ruan et al., 2021, pp. 10–11; Kim et al., 2021, pp. 1–7). Furthermore, GKT137831’s progression into Phase II clinical trials for other indications such as diabetic nephropathy and primary biliary cholangitis demonstrates that the compound is orally bioavailable and has an acceptable safety profile in human subjects (Elbatreek et al., 2020, pp. 12–14; Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 21–26; Buglak et al., 2018, Art. 2468457). This reduces the developmental risk when considering repurposing for dry AMD since chronic administration is feasible and the regulatory pathway may be accelerated based on existing human data.

On the other hand, significant concerns related to the specificity of GKT137831’s mechanism remain. Conflicting evidence suggests that some of the in vitro inhibitory effects attributed to the compound may be partly due to assay artifacts or non-specific redox-modulating activities rather than bona fide NOX inhibition (Vincent, 2021, pp. 79–80; Demircan et al., 2022, pp. 12–13). These findings raise critical questions regarding the extent to which the protective effects observed in retinal and other disease models are directly mediated by diminution of NOX-derived ROS. Additionally, while preclinical studies in diabetic and ischemic retinopathy models have provided promising results, there is a paucity of data directly linking GKT137831 to improved outcomes in dry AMD specifically. The absence of studies evaluating blood–retinal barrier permeability, retinal tissue pharmacokinetics, and direct effects on RPE mitochondrial function in the context of AMD remains a notable gap (Cai et al., 2024, pp. 8–10; Gaskin et al., 2021, pp. 15–17). Moreover, some reports indicate that the antiproliferative or cytoprotective effects observed with GKT137831 are independent of NOX1/4 expression, suggesting that its mechanism of action in certain cell types may be more complex than initially postulated (Demircan et al., 2022, pp. 2–3, 12–13).

Given these considerations, the overall evaluation is cautiously optimistic. GKT137831, with its favorable oral bioavailability and established safety profile from Phase II clinical trials in non-ocular indications, represents a promising candidate for repurposing given the central role of oxidative stress and NOX-mediated ROS production in dry AMD. Its capacity to reduce ROS levels, modulate inflammatory responses, and potentially preserve mitochondrial function in RPE cells offers a compelling therapeutic rationale (Ruan et al., 2021, pp. 10–11; Kim et al., 2021, pp. 1–7; Shi, 2024, pp. 21–24).  

Furthermore, detailed mechanistic studies employing genetic knockdown approaches in RPE cells, coupled with advanced imaging techniques to monitor mitochondrial dynamics, would be critical to discern whether the expected effects are indeed mediated via NOX inhibition or through alternative, non-NOX-related redox pathways (Demircan et al., 2022, pp. 2–3, 12–13). Given that some reports have reported paradoxical increases in ROS in other cell types treated with GKT137831, it is imperative to understand its cell-type-specific and context-dependent effects to mitigate unintended pro-oxidant consequences in the delicate retinal environment (Demircan et al., 2022, p. 13; Vincent, 2021, pp. 13–17).

In summary, the strengths of GKT137831 lie in its dual functional profile as both a NOX inhibitor and an ROS scavenger, its documented efficacy in preclinical models of oxidative stress-induced pathologies, and its demonstrated oral bioavailability with favorable safety outcomes in human trials for other indications (Elbatreek et al., 2020, pp. 12–14; Buglak et al., 2018, Art. 2468457; Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 21–26). These attributes strongly support the rationale for its repurposing in dry AMD, where oxidative stress is a key pathogenic driver. Nonetheless, weaknesses including unresolved mechanistic ambiguities, potential off-target effects, and limited direct evidence in AMD-relevant models call for rigorous additional research before clinical translation can be confidently pursued (Vincent, 2021, pp. 79–80; Demircan et al., 2022, pp. 2–3, 12–13). Overall, while there is considerable promise in targeting NOX-mediated oxidative stress in the RPE with a compound like GKT137831, the current evidence base necessitates further specific validation within the context of dry AMD. Such an approach would ideally involve a series of well-designed in vitro and in vivo experiments to ascertain pharmacodynamics, tissue distribution within ocular compartments, and ultimately, the functional outcome measures relevant to RPE health and retinal integrity under conditions that closely mimic the human disease state (Cai et al., 2024, pp. 8–10; Gaskin et al., 2021, pp. 15–17; Ruan et al., 2021, pp. 10–11).  

The next steps for our research team should include initiating experiments using cultured RPE cell lines exposed to oxidative stress paradigms (e.g., high-glucose, light-induced stress) to determine whether GKT137831 effectively preserves mitochondrial function and decreases apoptotic markers. Parallel studies in animal models of dry AMD, particularly those that recapitulate key features such as drusen formation, RPE degeneration, and subsequent photoreceptor loss, will be essential to elucidate the in vivo efficacy of the compound (Ruan et al., 2021, pp. 10–11; Kim et al., 2021, pp. 24–28). Ultimately, while the translational potential is high given its clinical safety record and oral formulation, the mechanistic uncertainties must be thoroughly addressed to confidently advocate for clinical trials in dry AMD patients. In conclusion, GKT137831 remains an attractive candidate for repurposing in dry AMD, provided that further targeted research can unequivocally demonstrate its beneficial effects on RPE mitochondrial health and overall retinal preservation (Elbatreek et al., 2020, pp. 12–14; Sassetti et al., 2021, pp. 21–26; Ruan et al., 2021, pp. 10–11).
