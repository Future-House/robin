Proposal for Mitochonic Acid-5 (MA-5; MedChemExpress HY-104308)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
Mitochonic Acid-5 (MA-5; MedChemExpress HY-104308) is a synthetic, mitochondria‐targeted small molecule originally discovered for its ability to improve mitochondrial function in models of cellular aging. MA-5 belongs to the class of mitochondrial modulators that have been shown to enhance adenosine triphosphate (ATP) production, reduce mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mtROS), and extend lifespan in non‐ocular systems such as fibroblasts and Caenorhabditis elegans. The current hypothesis proposes that MA-5 binds directly to mitofilin (also known as Mic60), a key scaffolding protein in the inner mitochondrial membrane that is essential for the proper organization and stability of mitochondrial cristae. This interaction is anticipated to stabilize cristae architecture, thereby preserving optimal assembly of the electron transport chain (ETC) complexes and enabling improved mitochondrial bioenergetics—a mechanism particularly appealing in the context of dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD), where cristae disorganization and mtROS accumulation in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells have been associated with disease pathology (Ji et al., 2021; Ferrington et al., 2020).

Therapeutic History:  
While MA-5 itself has not been previously evaluated in ocular diseases, compounds that modulate mitochondrial function have a long history in preclinical studies for age-related conditions. Several mitochondrial-targeted agents—for example, SkQ1 and elamipretide—have demonstrated promise in ameliorating mitochondrial defects in RPE models and in early clinical trials targeting dry AMD (Ji et al., 2021; Tong et al., 2022). In other systems, MA-5 has been reported to improve ATP production, reduce mtROS, and activate the mitochondrial unfolded protein response (UPRmt) in fibroblast and C. elegans models. Such beneficial effects on mitochondrial function have contributed to the rationale for repurposing MA-5 for dry AMD. Moreover, research in related domains suggests that stabilizing mitochondrial cristae via targeting scaffold proteins such as mitofilin is mechanistically sound given observations of altered mitofilin expression and cristae disruption in AMD RPE (Ferrington et al., 2020; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021). However, direct usage of MA-5 in retinal disorders remains unexplored, and its potential has yet to be validated in RPE-specific in vitro or in vivo models.

Mechanism of Action:  
At the molecular level, MA-5 is proposed to exert its beneficial effects by physically interacting with mitofilin/Mic60, a protein integral to the maintenance of inner mitochondrial membrane architecture and cristae organization. Mitofilin is recognized for its role in stabilizing the cristae junctions and in facilitating the proper assembly and function of the ETC complexes (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Ferrington et al., 2020). In dry AMD, mitochondrial cristae loss has been documented in RPE cells, suggesting that disrupted mitofilin function may underlie some of the bioenergetic deficiencies seen in the disease (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Ferrington et al., 2020). By binding to mitofilin, MA-5 is expected to reinforce cristae integrity; this stabilization can promote the efficient assembly of ETC complexes, thereby leading to improved oxidative phosphorylation and ATP production. Concurrently, enhanced cristae organization is likely to reduce electron leakage from the ETC and lower the production of reactive oxygen species, which in excess contribute to oxidative damage and RPE cell death (Ji et al., 2021; Tong et al., 2022).

In addition, emerging evidence suggests that perturbations in mitochondrial structure can trigger the mitochondrial unfolded protein response (UPRmt), a conserved stress-response pathway that upregulates mitochondrial chaperones such as mtHsp70 and other proteostasis factors to cope with misfolded proteins and oxidative damage. MA-5 is hypothesized not only to stabilize the physical architecture of the mitochondria but also to activate UPRmt pathways, thereby enhancing the endogenous capacity of RPE cells to detoxify ROS and repair mitochondrial damage. Activation of UPRmt could lead to increased expression of mitochondrial chaperones, improved clearance of damaged proteins, and a restoration of redox balance within the mitochondria. This dual mechanism—direct structural stabilization via mitofilin binding and indirect activation of UPRmt—positions MA-5 as a potentially powerful agent for correcting mitochondrial dysfunction in dry AMD (Tong et al., 2022; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).

Expected Effect:  
If MA-5 performs as hypothesized in RPE cells, several beneficial effects are anticipated. First, by binding mitofilin and stabilizing cristae architecture, MA-5 should restore proper assembly and function of ETC complexes leading to increased ATP production. Such a recovery in mitochondrial bioenergetics is particularly critical in RPE cells, given their high energy demands for processes such as phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments and maintenance of the visual cycle (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Tong et al., 2022). Second, the stabilization of cristae, alongside the potential activation of the UPRmt, is expected to reduce mtROS production by minimizing electron leakage from complex I and III of the ETC. Lower ROS levels would alleviate oxidative stress—a known driver of RPE degeneration and dry AMD progression that has been documented in multiple studies (Ji et al., 2021; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021). Third, the activation of UPRmt may upregulate key mitochondrial chaperones such as mtHsp70, facilitating improved protein folding, enhanced clearance of damaged proteins, and overall better maintenance of mitochondrial homeostasis. These cellular changes should collectively promote RPE survival and function, thus potentially slowing or even reversing some aspects of the cellular degenerative processes seen in dry AMD (Ji et al., 2021; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).

Furthermore, since mitofilin/Mic60 is an abundantly expressed and essential component in RPE mitochondria—as evidenced by its upregulation in early AMD patient samples—the MA-5-mediated stabilization of this protein is expected to yield measurable improvements in mitochondrial parameters in cellular assays. Such improvements would include increased mitochondrial membrane potential, enhanced basal and maximal respiration, and a reduction in apoptotic markers associated with mitochondrial damage. Ultimately, if these in vitro effects translate into in vivo efficacy, MA-5 could contribute to a novel treatment modality that directly addresses the underlying mitochondrial dysfunction in dry AMD (Ferrington et al., 2020).

Overall Evaluation:  
The therapeutic candidate MA-5 presents a highly promising, mechanistically driven approach to addressing dry AMD by targeting mitochondrial dysfunction—a central hallmark of the disease. Its proposed dual mechanism of binding to mitofilin to stabilize cristae architecture and activating the UPRmt to enhance antioxidant defenses directly aligns with the documented mitochondrial pathology in RPE cells. Strengths of this candidate include the following:  
– The strategy of targeting mitofilin is supported by numerous observations that indicate altered mitofilin levels and cristae disorganization in AMD, suggesting that restoring cristae integrity may ameliorate mitochondrial deficits (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Ferrington et al., 2020).  
– MA-5’s documented capacity to improve ATP production and reduce mtROS in non-ocular models indicates a favorable mitochondrial modulation profile that is likely transferable to RPE cells, where energy production and redox homeostasis are critical (Ji et al., 2021; Tong et al., 2022).  
– Activation of UPRmt represents an innovative therapeutic angle that not only protects mitochondrial structure but also boosts cellular defenses against oxidative stress—a pathway that has been implicated in maintaining RPE cell viability under conditions of sustained stress (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Tong et al., 2022).  

Despite these promising aspects, several points of caution must be noted. First, while the beneficial effects of MA-5 have been demonstrated in fibroblast and C. elegans models, no direct data exist regarding its efficacy in RPE cells or in animal models of dry AMD. The translation of effects from non-ocular systems to the highly specialized RPE must be rigorously evaluated. Second, the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of MA-5 in the context of ocular delivery are unknown at this time; the compound’s ability to achieve sufficient retinal concentration, while minimizing systemic toxicity, remains an open question. Third, the precise binding affinity and specificity of MA-5 for mitofilin in RPE mitochondria have not yet been characterized. Off-target effects or unintended modulation of mitochondrial dynamics may lead to adverse outcomes if the drug’s action is not adequately localized within the retina. Finally, activation of UPRmt, while beneficial in the context of mitochondrial stress, may also have complex downstream effects that require careful titration to avoid potential maladaptive consequences in long-term treatment scenarios (Buonfiglio et al., 2024; Ferrington et al., 2020).

In summary, the proposed mechanism for MA-5 is conceptually attractive given the wealth of literature linking mitochondrial cristae disruption and excessive ROS production to dry AMD pathology. The candidate’s potential to bind mitofilin, stabilize mitochondrial structure, and activate a protective UPRmt aligns well with the current understanding of mitochondrial deficits in RPE cells observed in AMD (Ji et al., 2021; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021). However, significant preclinical work is needed to validate its efficacy specifically in the RPE environment. Studies should include detailed binding assays to confirm the interaction between MA-5 and mitofilin, evaluation of mitochondrial bioenergetics and redox parameters in RPE cell cultures, and ultimately, assessments in animal models of dry AMD that recapitulate key aspects of mitochondrial dysfunction. Provided these experimental hurdles can be overcome and favorable pharmacokinetic profiles established for ocular delivery, MA-5 may represent a novel and promising candidate for repurposing in the treatment of dry AMD (Nashine, 2021; Tong et al., 2022).

Overall, while there is robust mechanistic rationale and encouraging preclinical evidence from other models supporting the concept of mitochondrial stabilization via mitofilin targeting, the transition of MA-5 into the dry AMD arena is contingent upon a series of critical validation steps in relevant ocular models. The strengths of MA-5 lie in its potential to address the upstream causes of RPE degeneration by restoring mitochondrial integrity and function, which is a major unmet need in dry AMD therapeutics. The weaknesses and uncertainties, however, underscore the need for a focused series of seriatim experiments to fully characterize its effects in RPE cells and to develop suitable formulations for ocular administration. Based on the current evidence drawn from mitochondrial pathology in AMD and the functional roles of mitofilin/Mic60 in maintaining cristae integrity, MA-5 merits further investigation as a repurposed candidate for dry AMD, with the caveat that extensive preclinical testing is required before clinical translation can be considered (Ji et al., 2021; Ferrington et al., 2020; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Tong et al., 2022).
