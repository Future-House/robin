Proposal for Mdivi-1 (Sigma-Aldrich M0199)

1. Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Mdivi-1, known as mitochondrial division inhibitor 1 (catalog number Sigma-Aldrich M0199), is a synthetic small-molecule compound originally developed for research into mitochondrial dynamics. It belongs to a class of therapeutic agents that modulate mitochondrial fission by targeting the dynamin‐related protein 1 (DRP1). DRP1 is an essential GTPase responsible for driving mitochondrial fission, a process that maintains mitochondrial quality control but, when overactivated, can lead to fragmentation associated with cellular stress and apoptosis. Mdivi-1 was discovered via compound screening experiments aimed at identifying inhibitors of DRP1’s GTPase activity and has been characterized extensively in various in vitro and in vivo studies. Its discovery was motivated by the need to therapeutically intervene in pathologies where aberrant mitochondrial fission contributes to cellular dysfunction. As a representative of DRP1 inhibitory agents, Mdivi-1 has been widely used as an investigational tool in studies related to neurodegeneration, ischemia, and ocular diseases, where mitochondrial integrity is a key determinant of cell survival (Cueva-Vargas et al., 2025; Rong et al., 2020). Its synthetic origin and well-documented pharmacological profile render it a candidate for drug repurposing, with potential applications extending into the treatment of dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) by addressing mitochondrial dysfunction in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells.

2. Therapeutic History  
Mdivi-1 has a robust preclinical research history across a variety of models that involve oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction. Initial studies demonstrated that in retinal endothelial cells and neuronal tissues, inhibition of DRP1 by Mdivi-1 prevented excessive mitochondrial fragmentation under conditions of acute stress. In models of glaucoma, for instance, DRP1 overactivation associated with ocular hypertension leads to mitochondrial fragmentation and retinal ganglion cell death, and administration of Mdivi-1 was found to rescue these cells by preserving mitochondrial network integrity (Kim et al., 2015; Ji et al., 2021). Similarly, in photoreceptor cells following retinal detachment, Mdivi-1 treatment reduced the activation of apoptotic pathways—through downregulation of cleaved Caspase-3 and Bax—and consequently preserved cell morphology and function, indicating a protective effect mediated through the inhibition of DRP1-dependent fission (She et al., 2018). Furthermore, in ARPE-19 cells, which are commonly used as a model for RPE behavior, Mdivi-1 has been shown to mitigate the cytotoxic effects of oxidative stress induced by hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) by reducing mitochondrial fragmentation and cytochrome c release, thereby improving mitochondrial membrane potential and ATP production (Yako et al., 2021). Although Mdivi-1 has not yet been evaluated in large-scale clinical trials for dry AMD specifically, its use in diverse ocular models—ranging from diabetic retinopathy to retinal ischemia—provides compelling evidence that its mechanism could be beneficial in conditions marked by oxidative stress and RPE dysfunction, two hallmark features of dry AMD (Yako et al., 2021). These cumulative findings support the repurposing hypothesis that targeting DRP1-mediated mitochondrial fission could restore mitochondrial function and survival in RPE cells, thereby halting or slowing the degenerative cascade observed in dry AMD.

3. Mechanism of Action  
At the molecular level, DRP1 is responsible for mediating mitochondrial fission through the constriction and subsequent division of mitochondrial membranes. Upon receiving various stress signals, DRP1 is recruited from the cytosol to the mitochondrial outer membrane where it oligomerizes and hydrolyzes GTP, driving membrane scission. Mdivi-1 specifically inhibits the GTPase activity of DRP1, thereby preventing its self-assembly and subsequent mitochondrial recruitment. This inhibition effectively blocks the fission process that is typically upregulated under conditions of oxidative stress and other cellular insults (Cueva-Vargas et al., 2025; Rong et al., 2020). Detailed biochemical investigations have demonstrated that when DRP1 activity is unopposed, mitochondrial fragmentation ensues, leading to diminished mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, decreased oxygen consumption rate (OCR), reduced ATP synthesis, and an increase in the generation of mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mtROS) (Yako et al., 2021). Mdivi-1 counters these deleterious cellular events by preserving the integrity of the mitochondrial network, enabling enhanced ATP production, and maintaining the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP). Moreover, by inhibiting DRP1, Mdivi-1 reduces the stress-induced release of cytochrome c from the mitochondria—a central step in the intrinsic apoptotic pathway—thus lowering the activation of downstream caspases (She et al., 2018; Yako et al., 2021). In addition to these primary actions, some studies have noted that Mdivi-1 has reversible inhibitory effects on mitochondrial complex I, suggesting that its impact on mitochondrial bioenergetics may be multifaceted (Yako et al., 2021). Such dual actions may serve to further stabilize mitochondrial function under stress.

4. Expected Effect  
Under conditions relevant to dry age-related macular degeneration, such as chronic oxidative stress incident on RPE cells, Mdivi-1 is anticipated to exert a multi-pronged cytoprotective effect. In the proposed assay using H₂O₂-treated ARPE-19 cells—a well-established in vitro model to mimic the oxidative stress environment of dry AMD—the primary expected effect is the inhibition of DRP1-mediated mitochondrial fission (Yako et al., 2021). By stabilizing mitochondrial dynamics, Mdivi-1 should also improve cellular bioenergetics through enhanced OCR and increased ATP production, both critical parameters that are typically compromised in stressed RPE cells (Yako et al., 2021). The anticipated outcome of these events is a reduction in cell death and apoptosis, as well as improved phagocytic function—an essential role of RPE cells in the maintenance of retinal homeostasis. Furthermore, by mitigating the accumulation of mtROS, Mdivi-1 is expected to lessen oxidative stress, thereby curbing the degenerative processes that are a central component of dry AMD pathophysiology (Yako et al., 2021; Cueva-Vargas et al., 2025). In essence, the mechanistic intervention afforded by Mdivi-1 aims to interrupt the cycle of mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative damage that leads to RPE cell apoptosis, which is a critical driver of disease progression in dry AMD (Yako et al., 2021).

5. Overall Evaluation  
In evaluating Mdivi-1 as a potential therapeutic candidate for dry age-related macular degeneration, several strengths and weaknesses emerge from the current body of literature. Among its strengths, Mdivi-1 is underpinned by a strong mechanistic rationale: the inhibition of DRP1-mediated mitochondrial fission is a well-validated approach to restore mitochondrial function in the face of oxidative stress. Preclinical studies have consistently demonstrated that Mdivi-1 preserves mitochondrial network integrity, reduces the production of harmful reactive oxygen species, and prevents the release of apoptogenic factors, thereby facilitating cell survival in retinal models (Cueva-Vargas et al., 2025; Rong et al., 2020; Yako et al., 2021). Its efficacy in multiple ocular contexts—including models of glaucoma, retinal detachment, and diabetic retinopathy—highlights the versatility of its cytoprotective actions, which may be directly relevant to the RPE dysfunction and oxidative injury observed in dry AMD (Kim et al., 2015; She et al., 2018). From a biochemical standpoint, the ability of Mdivi-1 to enhance mitochondrial bioenergetics by improving OCR and ATP production, while simultaneously preserving mitochondrial membrane potential, addresses essential aspects of RPE cell metabolism that are often impaired in dry AMD (Yako et al., 2021). Furthermore, the compound’s capacity to reduce apoptotic signaling, as evidenced by decreased cytochrome c release and lower caspase activity, is particularly promising given the established link between mitochondrial-mediated apoptosis and RPE cell loss in dry AMD (Yako et al., 2021). Despite these promising attributes, several limitations and challenges deserve consideration. First, while the preclinical evidence supporting Mdivi-1’s efficacy is compelling, there remains a notable gap in clinical data, particularly in the context of dry AMD (Yako et al., 2021). Another potential weakness arises from the dual inhibitory effects of Mdivi-1. Some investigations have reported that Mdivi-1 also exhibits reversible inhibition of mitochondrial complex I, which could complicate its therapeutic profile by inadvertently affecting normal mitochondrial respiration (Yako et al., 2021). Moreover, the chronic inhibition of DRP1 raises important questions about the broader physiological implications of interfering with mitochondrial dynamics (Mohammad & Kowluru, 2022; She et al., 2018). Furthermore, the in vitro models currently used, predominantly involving H₂O₂-treated ARPE-19 cells, may not entirely mimic the in vivo microenvironment of the human retina in dry AMD (Yako et al., 2021). The overall therapeutic window and safety profile of Mdivi-1 also require further elucidation (Yako et al., 2021). Despite these challenges, the mechanistic insights generated from a diverse array of experimental models suggest that Mdivi-1 holds considerable promise as a repurposed therapeutic candidate for dry AMD. Its capacity to restore mitochondrial integrity, mitigate oxidative stress, and inhibit apoptotic cascades directly addresses several of the pathogenic mechanisms implicated in dry AMD. Given that current treatments for dry AMD are limited and mainly supportive, a disease-modifying therapy that targets mitochondrial dysfunction would represent a significant advancement in the management of this debilitating disorder (Cueva-Vargas et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2015). In summary, Mdivi-1’s strengths lie in its well-characterized mechanism of action, demonstrated efficacy in preserving mitochondrial dynamics under stress, and its potential to enhance cell survival in models of retinal degeneration (Cueva-Vargas et al., 2025; Ji et al., 2021; Kim et al., 2015; Yako et al., 2021).

Future studies must address the nuances of chronic DRP1 inhibition, optimize dosing regimens, evaluate long-term safety, and conduct thorough pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profiling in ocular tissues to fully realize the potential of Mdivi-1 in the clinical management of dry AMD (Buonfiglio et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025; Rong et al., 2022; Yako et al., 2021).

In conclusion, the evidence provided by numerous preclinical studies strongly indicates that Mdivi-1’s inhibition of DRP1-mediated mitochondrial fission offers a promising therapeutic strategy for dry age-related macular degeneration. The compound’s ability to maintain mitochondrial network integrity, enhance bioenergetics, and suppress apoptotic cascades under conditions of oxidative stress in RPE cells is particularly relevant to the pathophysiology of dry AMD. Although challenges remain in addressing the potential off-target effects and ensuring long-term safety in the clinical setting, the mechanistic rationale and supporting preclinical data justify further investigation into the utility of Mdivi-1 as a repurposed candidate for this currently untreatable condition. As the drug development process progresses, a focused effort on meticulous in vivo testing, formulation development for ocular delivery, and comprehensive pharmacological evaluations will be critical to harnessing the full therapeutic potential of Mdivi-1 for patients with dry AMD (Cueva-Vargas et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2015; She et al., 2018; Yako et al., 2021).
