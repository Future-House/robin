Proposal for MCC950 (Selleckchem S8643)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
MCC950 is a synthetic small-molecule inhibitor that belongs to the diarylsulfonylurea class of compounds originally identified through screening efforts aimed at inhibiting IL-1β processing. Its chemical origins trace back to early studies (circa 2001) that recognized diarylsulfonylurea molecules as cytokine-release inhibitory drugs (CRIDs) before the advent of specific inflammasome inhibitors (Coll et al., 2015). Subsequent medicinal chemistry efforts led to the selection, optimization, and detailed characterization of one member of this series that came to be known as MCC950. Synthetically, MCC950 is produced through a multistep sequence that includes key transformations such as Friedel–Crafts acylation of an indene derivative, acid-promoted cyclization, direct nitration, and subsequent conversion into its final diarylsulfonylurea form, as detailed in the original publication’s Online Methods and Supplementary Materials (Coll et al., 2015; Keuler et al., 2022). Developed initially as an inhibitor of IL-1β post-translational processing, MCC950 was repurposed as a potent and highly selective inhibitor of the NLRP3 inflammasome, exhibiting nanomolar potency (approximately 7.5 nM in murine bone marrow–derived macrophages) with a favorable selectivity profile over other inflammasomes such as AIM2, NLRC4, or NLRP1 (Coll et al., 2015; Das et al., 2021; Hill et al., 2017). Marketed under various identifiers – one of which is the Selleckchem product code S8643 – MCC950 represents a well-characterized chemical tool that has continued to serve as both a probe for studying NLRP3 biology and a preclinical candidate in inflammatory disease models (Das et al., 2021; Sharma et al., 2023).

Therapeutic History:  
MCC950 has been extensively evaluated in preclinical studies targeting inflammatory diseases driven by aberrant NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Its discovery followed earlier observations that sulfonylurea agents such as glyburide, traditionally used in type 2 diabetes for their insulin-secretagogue activity via KATP channel inhibition, also inhibited NLRP3-driven processes albeit at micromolar concentrations (Chauhan et al., 2020; Coll et al., 2015; Hill et al., 2017). In contrast, MCC950 exhibits a markedly improved potency at nanomolar levels and a higher degree of specificity. Preclinical evaluations demonstrated that MCC950 effectively blocks both the canonical and noncanonical activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, resulting in suppressed activation of caspase-1 and reduced IL-1β secretion (Das et al., 2021; Hill et al., 2020). Its efficacy has been validated in a wide range of inflammatory disease models including cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes (CAPS), multiple sclerosis models such as experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE), pulmonary and cutaneous inflammation, Parkinson’s disease, Alzheimer’s disease, and even models of kidney and liver fibrosis (Das et al., 2021; Narros-Fernández et al., 2022; Sharma et al., 2023). Although MCC950 has not yet been formally used in clinical applications—and indeed its clinical development was reportedly halted due to safety concerns such as elevated transaminases and organ structural damage in preclinical toxicity studies (Johansson et al., 2025)—its robust preclinical record has established it as the prototype NLRP3 inhibitor. There is emerging interest in exploiting its mechanism for diseases wherein inflammasome-driven pathology is central. While direct data on the use of MCC950 for dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) are not available in the literature, the growing evidence implicating the NLRP3 inflammasome in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) dysfunction suggests that repurposing MCC950 for dry AMD is a scientifically plausible strategy (Coll et al., 2015; Das et al., 2021; Keuler et al., 2022).

Mechanism of Action:  
The mechanism of action of MCC950 is one of its most compelling features. At the molecular level, MCC950 selectively inhibits the NLRP3 inflammasome by directly binding to the NACHT domain, specifically targeting the Walker B motif that is essential for ATP hydrolysis and subsequent conformational changes required for inflammasome assembly (Hill et al., 2020; Sharma et al., 2023). In doing so, MCC950 prevents the oligomerization of NLRP3 and the recruitment of the adaptor protein ASC, thereby abrogating the activation of caspase-1 and the maturation and release of proinflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-18 (Coll et al., 2015; Keuler et al., 2022). Structural analyses, including photoaffinity labeling and surface plasmon resonance (SPR) studies, have confirmed the high affinity binding (with SPR-derived K_D values in the low nanomolar range) of MCC950 to its target within NLRP3 (Coll et al., 2019; Hill et al., 2017). Importantly, MCC950 does not interfere with upstream ionic events, such as K^+ efflux or Ca^2+ signaling, which are also involved in NLRP3 activation pathways; rather, its action is confined to the blockade of ATP hydrolysis, offering a direct means to “lock” NLRP3 in an inactive, closed conformation (Das et al., 2021; Keuler et al., 2022; Li, 2020). This precise targeting underpins its exceptional potency and selectivity among inflammasome inhibitors. Moreover, a body of literature has explored the structure–activity relationships (SAR) surrounding the diarylsulfonylurea scaffold of MCC950, confirming that modifications to key structural motifs (for instance, the s-hexahydroindacene group) can have significant impacts on both inhibitory potency and chemical stability; these insights have spurred the design of hybrid molecules that further exploit MCC950’s pharmacophore for dual-target activities (Hill et al., 2017).

Expected Effect in Dry Age-Related Macular Degeneration:  
The pathophysiology of dry AMD is characterized by the progressive degeneration of the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), which results from chronic cellular stress—largely driven by mitochondrial dysfunction and elevated reactive oxygen species (ROS) production—that culminates in an inflammatory cascade. A growing body of evidence implicates the NLRP3 inflammasome as a critical mediator in this process; in the RPE, mitochondrial ROS can instigate NLRP3 activation, leading to the cleavage of caspase-1 and the release of proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, which in turn exacerbate RPE atrophy and compromise the integrity of the retinal barrier (Coll et al., 2015; Das et al., 2021). The central hypothesis driving the repurposing of MCC950 for dry AMD is that by selectively inhibiting NLRP3 activation, MCC950 would interrupt this pathogenic cycle. Specifically, its potent inhibition of ATP hydrolysis within the NACHT domain would prevent the inflammasome assembly even in the presence of mitochondrial ROS, thereby blocking caspase-1 activation and subsequent IL-1β secretion. In preclinical models where MCC950 has been tested—such as sodium-iodate-induced damage and laser-induced injury—this compound has been shown to suppress NLRP3-dependent cytokine secretion and preserve barrier function (Coll et al., 2015; Das et al., 2021; Narros-Fernández et al., 2022). Extrapolating these observations to the context of dry AMD, one would expect that targeted inhibition of the NLRP3 inflammasome in RPE cells (which are known to express and be affected by proinflammatory cytokines) would confer protection against mitochondrial ROS-induced damage, maintain RPE integrity, and ultimately slow or halt the progression of geographic atrophy that typifies the dry form of AMD. Additionally, since microglial activation and subsequent inflammatory signaling are also contributing factors in dry AMD, the dual inhibitory effect on both RPE and resident microglia further supports the anticipated beneficial impact of MCC950 in this disease setting (Li, 2020; Sharma et al., 2023).

Overall Evaluation:  
MCC950 presents several compelling strengths as a potential therapeutic candidate for dry age-related macular degeneration. First, its proven high potency (with an IC_50 in the low nanomolar range) and its exquisite selectivity for the NLRP3 inflammasome make it a highly attractive candidate for modulating the pathological inflammation observed in dry AMD (Coll et al., 2015; Das et al., 2021). The detailed elucidation of its mechanism of action—specifically, its direct binding to the Walker B motif within the NACHT domain of NLRP3, thereby preventing the critical ATP-driven conformational changes necessary for inflammasome assembly—provides a clear rationale for its use in conditions where aberrant inflammasome activation is pathogenic (Hill et al., 2017; Hill et al., 2020; Sharma et al., 2023). This mechanism directly addresses one of the key molecular events implicated in RPE atrophy: the mitochondrial ROS-induced activation of NLRP3 leading to sustained release of IL-1β and caspase-1-mediated cell death. If MCC950 can similarly suppress these events in RPE and microglia, it is expected to preserve barrier function and cellular viability in the retina, thereby potentially altering the course of dry AMD.

However, several challenges and weaknesses must be considered in the overall evaluation of MCC950 for dry AMD. One major concern is the clinical safety profile of MCC950. Although it has displayed robust anti-inflammatory activity in numerous preclinical models, clinical development efforts involving MCC950 were reportedly halted in part due to safety concerns—including elevations in transaminases and signs of organ structural damage (Johansson et al., 2025). This systemic toxicity is particularly relevant when considering chronic diseases such as AMD, which require long-term treatment. That said, repurposing MCC950 for a localized indication like dry AMD might allow for alternative routes of administration (for instance, intravitreal injection) that could mitigate systemic exposure and reduce the risk of adverse systemic effects. Additionally, the pharmacokinetic challenges noted in certain contexts—such as its poor penetration when adapted as a PET radiotracer for CNS imaging (Hill et al., 2020)—may or may not translate to ocular tissues, where barriers and tissue distribution differ from those of the brain.

Another potential limitation is that, to date, there is limited direct evidence supporting the use of MCC950 in ocular models or specifically in RPE cells. While its efficacy in a variety of inflammatory and neurodegenerative models bodes well for its potential utility in dry AMD, further targeted research is required to confirm that the mechanisms at play in RPE degeneration align sufficiently with those observed in the well-characterized models of CAPS, EAE, and other inflammatory disorders (Das et al., 2021; Narros-Fernández et al., 2022). Moreover, the possibility of unintended off-target effects remains a concern in any repurposing strategy, and the selectivity profile, although excellent in controlled in vitro settings, must be rigorously validated in the complex microenvironment of the retina.

From a formulation and delivery standpoint, achieving the correct drug concentration in the retinal tissues poses an additional challenge. The ocular bioavailability of MCC950, especially given its physicochemical properties as a diarylsulfonylurea, will need to be thoroughly evaluated. The opportunity to leverage existing ophthalmic delivery platforms—such as sustained-release intravitreal implants or nanoparticle-mediated delivery—could provide solutions that maximize the therapeutic effect while minimizing potential toxicity.

In summary, MCC950 stands out as a promising candidate owing to its highly selective inhibition of the NLRP3 inflammasome, a mechanism intimately linked with the pathogenesis of dry AMD. Its background as a diarylsulfonylurea with well-documented preclinical efficacy in suppressing IL-1β release and blocking caspase-1 activation provides a strong mechanistic foundation. The compound’s history of preclinical success in multiple inflammatory disease models, combined with detailed insights into its mechanism of action, supports its potential repurposing for dry AMD—especially given that inflammasome-mediated inflammation is a key driver of RPE cell degeneration. Nonetheless, caution is warranted due to prior safety concerns encountered in systemic applications, and these must be addressed through careful evaluation of ocular-specific pharmacokinetics, dosing paradigms, and delivery methods. Ultimately, with rigorous preclinical validation in relevant ocular models and potential formulation optimization to ensure localized protection with minimal systemic exposure, MCC950 could provide an innovative therapeutic approach to halting or slowing the progression of dry AMD (Coll et al., 2015; Das et al., 2021; Hill et al., 2020; Johansson et al., 2025; Li, 2020; Sharma et al., 2023).
