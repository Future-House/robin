Proposal for EUK-8 (Enzo Life Sciences)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
EUK-8 is a synthetic catalytic antioxidant that belongs to the manganese‐based salen complexes, a class of low molecular weight enzyme mimetics engineered to replicate key activities of endogenous antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rouco et al., 2020a). This compound is synthesized via a condensation reaction between salicylaldehyde derivatives and diamines to form a bis‐Schiff base (salen) ligand, which then coordinates to a manganese ion, thereby producing the characteristic MnN₂O₂ planar core responsible for its redox activity (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rosenthal et al., 2011). The manganese center in EUK-8 undergoes reversible redox cycling between Mn(III) and Mn(II) oxidation states; this reversible cycling is essential for its dual enzyme-mimetic action, as it enables catalytic dismutation of superoxide radicals into oxygen and hydrogen peroxide and further conversion of hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen, thereby mimicking the combined action of SOD and catalase (Fernandes, 2010; Rouco et al., 2020a). The salen complex scaffold not only confers redox activity but also provides a tunable electronic and steric environment through modification of substituents on the ligand framework, which in turn dictates the redox potential, proton-dissociation properties, and overall catalytic efficacy (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rouco et al., 2020b). Because of its synthetic origin, EUK-8 is both cost-effective and easily reproducible, making it an appealing candidate for repurposing in therapeutic areas where enhanced antioxidant defenses are required, such as in the treatment of dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) (Rouco et al., 2020a; Rosenthal et al., 2011).

Therapeutic History:  
The EUK series of compounds, which includes EUK-8, have been extensively studied in a plethora of preclinical models addressing a wide range of diseases linked to oxidative stress, including ischemic brain injury, neurodegeneration, cardiac injury, and even lifespan extension models in Caenorhabditis elegans and SOD2 knockout mice (Rouco et al., 2020a). Biochemical studies have demonstrated that EUK-8 exhibits both superoxide dismutase and catalase-like activities at submicromolar concentrations, providing significant protection in vitro and in vivo in models of oxidative injury (Bani & Bencini, 2012). While EUK-8 itself has not been directly evaluated in clinical trials for dry AMD, its protective effects have been documented in several models of neuronal and cardiac injury where mitochondrial dysfunction and excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production are central to the pathology (Fernandes, 2010; Rouco et al., 2020a). The pharmacological application of salen manganese complexes, including EUK-8, extends to neuroprotective approaches, such as in models of Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s, and ischemia–reperfusion injury, suggesting that similar mechanisms may be beneficial in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells, which are notably vulnerable to oxidative stress in dry AMD (Rouco et al., 2020b; Yan et al., 2010). Additionally, the literature reveals that analogous compounds in the EUK series have been examined for their anti-inflammatory, anti-apoptotic, and mitochondrial protective properties in diverse cellular and animal models, lending further credence to their potential for repurposing in ocular degenerative diseases (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rosenthal et al., 2009).

Mechanism of Action:  
At the molecular level, EUK-8 functions as a catalytic antioxidant by mimicking two key enzymes involved in the detoxification of reactive oxygen species. First, it catalyzes the dismutation of superoxide (O₂•⁻) by virtue of its manganese center undergoing cyclic redox reactions between the Mn(III) and Mn(II) states; in one half-reaction, the Mn(III) state accepts an electron from superoxide to become Mn(II), while in the subsequent half-reaction, Mn(II) is reoxidized to Mn(III) by another molecule of superoxide, resulting in the conversion of superoxide to molecular oxygen and hydrogen peroxide (Fernandes, 2010; Batinić-Haberle et al., 2016). Second, EUK-8 mimics catalase activity by decomposing the generated hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen; it is proposed that hydrogen peroxide reacts with the manganese center to generate a transient high-valent oxo–Mn species, which is then reduced back to the resting state in the presence of a reductant, thus completing the catalytic cycle (Yan et al., 2010; Bani & Bencini, 2012). The structural integrity of the salen ligand is critical in modulating the redox potential of the manganese center, which, when tuned to an optimal range, ensures efficient electron transfer with reactive species while minimizing the risk of metal ion dissociation or inadvertent pro-oxidant activity (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Durand, 2013). In cellular contexts, the dual SOD/catalase activity of EUK-8 is especially relevant to mitochondria, which are the primary source of intracellular ROS; by reducing mitochondrial superoxide and decomposing hydrogen peroxide, EUK-8 helps to preserve mitochondrial respiration and prevent oxidative mitochondrial damage, which is a key contributor to the pathogenesis of dry AMD (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rouco et al., 2020a). Moreover, the reduction of mitochondrial ROS is anticipated to attenuate the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, a multiprotein complex that is known to be triggered by oxidative stress and has been implicated in inflammasome-mediated inflammatory responses in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) (Rouco et al., 2020a; Bani & Bencini, 2012). The overall biochemical and mechanistic profile of EUK-8, therefore, aligns well with the need to lower mtROS and preserve cellular homeostasis in tissues subjected to chronic oxidative stress.

Expected Effect in Dry Age-Related Macular Degeneration:  
Dry age-related macular degeneration is characterized by the degeneration of the RPE, a process driven by cumulative oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and chronic low-grade inflammation leading to cellular senescence and eventual retinal atrophy (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Batinić-Haberle et al., 2016). Given that the RPE expresses high levels of mitochondrial enzymes, including MnSOD, and relies on efficient mitochondrial respiration for normal function, the preservation of mitochondrial integrity is paramount (Fernandes, 2010; Rouco et al., 2020a). EUK-8 is expected to enter RPE cells and preferentially localize to mitochondria where its catalytic dual SOD/catalase activity would reduce the burden of superoxide and hydrogen peroxide, thereby mitigating oxidative damage to mitochondrial proteins, lipids, and DNA (Rosenthal et al., 2011; Rosenthal et al., 2009). By maintaining lower levels of mtROS, EUK-8 would be anticipated to stave off the mitochondrial dysfunction that underlies RPE degeneration and to prevent the activation of pro-inflammatory signaling cascades such as those mediated by the NLRP3 inflammasome, thus potentially preserving cellular viability and function (Batinić-Haberle, 2013; Bigham & Wilson, 2023). In addition, previous studies with related manganosalen complexes have shown that treatment can lead to the reversal of age-related cognitive deficits and the inhibition of pro-apoptotic pathways in neuronal tissues, which are mechanistically analogous to the crises faced by RPE cells in dry AMD (Rouco et al., 2020a; Rouco et al., 2020b). Given that the anticipated mechanism involves modulation of mitochondrial redox balance and prevention of inflammation, the expected molecular targets in RPE cells include key mitochondrial respiratory chain components, antioxidant response elements such as Nrf2, and inflammasome components including caspase-1 and interleukin-1β, all of which have been implicated in the progression of dry AMD (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Durand, 2013). The ability of EUK-8 to act at submicromolar doses further enhances its appeal, as such potency suggests that therapeutic effects may be achieved with minimal exposure, reducing the likelihood of off-target effects and toxicity (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Fernandes, 2010).

Overall Evaluation:  
From a drug development perspective, EUK-8 emerges as a promising candidate for repurposing in the treatment of dry age-related macular degeneration, primarily because its dual enzyme-mimetic properties address two central features of RPE pathology—oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rouco et al., 2020a). One of the major strengths of EUK-8 lies in its robust catalytic activity; being a regenerative, non-stoichiometric antioxidant means that even at submicromolar concentrations, it can exert sustained protective effects by continuously dismutating ROS, in contrast to traditional antioxidants that are consumed in the process (Rosenthal et al., 2011; Rouco et al., 2020a). Its low molecular weight and non-peptidic nature facilitate cellular uptake and mitochondrial penetration, critical features for targeting the RPE where mitochondrial production of ROS is an important driver of cell injury (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rouco et al., 2020a). Furthermore, the extensive preclinical history of manganosalen compounds in models of neuroprotection, cardiac ischemia, and aging provides a solid rationale for their protective capacity in tissues that are equally susceptible to oxidative damage, even if direct studies in dry AMD are limited at present (Rouco et al., 2020a; Rouco et al., 2020b).

Nevertheless, several weaknesses and challenges must be addressed before EUK-8 can be confidently advanced as a therapeutic for dry AMD. First, despite the promising preclinical data in other models, there is a paucity of direct studies evaluating EUK-8 in ocular or RPE-specific models. Dry AMD has its own unique microenvironment, including features such as the Bruch’s membrane, the blood-retinal barrier, and specialized immune processes, which might affect drug delivery and efficacy (Bigham & Wilson, 2023; Yan et al., 2010). Second, the stability of EUK-8 in the complex milieu of the eye must be rigorously tested, especially because the aqueous and proteinaceous environments found in ocular tissues could potentially lead to ligand dissociation or redox inactivation (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rouco et al., 2020a). Third, although EUK-8’s catalytic nature means that it is less likely to be consumed during therapy compared to stoichiometric antioxidants, there is always the risk that under certain pathological conditions, such as in the presence of excess hydrogen peroxide or other reactive species, it could exhibit paradoxical pro-oxidant effects if the redox balance is not carefully controlled (Batinić-Haberle et al., 2012; Bani & Bencini, 2012). Fourth, formulation and delivery challenges specific to ocular therapeutics must be overcome; for instance, ensuring adequate penetration across the inner limiting membrane and achieving sufficient concentrations within the RPE are non-trivial hurdles that have derailed otherwise promising compounds in the past (Batinić-Haberle et al., 2016; Rosenthal et al., 2009). Finally, comprehensive toxicity and pharmacokinetic studies in ocular models are required since systemic dosing data from neurological or cardiovascular models may not transcribe directly to the unique setting of the retina (Batinić-Haberle, 2013; Fernandes, 2010).

In summary, the therapeutic candidate EUK-8 exhibits several key attributes that make it an attractive option for addressing the oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction underlying dry age-related macular degeneration. Its synthetic origin and scalable chemistry, combined with potent dual SOD/catalase enzyme-mimetic activity, have already been validated extensively in diverse preclinical models of oxidative injury (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rouco et al., 2020a). The molecular mechanism—involving redox cycling of a manganese center coordinated by a salen ligand—supports the hypothesis that EUK-8 can effectively lower mtROS levels and preserve mitochondrial respiration in cells, such as the retinal pigment epithelium, that are critically dependent on mitochondrial function (Fernandes, 2010; Yan et al., 2010). This mechanistic rationale is further bolstered by studies demonstrating that similar manganosalen complexes can reverse age-related functional deficits and prevent neurodegenerative changes in animal models, suggesting that the beneficial effects observed in cardiac and neuronal tissues might extend to the RPE (Rouco et al., 2020a; Rouco et al., 2020b).

The expected effect of EUK-8 in the context of dry AMD is twofold: first, by acting as both a superoxide dismutase and catalase mimic, it is anticipated to reduce the overall oxidative burden within RPE mitochondria, preserving cellular respiration and preventing damage to key mitochondrial constituents; and second, by mitigating the oxidative activation of the inflammasome (particularly the NLRP3 complex), EUK-8 should help prevent the chronic inflammatory environment that exacerbates RPE degeneration in dry AMD (Rouco et al., 2020a; Bani & Bencini, 2012). This dual mode of action not only targets the root cause of oxidative damage but also addresses a major downstream consequence—persistent inflammation—that has been implicated in the progression of dry AMD (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rosenthal et al., 2009).

Overall, while the preclinical evidence underscores the strong potential of EUK-8 as a repurposed therapeutic candidate for dry AMD, its translational success will depend critically on several factors. Strengths of EUK-8 include its well-documented catalytic efficiency, proven efficacy in multiple oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction models, and the capability for extensive structure–activity relationship optimization that allows fine-tuning of its redox properties (Fernandes, 2010; Bani & Bencini, 2012). However, weaknesses such as the lack of ocular-specific preclinical data, potential formulation and delivery challenges specific to the retinal environment, and the need for rigorous ocular toxicity and pharmacokinetic studies remain to be addressed (Bigham & Wilson, 2023; Yan et al., 2010). In the absence of direct evidence in RPE cells or AMD models, it is essential that further studies be conducted to confirm that EUK-8’s in vitro antioxidant effects translate into meaningful protection of RPE mitochondrial function and inhibition of inflammasome activation in vivo (Durand, 2013; Rosenthal et al., 2009). Furthermore, the development of targeted delivery systems—possibly involving nanoparticle encapsulation, intravitreal injections, or sustained-release formulations—may be required to ensure adequate drug concentration at the site of action while minimizing systemic exposure and potential off-target effects (Batinić-Haberle, 2013; Fernandes, 2010).

From a strategic standpoint, the repurposing of EUK-8 for dry AMD appears promising based on its robust mechanistic foundation and extensive preclinical history in related oxidative stress models; however, a focused research program involving detailed studies in ocular tissues, optimization of drug delivery to the retina, and comprehensive toxicity assessments is warranted to overcome the current limitations and uncertainties (Rouco et al., 2020a; Rouco et al., 2020b). If these challenges can be successfully addressed, EUK-8 has the potential to fulfill an unmet clinical need by protecting RPE mitochondria from oxidative damage, thereby slowing the progression of dry AMD and preserving visual function in affected patients (Fernandes, 2010; Rouco et al., 2020a).  

In conclusion, as a candidate drug for dry age-related macular degeneration, EUK-8 offers a compelling therapeutic strategy that leverages its dual SOD and catalase mimetic activities to target the central biochemical derangements in the RPE associated with oxidative stress and inflammation (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rouco et al., 2020a). The natural alignment of its mechanism with the pathophysiological features of dry AMD—namely, mitochondrial dysfunction, excessive ROS production, and inflammasome activation—supports the hypothesis that EUK-8 could restore redox balance and inhibit degenerative processes in the retina (Rouco et al., 2020a; Bani & Bencini, 2012). Nonetheless, the ultimate utility of EUK-8 will depend on its ability to overcome delivery and stability challenges in the ocular environment and on the generation of robust preclinical data demonstrating efficacy in relevant AMD models. Given these considerations, it is recommended that targeted in vitro and in vivo studies be prioritized to validate the therapeutic potential of EUK-8 in dry AMD, with particular attention to mitochondrial function, inflammasome activity, and retinal cell survival parameters (Bani & Bencini, 2012; Rosenthal et al., 2009).
