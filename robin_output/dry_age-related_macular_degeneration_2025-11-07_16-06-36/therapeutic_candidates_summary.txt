Therapeutic Candidate 1:
Candidate: Elamipretide (SS-31; MedChemExpress HY-103007)
Hypothesis: SS-31 binds mitochondrial cardiolipin in RPE cells, stabilizing inner membrane cristae under oxidative stress, preserving ATP synthesis and preventing apoptosis-driven dry AMD progression.
Reasoning: SS-31 selectively localizes to mitochondria to bind cardiolipin, improving electron transport efficiency and reducing ROS leakage. Preclinical RPE models show SS-31 rescues Δψm (JC-1), lowers MitoSOX signal, and prevents cell death in NaIO3- and H2O2-induced RPE injury. Its favorable PK (s.c. t1/2 ~2.5 h) and pilot human safety profile support rapid translation for geographic atrophy.

Therapeutic Candidate 2:
Candidate: Mitoquinone mesylate (MitoQ; Cayman 10012005)
Hypothesis: MitoQ accumulates in RPE mitochondria via the triphenylphosphonium cation, scavenging superoxide at complex I/III release sites to restore Δψm and slow RPE degeneration in dry AMD.
Reasoning: MitoQ’s ubiquinone moiety reduces mtROS and preserves membrane potential in ARPE-19 under H2O2, as measured by MitoSOX and TMRM assays. Oral and topical PK studies demonstrate retinal uptake (~1% of plasma) and excellent safety across >200 human subjects. Its specific mitochondrial localization and robust antioxidative action provide a strong mechanistic rationale against RPE oxidative injury.

Therapeutic Candidate 3:
Candidate: SkQ1 (10-(6′-plastoquinonyl)-decyltriphenylphosphonium; Sigma SML0967)
Hypothesis: SkQ1 targets RPE mitochondria to quench lipid peroxyl radicals and superoxide, preventing Δψm collapse under chronic photo-oxidative stress and mitigating drusen-associated mitochondrial dysfunction.
Reasoning: SkQ1’s plastoquinone core and TPP+ linker yield potent removal of O2•– and lipid radicals at nanomolar concentrations, demonstrated by JC-1 and DCFDA assays in ARPE-19. In vivo OXYS rat and macaque studies show SkQ1 eye drops deliver drug to posterior segment with minimal systemic exposure, reducing RPE vacuolization and preserving ERG function. Its established ocular safety in human dry-eye trials supports repurposing for geographic atrophy.

Therapeutic Candidate 4:
Candidate: MitoTEMPO (InvivoGen antiox15)
Hypothesis: MitoTEMPO accumulates in RPE mitochondria, catalytically dismuting superoxide and preventing lipid peroxidation to maintain bioenergetic function in geographic atrophy.
Reasoning: As a TPP+-linked piperidine nitroxide, MitoTEMPO reduces MitoSOX signal and restores Δψm in H2O2-challenged ARPE-19 cells and RPE of Ccl2−/−/Cx3cr1−/− mice. Although lacking clinical ocular PK, rodent studies show tolerability up to 5 mg/kg with preserved hepatic and renal safety. Its SOD-mimetic specificity and mitochondrial targeting align mechanistically with RPE oxidative injury in dry AMD.
