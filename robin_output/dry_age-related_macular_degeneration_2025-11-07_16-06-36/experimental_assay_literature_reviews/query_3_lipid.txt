Query: Lipid metabolism dysregulation and drusen formation in dry AMD: pathways for cholesterol, phospholipid and retinaldehyde deposition in RPE cells, in vitro modeling of drusen components, lipid overload paradigms, and accumulation assays using BODIPY, Nile red, and targeted lipidomics

1. LXR Agonist to Enhance Cholesterol Efflux  
Hypothesis: Activating LXR in RPE under LDL-overload reduces drusen-like lipid droplets.  
Assay: Treat ARPE-19 or primary human RPE with T0901317 plus native LDL; quantify neutral lipids by BODIPY493/503, track cholesterol, cholesteryl esters and phospholipids via targeted lipidomics.

2. APOE Isoform–Dependent Phospholipid Handling  
Hypothesis: APOE4–expressing RPE accumulates more drusen-like phospholipids than APOE3.  
Assay: Lentiviral transduction of APOE2/3/4 in ARPE-19, lipid overload with photoreceptor outer-segment debris; stain droplets by Nile Red and profile phosphatidylcholine species by LC-MS.

3. ABCA1 Knockdown–Driven Cholesterol Aggregation  
Hypothesis: Loss of ABCA1 in RPE impairs cholesterol export and promotes drusen deposition.  
Assay: siRNA ABCA1 in primary RPE + acetylated LDL; visualize free cholesterol with filipin and neutral lipids with BODIPY; quantify efflux to ApoA-I acceptors and lipid composition by MS.

4. Lysosomal Acid Lipase (LIPA) Inhibition  
Hypothesis: Blocking LIPA leads to cholesteryl ester buildup and drusen precursor formation.  
Assay: Treat RPE with lalistat-1 under high cholesterol conditions; stain esterified lipid droplets by BODIPY C12 and measure cholesteryl esters by targeted shotgun lipidomics.

5. Autophagy Blockade Amplifies Lipid Retention  
Hypothesis: Impaired autophagy (via chloroquine) in RPE fed oxidized POS increases drusen-like deposits.  
Assay: Feed primary RPE oxidized photoreceptor outer segments ± chloroquine; co-stain LC3 and Nile Red; quantify lipid droplet number/size and analyze lipid species by LC-MS.

6. PPARα Activation to Reduce Oxysterols  
Hypothesis: Fenofibrate diminishes 7-ketocholesterol–induced phospholipid accumulation in RPE.  
Assay: Challenge RPE with 7-ketocholesterol ± fenofibrate; stain with BODIPY and quantify oxysterols and phospholipids by targeted lipidomics.

7. CETP Inhibition Blocks Cholesterol Transfer  
Hypothesis: Anacetrapib in RPE–endothelial cocultures reduces HDL-to-RPE cholesterol flux and drusen‐like formation.  
Assay: Radiolabeled HDL cholesterol efflux assay ± anacetrapib; stain residual RPE droplets with Nile Red; lipidomic profiling of cell and media fractions.

8. SR-BI Blockade of HDL Uptake  
Hypothesis: BLT-1–mediated SR-BI inhibition reduces HDL‐derived lipid deposition in RPE.  
Assay: Treat RPE with HDL ± BLT-1; quantify internalized lipids by BODIPY and bead-based lipidomics for HDL‐specific phospholipids.

9. PLTP Overexpression Drives Extracellular Phospholipid Matrix  
Hypothesis: Elevated PLTP in RPE enhances phospholipid transfer to Bruch’s membrane–like matrix.  
Assay: Adenoviral PLTP overexpression in RPE; collect ECM deposits, stain with Nile Red and perform targeted phosphatidylcholine/glycerophospholipid analysis.

10. Visual‐Cycle Modulation of Retinaldehyde Deposition  
Hypothesis: Emixustat reduces all-trans-retinal overload and consequent drusen‐like autofluorescent deposits.  
Assay: Expose RPE to excess at-RAL ± emixustat; measure retinaldehyde by HPLC-MS, stain lipid droplets with BODIPY, assess autofluorescent deposit formation.

11. SREBP2 Inhibition to Curtail Cholesterol Synthesis  
Hypothesis: Fatostatin–mediated blockade of SREBP2 in RPE under lipid stress decreases de novo cholesterol and drusen deposits.  
Assay: Treat lipid-overloaded RPE with fatostatin; quantify lipid droplets by Nile Red and track mevalonate pathway intermediates via targeted metabolomics.

12. NPC1 Overexpression to Enhance Lysosomal Export  
Hypothesis: Boosting NPC1 in RPE improves lysosomal cholesterol trafficking, reducing drusen‐like aggregates.  
Assay: Transfect RPE with NPC1‐GFP; overload with LDL; stain free cholesterol by filipin and lipids by BODIPY; lipidomic analysis of secreted aggregates.

13. GPAT1 Inhibition to Limit TAG Droplets  
Hypothesis: FSG67 inhibition of glycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase-1 in RPE reduces triacylglycerol droplet formation.  
Assay: Treat RPE with FSG67 under fatty acid overload; stain with Nile Red; quantify TAG species by targeted lipidomics.

14. miR-33 Antagomir to Upregulate Cholesterol Transporters  
Hypothesis: miR-33 inhibition in RPE increases ABCA1/ABCG1, enhancing cholesterol efflux and preventing drusen.  
Assay: Deliver locked‐nucleic‐acid miR-33 inhibitor to RPE; lipid overload; measure transporter expression, cholesterol efflux, and droplet burden via BODIPY.

15. HDL-Mimetic Peptide Therapy  
Hypothesis: 22A peptide fosters cholesterol efflux from lipid-laden RPE, reducing drusen‐like droplet formation.  
Assay: Incubate RPE with oxidized LDL then 22A; stain droplets with Nile Red; perform targeted lipidomics of cell and media fractions.
