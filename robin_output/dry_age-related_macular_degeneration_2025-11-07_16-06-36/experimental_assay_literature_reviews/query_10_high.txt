Query: High-throughput phenotypic screening assays for dry AMD therapeutics in RPE cells: multiplexed readouts including cell viability, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, lipofuscin accumulation, complement activation, inflammatory cytokines, and automated image-based analytics

1. KEAP1–Nrf2 pathway activation  
Hypothesis: Small‐molecule inhibitors of the KEAP1–Nrf2 interaction will upregulate antioxidant genes in RPE, reduce H2O2–induced ROS (H2DCFDA), preserve mitochondrial membrane potential (TMRM), decrease lipofuscin autofluorescence, and dampen complement C3b deposition.

2. PARP1 inhibition to preserve NAD+ and mitochondrial function  
Hypothesis: Under oxidative stress, PARP1 hyperactivation depletes NAD+ and impairs mitochondrial ATP production. PARP inhibitors will restore NAD+ levels, maintain Δψm, improve cell viability, and reduce inflammasome‐driven IL-1β/IL-18 release.

3. mTOR inhibition to boost autophagy and lipofuscin clearance  
Hypothesis: Chronic mTOR signaling blockade (e.g., via low‐dose rapamycin analogs) will increase LC3‐II puncta, accelerate lipofuscin degradation, rescue mitochondrial morphology, and lower secretion of proinflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-8).

4. Small peptides mimicking complement factor H (CFH)  
Hypothesis: CFH‐mimetic peptides will competitively block C3 convertase assembly on RPE, decrease C3b and membrane attack complex (C5b-9) deposition, improve cell viability, and attenuate downstream inflammatory signaling.

5. NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition  
Hypothesis: Direct inhibitors of NLRP3 (e.g., MCC950 derivatives) will prevent ASC speck formation, reduce caspase-1 activation, lower IL-1β/IL-18 levels, protect mitochondrial integrity, and sustain RPE survival under drusen‐like stress.

6. Mitochondria‐targeted antioxidants (MitoQ/SkQ1 analogs)  
Hypothesis: Lipophilic quinone derivatives targeted to mitochondria will scavenge intramitochondrial ROS, maintain Δψm, reduce mitochondrial fragmentation, decrease lipofuscin deposition, and blunt complement activation.

7. SIRT3 activation to boost mitochondrial biogenesis  
Hypothesis: Small‐molecule SIRT3 activators will enhance PGC-1α–mediated mitochondrial biogenesis, elevate mitochondrial DNA copy number, reduce oxidative damage, and limit secretion of inflammatory cytokines in aged RPE.

8. RIPK1/RIPK3 necroptosis blockade  
Hypothesis: Inhibitors of RIPK1 or RIPK3 kinases will prevent MLKL‐mediated necroptosis, preserve RPE membrane integrity, reduce DAMP release, and thus lower both complement activation and cytokine secretion.

9. Lysosomal acidification enhancers  
Hypothesis: Compounds that stimulate V-ATPase activity or increase lysosomal proton gradients (e.g., small‐molecule enhancers of clathrin-mediated trafficking) will accelerate phagosome–lysosome fusion, clear autofluorescent lipofuscin, and improve cell survival.

10. miR-146a mimic delivery via lipid nanoparticles  
Hypothesis: Augmenting miR-146a in RPE will downregulate TRAF6/IRAK1, dampen NF-κB signaling, lower IL-6/IL-8 secretion, reduce oxidative and complement stress, and preserve cell viability.

11. AMPK agonists to promote mitophagy  
Hypothesis: Pharmacological AMPK activation (e.g., AICAR or direct small‐molecule activators) will enhance ULK1‐dependent autophagy, clear dysfunctional mitochondria, reduce ROS, and mitigate inflammatory cytokine release.

12. Epigenetic upregulation of membrane complement regulators  
Hypothesis: HDAC or DNMT inhibitors will increase cell-surface expression of CD46, CD55, and CD59 on RPE, diminish C3/C5b-9 deposition, and improve resistance to complement‐mediated injury.

13. Spermidine supplementation to modulate polyamine metabolism  
Hypothesis: Exogenous spermidine will induce autophagy, enhance lipofuscin clearance, protect mitochondrial function, lower oxidative stress, and reduce complement activation in RPE.

14. RAGE (receptor for advanced glycation end products) antagonists  
Hypothesis: Small‐molecule or antibody inhibitors of RAGE will prevent AGE‐driven NF-κB activation, reduce ROS and cytokine release, protect mitochondrial function, and decrease complement deposition.

15. Exosome‐mediated PGC-1α delivery  
Hypothesis: Engineering MSC‐derived exosomes loaded with PGC-1α mRNA or protein will transfer mitochondrial‐biogenesis signals to RPE, boost antioxidant defenses, lower lipofuscin, and attenuate inflammatory/complement responses.
