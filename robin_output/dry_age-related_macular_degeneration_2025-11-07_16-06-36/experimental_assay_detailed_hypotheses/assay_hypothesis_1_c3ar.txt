Assay Overview  
• Goal  
  – To model local complement‐driven inflammation in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells, a key early event in dry AMD.  
  – Specifically, to test whether pharmacological blockade of the C3a receptor (C3aR) on RPE cells diminishes inflammasome activation and pyroptosis in response to complement‐rich serum.  

• Biological system  
  – Human ARPE-19 cell line (or primary human RPE) cultured to confluence (± low-serum differentiation for 2–3 weeks).  
  – Challenge with 10–20% pooled human serum (as a source of activated complement fragments, especially C3a).  

• Intervention  
  – Pre- or co-treatment with a selective C3aR antagonist (small molecule or peptide) at dose ranges informed by published IC₅₀ values (e.g., 100 nM–10 µM).  

• Readouts  
  1. Inflammasome activation  
     – Caspase-1 cleavage (p20 fragment) by Western blot.  
     – ASC speck formation by immunofluorescence.  
  2. Cytokine release  
     – IL-1β and IL-18 in culture supernatant by ELISA.  
  3. Pyroptotic cell death  
     – LDH release assay or propidium iodide uptake by fluorescence.  
  4. Complement deposition (optional)  
     – C3b/iC3b immunostaining on RPE monolayer.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Complement dysregulation is central to dry AMD pathogenesis  
  – Genetic: CFH, C3 and CFI polymorphisms confer AMD risk by altering complement activation thresholds (Hageman et al., 2005; Fritsche et al., 2016).  
  – Histology: Drusen and RPE basal deposits are enriched in C3 fragments and terminal complement complexes (Johnson et al., 2001).  
• C3a–C3aR axis in RPE inflammation  
  – RPE cells express functional C3aR, and C3a–C3aR interaction induces intracellular Ca²⁺ flux, NF-κB activation, and pro-inflammatory cytokine production (Bora et al., 2011).  
  – Autocrine loops: RPE can synthesize C3, amplify C3a generation locally, and thus sustain inflammasome activation (Ricklin & Lambris, 2013).  
• Inflammasome and pyroptosis in dry AMD  
  – NLRP3 inflammasome activation in RPE is triggered by oxidative stress, complement fragments, and drusen components, leading to IL-1β/IL-18 release and RPE cell death (Tarallo et al., 2012; Doyle et al., 2012).  
  – Elevated IL-1β levels are detected in aqueous humor of AMD patients, correlating with RPE dysfunction (Kauppinen et al., 2016).  

Previous Use  
• Mechanistic studies in ARPE-19  
  – Bora et al. (2011) used C3aR antagonists to show reduction of IL-6 and IL-8 secretion in response to exogenous C3a.  
  – Tarallo et al. (2012) demonstrated that complement activation products trigger NLRP3 activation and that complement inhibitors reduce caspase-1 cleavage.  
• Drug discovery applications  
  – Complement inhibitors (e.g., compstatin analogs) have been screened in RPE‐based assays for reduction of C3b deposition and cytokine release (Reis et al., 2012).  
  – Small-molecule C3aR antagonists have been profiled in inflammatory cell assays; translation to RPE systems remains limited but conceptually straightforward.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  • Pathophysiological relevance  
    – Directly addresses a genetically and histologically validated mechanism in dry AMD.  
  • Quantifiable, high-throughput compatible  
    – ELISA, LDH assays and WB are robust, scalable, and amenable to 96- or 384-well formats.  
  • Rapid kinetics  
    – Complement‐driven inflammasome activation and cytokine release occur within hours, facilitating timely readouts.  
  • Target specificity  
    – C3aR antagonism isolates the role of C3a (versus upstream C3 or downstream C5 pathways).  

Weaknesses  
  • Cell model limitations  
    – ARPE-19 cells may not fully recapitulate primary RPE phenotype or polarized monolayer properties.  
    – Lack of Bruch’s membrane, choroidal endothelial and immune cell interactions.  
  • Serum as complement source  
    – Pooled human serum is variable in activity; activation state must be tightly controlled (e.g., heat‐inactivated controls, standardized complement titers).  
  • Incomplete disease modeling  
    – Does not capture drusen accumulation, chronic oxidative stress, or long‐term ECM alterations.  
  • Translational gap  
    – Efficacy in vitro may not predict in vivo performance due to pharmacokinetics, tissue penetration, and off-target effects.  

Conclusion  
This C3aR antagonism assay in RPE cultures is a mechanistically grounded, experimentally tractable model for evaluating complement‐targeted therapeutics in dry AMD. It aligns with human genetic and clinical data implicating C3a–C3aR–inflammasome signaling in RPE pathology. However, results should be interpreted alongside complementary assays (e.g., 3D RPE–Bruch’s membrane co-cultures, oxidative stress models) to build a robust preclinical package.
