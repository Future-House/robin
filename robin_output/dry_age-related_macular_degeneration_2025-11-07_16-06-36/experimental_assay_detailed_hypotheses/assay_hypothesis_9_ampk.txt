Assay Overview  
•  Pathway modeled: mitochondrial quality control via AMPK‐stimulated mitophagy in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE).  Mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired removal of damaged mitochondria are hallmarks of dry AMD.  
•  Readouts:

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Mitochondrial dysfunction in dry AMD  
   •  Multiple clinical and histopathological studies show accumulation of enlarged, dysfunctional mitochondria in RPE overlying drusen (Feher et al., 2006; Golestaneh et al., 2017).  
   •  Proteomic analyses of AMD donor eyes reveal downregulation of key mitophagy regulators (PINK1, Parkin) and AMPK pathway members (Kaarniranta et al., 2018).  
   •  Animal and in vitro models of RPE senescence recapitulate reduced mitochondrial turnover, increased ROS, and RPE atrophy (Mitter et al., 2014; Liu et al., 2014).  

2. AMPK as a master regulator of RPE homeostasis  
   •  AMPK senses energetic stress and directly phosphorylates ULK1 to initiate autophagy/mitophagy (Egan et al., 2011).  
   •  In RPE, AMPK activation protects against oxidative insult, preserves mitochondrial membrane potential, and reduces lipofuscin accumulation (Kim et al., 2017; Hanus & Palczewski, 2019).  
   •  Clinical correlations: low p-AMPK levels in RPE of aged/diseased eyes (Wang et al., 2018) support its central role.  

Previous Use  
•  Mito-Keima reporter system first described by Katayama et al. (2011) and has become the gold standard for measuring mitophagy flux in live cells.  
•  In RPE models:  
   –  Golestaneh et al. (2017) used mito-Keima to show impaired basal mitophagy in AMD patient–derived iPSC-RPE and partial rescue by overexpressing Parkin.  
   –  Kaarniranta et al. (2018) demonstrated that pharmacological AMPK activation increased mitophagy and improved RPE barrier function in vitro.  
•  Drug discovery: screening of small-molecule AMPK activators in neuronal and cardiomyocyte lines has yielded compounds now in early clinical trials for metabolic and neurodegenerative diseases (Ju et al., 2018).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
•  Direct, quantitative measure of mitophagy flux in the disease‐relevant RPE cell type.  
•  Amenable to medium‐throughput screening using automated imaging platforms.  
•  Targets a well-validated pathogenic node (mitochondrial turnover) with strong biomedical and clinical support.  
•  Orthogonal biochemical readouts (p-AMPK, ULK1, LC3) increase confidence in hits.  

Weaknesses  
•  Overexpression of mito-Keima may alter mitochondrial dynamics or autophagy capacity.  
•  AMPK agonists (especially AICAR) can have off-target effects on adenosine receptors and nucleotide pools.  
•  ARPE-19 cells do not fully recapitulate adult human RPE phenotype (polarization, melanosomes, drusen‐like deposits). iPSC-RPE are more physiological but less robust in high‐throughput format.  
•  In vitro assay cannot model interplay with photoreceptors, choroidal vasculature, or inflammatory milieu present in vivo.  

Conclusion:  
An AMPK agonist–stimulated mitophagy assay in mito-Keima–expressing RPE provides a mechanistically grounded, quantifiable platform to identify compounds that restore mitochondrial quality control—a key deficit in dry AMD. Careful selection of AMPK modulators, use of physiologically relevant RPE models, and integration of orthogonal readouts will maximize translatability.
