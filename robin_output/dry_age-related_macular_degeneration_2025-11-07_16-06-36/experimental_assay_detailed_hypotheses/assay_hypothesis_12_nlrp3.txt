Assay Overview  
• Purpose: Quantify assembly of the NLRP3 inflammasome (“specks”) in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells as a surrogate for inflammasome-driven inflammation in dry AMD.  
• Read-out: Automated fluorescence microscopy of ASC-GFP foci (“specks”) per cell. Cells are imaged after stimulation, and speck number, size and percent of speck-positive cells are calculated by high-content image analysis.  
• Biological model: An RPE line (e.g., ARPE-19 or primary human iPSC-derived RPE) stably transduced with ASC tagged with GFP. Cells are “primed” with a TLR agonist (LPS) to induce pro-IL-1β and pro-NLRP3, then challenged with ATP (or Nigericin) to trigger K⁺ efflux and rapid oligomerization of NLRP3–ASC into visible specks. Test compounds are added either before or during second-signal stimulation to assess their ability to inhibit speck formation.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• NLRP3 in dry AMD pathogenesis: Multiple groups have shown that RPE from donors with geographic atrophy or overlying drusen have elevated NLRP3, ASC and caspase-1 activation (Tarallo et al., Nat Med 2012; Kauppinen et al., 2012).  
• Alu RNA–DICER1 axis: DICER1 loss in RPE leads to accumulation of Alu retroelements, which act as endogenous danger signals to activate NLRP3 via the RNA‐sensor pathway, culminating in caspase-1–dependent IL-18 release and RPE death (Tarallo et al., Nat Med 2012).  
• Drusen components: Cholesterol crystals, Aβ and other drusen constituents have been shown to activate NLRP3 in macrophages and RPE (Kroemer et al., J Clin Invest 2016; Doyle et al., Aging Cell 2012). These findings tie drusen biochemistry to inflammasome activation in AMD.  
• Clinical correlations: Elevated IL-1β, IL-18 and caspase-1–cleaved ASC have been detected in aqueous humor and RPE/choroid extracts from AMD patients (Nian et al., Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 2015; Doyle et al., JCI Insight 2017). Genetic variants in inflammasome regulators (e.g., CARD8) have been linked to AMD risk in some cohorts.  

Previous Use  
• Discovery of MCC950: The NLRP3 inhibitor MCC950 was characterized by its ability to block ASC speck formation in LPS/ATP-stimulated macrophages and RPE‐ASC-GFP cells (Coll et al., Nat Med 2015). It proved that speck-count inhibition strongly correlates with reduced IL-1β/IL-18 secretion.  
• Mechanistic insights: Tarallo et al. used a similar RPE inflammasome assay to demonstrate that Alu RNA–induced speck formation is NLRP3-dependent, and that caspase-1 inhibitors or NLRP3 knockdown rescues RPE viability in vitro and in vivo.  
• Screening platform: Automated speck quantification has been adapted for 96- and 384-well formats to identify both small-molecule and biologic inflammasome inhibitors in diverse cell types, including primary RPE (Kummer et al., Sci Rep 2014).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
1. Direct measure of inflammasome assembly: Speck formation is the proximal event in NLRP3 activation, preceding cytokine release and pyroptosis.  
2. Quantitative, high-throughput compatible: Automated image analysis yields robust IC₅₀ determinations.  
3. Disease relevance: RPE inflammasome activation is well‐documented in dry AMD and recapitulates a key sterile inflammatory mechanism.  
4. Target engagement: Provides a clear mechanistic read-out for NLRP3 inhibitors, including molecules not directly assayed by IL-1β/IL-18 ELISA.  

Weaknesses  
1. Artificial priming/stimulation: LPS is not encountered in the subretinal space; priming may not mimic TLR2/3-driven or Alu RNA–driven pathways in AMD.  
2. Cell line limitations: ARPE-19 cells differ from adult human RPE in polarization, phagocytic function and gene expression; primary or iPSC-derived RPE may be more predictive but are lower throughput.  
3. Acute vs. chronic insult: A short (2–6 h) ATP challenge may not capture chronic low‐grade NLRP3 activation driven by drusen or oxidative stress.  
4. Phenotypic scope: Speck inhibition does not inform on downstream events such as RPE phagocytosis, tight junction integrity or mitochondrial health—key aspects of AMD progression.  

Conclusion  
The ASC-GFP speck assay in RPE provides a focused, high-content read-out of NLRP3 inflammasome assembly directly implicated in dry AMD. It is a powerful tool to triage and characterize NLRP3-targeted therapeutics, but should be integrated into a cascade that includes more physiologic priming stimuli (e.g., Alu RNA, drusen extracts), primary human RPE models and assays of RPE viability and function to fully assess translational potential.
