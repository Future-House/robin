Assay Overview  
This assay models one key facet of dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) pathogenesis—lipid accumulation within retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells and drusen formation in Bruch’s membrane. Primary human RPE or iPSC-derived RPE are first lipid-loaded (e.g., by exposure to oxidized low-density lipoprotein or fatty acids) to mimic the neutral lipid droplets seen in AMD. Cells are then treated with an LXR agonist (commonly T0901317) to activate liver X receptors α/β, which transcriptionally up-regulate cholesterol efflux transporters (ABCA1, ABCG1). Readouts include:  
 • BODIPY493/503 fluorescence imaging or high-content microscopy to quantify neutral lipid droplet number, size, and fluorescence intensity per cell.  
 • Mass spectrometry–based lipidomics (targeted or untargeted) of cellular lysates and conditioned medium to measure changes in cholesterol esters, triglycerides, free cholesterol, and oxysterols.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Lipid Accumulation and Drusen Formation: Histological studies (Curcio et al., 2011) show that drusen in AMD contain a high proportion of neutral lipids—cholesteryl esters and triglycerides—derived in part from RPE. These deposits are implicated in Bruch’s membrane stiffening, impaired nutrient exchange, and chronic RPE stress.  
2. RPE Dysfunction: Excess intracellular lipids promote oxidative stress, inflammasome activation, and complement dysregulation in RPE (e.g., elevated NLRP3 activity), all central to dry AMD progression.  
3. LXR Pathway in the Retina: LXRs are expressed in RPE and choroidal endothelium. Activation of LXR induces ABCA1/G1, promotes lipid efflux to ApoE-containing acceptors, and limits drusen-like basal deposits in animal models (Wang et al., 2018). Polymorphisms in lipid-handling genes (including those regulated by LXR) correlate with AMD risk.  

Previous Use  
• Atherosclerosis Research: The LXR-driven cholesterol efflux assay is a gold-standard screen in cardiovascular drug discovery, where macrophages are lipid-loaded then treated with LXR agonists to identify compounds that enhance ABCA1-mediated cholesterol export.  
• AMD-Focused Studies: A handful of groups have adapted this approach to RPE—Wang et al. demonstrated that T0901317 reduced lipid droplet burden and suppressed complement activation in cultured RPE. Maréchal-Drouard et al. used BODIPY imaging to show that LXR activation modified RPE lipid profiles and downstream inflammatory markers.  
• Key Discoveries: These studies have linked enhanced RPE cholesterol efflux to reduced drusen-like deposit formation in rodent models and attenuation of inflammasome signaling in vitro.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
 • Disease relevance: Directly addresses lipid build-up, one of the earliest and most accessible pathological features of dry AMD.  
 • Quantitative and scalable: BODIPY fluorescence and MS lipidomics allow medium- to high-throughput screening of compound libraries.  
 • Mechanistic insight: Ties compound activity to LXR pathway activation and downstream transporter expression (ABCA1/G1), enabling on-target validation.  
 • Translational potential: Hits can be further profiled for efficacy in RPE–Bruch’s membrane co-culture and in vivo AMD models.  

Weaknesses  
 • Oversimplification: Lipid loading and efflux in 2D RPE monolayers do not recapitulate the full complexity of drusen formation (e.g., involvement of Bruch’s membrane, choroid, microglia, complement system).  
 • Off-target effects: T0901317 is a pan-LXR agonist with known liver and systemic lipid metabolism side effects; assay hits may share such liabilities.  
 • Incomplete pathology coverage: Does not address oxidative damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, or inflammatory cascades independent of lipid accumulation.  
 • Readout specificity: BODIPY493/503 primarily stains neutral lipids but cannot distinguish cholesterol esters from triglycerides without complementary MS.  

Conclusion  
The LXR agonist–driven lipid efflux assay is a robust, mechanism-based cell culture tool for screening and mechanistic interrogation of compounds that alleviate RPE lipid overload—a hallmark of early dry AMD. While powerful for identifying on-target modulators of cholesterol efflux, it should be complemented by assays modeling oxidative stress, complement activation, and Bruch’s membrane dynamics to fully capture the multifactorial nature of dry AMD.
