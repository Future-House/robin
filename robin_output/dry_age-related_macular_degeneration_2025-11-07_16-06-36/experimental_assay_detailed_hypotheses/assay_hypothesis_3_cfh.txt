Assay Overview  
• Assay concept  
  – Create an RPE cell–based model of complement dysregulation by knocking out CFH in ARPE-19 cells via CRISPR/Cas9.  
  – Challenge CFH–/– ARPE-19 monolayers with normal human serum (NHS) to trigger complement activation on the apical surface.  
  – “Rescue” complement regulation with recombinant human CFH (either wild-type or Y402H risk variant) added back into the serum prior to challenge.  

• Readouts  
  1. Complement deposition  
     • C3b/iC3b and membrane attack complex (C5b-9) deposition measured by immunofluorescence microscopy (quantified by high-content imaging) or by cell-associated ELISA/Western blot.  
     • Soluble activation fragments (C3a, C5a) in supernatants measured by sandwich ELISA.  
  2. RPE viability/injury  
     • MTT or resazurin assay for metabolic activity.  
     • LDH release assay for membrane integrity.  
     • Optionally, TUNEL or Annexin V staining for apoptosis.  

• Biological materials  
  – ARPE-19 cell line, CFH knockout via CRISPR/Cas9  
  – Pooled normal human serum (source of complement)  
  – Recombinant human CFH (WT and Y402H)  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Central role of complement in dry AMD  
  – Genetic: The CFH Y402H single-nucleotide polymorphism is among the strongest genetic risk factors for dry AMD, conferring up to a 7-fold increased risk (Hughes et al., 2010).  
  – Pathology: Drusen in AMD eyes are enriched in C3 fragments, C5b-9, and other complement components; impaired CFH binding to Bruch’s membrane and CRP leads to local over-activation of the alternative pathway.  
  – Clinical: Elevated plasma levels of activation fragments (C3a, Ba) correlate with disease progression; early clinical trials of complement inhibitors (e.g., anti-factor D, anti-C5) have been conducted, underscoring the pathway’s therapeutic relevance.  
• RPE susceptibility  
  – RPE is both a source and target of complement proteins; uncontrolled complement activation on RPE surfaces leads to sublethal injury, chronic inflammation, and eventually geographic atrophy.  
  – In vitro, complement-mediated deposition of C3b and MAC on RPE monolayers induces oxidative stress, pro-inflammatory cytokine release, and cell death—hallmarks of dry AMD pathogenesis.  

Previous Use  
• Mechanistic studies  
  – ARPE-19 cells exposed to NHS have been used to demonstrate that the CFH Y402H variant is less effective at limiting C3b deposition on RPE (Geerlings et al., 2017).  
  – CFH knockout or knock-down models in RPE have recapitulated heightened complement activation and provided a platform to dissect interactions between CFH, glycosaminoglycans, and CRP.  
• Early screening efforts  
  – Researchers have used similar assays to profile small-molecule and peptide inhibitors of factor D or to test monoclonal antibodies (e.g., anti-C5) for their ability to reduce complement deposition on RPE monolayers.  
  – Key insights: identification of binding interfaces on CFH critical for surface regulation; validation of C3 activation fragment measurements as surrogate biomarkers in vitro.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  – Disease relevance: Directly models a major genetic risk mechanism (CFH Y402H) in the primary cell type affected in dry AMD.  
  – Quantitative: Multiple orthogonal readouts (C3b, C5b-9, cell viability) enable robust assessment of therapeutic impact.  
  – Rescue design: Clear internal control by comparing WT versus risk-variant CFH in the same genetic background.  
  – Adaptable for medium throughput: High-content imaging or plate-based ELISAs can screen panels of complement modulators.  

Weaknesses  
  – ARPE-19 limitations: Immortalized cell line may not fully recapitulate primary RPE phenotypes (polarity, barrier function, gene expression).  
  – Serum source: Pooled serum may not reflect ocular microenvironment concentrations of CFH, factor D, or other regulators.  
  – Acute vs. chronic: A short-term complement challenge cannot model long-term drusen formation, RPE–Bruch’s membrane interactions, or cumulative cell stress.  
  – Single-pathway focus: AMD is multifactorial (oxidative stress, lipid dysregulation, inflammation); this assay addresses complement alone and may miss effects on other pathogenic axes.  

Conclusion  
This CFH knockout/rescue assay in ARPE-19 cells offers a focused, genetically tractable platform to evaluate how candidate therapeutics modulate complement activation on RPE. It is particularly well suited for early-stage screening of complement pathway inhibitors but should be complemented by more complex models (primary RPE, co-culture with choroidal cells, or organoids) to capture the full pathobiology of dry AMD.
