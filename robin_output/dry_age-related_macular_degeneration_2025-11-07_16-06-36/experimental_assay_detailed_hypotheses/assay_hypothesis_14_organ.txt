Assay Overview  
• Goal: Model RPE–choriocapillaris barrier integrity under physiologic flow and test compounds that preserve or restore barrier function in dry AMD.  
• Cells and materials: Primary or iPSC-derived human RPE cells seeded on the upper side of a porous, ECM-coated microfluidic membrane; human choroidal microvascular endothelial cells (hCMECs) on the lower side. A thin layer of collagen IV, fibronectin and laminin mimics Bruch’s membrane. Medium flows independently on each side at low shear (0.5–2 dyn/cm2).  
• Readouts:  
   – Real-time transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) sensors integrated into the chip.  
   – Fluorescent-dextran (10–70 kDa) permeability assays: quantitation of dextran flux from the apical (RPE) to basal (choroid) channel by time-lapse fluorescence.  
   – High-content imaging of tight-junction proteins (ZO-1, occludin) and cell viability (live/dead dyes).  
   – Optionally, oxidative-stress challenge (e.g. H2O2, blue-light irradiation) or complement-activation stimuli (C3a, C5a) can be applied to the apical side to simulate early AMD insults.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Centrality of RPE–Bruch’s–choriocapillaris axis in dry AMD:  
   – Clinically, geographic atrophy correlates with RPE cell loss and choriocapillaris dropout (Sarks et al. 2018; Mullins et al. 2011).  
   – Aging Bruch’s membrane thickening and lipidization impair nutrient/waste exchange (Curcio et al. 2014), promote RPE dysfunction and barrier breakdown.  
   – RPE tight-junction disassembly precedes overt cell death in AMD (Ghosh et al. 2017). Barrier compromise permits pro-inflammatory and complement factors to accumulate sub-RPE, fueling drusen formation (Anderson et al. 2010).  
• Flow and shear stress as modulators of endothelial and RPE physiology:  
   – Choriocapillaris endothelial cells are exposed to unique hemodynamics. Shear influences tight junction expression and cytokine secretion (Fleming 2014).  
   – Static Transwell systems fail to capture mechanotransduction effects on barrier integrity (Achberger et al. 2019).  

Previous Use  
• Achberger et al. (Nature Communications 2019): Developed a full “retina-on-chip” including RPE, retinal neurons and choroidal endothelium. Demonstrated that oxidative stress decreased TEER and increased dextran leakage; they rescued barrier function with the antioxidant lutein.  
• Wang et al. (Lab on a Chip 2017): Focused on RPE–endothelium co-culture under flow. Validated that VEGF from stressed RPE increased endothelial permeability; anti-VEGF partially restored barrier.  
• Pharmacological screening: Wu et al. (2021) used a simpler RPE–endothelial Transwell with shear to identify small molecules that stabilize ZO-1 under H2O2 challenge. Their top hit is now in early in vivo testing.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Physiologic relevance: Co-culture under flow on an ECM-mimetic membrane better recapitulates in vivo mass transport, cell–cell contacts and mechanobiology than static Transwells.  
• Multiplexed, non-destructive end points (TEER, real-time permeability, live imaging) enable time-course studies and early go/no-go decisions.  
• Flexibility: Can introduce oxidative, inflammatory or complement stimuli apically to model progressive dry AMD insults.  
• Human primary/iPSC-derived cells reduce species-translation gaps.  

Weaknesses  
• Lack of immune cells (microglia, macrophages) and complement-producing cell types limits modeling of drusen-associated inflammation.  
• Does not include photoreceptors or inner retina—cannot assess outer-retinal neuroprotection or RPE-driven retinoid cycling.  
• Bruch’s membrane mimic is simplified; true aged Bruch’s has complex multilamellar lipid deposits and advanced glycation end-products.  
• Throughput is modest (10–20 chips/run), device costs and technical expertise present a barrier for large screening campaigns.  

Conclusion  
An RPE–choroid organ-on-chip barrier assay addresses a core dry AMD pathomechanism—breakdown of the outer-blood–retinal barrier—and provides physiologically relevant readouts not achievable in static cultures. While it cannot capture the full complexity of drusen biogenesis or immune-mediated atrophy, it is a powerful mid-throughput platform for triaging barrier-protective drug candidates prior to in vivo validation.
