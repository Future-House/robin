Assay Overview  
– Biological question modeled: Sublytic complement attack on retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) leading to endoplasmic‐reticulum (ER) stress and apoptosis—a mechanism strongly implicated in dry AMD pathogenesis.  
– Assay workflow:  
  • Culture ARPE-19 or primary human RPE monolayers under polarizing conditions.  
  • Incubate with human complement-active serum plus a low concentration of anti-RPE surface antibody to generate sublytic membrane attack complex (MAC) deposition (C5b-9) without immediate lysis.  
  • Readouts:  
      – ER stress/UPR markers: CHOP (DDIT3) induction by qPCR or Western blot; BiP/GRP78, phospho-PERK, spliced XBP1 by RT-PCR.  
      – Apoptosis: TUNEL staining or Annexin V/PI flow cytometry at 6–24 h post-challenge.  
      – MAC quantification: immunofluorescence for C5b-9 on RPE monolayer.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Complement dysregulation in dry AMD:  
   • Genetic studies link CFH Y402H, C3, CFB/CFI and C9 variants to dry AMD risk (Hageman et al. 2005; Fritsche et al. 2013).  
   • Histology of AMD donor eyes shows sublytic MAC deposits at RPE–Bruch’s membrane (Mullins et al. 2011; Nozaki et al. 2006).  
2. ER stress and RPE degeneration:  
   • Post-mortem AMD RPE exhibit elevated ER stress markers CHOP and BiP (Umeda et al. 2015).  
   • In vitro, sublytic MAC triggers UPR activation in RPE, leading to CHOP‐mediated apoptosis (Balasubramanian et al. 2011; Wu et al. 2015).  
3. Clinical correlation:  
   • Drusen accumulation contains complement components and UPR‐related proteins (Johnson et al. 2011).  
   • Progressive geographic atrophy correlates with RPE dropout in regions of chronic complement activation (AREDS2 imaging data).  

Previous Use  
– Balasubramanian et al. (2011) first demonstrated that sublytic C5b-9 on ARPE-19 induces PERK phosphorylation and CHOP upregulation, linking complement attack to ER stress.  
– Wu et al. (2015) used this assay to show that genetic knockdown of C5 in RPE reduces CHOP induction, supporting C5b-9 as the proximal trigger.  
– Planqué et al. (2015) screened complement‐regulating peptides for their ability to prevent sublytic MAC deposition and downstream UPR markers.  
– Nozaki et al. (2006) in mouse RPE/albino rat models demonstrated that anti-C5 antibody reduces sublytic MAC and RPE apoptosis, validating complement as a therapeutic target.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Disease relevance: Directly models complement‐mediated RPE injury, a core mechanism in dry AMD.  
• Mechanistic readouts: Quantifies both proximal (MAC deposition) and distal (ER stress/apoptosis) events, enabling target‐specific profiling.  
• Throughput: Adaptable to 96-well format for early‐stage compound screening.  
• Translatability: Assay endpoints (CHOP, C5b-9) can be cross‐validated in ex vivo donor tissue.  

Weaknesses  
• Cell model limitations: ARPE-19 may not recapitulate all features of adult, polarized human RPE (e.g., melanosomes, tight junction fidelity). Primary RPE are more physiological but variable and lower throughput.  
• Serum variability: Human complement activity varies by donor and batch—requires rigorous titering and standardization.  
• Microenvironment absence: Lacks drusen components (lipids, oxidized proteins) and Bruch’s membrane interactions that modulate complement activation in vivo.  
• Sublytic window narrow: Achieving MAC levels that trigger ER stress without overt cell lysis demands careful optimization for each serum lot and cell passage number.  

Conclusion  
The sublytic MAC–induced ER stress assay robustly models a genetically and histologically validated pathogenic cascade in dry AMD. It offers mechanistic insight and scalable readouts ideal for screening complement inhibitors or UPR modulators. However, its reliance on simplified RPE monocultures and variable human serum complement underscores the need to pair hits with more complex models (e.g., RPE–Bruch’s membrane co-cultures or organoids) before advancing candidates toward in vivo proof-of-concept.
