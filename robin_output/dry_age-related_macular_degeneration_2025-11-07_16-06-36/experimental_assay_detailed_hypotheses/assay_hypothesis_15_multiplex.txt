Biomedical Evidence  
• Centrality of RPE dysfunction in dry AMD: Loss of RPE viability and function underlies geographic atrophy (Pennington & DeAngelis 2016; Ambati & Fowler 2012).  
• Oxidative stress and mitochondrial decline: Age-related accumulation of lipofuscin and decreased mitochondrial reserve predispose RPE to ROS-mediated damage (Feher et al. 2006; Jarrett & Boulton 2012).  
• Energy failure: Diminished ATP correlates with impaired phagocytosis and cell death in AMD RPE (Ferrington et al. 2016).  
• Complement overactivation: Genetic and histopathological studies link polymorphisms in CFH, C3 and related regulators to drusen formation and RPE cell loss via uncontrolled complement deposition (Anderson et al. 2010; Liao et al. 2016).  
• Multiparametric perturbations capture pathway cross-talk: Oxidative stress exacerbates complement activation and vice versa, suggesting that assessing them in parallel increases biologic relevance (Shen et al. 2018).  

Previous Use  
• Single-endpoint adaptations: Separate ATP, ROS or mitochondrial assays have been widely used to profile antioxidants and mitochondrial stabilizers in RPE (e.g., H2O2 challenge screens).  
• Early multiplex prototypes: Shen et al. (2018) combined ROS and viability in a 96-well AESA RPE screen to identify novel antioxidant scaffolds. Liao et al. (2016) used high-content imaging to quantify C3b on RPE, validating complement inhibitors.  
• Proof-of-concept screens: A handful of academic groups have piloted 384-well phenotypic screens in ARPE-19—for example, identification of small-molecule inducers of mitophagy that protect against H₂O₂-induced cell loss (Hargrove et al. 2019).  
• Scientific breakthroughs: These multiplex efforts yielded first-in-class modulators of RPE oxidative resilience and early leads for complement blockade, though none have yet progressed to clinical dry AMD trials.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Disease relevance: Directly assays pathways genetically and histologically validated in dry AMD pathogenesis.  
• Multiplex synergy: Simultaneous quantification uncovers compounds with multi-modal protective profiles that single-endpoint screens miss.  
• Throughput and automation: 384-well format and automated image analysis accelerate hit discovery and triage.  
• Quantitative phenotypic scoring: Enables ranking by potency, efficacy and mechanistic fingerprint.  

Weaknesses  
• Cell line limitations: ARPE-19 may not fully recapitulate polarized, pigmented iPSC-derived RPE; differences in complement regulator expression can affect assay window.  
• Simplified microenvironment: Lack of Bruch’s membrane, choroidal endothelial cells and sub-RPE matrix components limits modeling of drusen dynamics and chronic complement activation.  
• Complement assay complexity: In vitro C3b deposition may not fully reproduce in vivo assembly of membrane attack complex or regulatory feedback loops.  
• Fluorescence interference: Chemical autofluorescence or quenching can confound ROS and TMRM readouts—stringent counterscreens required.  
• Translational predictivity: Hits require follow-up in 3D organoids and animal models to assess long-term RPE survival and GA progression.  

Conclusion  
Overall, this multiplex phenotypic high-throughput assay is a powerful early-stage tool to identify compounds that bolster RPE energy metabolism, limit oxidative damage and curb complement deposition—three pillars of dry AMD pathobiology. Strategic refinement (e.g., use of iPSC-RPE, matrix scaffolds or co-culture elements) and rigorous secondary validation will be essential to translate initial hits into bona fide dry AMD therapeutics.
