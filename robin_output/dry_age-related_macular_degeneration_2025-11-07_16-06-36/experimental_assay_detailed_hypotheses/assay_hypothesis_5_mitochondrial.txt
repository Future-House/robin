Assay Overview  
• Disease aspect modeled: Mitochondrial oxidative stress and bioenergetic failure in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), a core feature of dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD).  
• Readouts:  
  – MitoSOX fluorescence to quantify mitochondrial superoxide production.  
  – Seahorse XF analysis of oxygen consumption rate (OCR) to assess basal respiration, ATP‐linked respiration, maximal respiratory capacity and spare respiratory capacity.  
  – TMRE (tetramethylrhodamine ethyl ester) fluorescence or flow cytometry to measure mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) as a rapid indicator of mitochondrial health.  
• Biological material: Primary human RPE cultures (ideally donor‐matched AMD vs. non‐AMD), ARPE-19 or hTERT-RPE1 cell lines exposed to sublethal oxidative insults (e.g., H₂O₂, rotenone) in the presence or absence of the mitochondria‐targeted antioxidant mitoQ.

Biomedical Evidence  
• Mitochondrial dysfunction is a hallmark of dry AMD. Postmortem AMD eyes show morphological and biochemical alterations in RPE mitochondria (Feher et al., 2006): reduced cristae density, impaired electron transport chain (ETC) complex activities and elevated reactive oxygen species (ROS).  
• Clinical correlation: Patients with early/intermediate AMD exhibit biomarkers of systemic oxidative stress and reduced retinal bioenergetics by noninvasive imaging (e.g., flavoprotein autofluorescence).  
• Pathogenic cascade: Chronic mitochondrial ROS in RPE triggers lipid peroxidation, inflammasome activation (NLRP3), complement dysregulation and eventual RPE atrophy.  
• MitoQ mechanism: Lipophilic triphenylphosphonium conjugate that accumulates several‐hundred‐fold in the mitochondrial matrix, scavenging superoxide and preventing cardiolipin oxidation—thereby preserving ETC integrity and ATP generation. Ferrington et al. (2017) showed that mitoQ reduces MitoSOX signal, restores OCR parameters and mitigates apoptosis in AMD donor RPE under oxidative stress.

Previous Use  
• Feher et al. (2006) characterized mitochondrial structural and functional defects in human AMD RPE; although not directly testing mitoQ, they established the rationale for mitochondria‐targeted therapies.  
• Ferrington and colleagues (2017) applied a protocol nearly identical to this assay: primary RPE from AMD and control donors were stressed with H₂O₂, treated with mitoQ, then assessed by MitoSOX, Seahorse and TMRE. They reported significant rescue of mitochondrial function in AMD RPE but minimal effect in non-AMD controls, underscoring disease specificity.  
• Variants of the assay have been used in high-content screens for novel mitochondrial protectants in other neurodegenerative models (e.g., Parkinson’s disease dopaminergic neurons), demonstrating its adaptability for medium-throughput drug discovery.

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Directly interrogates a core pathogenic mechanism in dry AMD (RPE mitochondrial dysfunction).  
• Multiplexed, quantitative endpoints (ROS, membrane potential, bioenergetics) enhance assay robustness and reduce false positives.  
• Amenable to both primary human RPE and immortalized lines—facilitates donor‐specific phenotyping and higher throughput.  
• Proven “bench to biology” track record with mitoQ, validating its predictive value for therapeutic efficacy.  

Weaknesses  
• RPE monocultures lack the native subretinal microenvironment (photoreceptor outer segments, Bruch’s membrane, complement factors, microglia) that modulate oxidative stress responses.  
• Oxidative insult paradigms (H₂O₂, rotenone) may not fully recapitulate chronic, low-grade stress seen in vivo.  
• MitoQ’s lipophilicity and uptake kinetics in vitro may differ from clinical pharmacokinetics; hits may require substantial medicinal chemistry optimization.  
• Assay does not address non‐mitochondrial contributors to dry AMD (complement dysregulation, lipid deposition, autophagy impairment).  

Conclusion: This mitochondrial ROS-focused RPE assay is a highly relevant first‐line screen for dry AMD therapeutics aiming to preserve RPE bioenergetics. Its quantitative, mechanism‐driven readouts and demonstrated use with mitoQ make it a strong platform for identifying lead compounds. However, hits should be validated in more complex co-culture or organotypic models that incorporate complement, photoreceptor–RPE interactions and chronic stress paradigms before advancing to in vivo studies.
