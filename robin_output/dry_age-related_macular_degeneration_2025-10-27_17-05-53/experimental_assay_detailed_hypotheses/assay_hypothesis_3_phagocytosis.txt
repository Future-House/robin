Assay Overview  
•  Assay principle and readouts  
   –  Models one key RPE function—daily phagocytosis and lysosomal digestion of photoreceptor outer segments (POS)—that is compromised in dry AMD.  
   –  Fluorescently label bovine (or porcine) POS and feed them to RPE monolayers (ARPE-19 or iPSC-derived RPE) in 96-well plates.  
   –  After a 4 h pulse, quench surface-bound POS with trypan blue, then quantify internalized POS by high-content fluorescence microscopy or flow cytometry.  
   –  In parallel wells, measure lysosomal activity via a cathepsin D fluorogenic substrate or activity assay to gauge digestive capacity.  

•  Biological material  
   –  ARPE-19: an immortalized human RPE line that is easy to culture but shows limited polarization and lower phagocytic throughput.  
   –  iPSC-RPE: patient- or donor-derived RPE differentiated in vitro, exhibiting more native polarity, tight junctions, melanosomes, and higher phagocytic capacity, albeit with greater cost and batch-to-batch variability.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1.  Centrality of RPE phagocytosis to AMD pathogenesis  
   –  The RPE daily ingests ∼10 % of photoreceptor outer segments; this homeostatic clearance prevents accumulation of toxic by-products (Kevany & Palczewski, 2010).  
   –  Age-related decline in phagocytic efficiency correlates with lipofuscin buildup (Sparrow & Boulton, 2005; Feeney-Burns et al., 1987), formation of drusen and subretinal deposits (Curcio et al., 2001), and eventual RPE atrophy (Mitter et al., 2014).  
   –  Lysosomal dysfunction—reduced cathepsin D activity, impaired autophagosome-lysosome fusion—has been documented in dry AMD donor eyes (Kaarniranta et al., 2013; Mitter et al., 2014).  
   –  Clinically, geographic atrophy (GA) in dry AMD patients shows focal RPE loss where phagocytic and degradative machinery is overwhelmed (Sarks et al., 2007).  

2.  RPE-POS interaction pathways as therapeutic targets  
   –  Key receptors (MerTK, αvβ5 integrin) and downstream signaling (FAK, PI3K) orchestrate POS binding and engulfment (Finnemann et al., 2003; Nandrot et al., 2004).  
   –  Enhancing these pathways or boosting lysosomal proteases (e.g., cathepsin D) is hypothesized to restore RPE health and slow GA progression.  

Previous Use  
•  High-content POS phagocytosis assays have been used to screen small-molecule libraries for enhancers of RPE function (Mazzoni et al., 2019).  
•  Discovery of critical regulators  
   –  Identification of small-molecule agonists of MerTK signaling that augment uptake (Finnemann lab, 2012).  
   –  Autophagy modulators (e.g., TFEB activators) shown to increase cathepsin D expression and POS clearance in iPSC-RPE (Kim et al., 2019).  
•  Mechanistic insights  
   –  Demonstration that age-pigment lipofuscin components (A2E) inhibit phagosome maturation and cathepsin D (Sparrow et al., 2010).  
   –  Validation that genetic variants in lysosomal genes correlate with impaired RPE digestion in patient iPSC-RPE lines (Carr et al., 2017).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
•  Pathophysiological relevance—directly measures the core RPE defect linked to dry AMD and GA.  
•  Functional and quantitative—captures both uptake and degradative stages via complementary readouts.  
•  Medium-throughput format—amenable to compound screening and dose–response curves.  
•  Modular—can substitute ARPE-19 with patient-specific iPSC-RPE to model genetic risk factors.  

Weaknesses  
•  Cell line limitations—ARPE-19 lacks full polarization, has lower phagocytic capacity and altered gene expression compared to native RPE (Ablonczy et al., 2011).  
•  POS variability—batch-to-batch differences in isolation, labeling efficiency, and species differences may impact assay reproducibility.  
•  Oversimplification—does not capture complement dysregulation, inflammatory milieu, Bruch’s membrane changes, or interactions with choroidal vasculature critical in AMD.  
•  Readout specificity—enhanced fluorescence uptake could reflect increased adhesion rather than true internalization unless quenching is rigorously validated.  

Conclusion  
A POS-phagocytosis assay in 96-well format represents a scientifically grounded, function-based screen for dry AMD therapeutics, especially those aimed at restoring RPE homeostasis. To maximize translational value, it should be paired with assays of complement activation, oxidative stress, and RPE survival, and—where feasible—employ well-characterized, patient-derived iPSC-RPE to capture genetic and phenotypic diversity.
