Assay Overview  
This complement‐deposition inhibition assay models local overactivation of the alternative complement pathway on retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells, a key pathogenic event in dry AMD. Confluent monolayers of primary human RPE or the ARPE-19 cell line are seeded in 96-well plates and pretreated with test compounds. Complement activation is initiated by adding 10% normal human serum (NHS) under conditions that favor C3 convertase formation. After a defined incubation (e.g. 1–2 h at 37 °C), wells are washed and fixed, and deposition of C3b and the terminal membrane attack complex (C5b-9 or MAC) is quantified either by:  
 • Antibody‐based sandwich ELISA on cell lysates or supernatants (quantitative bulk readout)  
 • High‐content immunofluorescence imaging (96-well HCS) using fluorescent antibodies against C3 fragments and C5b-9 (cell‐by‐cell localization and morphological context)  
A reduction in C3b and/or MAC signal relative to vehicle controls indicates functional complement inhibition by the test agent.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Genetic and histopathological data place complement dysregulation at the center of dry AMD pathogenesis. The CFH Y402H polymorphism and variants in C3, CFB and CFI confer increased AMD risk, demonstrating that inadequate regulation of the alternative pathway drives local inflammation (Hageman et al., 2005; Fritsche et al., 2016).  
2. Drusen from AMD donor eyes are enriched in complement components (C3, C5b-9) and regulators, supporting in vivo complement activation on or near RPE/Bruch’s membrane (Anderson et al., 2002; Crabb et al., 2002).  
3. Elevated levels of systemic and ocular complement activation products (C3a, C5a, Ba) correlate with disease progression and lesion size in geographic atrophy, linking complement activity to clinical outcomes (Schick et al., 2017).  
4. In vitro, complement‐mediated sublytic MAC deposition on RPE induces pro‐inflammatory cytokine release (IL-6, IL-8), oxidative stress and ultimately RPE cell dysfunction or death—hallmarks of dry AMD (Espinosa-Heidmann et al., 2006; Xu et al., 2009).  

Previous Use  
• Espinosa-Heidmann et al. (2006) demonstrated C5b-9 deposition on RPE in a primate culture model and showed that complement inhibition preserves RPE integrity.  
• Shen et al. (2012) used ARPE-19 + serum to screen small-molecule and peptide inhibitors of C3 convertase, identifying lead compounds that reduced C3b deposition in vitro.  
• Factor H–based biotherapeutics (FH-SCR constructs) have been evaluated in this assay format to establish proof-of-mechanism for complement regulation on RPE (Clark et al., 2014).  
These studies established assay reproducibility, defined dynamic signal windows for C3b and MAC, and correlated in vitro complement blockade with protection of RPE cellular viability.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Disease relevance: Directly measures a genetically and histopathologically validated pathogenic mechanism in dry AMD.  
• Functional endpoint: Quantifies engagement of the complement cascade at two distinct steps (C3 activation and MAC assembly).  
• Throughput: Adaptable to 96-well (or 384-well) formats for medium-throughput screening and dose–response profiling.  
• Versatility: Can accommodate primary human RPE (more biologically faithful) or ARPE-19 for cost and scalability.  

Weaknesses  
• Simplified microenvironment: Lacks Bruch’s membrane, extracellular matrix changes and drusen constituents that modulate complement in vivo.  
• Serum variability: Donor-to-donor differences in complement activity require batch qualification or heat-inhactivated controls.  
• Cell model limitations: ARPE-19 may not fully recapitulate polarity, pigmentation and gene expression of native RPE; primary cells have limited expandability.  
• Downstream readouts: Does not capture long‐term RPE dysfunction, oxidative stress responses or inflammasome activation seen in chronic AMD.  

Conclusion  
This complement‐deposition inhibition assay offers a direct, mechanistically grounded platform to evaluate candidate therapeutics targeting complement overactivation in dry AMD. It is particularly well suited for early‐stage screening and hit validation of complement modulators, although complementary assays addressing RPE viability, oxidative stress and matrix interactions will be needed to fully assess therapeutic potential.
