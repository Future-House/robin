Assay Overview  
• Pathogenic axis modeled – Chronic oxidative stress–induced mitochondrial dysfunction in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), a hallmark of dry AMD pathogenesis. Human RPE lose mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψm) and deplete ATP under oxidative challenge.  
• Readouts –  
  – Δψm measured by tetramethylrhodamine ethyl ester (TMRE) fluorescence (quantitative microplate reader or high-content imager)  
  – Cellular ATP measured by luminescent ATPlite assay  
  Dual readout provides complementary information on mitochondrial integrity and cellular bioenergetics.  
• Biological material – ARPE-19 or iPSC-derived RPE monolayers in 96-well plates. After 2 h challenge with H₂O₂ (100–200 µM), cells are treated with test compounds for 16–24 h prior to TMRE and ATP measurements.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Mitochondrial dysfunction is central in dry AMD: Multiple groups (Ferrington et al., 2017; Ferrington et al., 2020) have shown RPE from AMD donors exhibit lower Δψm, reduced complex I activity, and impaired oxidative phosphorylation.  
• Oxidative stress as a driver: Environmental (light, smoking) and endogenous (lipofuscin accumulation) sources generate reactive oxygen species in the subretinal space. RPE mitochondria are repeatedly hit, leading to cumulative DNA damage, lipid peroxidation, and ATP depletion (Beatty et al., 2000; Jang et al., 2017).  
• Clinical correlations: In vivo imaging of atrophic regions shows thinning of RPE and photoreceptors, consistent with impaired RPE support functions driven by mitochondrial failure (Pennington and DeAngelis, 2016). Low serum markers of mitochondrial health correlate with accelerated geographic atrophy progression (Neale et al., 2018).  
• Therapeutic relevance: Rescue of Δψm and ATP protects RPE against apoptosis and sustains phagocytic capacity, two processes compromised in dry AMD (Zhou et al., 2019; Han et al., 2020).  

Previous Use  
• Screening antioxidants and mitochondrial enhancers: Early screens using ARPE-19 + H₂O₂ identified novel small-molecule antioxidants (e.g., novel triterpenoids) that preserved TMRE signal and ATP levels (Goldstein et al., 2018).  
• Discovery of mito-protective peptides: Investigators applied this dual‐readout to validate mitochondria‐targeted peptides that restore Δψm and delay RPE cell death under oxidative stress (Zhou et al., 2019).  
• Mechanistic insights: Co-application of autophagy inhibitors showed that mitochondrial rescue correlates with improved mitophagy flux in RPE, linking energetic status to organelle quality control (Kannan et al., 2021).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Disease relevance – Directly models a well-validated driver of RPE atrophy in dry AMD.  
• Throughput – 96-well compatible; dual readout enables internal cross-validation.  
• Quantitative and sensitive – TMRE and ATP luminescence have broad dynamic ranges, permitting detection of partial as well as full rescue.  
• Adaptability – Can be extended to primary or patient-derived iPSC-RPE, and combination with high-content imaging to capture additional endpoints (ROS, cell death).  

Weaknesses  
• Model fidelity – ARPE-19 cells differ in mitochondrial content and polarization from native RPE; H₂O₂ is an acute stressor and may not recapitulate chronic low-grade oxidative injury seen in vivo.  
• Single-axis focus – Neglects complementary disease processes (inflammation, complement activation, drusen formation, autophagic dysfunction) that also contribute to RPE loss.  
• False positives/negatives – General mitochondrial poisons or uncouplers may artifactually influence TMRE or ATP signal without true therapeutic relevance.  
• Endpoint timing – Optimal timing and dosing for injury and treatment can vary between cell sources; requires careful titration.  

Conclusion  
The mitochondrial function rescue assay offers a robust, high-throughput platform targeting a core pathogenic mechanism of dry AMD—RPE energy failure under oxidative stress. Its dual readout of Δψm and ATP provides complementary validation of compound activity. To maximize translational value, it should be implemented in disease-relevant RPE models (e.g., patient iPSC-RPE), potentially multiplexed with inflammatory or phagocytic endpoints, and integrated into a panel of assays reflecting the multifactorial nature of dry AMD.
