Proposal for MSL-7 (Cayman Chemical catalog #14818), a potent small-molecule TFEB activator via calcium–calcineurin signaling.

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
MSL-7 is a potent, synthetic small‐molecule activator of transcription factor EB (TFEB) that was discovered through high‐content screening methodologies and is commercially available (Cayman Chemical catalog #14818). This candidate belongs to a novel class of autophagy enhancers that exploit the calcium–calcineurin signaling pathway rather than the classical mTOR inhibition mechanism to induce nuclear translocation of TFEB (Lim et al., 2018). Unlike many mTOR inhibitors that produce a broader range of kinase and metabolic inhibition with considerable off‐target effects, MSL-7 is designed to selectively elevate cytosolic calcium in target cells, leading to the activation of the phosphatase calcineurin and subsequent dephosphorylation of TFEB (Lim et al., 2018). Subsequently, dephosphorylated TFEB translocates to the nucleus, where it binds to coordinated lysosomal expression and regulation (CLEAR) motifs and activates the transcription of genes involved in lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Chen et al., 2021). As a member of the small-molecule TFEB activators, MSL-7 represents a targeted therapeutic approach that has been primarily studied for metabolic disorders and neurodegenerative conditions and now is being considered for repurposing in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) due to the critical role of lysosomal dysfunction in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) pathology (Lim et al., 2018; Abokyi & Tse, 2025).

Therapeutic History:  
The therapeutic history of molecules that modulate TFEB activity spans multiple disease areas, including metabolic syndrome, neurodegeneration, and lysosomal storage disorders. MSL-7 itself has been evaluated in preclinical models for metabolic syndrome, showing promising metabolic improvements via its activation of the calcium–calcineurin–TFEB axis (Lim et al., 2018). In these studies, MSL-7 reversed metabolic changes through enhanced autophagic flux and improved lysosomal clearance, setting a precedent for its application in diseases where accumulation of toxic aggregates is central to pathology (Lim et al., 2018). Although MSL-7 has not been directly used for the treatment of dry AMD in clinical studies, considerable evidence from biochemical and cellular studies supports the concept that activation of TFEB can ameliorate age-dependent RPE dysfunction by promoting the clearance of lipofuscin and other toxic aggregates (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Falcão et al., 2025). Prior studies of other TFEB activators—such as trehalose, which has been shown to enhance autophagic clearance in RPE cells—provide a robust rationale for the application of MSL-7 in retinal degenerative models (Abokyi & Tse, 2025). Moreover, the involvement of the autophagy–lysosomal network in maintaining retinal health and its impairment in AMD have been well documented, suggesting that strategies to enhance autophagic clearance via TFEB activation are both mechanistically sound and therapeutically appealing (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Falcão et al., 2024).

Mechanism of Action:  
The molecular mechanism of MSL-7 centers on its ability to modulate intracellular calcium dynamics, which is critical for the activation of the phosphatase calcineurin. Under physiological conditions, TFEB remains phosphorylated and sequestered in the cytoplasm by the action of mTORC1 and possibly other kinases; however, when MSL-7 is applied, it elevates cytosolic calcium levels specifically in RPE cells, thereby leading to the activation of calcineurin via calcium–calmodulin interactions (Lim et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2020). Activated calcineurin then dephosphorylates TFEB at key serine residues, resulting in its release from cytoplasmic retention complexes and enabling its translocation to the nucleus (Lim et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2020). Once in the nucleus, TFEB binds to CLEAR network promoters, initiating the transcription of a suite of genes that are essential for lysosomal biogenesis, catabolic enzyme production, and enhanced autophagic flux (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Chen et al., 2021). This transcriptional upregulation leads to an increased lysosomal capacity, improved degradation of intracellular aggregates, and enhanced recycling of damaged organelles, which is particularly beneficial in the context of RPE cells that are burdened with the accumulation of lipofuscin and A2E—byproducts of photoreceptor outer segment degradation (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Falcão et al., 2024). The specificity of MSL-7 for the calcium–calcineurin pathway is a significant advantage, as it circumvents typical mTOR-dependent pathways and thereby minimizes the risk of widespread metabolic disruption, offering a focused therapeutic effect on the autophagy–lysosomal system (Lim et al., 2018). In addition, the robustness of the mechanism is enhanced by the fact that TFEB activation has been shown to be beneficial in a number of models related to protein aggregation and cellular clearance, including neuronal and hepatic systems, thus providing a biochemical blueprint for its potential utility in RPE repair and dry AMD intervention (Abokyi & Tse, 2025).

Expected Effect:  
When applied to human iPSC-derived RPE models of dry AMD that exhibit lipofuscin accumulation, MSL-7 is hypothesized to restore cellular homeostasis by robustly enhancing autophagic flux and lysosomal biogenesis. The expected cellular effects include an elevated clearance of lipofuscin-like granules and A2E, which are hallmark toxic deposits in RPE cells and are directly implicated in the pathogenesis of dry AMD (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Falcão et al., 2025). By upregulating CLEAR network genes via the nuclear activity of TFEB, MSL-7 is anticipated to increase the number and functional capacity of lysosomes, thereby accelerating the degradation of damaged cellular components and misfolded proteins that contribute to RPE atrophy (Falcão et al., 2024; Abokyi & Tse, 2025). This enhanced clearance mechanism is particularly relevant given that the accumulation of oxidized biomolecules and damaged organelles in RPE cells is a critical factor that underlies aging-associated retinal degeneration, as evidenced by multiple studies linking TFEB activity to neuroprotection in the retina (Abokyi & Tse, 2025). Moreover, the augmentation of autophagic capacity is expected not only to mitigate the deleterious buildup of cellular waste but also to reduce pro-inflammatory signaling and oxidative stress—a dual mechanism that could slow disease progression and preserve visual acuity in patients with dry AMD (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Falcão et al., 2025). In vitro assays using human iPSC-derived RPE cells will offer a rigorous platform to measure these effects, including assessments of lysosomal marker expression, autophagy flux (as evidenced by LC3-II conversion), and quantification of lipofuscin reduction (Falcão et al., 2024; Abokyi & Tse, 2025). Additionally, the selective reactivation of lysosomal degradation pathways through TFEB translocation is expected to help “reset” the metabolic and catabolic balance in aged RPE cells, thereby contributing to cellular rejuvenation and neuroprotection in the retinal environment (Abokyi & Tse, 2025).

Overall Evaluation:  
The overall evaluation of MSL-7 as a potential therapeutic candidate for dry AMD is highly encouraging, primarily based on its mechanistic precision, preclinical dosing feasibility, and the strong theoretical framework underpinning TFEB activation in cellular clearance. One of the key strengths of MSL-7 lies in its ability to selectively manipulate the calcium–calcineurin–TFEB axis without broadly affecting other kinase-driven pathways such as mTOR. This specificity offers the promise of minimizing off-target effects and systemic toxicity, an important consideration particularly in treatments aimed at chronic conditions like dry AMD where long-term administration is likely (Lim et al., 2018). The established efficacy of similar TFEB activators in metabolic and neurodegenerative paradigms further supports the translational potential of MSL-7; studies have demonstrated that enhancing autophagic flux via TFEB leads to dramatic improvements in lysosomal function and aggregate clearance, outcomes that are directly translatable to the pathological features observed in AMD (Abokyi & Tse, 2025). Moreover, the experimental paradigm of using human iPSC-derived RPE models represents a state-of-the-art strategy, enabling direct observation of the drug’s effect on human retinal cells that model the disease pathology, thereby increasing the relevance of preclinical findings to clinical outcomes (Abokyi & Tse, 2025).

Despite these strengths, several challenges and weaknesses must be acknowledged. First, while the mechanism of action of MSL-7 is well elucidated in non-ocular systems, direct evidence for its efficacy and safety in retinal models is still emerging; specifically, there remains a gap in data demonstrating effective delivery, sustained bioavailability, and long-term safety in ocular tissues (Lim et al., 2018; Abokyi & Tse, 2025). It is also critical to rigorously assess whether the elevation of cytosolic calcium, while sufficient to activate calcineurin, might inadvertently trigger other calcium-dependent pathways that could lead to cytotoxicity or dysregulation of normal RPE function over extended periods (Lim et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2020). Additionally, although the preclinical pharmacokinetic profiles in murine studies have been reported to be favorable, ocular pharmacokinetic and biodistribution assessments are essential given the unique anatomical and physiological barriers presented by the eye (Lim et al., 2018; Abokyi & Tse, 2025). Another potential limitation is related to the scalability of the therapeutic effect in the complex environment of the human retina, where multiple pathways contribute to disease progression, and it is conceivable that combination therapies may be required to achieve substantial clinical improvement in dry AMD patients (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Falcão et al., 2024). Moreover, while the calcineurin–TFEB pathway is a promising target, its modulation must be finely balanced to avoid excessive autophagic activation, which in some contexts has been associated with detrimental cellular outcomes or even oncogenesis (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Benedetti, 2020).

In summary, the evidence supporting MSL-7 as a repurposed candidate for dry AMD is compelling based on its mechanistic action that directly addresses the pathological accumulation of toxic cellular debris in RPE cells. The ability of MSL-7 to selectively induce TFEB activation via a calcium–calcineurin-mediated pathway distinguishes it from less specific autophagy enhancers and offers the dual benefit of enhanced lysosomal capacity along with improved autophagic flux (Lim et al., 2018). The accumulated literature describing the role of TFEB in neuroprotection and autophagy in the retina, coupled with the favorable pharmacological profile observed in other disease models, provides a strong foundation for the potential translation of MSL-7 into a viable therapeutic option for dry AMD (Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Falcão et al., 2025).

Given these considerations, further investigations into MSL-7 should include comprehensive in vitro assays using human iPSC-derived RPE cells to quantify improvements in autophagic flux and the clearance of lipofuscin and A2E aggregates, as well as detailed in vivo studies employing animal models that recapitulate features of dry AMD (Abokyi & Tse, 2025). Parallel efforts should also focus on optimizing drug delivery techniques to the retina to ensure adequate ocular distribution while minimizing systemic exposure. Detailed toxicological assessments will be necessary to determine the long-term safety profile of MSL-7 in retinal tissues, particularly given the chronic nature of AMD treatment regimens (Lim et al., 2018).

In conclusion, while there are clear hurdles that must be overcome before MSL-7 can be considered for clinical trials in dry AMD patients, the mechanistic rationale and preclinical promise of this candidate justify continued research and development. The specificity of MSL-7’s action via the calcium–calcineurin–TFEB pathway provides a targeted approach to enhance the autophagy–lysosomal network in RPE cells, thereby addressing one of the core pathological processes in dry AMD. Continued corroborative data, robust preclinical studies, and strategic formulation approaches will ultimately determine whether MSL-7 can fulfill its promise as a novel therapeutic avenue for this currently unmet clinical need (Lim et al., 2018; Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Abokyi & Tse, 2025).
