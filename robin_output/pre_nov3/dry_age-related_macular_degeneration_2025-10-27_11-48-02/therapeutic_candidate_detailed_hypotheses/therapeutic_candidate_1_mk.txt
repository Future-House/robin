Proposal for MK-2206 (Selleckchem catalog #S1078), an allosteric pan-AKT inhibitor with high potency against AKT2.

I. Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
MK-2206 is a small‐molecule, allosteric inhibitor that belongs to the class of pan‐AKT inhibitors, originally developed as an oncology therapeutic to target the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. It was discovered through iterative medicinal chemistry efforts employing high‐throughput screening and structure‐based drug design to optimize for potency, selectivity, and oral bioavailability. Unlike ATP‐competitive inhibitors, MK-2206 binds to an allosteric site on AKT isoforms, thereby altering their conformation and attenuating kinase activity without directly competing with ATP. This class of compounds has a well‐documented history in oncology due to the central role of AKT in promoting cell proliferation, survival, and resistance to apoptotic signals, and MK-2206 has been extensively characterized in both in vitro and in vivo models of cancer (Coleman et al., 2021; Rehan et al., 2014). Its chemical synthesis and subsequent optimization yielded an orally bioavailable compound with a favorable ADME (absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion) profile and a defined maximum tolerated dose (MTD) in phase I clinical trials, making it a strong candidate for potential repurposing (Sangai et al., 2012).

II. Therapeutic History  
Historically, MK-2206 has been the subject of a wide range of preclinical and clinical investigations in the field of oncology. In early clinical studies, MK-2206 was evaluated as a monotherapy in advanced solid tumors and in combination with other agents—such as paclitaxel in metastatic breast cancer and with gefitinib in non-small cell lung cancer—in order to exploit its ability to abrogate AKT-mediated resistance mechanisms (Coleman et al., 2021). The compound has demonstrated its capacity to decrease levels of phosphorylated AKT at Threonine 308 and Serine 473 in treated cell lines and xenograft models, thereby diminishing downstream signaling cascades that promote tumor cell survival (Sangai et al., 2012). Despite these advances in oncology, the application of MK-2206 has not been previously reported in ocular conditions, including dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD). However, recent genetic and functional studies have implicated hyperactive AKT2 signaling in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) as a driver of lysosomal dysfunction in dry AMD (Buniello et al., 2025). This emerging evidence, coupled with the wealth of preclinical and clinical data on MK-2206, provides a compelling rationale for repurposing the compound to target dry AMD, particularly in cases associated with CFH variants and other genetic risk factors influencing lysosomal/autophagy pathways.

III. Mechanism of Action  
The molecular mechanism of MK-2206 centers on its ability to bind allosterically to AKT isoforms, including AKT1 and AKT2, and induce conformational changes that inhibit the enzyme’s catalytic function. This binding event results in a dose-dependent reduction in the phosphorylation of AKT targets, thereby curtailing the downstream signaling that is critical for cell survival and proliferation (Rehan et al., 2014; Sangai et al., 2012). In the context of cancer, these changes translate to a reduction in markers of tumor proliferation and, in combination studies, enhanced cytotoxic effects when paired with chemotherapeutic agents (Coleman et al., 2021; Sangai et al., 2012).

For dry AMD, the mechanistic interest lies in the role of AKT2 in the pathology of the disease. Recent literature indicates that hyperactivation of AKT2 in RPE cells leads to phosphorylation of transcription factor EB (TFEB), a master regulator of the Coordinated Lysosomal Expression and Regulation (CLEAR) network (Buniello et al., 2025). Phosphorylated TFEB is sequestered in the cytosol, thereby preventing its nuclear translocation and the induction of genes required for lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy. Under conditions of chronic oxidative stress and extracellular deposit accumulation—hallmarks of dry AMD—impaired TFEB function leads to lysosomal insufficiency, accumulation of toxic waste such as lipofuscin and A2E, and ultimately RPE dysfunction. MK-2206, by inhibiting hyperactive AKT2, is hypothesized to relieve the phosphorylation blockade on TFEB. This would allow TFEB to translocate to the nucleus, activate the expression of the CLEAR network, and restore lysosomal biogenesis and autophagic flux in RPE cells (Buniello et al., 2025).

Numerous computational and molecular docking studies have further characterized the interaction between MK-2206 and its targets. These studies have pinpointed the key residues within the AKT kinase domain that interact with MK-2206, thereby delineating a molecular basis for the observed decrease in p-AKT levels upon treatment (Rehan et al., 2014; Pal et al., 2010). The specificity of MK-2206 for AKT isoforms is crucial given that complete ablation of all AKT signaling could be deleterious; the goal is instead to selectively dampen the hyperactive AKT2 signaling that is deleterious in RPE cells while avoiding disruption of essential survival pathways in other tissues (Song et al., 2019; Buniello et al., 2025).

IV. Expected Effect  
In the proposed translational paradigm for dry AMD, MK-2206 is expected to exert a beneficial effect on RPE cell physiology by reversing the lysosomal and autophagic defects that underpin the disease pathology. In vitro assays using induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)–derived RPE cells—cells that recapitulate many aspects of native RPE function—have shown that aberrant AKT2 activity correlates with decreased CLEAR network expression, accumulation of extracellular waste deposition, and compromised mitochondrial function (Buniello et al., 2025). MK-2206 is hypothesized to counteract these pathological changes via its inhibition of AKT2. By reducing AKT2-mediated phosphorylation of TFEB, the compound is expected to facilitate TFEB nuclear translocation, thereby restoring transcriptional activation of lysosomal and autophagy genes. The net effect of this intervention would include enhanced lysosomal biogenesis, improved autophagic flux, and accelerated clearance of deleterious lipofuscin aggregates and A2E deposits—a key pathological substrate in dry AMD.

In practical terms, in an iPSC-derived RPE assay, treatment with MK-2206 would likely be monitored through several molecular and functional endpoints. First, a reduction in phosphorylated AKT (particularly at sites indicative of AKT2 activity) would be assessed using Western blot analysis with phospho-specific antibodies (Sangai et al., 2012). Concurrently, the nuclear versus cytosolic localization of TFEB could be evaluated via immunocytochemistry or subcellular fractionation, with an expected increase in nuclear TFEB following treatment. Downstream, transcriptional profiling of CLEAR network genes (which are known to be critical for lysosomal function) would be performed, with anticipated upregulation indicating restored lysosomal biogenesis. Furthermore, functional assays to evaluate autophagy, such as monitoring LC3-II accumulation and p62 degradation kinetics, would provide additional supportive evidence that MK-2206 improves autophagic flux in RPE cells. These cellular changes should correlate with a reduction in extracellular waste deposits and improved RPE mitochondrial function, as observed in analogous preclinical studies using genetic and pharmacological modulation of the AKT2/TFEB axis (Buniello et al., 2025; Pal et al., 2010).

Preclinical validation in animal models, such as those employing genetically modified mice that replicate the CFH Y402H variant or those with AKT2 hyperactivation, would be crucial to further confirm these mechanistic insights. In such models, MK-2206 administration is expected to result in measurable improvements in retinal structure and function, as assessed by techniques like electroretinography (ERG), optical coherence tomography (OCT), and histological analysis of the RPE. The translational attractiveness of MK-2206 is augmented by its well-documented pharmacokinetic safety profile from oncology trials, which provides confidence that repurposing the drug for ocular indications will have a decreased barrier to clinical testing (Coleman et al., 2021; ClinicalTrials.gov, 2008).

V. Overall Evaluation  
When considering MK-2206 as a candidate for repurposing in the treatment of dry AMD, several strengths and weaknesses emerge from the comprehensive literature review.

Strengths:  
One of the principal advantages of MK-2206 lies in its well-characterized profile in oncology. The robust clinical and preclinical data demonstrate its efficacy in reducing p-AKT levels and inhibiting downstream signaling pathways that contribute to pathological cell proliferation and survival (Coleman et al., 2021; Rehan et al., 2014). This same mechanism of action is directly relevant to the pathogenesis of dry AMD, where hyperactive AKT2 disrupts the normal function of TFEB and the CLEAR network, leading to lysosomal dysfunction (Buniello et al., 2025). The fact that MK-2206 is orally bioavailable and has undergone extensive evaluation in phase I and II clinical studies provides a clear translational pathway, as pharmacokinetics, dosing regimens, and safety profiles are already established. This reduces the time and cost associated with early drug development phases, a considerable advantage when repurposing a drug for a new indication. In addition, the specificity of an allosteric inhibitor like MK-2206 offers a mechanism to modulate AKT activity in a context-dependent manner that is less likely to completely ablate signaling and cause systemic toxicity, which is particularly important in a chronic condition such as dry AMD (Sangai et al., 2012). Moreover, the hypothesis that MK-2206 will restore TFEB nuclear translocation provides a mechanistically coherent explanation for how targeting AKT2 can lead to improved lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy in RPE cells—a critical unmet need in dry AMD. This therapeutic intervention targets the core defect in lysosomal/autophagy dysfunction, which is supported by contemporary genetic studies linking AKT2 hyperactivation to accumulation of drusen-associated materials and cellular stress in the RPE (Buniello et al., 2025).

Weaknesses:  
Despite these promising attributes, several potential challenges must be considered in repurposing MK-2206 for dry AMD. First, while extensive oncology data exist, the therapeutic context of dry AMD poses unique challenges related to the ocular environment. The pharmacokinetic behavior of MK-2206 in retinal tissues and its ability to effectively penetrate the blood-retina barrier remain largely uncharacterized. The biodistribution in ocular tissues may differ significantly from that in solid tumors, necessitating additional preclinical studies to confirm effective tissue concentrations and minimize off-target effects in the eye. Second, although MK-2206 inhibits pan-AKT activity, a complete inhibition of AKT signaling might interfere with normal cellular functions in the RPE and other retinal cells. It will be crucial to achieve a balance where hyperactive AKT2 is specifically modulated without precipitating a global disruption of AKT-dependent survival pathways, which could potentially lead to adverse effects, including impaired cellular viability or unintended retinal toxicity (Song et al., 2019; Pal et al., 2010). Third, in oncology studies, a range of dose-limiting toxicities were reported, such as a dose-dependent skin rash and gastrointestinal disturbances; while these may be less critical in an ocular formulation, systemic exposure remains a concern when repurposing for a chronic, non-life-threatening condition like dry AMD (Coleman et al., 2021; Nitulescu et al., 2018). Furthermore, the existing clinical data on MK-2206 have focused on acute dosing regimens in cancer patients, and long-term safety data in the context of chronic administration for ocular indications are lacking. Finally, it is important to recognize that the majority of evidence linking hyperactive AKT2 and TFEB dysregulation to dry AMD originates from genetic and preclinical models, and the translation of these findings to human disease will require rigorous validation in relevant animal models and early-phase clinical trials (Buniello et al., 2025).

VI. Detailed Discussion and Future Directions  
The rationale for targeting the AKT2/TFEB axis in dry AMD is rooted in a deeper understanding of the molecular pathology of the disease. In normal RPE cells, TFEB plays a central role in orchestrating the expression of lysosomal genes and maintaining autophagic flux—a process essential for clearing the daily photoreceptor outer segment shedding and other intracellular waste products. In dry AMD, chronic oxidative stress, compounded by genetic predispositions such as the CFH Y402H variant, results in hyperactivation of AKT2. This hyperactivation leads to aberrant phosphorylation of TFEB, effectively sequestering it in the cytosol and thereby impairing the transcriptional activation of the CLEAR network (Buniello et al., 2025). Such dysregulation results in a cascade of pathological events, including impaired lysosomal function, accumulation of lipofuscin and A2E, and ultimately RPE cell degeneration.

MK-2206 is uniquely positioned to counteract this process because its primary mode of action is to inhibit the hyperactive kinase activity of the AKT isoforms, including AKT2, thereby reducing the inhibitory phosphorylation on TFEB. An effective restoration of TFEB nuclear translocation would, in theory, reinitiate the transcription of genes required for lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy. This molecular intervention has the potential to not only clear accumulated waste products but also to restore mitochondrial function in the RPE—an effect observed in preclinical studies involving other modulators of the AKT2 pathway in genetically modified mouse models and iPSC-derived RPE cells (Buniello et al., 2025).

Recent computational insights into the binding dynamics of MK-2206 further bolster the candidate’s credibility. Molecular docking studies have pinpointed the specific interactions between MK-2206 and the ATP-noncompetitive binding site of AKT, describing how its interaction with critical residues reduces the phosphorylation of downstream targets (Rehan et al., 2014). Such studies provide not only confirmatory evidence for the mechanism of action but also suggest that further optimization or modification of the inhibitor could potentially enhance its selectivity for AKT2 versus other isoforms if needed—an area that would be particularly beneficial for minimizing side effects in the sensitive retinal environment.

Preclinical efforts utilizing iPSC-derived RPE models are particularly informative because they closely mimic the cellular architecture and function of native RPE cells. In these models, pharmacological inhibition of AKT2 using MK-2206 is expected to reverse the phenotype associated with lysosomal dysfunction—namely, the accumulation of intracellular debris, reduced autophagic flux, and compromised mitochondrial activity. In studies that have investigated lysosomal clearance in the context of AMD, an intervention that restores TFEB nuclear localization has been associated with improvements in cell viability and an enhanced capacity to manage oxidative stress (Buniello et al., 2025). Moreover, if MK-2206 treatment results in measurable upregulation of CLEAR network genes, this molecular signature would serve as a robust biomarker for effective target engagement in the RPE.

The translational pathway for MK-2206 can leverage the extensive clinical data accrued in oncology. Phase I clinical trials in cancer patients have established a dosing regimen and safety profile that, while optimized for tumor suppression, may need to be re-evaluated in the context of ocular drug delivery. The repurposing strategy could involve localized drug delivery systems, such as intravitreal injections or sustained-release ocular implants, to achieve effective drug concentrations in the retina while minimizing systemic exposure and potential adverse effects. Alternatively, the oral bioavailability of MK-2206 is an advantage in that it offers a non-invasive route of administration, though achieving therapeutic concentrations in the eye via systemic administration will require careful pharmacokinetic studies to elucidate its ability to cross the blood-retina barrier (Coleman et al., 2021).

One critical parameter that remains to be fully addressed in the context of dry AMD is the selectivity of MK-2206 for AKT2. While many studies have characterized MK-2206 as a pan-AKT inhibitor, preclinical data suggest that its inhibition kinetics may favor certain isoforms under specific conditions (Sangai et al., 2012; Song et al., 2019). The hypothesis posits that selective inhibition of hyperactive AKT2 in the RPE is sufficient to restore TFEB function without completely abolishing all AKT signaling. This nuanced approach is essential because basal AKT activity is required for normal cell survival and metabolism. Future studies should therefore include detailed isoform-specific assays to ensure that the dosing of MK-2206 can be calibrated to achieve the desired reduction in AKT2 activity while preserving the necessary cellular functions mediated by other isoforms.

In addition to these mechanistic considerations, the broader context of dry AMD as a multifactorial disease cannot be overlooked. Dry AMD involves not only lysosomal dysfunction but also chronic inflammation, alterations in extracellular matrix remodeling, and changes in immune cell behavior in the retina. Therefore, while restoring lysosomal biogenesis and autophagic clearance via AKT2 inhibition is a compelling therapeutic strategy, it is likely to need integration with a broader treatment plan that addresses these additional pathogenic components. Combination therapy—where MK-2206 is used together with agents targeting oxidative stress or anti-inflammatory compounds—might yield synergistic benefits and should be a focus of subsequent preclinical investigation.

VII. Conclusion  
In summary, the repurposing of MK-2206 for dry age-related macular degeneration is supported by a strong mechanistic rationale and an extensive body of preclinical and clinical evidence from oncology. Its well-validated mechanism as an allosteric pan-AKT inhibitor, combined with emerging data implicating hyperactive AKT2 in the lysosomal dysfunction observed in RPE cells, positions MK-2206 as a promising therapeutic candidate for dry AMD (Buniello et al., 2025). The expected benefits are manifold: by inhibiting AKT2, MK-2206 is anticipated to relieve the inhibitory phosphorylation of TFEB, enabling its nuclear translocation, which in turn should activate the CLEAR gene network responsible for maintaining lysosomal biogenesis and autophagic flux. These molecular events are expected to facilitate the clearance of toxic deposits such as lipofuscin and A2E, ultimately protecting the RPE from oxidative stress and drusen-associated damage.

The strengths of MK-2206 include its established clinical safety, oral bioavailability, and detailed pharmacodynamic profile obtained from oncology studies, which together streamline its potential repurposing process. However, several challenges remain, including the need to validate its ocular pharmacokinetics, ensure isoform selectivity in the complex retinal environment, and optimize dosing regimens to prevent potential adverse effects associated with global AKT inhibition. Moreover, long-term safety and efficacy studies in appropriate preclinical models of dry AMD are essential to bridge the gap between preclinical promise and clinical application.

Overall, MK-2206 represents an attractive candidate for repurposing in dry AMD based on a robust molecular hypothesis, favorable pharmacological properties, and supportive preclinical data suggesting that modulation of the AKT2/TFEB axis can reverse key pathological features of the disease. Continued investigation, including targeted in vitro studies using iPSC-derived RPE cells and relevant animal models, is warranted to further define the therapeutic window and to establish whether the promising benefits observed in preclinical settings translate into meaningful clinical outcomes in patients with dry AMD (Buniello et al., 2025; Coleman et al., 2021; Rehan et al., 2014).

This comprehensive evaluation underscores the potential for MK-2206 to be repurposed as a novel therapeutic for dry AMD, addressing an unmet clinical need by directly targeting the lysosomal/autophagy defect at its molecular root. The combination of prior clinical validation in oncology, mechanistic clarity regarding TFEB-mediated lysosomal biogenesis, and the emerging role of AKT2 in the pathogenesis of dry AMD makes MK-2206 a promising candidate in the evolving landscape of AMD therapy. Further research is essential to determine its efficacy within the unique microenvironment of the retina and to optimize treatment parameters that maximize therapeutic benefits while minimizing adverse effects, thereby providing a strong foundation for subsequent clinical trials in patients with dry AMD (Buniello et al., 2025; Sangai et al., 2012).
