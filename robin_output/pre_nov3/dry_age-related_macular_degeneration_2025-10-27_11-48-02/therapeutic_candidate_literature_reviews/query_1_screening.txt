Query: screening literature describing high‐throughput small‐molecule screening strategies using autophagy and lipofuscin clearance assays in primary human or iPSC‐derived RPE cells or relevant animal models recapitulating dry AMD pathology, highlighting target validation, efficacy data, mechanism confirmation, and ADME/safety profiles of identified modulators

High‐throughput screening approaches have been applied using primary human and iPSC‐derived RPE cells, as well as animal models, to identify small‐molecule modulators that enhance autophagy and lipofuscin clearance in dry AMD pathology (cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 1-2). One line of work has utilized oxidative stress assays to identify compounds such as ciclopirox olamine that improve cell viability, mitochondrial function, and modulate apoptotic gene expression in RPE cells, providing proof‐of‐concept for screening strategies aimed at mitigating oxidative damage (cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 1-2, cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 9-11). 

In parallel, several studies have focused on modulating the AKT2/TFEB pathway—a critical regulator of lysosomal function and autophagy. HTS strategies in iPSC‐derived RPE cells and corresponding animal models (e.g., CFH variant models, Akt2 KI mice) have identified compounds like trehalose and specific AKT2 inhibitors that restore CLEAR gene expression, reduce secretory autophagy defects, and diminish extracellular waste accumulation associated with early AMD changes (ghosh2023theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 11-15, ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 6-6, ghosh2023theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 34-40). These investigations have included target validation through mechanistic studies that confirm pathway engagement and restoration of lysosomal function. Moreover, some reports report preliminary ADME and safety profiles, emphasizing the translational potential of these modulators (ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 6-6). 

Additional models using hiPSC-derived RPE cells have been employed to assess the efficacy of candidate drugs in reducing drusen deposits and restoring epithelial characteristics, further validating autophagy and lipofuscin clearance as relevant endpoints for AMD screening (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 1-2, lin2021humanpluripotentstemcellderived pages 9-10).

These screening strategies demonstrate that targeting oxidative stress and the AKT2/TFEB axis can yield compounds with validated mechanisms of action and acceptable preclinical profiles. They represent promising approaches for the development of therapeutics in dry AMD.
