Query: survey small‐molecule modulators (including clinically approved drugs, investigational compounds, and novel chemical entities) that have demonstrated enhancement of lysosomal function, autophagic flux, or direct lipofuscin degradation in RPE or related ocular cell models, with evidence of target engagement, dose‐response relationships, and in vivo efficacy in dry AMD or AMD‐like models

Small‐molecule modulators that target the mTOR/TFEB axis have demonstrated robust enhancement of lysosomal function and autophagic flux in RPE models. For instance, rapamycin, along with investigational compounds such as INK‐128 and compound H, activates TFEB via mTORC1 inhibition, leading to a reduction of autofluorescent granules—reported at approximately 30%—with clear dose‐dependent effects in cultured RPE cells (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 10-13). Novel drug delivery strategies, including the use of rapalogs incorporated into synthetic high-density lipoprotein nanoparticles, have been developed to optimize target engagement and mitigate adverse effects, further supporting translational potential (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7). Additionally, NMDA receptor antagonists, particularly Ro 25-6981, have demonstrated direct lipofuscin degradation via autophagic induction in ARPE-19 cells, with concentration-dependent clearance of A2E, a major lipofuscin component (lee2022nmdareceptorantagonists pages 1-2, lee2022nmdareceptorantagonists pages 9-10, lee2022nmdareceptorantagonists pages 6-9). Other agents, including curcumin and metformin, promote autophagic flux through AMPK activation, offering complementary mechanisms to reduce oxidative stress and toxic lipid accumulation (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 18-20, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 20-21). Preclinical findings, supported by clinical trial activity, underline the potential of these agents in dry AMD or AMD-like models (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 26-29, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 1-2, Clinical Trial Search: 9943769873c5).

Rapamycin and NMDA receptor antagonists reliably enhance lysosomal function and promote lipofuscin degradation in ocular models. Their dose-dependent effects and evolving in vivo delivery strategies indicate promise for use in dry AMD therapy.
