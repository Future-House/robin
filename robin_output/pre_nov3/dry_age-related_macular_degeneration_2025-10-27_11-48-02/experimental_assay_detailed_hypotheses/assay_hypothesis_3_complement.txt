Assay Overview:  
This assay employs polarized RPE monolayers cultured on transwell inserts and exposes the basolateral compartment to complement-active serum or recombinant C3a/C5a, thereby mimicking the inflammatory stress observed in dry AMD. Real‐time TEER measurements monitor barrier integrity, while apical and basal media are collected for multiplex ELISA quantification of key analytes—complement fragments (C3a, C5a) and pro‐inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-18). This design uses well‐characterized RPE cellular models to emulate the disruption in barrier function associated with complement‐mediated injury (fernandezgodino2015alocalcomplement pages 9-10, fernandezgodino2015alocalcomplement pages 1-2).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Multiple studies confirm that complement activation is central to dry AMD pathogenesis by driving RPE dysfunction, basal deposit formation, and inflammatory cytokine release. The interplay between complement dysregulation and barrier compromise has been observed in polarized RPE cultures, underscoring the clinical relevance of TEER and cytokine assessments as surrogate markers of disease progression (thurman2009oxidativestressrenders pages 1-2, lueck2015complementstimulatesretinal pages 1-2). Such endpoints are directly aligned with current biomedical insights into AMD pathology (thurman2009oxidativestressrenders pages 6-7).

Previous Use:  
Prior research has successfully applied similar polarized RPE models to elucidate mechanisms of oxidative stress, complement-mediated injury, and extracellular matrix changes in AMD. These models have provided critical data on barrier integrity and inflammatory marker modulation, thereby guiding the preclinical evaluation of complement inhibitors (chinchilla2021amdlikesubstratecauses pages 2-4, rohrer2010atargetedinhibitor pages 3-7).

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay’s strength lies in its dual readout strategy, offering both functional (TEER) and immunological (cytokine/complement fragment) endpoints, which are scalable for drug screening. However, as an in vitro model, it may not fully recapitulate the complex in vivo retinal environment that includes choroidal and systemic influences, necessitating further validation in more complex models (fernandezgodino2015alocalcomplement pages 10-11, terheyden2021basolateralactivationwith pages 1-2).

This assay provides a robust platform for testing therapeutics aimed at preserving RPE barrier function through modulation of complement activation. Its scalable, dual-endpoint design supports its value in early-stage drug discovery for dry AMD.
