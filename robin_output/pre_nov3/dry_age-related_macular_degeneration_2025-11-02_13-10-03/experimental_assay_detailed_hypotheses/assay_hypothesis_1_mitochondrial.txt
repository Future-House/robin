Assay Overview  
This assay models oxidative‐stress–induced mitochondrial dysfunction in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), a central early event in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD).  
• Cells: Primary human RPE or hiPSC-derived RPE differentiated to a polarized monolayer in 96-well plates.  
• Insult: A defined dose of H₂O₂ or controlled blue-light exposure to evoke mitochondrial damage at levels observed in dry AMD donor tissue (Golestaneh et al., 2017; Ferrington et al., 2018).  
• Readouts:  
  – Oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) measured in real time using a Seahorse XF Analyzer to assess basal respiration, ATP-linked respiration, proton leak and maximal respiratory capacity.  
  – Mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mtROS) quantified by MitoSOX fluorescence; mitochondrial membrane potential optionally measured using TMRM or JC-1.  
  – Total cellular ATP by luminescent assay as an orthogonal bioenergetic endpoint.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Centrality of mitochondrial dysfunction: Post‐mortem RPE from dry AMD patients shows fragmented mitochondria, decreased respiratory complex activity and mtDNA damage (Feher et al., 2006; Golestaneh et al., 2017).  
• Oxidative stress link: The high metabolic rate of RPE and continual phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments generate ROS; chronic low-grade ROS correlates with drusen formation and geographic atrophy progression (Kinnunen et al., 2012; Ferrington et al., 2018).  
• Clinical correlations: Mitochondrial haplogroup variants modulate AMD risk; systemic markers of mitochondrial dysfunction (circulating cell‐free mtDNA) are elevated in patients with early atrophy (Punzo et al., 2012; Shu et al., 2020).  
• Therapeutic relevance: Antioxidant supplementation in AREDS slowed progression in intermediate AMD but did not restore RPE bioenergetics; direct mitochondrial rescue remains an unmet need (Age-Related Eye Disease Study Research Group, 2001).  

Previous Use  
• Zhang et al. (2019) employed this Seahorse-based RPE assay to demonstrate that the mitochondria-targeted antioxidant MitoQ and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) improved OCR and reduced mtROS after H₂O₂ stress, correlating with increased phagocytic capacity and tight-junction integrity.  
• Ferrington and colleagues (2018) used similar flux analyses in primary AMD RPE to show impaired spare respiratory capacity versus controls, establishing a disease‐relevant baseline for screening.  
• High-throughput adaptations have screened small‐molecule libraries for mitochondrial biogenesis enhancers (via PGC-1α activation) and inhibitors of mitochondrial permeability transition in ARPE-19 cells (Gao et al., 2020).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Direct, quantitative bioenergetic and oxidative endpoints mechanistically tied to RPE dysfunction in dry AMD.  
• Medium‐throughput (96-well) format amenable to dose–response and replicate testing in early‐stage screening.  
• Validated historical precedents demonstrating assay responsiveness to known mitochondrial modulators and clear separation between diseased and healthy RPE phenotypes.  

Weaknesses  
• H₂O₂ or acute blue-light insult may not fully recapitulate chronic, multifactorial oxidative stress in vivo (e.g., lipofuscin photo‐oxidation, complement‐mediated inflammation).  
• RPE monolayer lacks interactions with photoreceptors, Bruch’s membrane and choriocapillaris; absence of immune cells may miss immunometabolic crosstalk.  
• hiPSC-RPE lines exhibit donor‐to‐donor variability in mitochondrial content and stress responses, complicating reproducibility.  
• Focuses solely on mitochondrial endpoints; may overlook compounds acting on lipid metabolism, complement regulation or autophagy—also implicated in dry AMD pathogenesis.  

In summary, a mitochondrial function assay in RPE provides a biologically and clinically grounded platform for identifying compounds that restore bioenergetics and counteract oxidative damage. It is ideally used as part of a tiered screening cascade—prior to more complex co‐culture or organoid models—to de-risk candidates targeting mitochondrial health in dry AMD.
