Proposal for GW3965 (Liver X receptor agonist; Sigma-Aldrich catalog G7035)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
GW3965 is a synthetic, small-molecule agonist of the liver X receptors (LXRs), the oxysterol-sensing nuclear receptors encoded by NR1H3 (LXRα) and NR1H2 (LXRβ) that heterodimerize with RXR and regulate transcriptional programs governing cholesterol/lipid homeostasis and inflammatory gene expression (classically inducing ABCA1/ABCG1 and repressing NF-κB–responsive genes) (Choudhary et al., 2020; Sene & Apte, 2014). In retinal contexts, LXR expression is documented in human RPE and neural retina, and LXR-immunopositive fragments are present in drusen from AMD donor eyes, with RPE LXR expression declining with age—supporting biological relevance of this axis to AMD pathogenesis (Choudhary et al., 2020). As a therapeutic class, LXR agonists have been widely explored preclinically for diseases of lipid dysregulation and inflammation; they enhance cholesterol efflux via ABCA1/ABCG1 and attenuate proinflammatory signaling in myeloid and glial cells, and in ocular models have shown protective effects against lipid accumulation, gliosis, and choroidal neovascularization (CNV) depending on ligand and model (Lei et al., 2017; Sene & Apte, 2014; Tang et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2023). GW3965 and the related agonist TO901317 cross the blood–brain barrier in rodents, an important practical consideration for systemic delivery to reach posterior eye tissues; however, detailed ocular pharmacokinetics for GW3965 are not reported in the provided literature (Choudhary et al., 2020).

The LXR agonist class carries known on-target metabolic liabilities—hepatic lipogenesis and hypertriglyceridemia via SREBP-1c induction—with efforts focused on improving tissue selectivity or local delivery to mitigate systemic adverse effects; some ligands (e.g., TO901317) also exhibit nuclear receptor off-targets (FXR/PXR), a confound that is less emphasized for GW3965 but still warrants scrutiny in development (Choudhary et al., 2020; Sene & Apte, 2014). In sum, GW3965 is a prototypical nonsteroidal LXRα/β agonist in a class with robust mechanistic rationale for addressing lipid accumulation and inflammation in AMD-relevant tissues (Choudhary et al., 2020).

Therapeutic History  
- Systemic and CNS applications: LXR agonists reduce atherosclerotic lesion development in mice via ABCA1/ABCG1–dependent reverse cholesterol transport but are limited by hypertriglyceridemia and hepatic steatosis; in CNS injury, GW3965 improved functional recovery after mild repetitive traumatic brain injury in mice in an apoE-dependent manner, indicating efficacy in neuroinflammation/neurodegeneration models (Choudhary et al., 2020; Sene & Apte, 2014).  
- Retinal/ocular preclinical data with GW3965:  
  - Dry AMD–relevant lipid/inflammation model (apoB100 mice): Dietary GW3965 for 5 months induced LXR target engagement in RPE–choroid (Abca1 +54%, Srebp1c +35% in n = 2) and produced significant morphological and molecular improvements: fewer hypopigmented fundus regions, reduced retinal gliosis (decreased GFAP), 77% reduction of APOE+ Bruch’s membrane length fraction, and ~81% fewer ADGRE1+ subretinal macrophage-like cells (borderline significance), with ultrastructural preservation of RPE basal infoldings and fewer sub-RPE deposits; body weight and systemic HDL/LDL/VLDL were unchanged, suggesting no overt systemic lipid perturbation over the dosing period (Choudhary et al., 2020). These data validate posterior-segment exposure and support disease-modifying activity on extracellular lipoprotein deposition and local inflammation in a model of early dry AMD (Choudhary et al., 2020).  
  - Cellular models of RPE lipid and oxidative stress: In primary human RPE and macaque CECs, GW3965 increased LXR promoter activity and induced canonical LXR targets (FASN, SREBF1) in an LXR-dependent manner (siNR1H3/NR1H2 reversed activity), and showed ligand-selective protection—GW3965 uniquely prevented mitochondrial injury and neutral lipid accumulation under fatty acid overload (palmitic acid, α-linolenic acid), whereas TO901317 protected against oxidant injury (NaIO₃, H₂O₂, cigarette smoke extract) (Choudhary et al., 2020). In oxysterol (7-ketocholesterol, 7KCh) injury models, systemic or cellular GW3965 reduced lipid droplet accumulation, decreased 7KCh deposition, preserved mitochondrial membrane potential, reduced ROS and apoptosis, and promoted mitophagy (reduced p62, LC3I/II, mTOR), with siLXRα partially abrogating mitochondrial protection, implicating LXRα as a key mediator (Xie et al., 2022). Notably, an earlier ARPE-19 study found no protection by 1 µM GW3965 against 25-OHC, 27-OHC, or 7-KC–induced cytotoxicity or mitochondrial membrane potential loss, underscoring model- and context-dependence of efficacy (Dasari, 2012).  
  - Ocular safety in mouse GW3965 studies is limited to statements of “absence of overt systemic toxicities” and unchanged systemic lipoproteins in the apoB100 experiment, with no detailed ocular PK or chronic ocular toxicology available in the provided documents; ARPE-19 viability decreases at ≥ 2.5 µM GW3965 suggests a narrow in vitro window but does not directly translate to ocular in vivo tolerability (Choudhary et al., 2020; Xie et al., 2022).  
- Class evidence with other LXR agonists in retina:  
  - Aβ₁–₄₀ retinal inflammation model: TO901317 (TO90) upregulated LXRα and ABCA1 in neuroretina and RPE/choroid, suppressed NF-κB signaling (reduced IκBα phosphorylation, reduced TNF-α/IL-6), inhibited the NLRP3–caspase-1–IL-1β pathway, lowered microglial activation, and preserved ERG function, highlighting dual anti-inflammatory and lipid-efflux pathway engagement via LXRα–ABCA1 (Lei et al., 2017).  
  - OxLDL-driven CNV: Intravitreal TO90 reduced CNV lesion area and lipid accumulation, suppressed NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation in RPE, and acted via ABCG1-mediated cholesterol handling in this context, indicating model- and tissue-specific transporter reliance (Wu et al., 2023).  
  - Topical ocular LXR agonist in aged mice restored tissue-specific cholesterol efflux capacity and decreased CNV, supporting local delivery as a strategy to avoid systemic metabolic AEs (Sene & Apte, 2014).  
- Target genetics and disease linkage:  
  - LXR pathway loss produces AMD-like pathology. Nr1h3−/− mice display extracellular neutral/oxidized lipid accumulation below RPE and within BrM (ORO+, E06+), APOE deposition, disrupted RPE basal infoldings, reduced choriocapillary fenestrations, and increased RPE autofluorescence; Nr1h2−/− mice accumulate large intracellular lipid droplets within RPE (lipoidal degeneration), indicating LXRα and LXRβ differentially regulate extracellular versus intracellular lipid homeostasis in the outer retina (Choudhary et al., 2020). LXR-deficient mice exhibit heightened proinflammatory mediators, whereas LXR activation represses inflammatory genes in ocular tissues (Nos2, Il6, Il1b), providing a mechanistic rationale for LXR agonism in dry AMD phenotypes (Choudhary et al., 2020).  
  - ABCA1/ABCG1 are essential for RPE lipid efflux; RPE-specific Abca1;Abcg1 deletion causes marked lipid accumulation, RPE morphological changes, and age-dependent retinal degeneration, with Abca1 playing a dominant role in early RPE lipid homeostasis (Storti et al., 2019). In iPSC-RPE and murine RPE/choroid exposed to hypoxia, LXR agonism increases ABCA1 expression and significantly increases cholesterol efflux while reducing intracellular lipid accumulation, though not fully normalizing lipid load—supporting class feasibility for correcting efflux defects in stress conditions (Peters et al., 2022).

No clinical trials or human ocular PK/safety for GW3965 in AMD are presented in the provided literature; complement readouts (C3/C5) and drusen clearance metrics are largely absent from GW3965-focused studies (Choudhary et al., 2020; Dasari, 2012). Claims that GW3965 modulates retinal complement (C3/C5) or reduces fundus C3b deposition are not supported in these sources (Choudhary et al., 2020; Dasari, 2012).

Mechanism of Action  
LXRs integrate lipid metabolism and inflammatory signaling through ligand-dependent transcriptional activation and transrepression mechanisms. GW3965 binds the ligand-binding pocket of LXRα/β, promoting heterodimerization with RXR and recruitment of coactivators to LXR response elements, upregulating cholesterol efflux and lipid-handling genes (e.g., ABCA1, ABCG1, APOE) and lipogenic genes (SREBF1, FASN), while transrepressing NF-κB–responsive inflammatory genes in myeloid and glial lineages (e.g., iNOS, COX-2, IL-6, IL-1β) (Choudhary et al., 2020; Choudhary & Malek, 2016; Sene & Apte, 2014). In RPE and choroidal endothelial models, LXR agonists induce FASN and SREBF1 in an LXR-dependent manner (siNR1H3/NR1H2 abrogates), confirming pathway engagement in AMD-vulnerable ocular cells; concomitantly, ligand-specific cytoprotective effects emerge under distinct stressors—GW3965 preferentially counters lipid overload and intracellular neutral lipid accumulation, aligning with its impact on lipid transport programs (Choudhary et al., 2020).

In vivo, dietary GW3965 in apoB100 mice increases LXR target expression (Abca1, Srebp1c) in RPE–choroid, decreases APOE deposition within Bruch’s membrane, reduces GFAP immunoreactivity, and lowers subretinal ADGRE1+ macrophage-like cells, consistent with enhanced cholesterol efflux and reduced extracellular lipoprotein deposition (Choudhary et al., 2020). Ultrastructurally, GW3965 preserves RPE basal infoldings and reduces sub-RPE deposits, consistent with restored lipid handling through LXR-controlled efflux programs (Choudhary et al., 2020).

At the level of innate immune signaling, LXR agonists suppress NF-κB activation by inhibiting IκBα phosphorylation/degradation and reducing nuclear translocation of p65, thereby reducing proinflammatory cytokine transcription; they also dampen activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome (reducing caspase-1 activation and mature IL-1β/IL-18) as shown convincingly with TO901317 in Aβ₁–₄₀ retinal inflammation (Lei et al., 2017). These anti-inflammatory mechanisms are compatible with the described LXR-dependent transrepression in macrophages, microglia, and astrocytes (e.g., suppression of iNOS, COX-2, TNF-α, IL-6) (Choudhary & Malek, 2016). While these data underpin strong anti-inflammatory effects of LXR activation, direct evidence that GW3965 downregulates complement genes (C3, C5) or reduces ocular complement activation in dry AMD models is not provided in the cited literature (Choudhary et al., 2020).

Mitochondrial protection and mitophagy modulation have emerged as additional LXR-mediated mechanisms in RPE under oxysterol stress; GW3965 preserves mitochondrial membrane potential, reduces ROS, and increases mitophagy flux (decreases p62, LC3I/II, mTOR), with siLXRα attenuating these effects—positioning LXRα as a principal isoform for mitochondrial homeostasis under lipid peroxidation stress (Xie et al., 2022). However, an ARPE-19 study reported no protection by 1 µM GW3965 against cytotoxicity or mitochondrial depolarization induced by several oxysterols, highlighting that efficacy depends on oxysterol species, concentrations, treatment timing, and cellular context (Dasari, 2012).

Genetic data clarify pathway control points: LXRα (Nr1h3) primarily governs extracellular lipid deposition and BrM/sub-RPE deposit formation; LXRβ (Nr1h2) controls intracellular RPE lipid droplets. Loss of LXR signaling establishes a proinflammatory state and compromises RPE structure, while pharmacological activation reverses many AMD-relevant readouts (Choudhary et al., 2020). In RPE lipid efflux, ABCA1 has a dominant role early, with ABCG1 contributing additively; loss of these transporters precipitates RPE degeneration, and LXR agonism can increase ABCA1 and cholesterol efflux in iPSC-RPE under hypoxia, reducing intracellular lipid burden (Peters et al., 2022; Storti et al., 2019).

Safety and selectivity considerations intrinsic to the mechanism include SREBP-1c–mediated lipogenesis and hypertriglyceridemia as on-target hepatic effects of systemic LXR agonists; off-target nuclear receptor activation is notable for TO901317 (FXR/PXR), whereas GW3965 is primarily an LXR agonist, a relative advantage if confirmed by broad profiling (Choudhary et al., 2020; Sene & Apte, 2014). Local ocular delivery has been shown to maintain efficacy (restored cholesterol efflux, reduced CNV) while potentially minimizing systemic lipogenesis (Sene & Apte, 2014).

Expected Effect  
Relative to the stated hypothesis—“GW3965 will modulate RPE lipid metabolism and innate immunity in dry AMD by activating LXRα/β, up-regulating cholesterol efflux transporters (ABCA1/G1) to clear drusen-associated lipids, and suppressing NF-κB–driven complement gene expression (C3, C5) and pro-IL-1β transcription”—the following expectations are supported or unsupported by the cited literature:

- RPE/outer-retina lipid metabolism and drusen-associated lipids:  
  • Supported: GW3965 activates LXR signaling in RPE–choroid in vivo (Abca1, Srebp1c induction), reduces sub-RPE/BrM lipoprotein deposition (APOE), preserves RPE ultrastructure, and reduces gliosis and subretinal macrophage accumulation in apoB100 mice (Choudhary et al., 2020). In vitro, GW3965 uniquely limits neutral lipid accumulation in RPE under fatty acid overload and protects mitochondria in lipid-stressed cells (Choudhary et al., 2020). GW3965 also reduces 7KCh/lipid droplet accumulation and oxidative stress in mouse retina and ARPE-19, with LXRα-dependence for mitochondrial stabilization, further supporting improved lipid handling/oxidative resilience (Xie et al., 2022). Given LXRα/β expression in human RPE and drusen localization, target expression is appropriate in the cell type being assayed (Choudhary et al., 2020).  
  • Mechanistic nuance: While ABCA1 upregulation is documented in vivo with GW3965, ABCG1 modulation was not reported in the apoB100 study; other retinal models emphasize ABCG1 (e.g., OxLDL/CNV with TO90), and RPE genetic studies assign a dominant early role to ABCA1 in efflux, implying transporter redundancy and context specificity (Choudhary et al., 2020; Storti et al., 2019; Wu et al., 2023). iPSC-RPE data show that LXR agonists can boost ABCA1 expression/efflux and reduce intracellular lipids under hypoxia, consistent with the hypothesis but without specific quantitative data for GW3965 in that model in the provided texts (Peters et al., 2022).  
- Innate immunity and inflammasome priming:  
  • Supported (class-level): LXR agonists transrepress NF-κB signaling and reduce proinflammatory mediators in myeloid/glial lineages; TO901317 in retina inhibited NF-κB (IκBα phosphorylation) and suppressed the NLRP3–caspase-1–IL-1β axis while upregulating LXRα/ABCA1 in neuroretina and RPE/choroid, preserving retinal function in Aβ-driven inflammation (Choudhary & Malek, 2016; Lei et al., 2017). GW3965 reduces markers of inflammation (GFAP, ADGRE1) in apoB100 mice, compatible with LXR-mediated immunomodulation in vivo (Choudhary et al., 2020).  
  • Not supported: Direct suppression of complement gene expression (C3, C5) or reduction in local complement activation (e.g., C3b deposition) by GW3965 is not demonstrated in the provided literature; complement endpoints were not reported in the apoB100 GW3965 experiments nor in the in vitro RPE studies cited (Choudhary et al., 2020). Although lipid dyshomeostasis can potentiate complement activation in AMD models, translation of LXR agonism to reduced ocular complement activation remains unproven in the current evidence base (Tang et al., 2024).  
- Assay-level outcomes we can reasonably expect:  
  • In RPE loaded with fatty acids or oxysterols: increased LXR target gene expression (SREBF1/FASN; ABCA1 in some contexts), decreased neutral lipid accumulation (Oil Red O), preserved mitochondrial membrane potential, reduced ROS and apoptosis, and increased mitophagy flux (Choudhary et al., 2020; Xie et al., 2022). A prior negative in vitro result under different oxysterol conditions underscores the need to align assay design with conditions where GW3965 efficacy has been observed (e.g., 7KCh or fatty acid overload) (Dasari, 2012).  
  • In vivo in lipid-driven early AMD models (e.g., apoB100): reduced APOE+ BrM burden, reduced GFAP and subretinal ADGRE1+ cells, improved OCT/fundus hypopigmentation, and preserved RPE ultrastructure; local Abca1/Srebp1c induction can serve as a posterior-segment target-engagement biomarker (Choudhary et al., 2020).  
  • Complement readouts (C3/C5, C3b): no expectation of effect can be justified based on the provided data; such endpoints must be prospectively tested (Choudhary et al., 2020).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Compelling target biology and human disease relevance: LXRα/β are expressed in human RPE/retina, decline with age, and LXR-immunopositive fragments localize to drusen in AMD eyes, directly linking the pathway to diseased tissue; genetic loss of LXRα/β recapitulates critical dry AMD features (extracellular BrM lipid deposition, oxidized lipid accumulation, RPE ultrastructural defects, and chronic inflammatory tone), providing strong target validation (Choudhary et al., 2020).  
- In vivo efficacy of GW3965 in a lipid-driven AMD-relevant model: In apoB100 mice, chronic dietary GW3965 engages LXR targets in RPE–choroid, reduces extracellular APOE deposition in Bruch’s membrane, improves retinal morphology and ultrastructure (RPE basal infoldings), and decreases gliosis and subretinal macrophages, collectively addressing core dry AMD pathobiology (lipid deposition and local inflammation) (Choudhary et al., 2020).  
- Mechanism-concordant cellular efficacy with ligand-specificity: In RPE models, GW3965 uniquely prevents lipid-induced mitochondrial injury and neutral lipid accumulation (contrasting with TO901317’s oxidant-protective bias), aligning with the central role of lipid dyshomeostasis in early dry AMD; GW3965 also protects against 7KCh lipotoxicity by stabilizing mitochondrial function and enhancing mitophagy via LXRα (Choudhary et al., 2020; Xie et al., 2022).  
- Class anti-inflammatory mechanisms that could secondarily reduce inflammasome priming: LXR agonists suppress NF-κB and reduce key cytokines; TO901317 in vivo demonstrates inhibition of the NLRP3–caspase-1–IL-1β axis while activating LXRα/ABCA1 in retina, indicating that LXR-driven lipid efflux and innate immune modulation can be co-engaged in ocular tissue (Lei et al., 2017).  
- Systemic exposure can reach the posterior segment; preliminary tolerability: LXR agonists cross the BBB and systemic GW3965 induced posterior eye target engagement (Abca1/Srebp1c) without changes in systemic HDL/LDL/VLDL in apoB100 mice over 5 months and without overt toxicities, supporting feasibility of oral or systemic routes for discovery-stage testing (Choudhary et al., 2020).

Weaknesses and Gaps  
- Complement pathway claims are unsupported for GW3965: No data in the provided literature show that GW3965 suppresses ocular C3/C5 expression or reduces local complement activation (e.g., C3b deposition) in AMD-relevant models; complement endpoints were not reported in the apoB100 GW3965 study or the in vitro RPE experiments presented (Choudhary et al., 2020). As such, the part of the hypothesis asserting complement suppression by GW3965 remains speculative.  
- Heterogeneity and context-dependence of in vitro efficacy: While several studies show robust protection by GW3965 under 7KCh or fatty acid overload, at least one ARPE-19 study documented no protection against cytotoxicity or mitochondrial depolarization induced by multiple oxysterols at 1 µM GW3965, highlighting dependence on ligand concentration, oxysterol species, timing, and cellular context (Dasari, 2012). This necessitates careful assay design and translational modeling.  
- Limited quantitative transporter data for GW3965 in ocular tissues: The apoB100 study reports Abca1/Srebp1c induction (small n) but not ABCG1; several cell studies measured FASN/SREBF1 without ABCA1/ABCG1 quantification; while class biology and genetic studies implicate ABCA1/ABCG1, direct, quantitative, dose–response data linking GW3965 to ABCA1/ABCG1 protein induction and functional efflux in human primary RPE are sparse in the provided literature (Choudhary et al., 2020; Storti et al., 2019).  
- Pharmacokinetics and safety: Ocular PK, tissue exposure, and ocular half-life are not defined for GW3965; systemic LXR agonists drive lipogenesis/hypertriglyceridemia via SREBP-1c and pose translational risk, although apoB100 mice did not show systemic lipoprotein changes under the reported conditions (Choudhary et al., 2020; Sene & Apte, 2014). TO901317 has known off-targets (FXR/PXR); while GW3965 is more selective, broad profiling in ocular tissues is not presented (Choudhary et al., 2020).  
- No human data in AMD and limited model breadth: There are no reported clinical trials or human ocular safety data for GW3965 in AMD; efficacy is established in one chronic lipid-driven mouse model (apoB100) with small sample sizes for some readouts, and in acute 7KCh models; validation across multiple dry AMD models (e.g., Cfh+/−, Abca4−/−, smoke exposure, aged Nr1h3−/−) with complement and functional endpoints is lacking (Choudhary et al., 2020; Tang et al., 2024).

Decision-Relevant Synthesis and Next Steps  
From a drug development perspective, GW3965 addresses two core pathobiologic features of early dry AMD—outer-retina lipid accumulation and chronic para-inflammation—through a single validated pathway with demonstrated ocular tissue engagement and morphologic rescue in vivo (apoB100), plus mechanistic cellular effects that match the lipid-driven disease milieu (Choudhary et al., 2020). These features support advancing GW3965 or an optimized LXR agonist into a rigorous preclinical program focused on dry AMD. However, the complement-suppression component of the stated hypothesis is not supported by the provided evidence and should be reframed: any effects of GW3965 on complement (C3/C5 expression or activation) remain untested and must be established de novo in AMD-relevant systems (Choudhary et al., 2020).

Given class liabilities, translation should prioritize ocular-targeted delivery (e.g., topical, suprachoroidal, or intravitreal formulations) to reduce systemic lipogenesis risk while achieving sufficient posterior-segment exposure; topical LXR agonism restored cholesterol efflux and reduced CNV in old mice, supporting the feasibility of local delivery (Sene & Apte, 2014). If systemic dosing is pursued for discovery-stage breadth, metabolic safety biomarkers (triglycerides, liver fat) should be rigorously monitored, and ocular target engagement confirmed by Abca1/Srebp1c induction in RPE–choroid (Choudhary et al., 2020).

Critical experiment set to de-risk and validate:  
- Pharmacology and PK/PD:  
  • Ocular PK in rodents and a larger species (e.g., rabbit, NHP) for GW3965, measuring retina/RPE–choroid concentrations after topical, periocular, and systemic dosing; correlate with ex vivo target gene induction (Abca1, Srebp1c) (Choudhary et al., 2020).  
- Mechanism in primary human RPE:  
  • Dose–response for GW3965 on ABCA1/ABCG1 mRNA/protein and functional cholesterol efflux (apoA-I/HDL acceptors), mitochondrial function (JC-1), ROS, and lipid accumulation (ORO/BODIPY) under physiologically relevant stresses (fatty acid overload, 7KCh, hypoxia); include siNR1H3/NR1H2 to define isoform contributions (Choudhary et al., 2020; Peters et al., 2022).  
- Disease model breadth and complement endpoints:  
  • Extend efficacy beyond apoB100 mice to models with complement dysregulation (e.g., Cfh+/− ± high-fat diet) and oxidative/lipid stress (Abca4−/−, cigarette smoke); measure complement transcripts (C3, C5), proteins (C3b/iC3b deposition in BrM/subretinal space), and activation (C5a) alongside lipid deposition (APOE, ORO/E06) and inflammation (GFAP, Iba1/ADGRE1) (Tang et al., 2024).  
- Functional outcomes:  
  • ERG and OCT endpoints to quantify photoreceptor/RPE function and structural preservation, as used in apoB100 and Aβ models (Choudhary et al., 2020; Lei et al., 2017).  
- Safety:  
  • Systemic lipids, hepatic histology, and ocular tolerability (IOP, corneal clarity, ERG) for local and systemic routes; refine formulation to maximize posterior-segment delivery with minimal systemic exposure (Sene & Apte, 2014).

Overall, GW3965 is a mechanistically coherent and preclinically supported candidate for dry AMD, with strengths in correcting lipid efflux defects and dampening local retinal inflammation, and with evidence of posterior-segment target engagement and morphologic rescue in a relevant mouse model (apoB100) (Choudhary et al., 2020). The major caveats are absence of demonstrated effects on ocular complement pathways, heterogeneity in in vitro oxysterol cytoprotection, limited ocular PK/safety data, and class metabolic liabilities—collectively necessitating a development strategy built around ocular delivery, rigorous PD biomarkers, and expansion into complement-relevant AMD models (Choudhary et al., 2020; Dasari, 2012; Sene & Apte, 2014).

Given these considerations, my recommendation is to advance GW3965 as a lead for ocular LXR agonism with the following framing: primary objective—demonstrate robust, dose-dependent correction of RPE lipid efflux and reduction of BrM/sub-RPE lipoprotein deposition and para-inflammation; exploratory objective—test whether LXR activation secondarily reduces complement priming/activation in models where complement is pathologically engaged, without assuming this effect a priori (Choudhary et al., 2020; Lei et al., 2017).
