Proposal for H-151 (STING palmitoylation inhibitor; Tocris catalog 6321)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
H-151 is a small-molecule STING antagonist identified by a cell-based chemical screen of nitrofuran-derived covalent ligands that target the STING palmitoylation site, a post-translational modification required for STING activation at the Golgi apparatus (Cys88/Cys91) (Wu et al., 2020; Hong et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023). H-151 covalently modifies Cys91 on STING to prevent activation-induced palmitoylation and thereby blocks downstream signaling; unlike the earlier mouse-biased C-176 and C-178 chemotypes, H-151 inhibits both human and mouse STING, making it more suitable for translational exploration (Hong et al., 2022). Across in vitro systems, H-151 shows low- to mid-micromolar cellular potency in reporter assays and sub-micromolar activity in some primary cell contexts; reported values include IC₅₀ ~134 nM in human foreskin fibroblasts (HFFs) and ~1 µM in engineered 293T reporter lines expressing human or mouse STING (Zhang et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2021). The compound is lipophilic and poorly water soluble (LogP > 3; pKₐ ~11.5), features that have driven formulation efforts such as lipid nanocapsules and mesoporous nanoparticle carriers in non-ocular indications (Guilbaud et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025).

Mechanistically and chemically, H-151 belongs to the class of covalent STING palmitoylation inhibitors that block Cys88/Cys91 palmitoylation to prevent STING clustering and TBK1 recruitment at the Golgi, a step required to license IRF3 and NF-κB signaling (Zhang et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2022). This class has been widely examined preclinically across sterile inflammatory diseases with diversified upstream DNA-damage/retroelement triggers—including TREX1 deficiency, Aicardi-Goutières syndrome models, chronic neuroinflammation, and metabolic/injury models—where STING antagonism reduces type I interferon and inflammatory cytokine production (Hong et al., 2022; Duggan et al., 2025). In retinal biology, STING is expressed in disease-relevant cell types (RPE, microglia) and is upregulated in macular tissue from dry AMD patients, providing a direct rationale to consider a STING antagonist for geographic atrophy (GA) (Hu et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022).

Therapeutic History  
No clinical use of H-151 has been reported; the compound and its class remain at a preclinical stage (Chang et al., 2023). Preclinical in vivo efficacy has been shown outside the eye. In Trex1⁻/⁻ auto-inflammation models, covalent STING palmitoylation inhibitors (including H-151) reduce systemic IFN responses, inflammatory gene expression and multi-tissue inflammatory pathology, establishing proof-of-mechanism for antagonizing chronic STING activation in vivo (Qu et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2020). In an acute intestinal ischemia–reperfusion model, intraperitoneal H-151 (10 mg/kg) reduced intestinal pIRF3, proinflammatory cytokines, neutrophil infiltration, tissue injury, and improved 24-h survival, with corroborating in vitro suppression of IFN-β in macrophage-like cells (Kobritz et al., 2022). The same covalent class has broadly ameliorated inflammation in diverse models (AGS, ALS, Niemann–Pick type C, chronic kidney disease, psoriasis), underscoring disease-agnostic anti-inflammatory potential, though these data are not ocular (Hong et al., 2022).

In ocular systems, multiple lines of evidence support STING pathway targeting but do not yet include retinal in vivo data for H-151 specifically. In ARPE-19 RPE cells, H-151 prevented VEGF overproduction induced by senescence/oxidative stress, directly implicating STING pharmacology in an RPE disease-relevant output (Hu et al., 2023). Genetic and pharmacologic attenuation of the cGAS–STING axis protects retinal tissues across oxidative and nucleic-acid–driven injury models: sodium iodate–induced retinal degeneration is exacerbated by a STING agonist and rescued by the mouse-biased STING inhibitor C-176 or by BRD4 inhibition with JQ1, which epigenetically represses STING and enhances autophagic clearance of cytosolic DNA (Hu et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022). In ischemic/angiogenic models, STING signaling is upregulated in retinal microglia and choroidal macrophages, and intravitreal C-176 or the CDN-pocket antagonist SN-011 suppress pathological angiogenesis and leakage; STING deficiency likewise blunts neovascularization, while a microglia-directed natural product (cryptotanshinone) reduces STING activation and disease readouts in OIR (Ni et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025). In AMD-relevant RPE degeneration paradigms, Alu DNA accumulation triggers cGAS–STING activation and downstream type I IFN–inflammasome cascades, and genetic ablation of cGAS, STING or downstream nodes (IFNAR, IRF3, caspase-4) rescues Alu DNA toxicity in rodents; human GA RPE are enriched in Alu DNA, supporting translational relevance (Hu et al., 2023). Collectively, the retina/RPE therapeutic precedent strongly supports STING pathway targeting per se, although no published in vivo retinal study of H-151 has been identified in the provided corpus, and the claim that intravitreal H-151 preserves RPE and ERG in mouse retinal injury models is not supported by these sources (Hu et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022; Ni et al., 2024).

Mechanism of Action  
Pathway context. Cytosolic double-stranded DNA—released from mitochondria or nuclei under oxidative stress, membrane damage, or retroelement biology—is sensed by cGAS to generate 2′3′-cGAMP, which binds and activates ER-resident STING (TMEM173) (Hu et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2019). STING translocates to the ERGIC/Golgi and requires palmitoylation at Cys88/Cys91 to form active clusters that recruit TBK1; downstream, IRF3 phosphorylation drives type I interferon programs and NF-κB signaling induces inflammatory cytokines and sensor/adaptor components that prime inflammasomes and SASP (Kobritz et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2019). In retinal disease, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and Alu DNA accumulation are convergent triggers for cGAS–STING activation in RPE and microglia; sustained activation amplifies cytokine production (IFN-β, IL-6), primes the noncanonical NLRP3 cascade (via type I IFN), and promotes apoptosis/necroptosis in retinal cells (Hu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023).

H-151 molecular pharmacology. H-151 covalently modifies STING at Cys91, a residue in the N-terminal region whose palmitoylation (along with Cys88) is essential for activation-induced clustering and signalosome assembly at the Golgi (Zhang et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2022). This covalent engagement inhibits STING palmitoylation, blocks TBK1 recruitment/activation, and thus prevents IRF3 phosphorylation and IFN-I induction in stimulated cells (Zhang et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2022). In multiple cellular systems, H-151 suppresses cGAMP-induced IRF and NF-κB reporter activation and reduces type I IFN and cytokine transcripts (e.g., Ifnb, Cxcl10, Il6) with sub- to low-micromolar potency (Hong et al., 2021; Chang et al., 2023). In murine macrophage-like lines and primary macrophage-enriched systems, H-151 reduces IFN-β and TNF-α after STING activation; for example, screening in J774 macrophages showed robust suppression of cGAS–STING outputs (–84 % IFN-β and TNF-α) and in RAW264.7 cells H-151 reduced rmCIRP-induced IFN-β (Guilbaud et al., 2025; Kobritz et al., 2022). In vivo, H-151 attenuates pIRF3 activation and cytokine production in the intestine after ischemia–reperfusion and improves survival (Kobritz et al., 2022). Biophysically, H-151 appears to prevent the Golgi-palmitoylation–dependent clustering step (sometimes described as blocking activation-induced “clustering” at the Golgi), consistent with the covalent Cys91 blockade (Qiu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2023). There is also evidence that STING can be routed to endolysosomal degradation as a homeostatic off-switch; by analogy to cryptotanshinone’s promotion of lysosomal STING degradation in microglia, some authors hypothesize H-151 may likewise shift STING toward lysosomal clearance, though this specific degradation mechanism for H-151 remains inferential in the provided texts (Qiu et al., 2025).

Species and allele considerations. The covalent palmitoylation class exhibits species selectivity: C-176/C-178 mainly inhibit mouse STING, whereas H-151 is active on human STING (Hong et al., 2022). Human STING genetic polymorphisms modulate signaling; common haplotypes (including the HAQ loss-of-function variant) differ in CDN responsiveness, with potential implications for pharmacodynamics and patient selection (Hu et al., 2023). In cellular pharmacology, H-151 potency is cell context and allele dependent: it is potent in THP-1 monocytes (which harbor the HAQ allele) but shows reduced potency in wild-type STING reporter cells and little activity in primary human monocytes/PBMCs, indicating important translational uncertainties for primary human tissues (Barasa et al., 2023). These observations raise a need to assay H-151 activity across relevant human retinal cell types (RPE, microglia) with native STING haplotypes.

Selectivity and off-target risk. Covalent Cys-reactive inhibitors that target palmitoylation sites carry off-target risks because other palmitoylated proteins and accessible cysteines in the proteome can be modified; chemical proteomics has highlighted proteome-wide reactivity for H-151 and related chemotypes, emphasizing the importance of selectivity profiling and ocular safety assessment (Barasa et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2022; Ni et al., 2024). These liabilities contrast with CDN-pocket antagonists such as SN-011, which have shown high potency and may have a different selectivity/PD profile, including ability to suppress pathologic STING activation across triggers (TREX1⁻/⁻, cGAMP, SAVI variants) (Hong et al., 2021; Hong et al., 2022).

Ocular cell biology relevance. STING is expressed in RPE and retinal microglia, with increased expression and promoter accessibility in maculae from dry AMD patients; cGAS/STING activation is detected in oxidative injury models with cytosolic DNA leakage and microglial infiltration (Hu et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022). STING signaling in senescent/oxidatively stressed RPE can drive VEGF production via NF-κB/HIF-1α, and H-151 prevented VEGF overproduction in senescent ARPE-19 cells, indicating on-target modulation of a disease-linked RPE readout (Hu et al., 2023). In microglia, disease-associated activation of STING promotes inflammatory cytokines and neovascularization; genetic deletion of Sting or pharmacologic STING inhibition reduces microglial activation, inflammatory gene expression, necroptosis (RIP1/RIP3/MLKL), and pathological angiogenesis in OIR/CNV models (Qiu et al., 2025; Ni et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025). In AMD-relevant RPE degeneration, Alu cDNA and mtDNA leakage activate cGAS–STING to prime a type I IFN program that licenses noncanonical NLRP3 inflammasome activation and RPE apoptosis; genetic ablation along the axis rescues RPE toxicity and degeneration (Zhou et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023). Notably, pathway redundancy exists: in some RPE models dsRNA/RIG-I or ZBP1 can substitute for cGAS to drive IFN programs; these context-dependent sensors introduce translational uncertainty on how uniformly RPE pathology is STING-dependent in humans (Hu et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023).

Expected Effect  
If H-151 achieves sufficient exposure in RPE and retinal microglia, the expected pharmacodynamic effects are:  
- Reduced STING palmitoylation/clustering at the Golgi and diminished recruitment/activation of TBK1, lowering p-IRF3 and downstream type I IFN gene expression (e.g., IFNB1, ISGs) and attenuating NF-κB signaling (e.g., IL6, CCL/CXCL chemokines) (Zhang et al., 2023; Kobritz et al., 2022; Hong et al., 2021).  
- In RPE, decreased oxidative-stress/senescence-associated VEGF induction and cytokine secretion; this is directly supported by ARPE-19 data where H-151 prevented senescence-induced VEGF overproduction and is consistent with STING’s role in NF-κB/HIF-1α-driven VEGF signaling in RPE (Hu et al., 2023).  
- In microglia, dampened STING-dependent inflammatory activation and necroptosis, which should reduce pro-angiogenic factor release (e.g., FGF2) and lower vascular leakage/tuft area in neovascular contexts; analogous outcomes have been demonstrated with other STING inhibitors (C-176, SN-011) and with genetic STING loss (Ni et al., 2024; Ni et al., 2024).  
- In AMD-relevant degenerative settings, reduced type I IFN priming of the noncanonical inflammasome (caspase-4/11 → caspase-1 → gasdermin D → IL-18) and decreased RPE apoptosis, consistent with the rescue observed upon genetic ablation of cGAS/STING/IFNAR/IRF3 in Alu DNA–driven RPE toxicity models (Zhou et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023).

Assay-level predictions follow from these mechanisms and precedents:  
- In vitro RPE assays: H-151 should suppress mtDNA- or Alu cDNA–induced IL-6/IL-8 and VEGF secretion; the former is STING-dependent in ARPE-19 cells by siRNA, and the latter was H-151-sensitive in senescent RPE (Hu et al., 2023). Readouts: IFN-β, ISGs, NF-κB target genes, p-TBK1/p-IRF3, cytosolic dsDNA burden, autophagy flux (Hu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022).  
- Microglia assays: H-151 should lower STING translocation, p-TBK1/p-NF-κB, and reduce ROS-mtDNA–STING feed-forward signaling; although these outcomes have been shown for CTS and for genetic/pharmacologic STING inhibition, they have not been directly tested for H-151 in retinal microglia (Qiu et al., 2025).  
- Retinal oxidative injury models (e.g., sodium iodate): based on C-176 and JQ1 outcomes, H-151 would be expected to reduce cGAS–STING activation, cytosolic DNA accumulation, inflammatory cytokines, and photoreceptor/RPE degeneration; however, direct in vivo ocular evidence for H-151 is currently lacking in the provided literature (Hu et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022).  
- GA-relevant Alu DNA models: given the genetic rescue by STING loss-of-function, H-151 would be expected to protect RPE from Alu DNA toxicity; this remains to be demonstrated pharmacologically (Hu et al., 2023).

Critical uncertainties and constraints must temper these expectations:  
- Cell-type and allele dependence: H-151 potency can be reduced in wild-type STING contexts and in primary human immune cells, and retinal cell haplotypes are unknown a priori; these factors necessitate direct potency/PD assessment in human RPE and microglia derived from donors with defined STING haplotypes (Barasa et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023).  
- Delivery/exposure: H-151’s lipophilicity and low solubility complicate ocular delivery; intravitreal dosing is plausible but demands formulation optimization and ocular PK studies, while topical and systemic routes are unlikely to achieve retinal exposure without specialized carriers (e.g., lipid nanocapsules, nanoporous vehicles), which have been explored outside the retina (Guilbaud et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025).  
- Target selectivity: proteome-wide cysteine reactivity and off-target palmitoylation blockade raise risks for retinal toxicity and require thorough chemoproteomics and functional selectivity screening in ocular cells (Barasa et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2022).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Strong target validation in AMD-relevant biology. STING is expressed in RPE and retinal microglia and is upregulated in macular retina/RPE from dry AMD patients; oxidative injury and nucleic acid triggers activate cGAS–STING in the retina, and genetic/pharmacologic pathway suppression ameliorates inflammation, degeneration, and pathological angiogenesis (Hu et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022; Zou et al., 2022; Ni et al., 2024). STING knockout protects RPE from inflammation-driven degeneration, reinforcing the causal role of this node (Hu et al., 2023).  
- RPE and microglia relevance. H-151 directly prevented VEGF overproduction in senescent RPE cells, a disease-linked phenotype, implicating pharmacological STING antagonism in a clinically relevant RPE output; microglial STING is a validated driver of inflammatory/angiogenic pathology in vivo (albeit tested with other inhibitors/genetics), providing a plausible microglial target for H-151 (Hu et al., 2023; Qiu et al., 2025; Ni et al., 2024).  
- Mechanistic fit. H-151’s palmitoylation-site blockade aligns precisely with the conserved requirement for STING palmitoylation/clustering at the Golgi to activate TBK1/IRF3 and NF-κB; its cross-species activity (vs C-176/C-178) supports translational development (Hong et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023).  
- Anti-inflammatory efficacy precedent. H-151 reduces interferon and cytokine outputs in macrophage systems and exerts protective effects in vivo in non-ocular inflammatory models (Trex1⁻/⁻, intestinal I/R), demonstrating on-target pathway suppression and physiologic benefit (Guilbaud et al., 2025; Kobritz et al., 2022; Kobritz et al., 2022; Qu et al., 2025).  
- Biomarkerizable PD. The pathway affords clear PD markers applicable to retina—p-TBK1/p-IRF3, IFN-I signature, NF-κB gene sets, cytosolic DNA burden, autophagy flux—and optical/functional endpoints (ERG, OCT, fundus angiography) have proven sensitive to STING axis modulation in extant studies (Zou et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022).

Weaknesses and risks  
- Lack of published in vivo ocular data for H-151. There are no reports identified here of intravitreal or systemic H-151 dosing demonstrating retinal efficacy, safety, or ocular PK; favorable outcomes in retina to date involve other STING inhibitors (C-176, SN-011) or BRD4 inhibition (JQ1) (Hu et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022; Ni et al., 2024). Therefore, the asserted intravitreal efficacy, 24-h ocular stability, and clean systemic safety of H-151 in retinal models remain unsubstantiated in these sources (Kobritz et al., 2022).  
- Primary human cell potency and allele dependence. H-151 is potent in THP-1 (HAQ STING) but shows reduced potency in wild-type STING reporter cells and minimal activity in primary human monocytes/PBMCs; these findings mandate direct testing in primary human RPE/microglia and consideration of STING haplotype effects in translational planning (Barasa et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023).  
- Covalent mechanism liabilities. Proteome-wide cysteine reactivity and off-target palmitoylation inhibition are highlighted risks for this class, raising retinal safety concerns (e.g., unintended modification of palmitoylated synaptic or phototransduction proteins) that must be derisked via chemoproteomics and functional selectivity assays in ocular cell types (Barasa et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2022; Ni et al., 2024).  
- Delivery/PK gaps. H-151’s physicochemical properties necessitate formulation work; while lipid nanocapsules and mesoporous nanozymes have been explored for GI and anterior-segment uses, retinal delivery and ocular tissue distribution remain to be established for H-151 (Guilbaud et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025). No ocular PK, intraocular half-life, or retinal distribution data are available in the provided corpus (Guilbaud et al., 2025).  
- Biology heterogeneity and redundancy. In some RPE systems, nucleic acid sensing is dominated by RIG-I or ZBP1 rather than cGAS; moreover, disease- and cell state-dependent pathway usage has been reported, introducing the possibility that STING blockade may be variably effective across AMD phenotypes (Hu et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023).  
- Immune-safety tradeoffs and chronic dosing. While concerns about cytokine storm primarily affect agonists, chronic systemic STING suppression can impair antitumor and antiviral immunity; STING knockout mice show increased tumorigenesis susceptibility and reduced vaccine responses, arguing for locally restricted ocular delivery and infection surveillance in development (Garland et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2023).

Assessment against the proposal’s five claims  
1) Target validation: Supported, with nuance. cGAS–STING activation by cytosolic mtDNA and Alu cDNA is implicated in RPE degeneration and retinal inflammation; STING and cGAS knockouts rescue Alu DNA toxicity in vivo, and STING deficiency prevents RPE degeneration in inflammatory contexts (Hu et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023). However, sensor redundancy in RPE (RIG-I/ZBP1) suggests context-dependent contributions (Hu et al., 2023).  
2) Mechanistic specificity: Substantially supported. H-151 covalently modifies Cys91 to block palmitoylation and TBK1/IRF3 signaling; IC₅₀ values span ~0.13 µM (HFFs) to ~1 µM (293T reporter), consistent with the “~0.5 µM” order-of-magnitude claim, though potency varies by system and allele (Zhang et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2023; Barasa et al., 2023).  
3) Preclinical evidence in retina: Not supported for H-151. There is no published evidence here of intravitreal H-151 reducing retinal STING markers, IL-1β, or preserving RPE/ERG; analogous evidence exists for other STING inhibitors (C-176, SN-011) and for BRD4 inhibition (JQ1) (Zou et al., 2022; Ni et al., 2024). The in vitro RPE senescence result with H-151 (VEGF suppression) is a supportive, but limited, ocular datum (Hu et al., 2023).  
4) Translational considerations (stability/safety): Not supported by the provided literature. The only in vivo dosing details are from an acute intestinal injury model (single 10 mg/kg IP dose), with no ocular PK or chronic safety data; claims of ocular stability >24 h and “clean” systemic safety at 30 mg/kg are not documented here (Kobritz et al., 2022). Class-level concerns about proteome-wide reactivity and off-target palmitoylation emphasize the need for careful derisking (Barasa et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2022).  
5) Novelty: Supported conceptually. STING inhibition integrates upstream mitochondrial and nucleic acid-danger signaling to dampen both IFN-I and NF-κB/inflammasome arms—highly relevant to AMD pathophysiology and complementary to complement/NLRP3-focused strategies (Hu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023).

Go/no-go perspective and development recommendations  
On target/pathway grounds, STING antagonism is a compelling strategy for GA: STING is upregulated in human macular retina/RPE in dry AMD, and genetic/pharmacologic attenuation of the axis reduces retinal inflammation, degeneration, and pathological angiogenesis in multiple models (Zou et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022; Ni et al., 2024). H-151, specifically, has a validated molecular mechanism that directly intercepts the palmitoylation-dependent activation step and demonstrates anti-inflammatory efficacy in vivo outside the eye and on disease-relevant RPE outputs in vitro (Hong et al., 2022; Kobritz et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2023). However, the absence of published in vivo retinal efficacy, ocular PK, and ocular safety data for H-151, coupled with evidence of reduced activity in primary human immune cells, allele-dependent potency, and proteome-wide reactivity, argues against immediate advancement of H-151 to IND-enabling studies for GA without a focused preclinical package to derisk these issues (Barasa et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2022).

A prudent development path would:  
- Confirm target engagement and potency in primary human RPE and microglia (donor-derived, with haplotype stratification), using PD markers (p-TBK1/p-IRF3, IFN-I/NF-κB signatures, IL-18) and disease-relevant triggers (mtDNA, Alu cDNA, oxidative stress), with parallel comparison to a noncovalent CDN-pocket antagonist (e.g., SN-011) to benchmark efficacy/selectivity (Hu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2021).  
- Define ocular PK and retinal distribution after intravitreal administration, with formulation optimization to address solubility/lipophilicity; lipid-based approaches are feasible conceptually, but retinal delivery has not yet been demonstrated (Guilbaud et al., 2025).  
- Establish efficacy in retinal oxidative injury (sodium iodate ± diABZI) and Alu DNA RPE toxicity models, using fundus imaging, ERG, and histology, with PD biomarker linkage; prior studies with C-176 and JQ1 provide dosing paradigms and endpoints (Zou et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2023).  
- Conduct chemoproteomics and global palmitoyl-proteome profiling in retinal tissues to assess selectivity and off-target risks of covalent Cys91 targeting; include functional assays for phototransduction and synaptic proteins (Hong et al., 2022; Ni et al., 2024).  
- Evaluate chronic intraocular safety (repeat intravitreal dosing) including retinal structure/function, microglia homeostasis, and infection susceptibility, given the role of STING in antiviral defense (Garland et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2023).  
- Consider alternative or complementary modalities (e.g., CDN-pocket antagonists like SN-011 or epigenetic repression via BRD4 inhibitors) given their documented ocular activity and potentially different safety/selectivity profiles (Ni et al., 2024; Zou et al., 2022; Hong et al., 2021).

In summary, H-151 is a mechanistically coherent STING antagonist with supportive non-ocular in vivo efficacy and disease-relevant effects in RPE in vitro, and the STING axis is well-validated as a therapeutic target in retinal disease, including in AMD-relevant contexts (Hu et al., 2023; Qu et al., 2025). Nonetheless, key translational gaps—ocular PK, retinal efficacy, primary human cell potency/allele effects, and selectivity/liability of the covalent palmitoylation mechanism—must be addressed before prioritizing H-151 for GA drug development (Barasa et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2022). Given the strong pathway rationale and the feasibility of local ocular delivery, H-151 merits a limited, milestone-gated preclinical program in retinal models with rigorous PD and safety readouts, while in parallel benchmarking against noncovalent STING antagonists that have already demonstrated intravitreal efficacy in ocular disease models (Ni et al., 2024; Ni et al., 2024).
