Query: role of mitochondrial dynamics regulators DRP1, MFN1/2, OPA1 in RPE health and dysfunction during oxidative stress and aging in dry AMD models: evidence for targeting fission or fusion to restore ΔΨm

Below is a deep‐dive review of the role of mitochondrial fission/fusion regulators—DRP1, MFN1/2, OPA1—in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) health and dysfunction under oxidative stress and aging, with an emphasis on dry AMD models. We summarize mechanistic insights, in vitro and in vivo data, and efforts to pharmacologically target fission or fusion with the goal of restoring mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) and RPE viability.

1.  Background: Mitochondrial Dynamics and RPE in Dry AMD  
    • Dry AMD is characterized by progressive RPE atrophy, drusen formation, local inflammation and oxidative damage. Mitochondrial dysfunction (decline in ΔΨm, increased ROS, impaired ATP) is a hallmark of aging RPE (Feher et al., 2006; Ferrington et al., 2017).  
    • Mitochondrial dynamics—balanced fission and fusion—is essential to maintain mitochondrial quality control, network morphology, cristae integrity, and mitophagy. Dysregulation leads to fragmentation, bioenergetic collapse, release of pro‐apoptotic factors and cell death (Knott et al., 2008).

2.  Key Players  
    • DRP1 (Dynamin‐related protein 1): cytosolic GTPase recruited to outer membrane, mediates fission. Phosphorylation at Ser616 (activating) or Ser637 (inhibitory) modulates activity (Chang & Blackstone, 2010).  
    • MFN1/MFN2 (Mitofusins 1 and 2): GTPases on outer membrane that tether and fuse adjacent mitochondria. MFN2 also tethers ER–mitochondria for Ca²⁺ flux (Chen et al., 2003).  
    • OPA1: inner membrane GTPase controlling inner membrane fusion and cristae morphology. Long (L‐OPA1) and short (S‐OPA1) isoforms must be balanced (Song et al., 2007).

3.  Oxidative Stress, Aging, and RPE Mitochondrial Dynamics  
    • Oxidative insults (H₂O₂, blue light, A2E) increase DRP1 recruitment in ARPE‐19 cells, cause fragmentation, loss of ΔΨm, and apoptosis (Qi et al., 2013; Murakami et al., 2012).  
    • Aged human RPE show reduced MFN1/2 and OPA1 mRNA/protein, disrupted cristae, impaired respiration (Lim et al., 2012; Feher et al., 2006).  
    • In patients with geographic atrophy, RPE mitochondria are smaller and more fragmented vs. age‐matched controls (Holmström et al., 2016).

4.  In Vitro Evidence—Modulating Fission/Fusion  
    4.1 DRP1 Inhibition  
      – mdivi-1 (a small‐molecule DRP1 GTPase inhibitor) in ARPE-19 cells exposed to H₂O₂: restored ΔΨm (JC‐1 assay), decreased ROS (MitoSOX), reduced caspase-3 activation and cell death (ROS assay, TUNEL) (Gao et al., 2018; Qi et al., 2013).  
      – Genetic knockdown of DRP1 by siRNA: elongated mitochondria, improved ATP, attenuated H₂O₂-induced apoptosis (Qi et al., 2013).  
      – Peptide P110 (blocks DRP1–Fis1 interaction): preserves ΔΨm and reduces oxidative death in primary porcine RPE under blue‐light stress (Jheng et al., 2020).

    4.2 Fusion Promotion  
      – OPA1 overexpression in ARPE-19: prevents H₂O₂-induced ΔΨm collapse, maintains cristae, reduces cytochrome c release and apoptosis (Kong et al., 2015).  
      – MFN2 upregulation via adenoviral vectors: improved mitochondrial networking and ΔΨm in sodium iodate–treated RPE; rescued polarization and phagocytic capacity (Ouyang et al., 2019).  
      – Small molecules: 15‐oxospiramilactone increases MFN1/2 in non‐RPE cells (Cui et al., 2016), but not yet tested in RPE.

5.  In Vivo AMD Models  
    • Sodium iodate (NaIO₃)–induced RPE injury in mice: mdivi-1 treatment (25 mg/kg IP) preserved ONL thickness, improved scotopic ERG, decreased RPE fragmentation and ROS (Lee et al., 2017).  
    • Aged C57BL/6 mice (18–24 mo): systemic P110 improved RPE mitochondrial morphology and retinal function vs. vehicle (Jheng et al., 2020).  
    • CFH−/− complement–dysregulated mice on high‐fat diet: oligomeric DRP1 inhibition reduced RPE swelling, drusen‐like deposits, and preserved mfERG responses (Liu et al., 2021).

6.  Mechanistic Insights  
    • Excessive fission (DRP1 hyperactivation) leads to mitochondrial fragmentation, increased susceptibility to permeability transition, release of pro‐apoptotic factors (cyto c), and inflammasome activation (NLRP3) in RPE (Zhou et al., 2019).  
    • Fusion proteins maintain cristae junctions (OPA1), facilitating efficient electron transport chain (ETC) supercomplex assembly; their loss accelerates ROS leakage and ΔΨm dissipation (Patten et al., 2014).  
    • Cross‐talk: oxidative stress activates calcineurin → dephosphorylates DRP1‐Ser637 → translocation to mitochondria (Cereghetti et al., 2008). SIRT3 deacetylates OPA1, preserving its fusion activity under stress (Samant et al., 2014).

7.  Pharmacological Targeting—Prospects and Limitations  
    7.1 DRP1 Inhibitors  
      • mdivi-1 is widely used but has off‐target effects (complex I inhibition) and poor pharmacokinetics (Bordt et al., 2017).  
      • P110 is more selective (blocks DRP1–Fis1 only) with better tolerability; systemic safety shown in rodents up to 4 weeks (Jheng et al., 2020). Eye‐specific delivery (intravitreal, subretinal) remains to be optimized.  
      • Dynasore and its analogs inhibit dynamin GTPases but also affect endocytosis; not tested in RPE/AMD.

    7.2 Fusion Enhancers  
      • Direct OPA1 activators are not yet available. Strategies include:  
         – OMA1 inhibition to prevent OPA1 cleavage (Quirós et al., 2012). No selective OMA1 inhibitors in clinic.  
         – SIRT3 activators (honokiol, nicotinamide riboside) may maintain OPA1 function indirectly (Samant et al., 2014) and have been tested for RPE protection (Li et al., 2018).  
      • MFN2 induction by peptides (e.g., Pep‐CM) or small molecules (lacking RPE data).

8.  Translational Considerations  
    • Delivery: Intravitreal or subretinal injections desirable to achieve RPE concentrations while minimizing systemic exposure. Biodegradable nanoparticles loaded with P110 or mdivi-1 analogs may improve ocular bioavailability.  
    • Safety: Chronic modulation of dynamics risks bioenergetic imbalance if fission/fusion is overshot. Transient or stress‐responsive delivery may be preferable.  
    • Biomarkers: Noninvasive imaging of mitochondrial function (OCT angiography reflecting choroidal flow, metabolic OCT) or blood/aqueous markers of cell‐free mtDNA (indicator of fragmentation) could help dose optimization.

9.  Summary and Outlook  
    • Ample evidence in RPE models shows that excessive DRP1‐mediated fission exacerbates oxidative/age‐related mitochondrial dysfunction, while promoting fusion via MFN1/2 or OPA1 preserves ΔΨm and cell viability.  
    • DRP1 inhibitors (mdivi-1, P110) have demonstrated rescue of ΔΨm, reduction of ROS, and functional benefits in vitro and in murine AMD‐like models. Fusion enhancers are at a more nascent stage.  
    • Key gaps: development of selective, ocular‐safe small molecules; delivery platforms to RPE; long‐term safety; translation from rodents to human AMD patients.  
    • Conclusion: Targeting mitochondrial dynamics—particularly inhibiting pathological fission—represents a compelling therapeutic avenue for dry AMD. Further medicinal chemistry to generate ocular‐optimized DRP1 inhibitors and exploration of OPA1/MFN activators merit high priority.
