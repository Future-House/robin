Assay Overview  
• Goal and disease relevance  
  – Models key early driver of dry AMD: chronic oxidative stress in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) leading to cumulative macromolecular damage, inflammation, and cell death.  
  – Specifically assays activation of the Nrf2-driven antioxidant response, a major cytoprotective pathway that is dysregulated in AMD.  
• Readouts  
  1. Nrf2 nuclear translocation: immunocytochemistry or immunoblot of nuclear vs. cytosolic fractions, quantified by high-content imaging or densitometry.  
  2. Downstream target induction: mRNA (qPCR) and/or protein levels (western blot, ELISA, high-content imaging) of canonical Nrf2 targets haeme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) and NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1). Optionally an ARE-luciferase reporter.  
  3. Cell viability under oxidative insult: e.g. tBHP or H₂O₂ challenge with/without compound.  
• Cell system  
  – Human RPE model: ARPE-19 or primary human RPE (hRPE), or iPSC-derived RPE monolayers differentiated to exhibit polarity and pigmentation.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Oxidative stress in AMD pathogenesis  
  – Multiple clinical and histopathological studies demonstrate accumulation of oxidative damage markers (8-OHdG, nitrotyrosine) in RPE and Bruch’s membrane from dry AMD donors. (Hollyfield et al. 2008; Sarks et al. 2007)  
  – Epidemiology links to lifestyle/environmental prooxidant exposures (smoking, light damage).  
• Nrf2 dysregulation in RPE and AMD  
  – Zhang et al. Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 2010: Nrf2 knockout mice display accelerated RPE degeneration and drusen-like deposits.  
  – Zhao et al. Free Radical Biol Med 2011: Aged Nrf2–/– mice exhibit AMD-like pathology: increased lipofuscin, complement activation, RPE atrophy.  
  – Human AMD donor eyes show attenuated Nrf2 nuclear localization and lower HO-1/NQO1 expression vs. age-matched controls (Felszeghy et al. 2019; Tosini et al. 2020).  
• Links to inflammation and complement  
  – Deficient antioxidant defenses exacerbate complement activation in RPE, tying oxidative stress and the immune‐complement axis – another pillar of dry AMD pathology.  

Previous Use in Drug Discovery  
• Reporter‐based high‐throughput screens for Nrf2 activators have used RPE cells or fibroblasts engineered with ARE-luciferase. These screens identified electrophilic natural products (e.g. sulforaphane) and synthetic triterpenoids (e.g. bardoxolone analogs).  
• In vitro validation in RPE: several small-molecule Nrf2 inducers reduced tBHP‐induced ROS and preserved cell viability in ARPE-19 monolayers (Zhou et al. Mol Vis 2012; Johnson et al. J Ocul Pharmacol Ther 2014).  
• Key discovery: Nrf2 activation not only lowers oxidative damage but also attenuates inflammasome activation in RPE (adding anti‐inflammatory benefit). (Tseng-Wen Lu et al. 2019)  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  – Direct relevance to a central and well‐validated AMD mechanism (oxidative RPE injury and impaired defense).  
  – Multiplexable: can couple Nrf2 localization, target induction, and cell‐survival readouts in a single experiment.  
  – Amenable to both low‐ and high‐throughput formats.  
  – Uses human RPE cells, preserving disease‐relevant signaling.  
  – Enables quantitative ranking of compounds by potency and efficacy in real cellular context.  
Weaknesses  
  – RPE cell lines (ARPE-19) may differ from native RPE in redox capacity and Nrf2 regulation; primary or iPSC-RPE are more predictive but lower throughput.  
  – In vitro oxidative insult (tBHP, H₂O₂) is acute and may not fully recapitulate chronic low‐grade stress in vivo.  
  – Nrf2 activation is pleiotropic; off-target effects (e.g. electrophilic stress or NRF1/3 cross-reactivity) may confound interpretation.  
  – Does not capture downstream complement dysregulation, drusen formation or cross-talk with microglia/macrophages.  
Conclusion  
This assay is a well‐supported, high‐value in vitro model for discovering and profiling small molecules that bolster the RPE antioxidant defense via Nrf2. It is especially useful in early‐stage triage of compounds targeting oxidative sequelae of dry AMD, though subsequent assays (e.g. co-culture with immune cells, complement activation models, 3D Bruch’s membrane mimics, or in vivo AMD models) are needed to confirm disease‐modifying potential.
