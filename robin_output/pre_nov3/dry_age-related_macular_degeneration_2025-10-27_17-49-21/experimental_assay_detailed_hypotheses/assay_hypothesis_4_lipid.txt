Assay Overview  
– Disease aspect modeled: oxidative damage–driven ferroptosis-like death of retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells, a process implicated in RPE atrophy and progression of dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD).  
– Readouts:  
  • Lipid peroxidation measured by C11-BODIPY581/591 fluorescence shift from red to green upon oxidation. Quantified by flow cytometry or high-content fluorescence microscopy.  
  • Cell viability (e.g. CellTiter-Glo or LDH release) as a secondary readout of death.  
  • Optionally, complementary biochemical assays for malondialdehyde (MDA) or 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE) adducts by ELISA or immunoblot.  
– Biological material: human RPE cell line (ARPE-19) or primary human RPE cultures. Cells are pre-loaded with C11-BODIPY, then challenged with a pro-oxidant insult (e.g. tert-butyl hydroperoxide, RSL3 or erastin) in the presence or absence of candidate ferroptosis inhibitors or antioxidants.  

Biomedical Evidence  
– Oxidative stress is a well-established driver of RPE dysfunction in dry AMD. Postmortem and in vivo imaging studies report increased lipid peroxidation products (MDA, isoprostanes) in AMD maculae. (Beatty et al., Exp Eye Res 2013; Hollyfield et al., PNAS 2008).  
– Iron accumulation and dysregulation in RPE and Bruch’s membrane have been documented in AMD, promoting Fenton chemistry and lipid peroxidation (Ugarte et al., Exp Eye Res 2013; Hahn et al., Free Radic Biol Med 2003).  
– Skouta et al. (Nat Chem Biol 2014) provided the first demonstration that pharmacological inhibitors of ferroptosis (e.g. ferrostatin-1) protect RPE cells from erastin- or RSL3-induced lipid peroxidation and death. They linked RPE ferroptosis-like death to key AMD phenotypes (drusen formation, RPE atrophy).  
– Genetic studies: decreased GPX4 activity and glutathione depletion sensitize RPE to oxidative insults (Holmgren et al., Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 2005). Polymorphisms in antioxidant defense genes correlate with AMD risk (Seddon et al., JAMA Ophthalmol 2013).  

Previous Use  
– Skouta et al. (2014) used a C11-BODIPY–based lipid peroxidation assay in ARPE-19 cells to discover and validate “ferrostatins” that suppress ferroptosis and RPE atrophy–related phenotypes.  
– Other groups have applied similar C11-BODIPY assays to screen small molecules or siRNA libraries targeting pro-oxidant pathways (e.g. ACSL4, 15-LOX) in RPE or neuronal cell models (Shintoku et al., Cell Chem Biol 2017; Doll et al., Nat Chem Biol 2019).  
– Several academic screens for new antioxidants or iron chelators have relied on C11-BODIPY readouts in RPE and photoreceptor precursor cells to prioritize leads for dry AMD.  

Overall Evaluation  

Strengths  
• Pathophysiological relevance: directly measures lipid peroxidation, a core driver of RPE degeneration in dry AMD.  
• Mechanistic insight: distinguishes ferroptotic from apoptotic/necrotic death (via rescue by ferroptosis inhibitors vs caspase inhibitors).  
• Quantitative, high-throughput compatible: flow cytometry or automated imaging enables screening of large libraries.  
• Proven track record: has yielded novel ferroptosis inhibitors and informed preclinical AMD models.  

Weaknesses  
• Oversimplification: monoculture of ARPE-19 or primary RPE lacks complement activation, microglial/inflammatory crosstalk and drusen matrix context critical in AMD.  
• Pro-oxidant insult may not fully recapitulate chronic, low-grade oxidative stress in vivo (acute H2O2 or erastin pulse vs decades of cumulative stress).  
• Cell line artifacts: ARPE-19 cells display reduced pigmentation, altered tight junctions and antioxidant defenses relative to native RPE.  
• Readout specificity: C11-BODIPY oxidation can be influenced by other ROS (not strictly lipid peroxides) and may require orthogonal validation (MDA/4-HNE assays, GPX4 activity).  

Summary  
A lipid peroxidation suppression assay using C11-BODIPY in RPE cells is a biologically and clinically relevant platform for early‐stage screening of dry AMD therapeutics, especially those targeting ferroptosis or oxidative damage. Its mechanistic specificity and scalability are clear strengths. However, it should be integrated into a panel of complementary assays—incorporating co-cultures (e.g. RPE–microglia), chronic stress models, and orthogonal biochemical readouts—to address its limitations and enhance predictive validity for the complex dry AMD milieu.
