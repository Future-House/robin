Assay Overview  
• Purpose and disease relevance  
  – Models a key feature of dry AMD pathogenesis: loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) under chronic oxidative or metabolic stress, which leads to cytochrome c release and apoptosis.  
  – Reflects early bioenergetic collapse observed in AMD donor tissue and in animal models.  

• Readouts and methodology  
  – Fluorescent ΔΨm-sensitive dyes such as JC-1 (aggregates emit red fluorescence at high ΔΨm, monomers green at low ΔΨm) or TMRE/TMRM (tetramethylrhodamine ethyl/ methyl ester; accumulation within mitochondria proportional to ΔΨm).  
  – Quantitation by plate reader (fluorescence intensity ratio), flow cytometry or high-content imaging to measure single-cell changes and subcellular localization.  
  – Endpoints can include ratio of red/green JC-1 fluorescence, absolute TMRE intensity, percentage of depolarized cells, and secondary caspase-3 activation assays.  

• Biological material  
  – Human RPE cell lines (ARPE-19), primary human fetal RPE, or preferably induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)–derived RPE from donors with or without AMD susceptibility alleles (e.g., CFH Y402H) to capture patient-specific mitochondrial vulnerability.  
  – Stress induction may involve chronic low-dose H2O2, blue-light irradiation plus A2E, high-glucose/low-serum media, or complement activation products.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Centrality of mitochondrial dysfunction in dry AMD  
   – Histopathology of post-mortem AMD eyes shows RPE with swollen mitochondria, disrupted cristae and reduced complex I/III protein levels (Feher et al., 2006; Ferrington et al., 2017).  
   – Transcriptomic and proteomic analyses from AMD donor RPE reveal downregulation of oxidative phosphorylation genes and upregulation of senescence and inflammasome pathways (Menon et al., 2019; Kannan & Sreekumar, 2006).  
   – Animal models (mtDNA damage in RPE, Sod2 knockdown) recapitulate RPE atrophy, drusen-like deposits, and photoreceptor degeneration (Justilien et al., 2007; Terluk et al., 2015).  
   – Clinical imaging (OCT, fundus autofluorescence) correlates geographic atrophy progression with zones of reduced RPE metabolic activity (Schmitz-Valckenberg et al., 2017).  

2. Mechanistic linkage to cell death  
   – Loss of ΔΨm is an early event triggering mitochondrial permeability transition, cytochrome c release, caspase activation and apoptosis in stressed RPE (Terluk et al., IO&VS 2015).  
   – Oxidative modification of mitochondrial DNA and proteins potentiates permeability transition pore opening (Campello & Scorrano, 2010).  

3. Therapeutic rationale  
   – Mitochondria-targeted antioxidants (MitoQ, SS-31/elamipretide) ameliorate ΔΨm loss and RPE death in vitro and slow atrophy in model systems (Klimova & Chandel, 2008; Burke et al., 2017).  

Previous Use  
• Drug discovery and screening  
  – High-throughput screens in ARPE-19 using JC-1 or TMRE to identify small molecules and peptides that stabilize ΔΨm under H2O2 or A2E-mediated stress. Early hits included mitochondrial stabilizers and redox modulators (e.g., SS peptides).  
  – Studies in iPSC-derived RPE from AMD patients demonstrated that compounds preserving ΔΨm also reduced inflammatory cytokine release and improved phagocytosis (Yoshida et al., 2019).  

• Key scientific findings  
  – Validation of elamipretide (SS-31) as a ΔΨm-preserving agent in RPE with Sod2 knockdown, leading to clinical evaluation in geographic atrophy (TAURIEL trials).  
  – Discovery that nicotinamide repletion restores NAD+ levels, preserves ΔΨm and prevents RPE senescence markers (Lin et al., 2016).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Disease relevance: Directly probes bioenergetic failure known to precede RPE loss in dry AMD.  
• Quantitative and scalable: Amenable to medium- to high-throughput formats for lead identification.  
• Modular: Can combine with co-stresses (A2E, complement fragments) to model multi-factorial disease environment.  
• Predictive biomarker: Early ΔΨm stabilization often correlates with downstream prevention of RPE apoptosis and inflammatory signaling.  

Weaknesses  
• Simplified model: Does not capture other hallmarks of AMD (lipid accumulation, complement dysregulation, drusen formation, Bruch’s membrane alterations).  
• Dye artefacts: Lipophilic cations can redistribute independently of ΔΨm; require careful controls (FCCP depolarization, dye concentration optimization).  
• Cell line limitations: ARPE-19 lack full polarization and mature mitochondrial networks; primary or iPSC-RPE better but costly and variable.  
• Off-target effects: Compounds preserving ΔΨm may act via general antioxidant or uncoupling mechanisms not specific to AMD pathobiology.  

Conclusion  
The mitochondrial membrane potential assay in stressed RPE closely models a central node in dry AMD pathogenesis—bioenergetic collapse leading to apoptosis. It has strong precedent in both mechanistic studies and early drug discovery, particularly for mitochondria-targeted therapeutics. While it should be complemented by assays addressing complement activation, lipid handling, and inflammation, ΔΨm measurement is a valuable, scalable tool for initial screening and mechanistic validation of dry AMD candidates.
