Proposal for MCC950

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
MCC950 (also known as CRID3, CP-456,773) is a small-molecule inhibitor of the NLRP3 inflammasome. It was first reported in 2015 by Coll et al. (2015) following a high-throughput screen for compounds that block NLRP3–dependent IL-1β release. Chemically, MCC950 is a diarylsulfonylurea derivative (structure: 2-cyano-N-[(1S)-1-(2-fluoro-4-sulfonylphenyl)-ethyl]-3-pyridinecarboxamide). It is wholly synthetic, prepared via stepwise sulfonylation of the aryl fluoride followed by urea coupling to the pyridine carboxamide moiety. MCC950 belongs to the class of inflammasome inhibitors—specifically those targeting the NLRP3 sensor—and represents the most potent and selective member of this chemical family to date.

Therapeutic History  
• Preclinical models of inflammatory and autoimmune disease: MCC950 has shown efficacy in mouse models of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE), cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes (CAPS), type 2 diabetes, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), and atherosclerosis (Coll et al., 2015; Vande Walle et al., 2019; Mridha et al., 2017).  
• No clinical trials in humans have yet completed; a phase I study by Pfizer (formerly Inhibrx) is reported but synopses are not publicly available (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.). Safety data in rodents indicates good tolerability up to 50 mg/kg daily with no overt organ toxicity.  
• Use in ocular disease: There is no published report of MCC950 being tested in dry AMD patients. However, preclinical studies using RPE cells (ARPE-19) and sodium iodate–induced RPE injury in mice have applied MCC950 intravitreally or systemically, demonstrating preservation of retinal structure and function (e.g., reduction of fundus autofluorescence, preservation of outer nuclear layer) (Lei et al., 2018; Doyle et al., 2020).

Mechanism of Action  
• Primary target: MCC950 binds directly to the NACHT domain of NLRP3, blocking its ATPase activity that is essential for conformational changes and oligomerization (Coll et al., 2015).  
• Inflammasome assembly: By preventing NLRP3’s ATP-driven conformational activation, MCC950 inhibits recruitment of ASC (apoptosis-associated speck–like protein), preventing caspase-1 recruitment and activation (Tapia-Abellán et al., 2019).  
• Downstream effects: Inhibition of caspase-1 activation blocks cleavage and maturation of IL-1β and IL-18, and prevents gasdermin D–mediated membrane pore formation and pyroptotic cell death.  
• Off-target profile: At concentrations up to 10 µM, MCC950 shows negligible activity against other NLRs (e.g., NLRC4, AIM2) or unrelated proteases and kinases (Coll et al., 2015).

Expected Effect in Proposed Assay  
Assay context: ARPE-19 cells treated with Alu RNA or H₂O₂, measurement of caspase-1 activity, IL-1β/IL-18 release, mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) via TMRM, and cell survival.  
• RPE expression of NLRP3: Studies confirm that human and murine RPE express NLRP3, ASC, and pro–caspase-1, and that DAMPs such as Alu RNA, A2E, or H₂O₂ induce inflammasome assembly (Tarallo et al., 2012; Doyle et al., 2012).  
• Caspase-1 inhibition: Literature shows that 1–10 µM MCC950 blocks caspase-1 activation by ~60–80% in LPS+ATP–stimulated macrophages and similarly in ARPE-19 exposed to Alu RNA (Lei et al., 2018).  
• Cytokine release: IL-1β and IL-18 levels in conditioned media are reduced by >70% at 10 µM MCC950 (Lei et al., 2018).  
• ΔΨm preservation: By halting the feed-forward cycle of mitochondrial ROS → NLRP3 activation → further mitochondrial damage, MCC950 prevents TMRM loss in RPE treated with oxidative stimuli (Doyle et al., 2020).  
• Pyroptosis reduction: In vitro LDH release assays show ~50% reduction in cell death at 10 µM MCC950 in RPE challenged with Alu RNA (Lei et al., 2018).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
1. Specificity and potency: MCC950 is among the most selective NLRP3 inhibitors, with nanomolar IC₅₀ for ATPase activity and minimal off-target effects.  
2. Strong preclinical efficacy: Multiple studies in RPE cell lines and NaIO₃ mouse models show preservation of RPE integrity, reduction of fundus autofluorescence, and maintenance of ONL thickness.  
3. Mechanism aligned with AMD pathogenesis: NLRP3 activation by Alu RNAs, drusen components, and oxidative stress is well documented; blocking this pathway directly addresses a central driver of RPE degeneration and chronic inflammation in dry AMD.

Weaknesses and Gaps  
1. Ocular pharmacokinetics/delivery: Systemic administration may not achieve therapeutic concentrations in the subretinal space; intravitreal dosing schedules, drug stability, and potential immunogenicity require assessment.  
2. Chronic dosing and safety: Dry AMD is a progressive, long-term disease; chronic inhibition of NLRP3 could impair host defense against pathogens or tissue repair. Long-term ocular safety data are lacking.  
3. Translational uncertainty: No data exist on MCC950 in larger animal models of AMD (e.g., non-human primates) or on functional endpoints such as electroretinography or visual acuity.  
4. Redundancy in inflammasome pathways: Other inflammasomes (AIM2, NLRC4) or caspase-8–mediated IL-1β maturation may compensate in chronic disease, potentially limiting efficacy.

Conclusion  
MCC950 is a highly promising candidate for dry AMD based on its targeted mechanism, strong in vitro and in vivo efficacy, and alignment with the known role of NLRP3 in RPE degeneration. Before advancing to human trials, key studies should address ocular delivery and pharmacokinetics, chronic dosing safety, and functional efficacy in a relevant large-animal model. If these challenges can be overcome, MCC950 represents one of the most rational, mechanism-based therapeutic strategies for inflammasome–driven RPE pathology in dry AMD.
