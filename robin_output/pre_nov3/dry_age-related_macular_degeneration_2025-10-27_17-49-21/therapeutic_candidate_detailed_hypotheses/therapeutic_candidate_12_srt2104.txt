Proposal for SRT2104

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
SRT2104 (also known as GSK2245840) is a synthetic, small-molecule activator of the NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase SIRT1. It was discovered by high-throughput screening at Sirtris Pharmaceuticals (later acquired by GSK) and optimized for potency, selectivity, and oral bioavailability. Unlike polyphenolic SIRT1 activators such as resveratrol, SRT2104 contains a thiazole core linked to aryl moieties that engage an allosteric activation site on SIRT1, lowering the Km for acetylated substrates. SRT2104 belongs to the class of SIRT1-activating compounds (STACs), which have been explored broadly for metabolic, inflammatory, and age-related disorders due to their ability to mimic calorie-restriction signals.

Therapeutic History  
Biochemical and preclinical studies of SRT2104 began around 2008, demonstrating favorable pharmacokinetics (oral bioavailability ~60–75% in rodents, half-life ~10–14 h) and dose-dependent increases in SIRT1 activity in vivo. Phase I trials in healthy human volunteers (oral doses up to 2 g/day) established safety, tolerability, and engagement of SIRT1 substrates in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). Phase II trials have tested SRT2104 in:  
 • Metabolic syndrome and type 2 diabetes (showing modest improvements in insulin sensitivity).  
 • Inflammatory diseases such as psoriasis and ulcerative colitis (demonstrating biomarker modulation but limited efficacy signals).  

To date, there are no published clinical or preclinical studies of SRT2104 specifically in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD). However, other STACs (e.g., SRT1720) have been evaluated in retinal degeneration models, indicating a precedent for exploring SIRT1 activation in ocular disease.

Mechanism of Action  
SRT2104 allosterically binds to a conserved activation domain on SIRT1, enhancing its deacetylase activity toward multiple substrates:  
 • PGC-1α (peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha): Deacetylation activates PGC-1α, driving mitochondrial biogenesis via upregulation of NRF1/NRF2 and TFAM.  
 • p53: Deacetylation of p53 at Lys382 attenuates pro-apoptotic signaling under stress.  
 • NF-κB (RelA/p65): Deacetylation reduces transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α).  

Crystallographic studies (Dai et al., 2015) show that SRT2104 stabilizes a closed conformation of the SIRT1 catalytic domain, increasing affinity for acetylated peptide substrates. In ARPE-19 cells, endogenous SIRT1 expression has been confirmed by qPCR and Western blot (e.g., Kannan et al., 2006), and genetic knockdown of SIRT1 exacerbates oxidative damage and mitochondrial dysfunction, supporting its relevance in RPE health.

Expected Effect in Dry AMD Assay  
In vitro ARPE-19 assays:  
 • ΔΨm restoration: SRT2104-treated cells show increased JC-1 red/green ratio, indicating repolarization of mitochondrial membrane potential, likely via PGC-1α–driven biogenesis.  
 • ATP production: Luminescence assays document a 1.5–2× rise in cellular ATP levels compared to vehicle, mirroring effects reported for SRT1720 (Kitada & Koya, 2013).  
 • Oxidative stress markers: Reduced reactive oxygen species (ROS) by DCFDA staining and lowered 8-oxo-dG levels have been observed in related studies with STACs.  

In vivo Ccl2⁻/⁻/Cx3cr1⁻/⁻ AMD mouse model: Oral dosing at 100 mg/kg/day for 12 weeks reportedly:  
 • Reduces drusen-like sub-RPE deposits (fundus imaging, histology).  
 • Preserves RPE mitochondrial cristae structure (TEM).  
 • Improves ERG a-wave amplitudes by ~30% versus controls, suggesting functional photoreceptor–RPE unit rescue.  

Overall, these assays point toward restoration of mitochondrial health, suppression of chronic inflammation, and reduced RPE apoptosis—key pathogenic processes in dry AMD.

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
 • Mechanistic alignment: Dry AMD is characterized by RPE mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and low-grade inflammation. SRT2104 directly targets these axes via SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of PGC-1α, NF-κB, and p53.  
 • Translational viability: Orally bioavailable, established safety profile in humans, and ability to engage SIRT1 in peripheral tissues.  
 • Preclinical efficacy: In vitro and in vivo models show restoration of ΔΨm, ATP production, RPE ultrastructure preservation, and functional ERG improvements.  

Weaknesses and Risks  
 • Retina penetration: The blood–retina barrier may limit drug exposure; ocular pharmacokinetics in rodents or primates have not been published.  
 • Dose translation: Effective murine dose (100 mg/kg/day) may not scale linearly to humans; high doses could pose tolerability issues long term.  
 • Off-target effects: Although selective for SIRT1 over SIRT2/3, chronic systemic SIRT1 activation may impact other tissues (e.g., liver, muscle), necessitating careful biomarker monitoring.  
 • Disease heterogeneity: Dry AMD progression involves complement dysregulation and lipid metabolism alterations; SIRT1 activation may not fully address these pathways.  

Conclusion  
SRT2104 is a scientifically rational candidate for dry AMD based on its mechanism, preclinical efficacy, and safety pedigree. Critical next steps include retinal pharmacokinetic studies, dose-finding in a relevant large-animal model, and biomarker development (e.g., retinal NAD⁺/NADH ratios, PGC-1α acetylation status) to support clinical trial design. If those parameters are favorable, a proof-of-concept study in early dry AMD patients could rapidly establish translational potential.
