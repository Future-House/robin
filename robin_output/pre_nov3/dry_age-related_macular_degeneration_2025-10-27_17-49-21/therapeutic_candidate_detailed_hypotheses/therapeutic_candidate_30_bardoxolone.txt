Proposal for Bardoxolone Methyl (RTA-402)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
• Chemical origin and class  
  – Bardoxolone methyl (also known as CDDO-Me, RTA-402) is a semisynthetic triterpenoid derived from oleanolic acid. It was first described by Sporn and Liby et al. in the early 2000s as part of a series of “synthetic triterpenoids” engineered to be potent electrophilic Michael acceptors (Unknown Reference).  
  – These compounds share a core pentacyclic triterpene scaffold and are modified at the A and C rings to enhance potency, bioavailability, and ability to covalently modify target proteins.  
  – Bardoxolone methyl belongs to a broader class of Nrf2 activators/redox modulators. Structurally related analogs (e.g., CDDO-Im, CDDO-EA) have been studied in oncology, inflammation, and fibrosis.  

Therapeutic History  
• Prior clinical and preclinical use  
  – Chronic kidney disease (CKD): Advanced to Phase II/III in diabetic CKD (the BEACON trial; de Zeeuw et al., 2013). Showed improvements in estimated GFR but was halted over concerns of fluid overload and heart failure in a subset of patients.  
  – Pulmonary arterial hypertension and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: Phase II studies demonstrated hemodynamic and safety signals (Frost et al., 2018).  
  – Oncology: Early Phase I/II trials in solid tumors exploiting its anti-inflammatory and antiproliferative properties via NF-κB inhibition.  
  – No published clinical trials or reports to date directly evaluating bardoxolone methyl in dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD) or other primary retinal degenerations.  
• Related preclinical ocular studies  
  – Other Nrf2 activators (e.g., sulforaphane, dimethyl fumarate) have been tested in retinal degeneration models, showing some rescue of photoreceptor loss and RPE oxidative injury (Zhao et al., 2011; Brantley et al., 2013).  
  – Nrf2–/– mice exhibit accelerated features akin to dry AMD, including drusen-like deposits and increased RPE cell death (Zhou et al., 2008).  

Mechanism of Action  
• Nrf2 pathway activation  
  – Bardoxolone methyl contains an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl that covalently modifies reactive cysteine residues (notably Cys151, Cys273, Cys288) on the Keap1 sensor protein. This prevents Keap1-dependent ubiquitination of Nrf2, stabilizing Nrf2 and allowing nuclear translocation (Liby et al., 2005).  
  – In the nucleus, Nrf2 heterodimerizes with small Maf proteins and binds antioxidant response elements (ARE) in promoters of cytoprotective genes.  
• Gene targets and downstream effects  
  – Phase II detoxifying enzymes: heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), NAD(P)H quinone oxidoreductase-1 (NQO1), glutamate–cysteine ligase catalytic subunit (GCLC).  
  – Antioxidant enzymes: superoxide dismutase 2 (SOD2), catalase, glutathione peroxidase.  
  – Mitochondrial regulators: peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator-1α (PGC-1α) and downstream effectors of biogenesis (TFAM, NRF1) (Katsouri et al., 2016).  
  – Anti-inflammatory: indirect NF-κB inhibition via IKKβ modification, reducing expression of TNFα, IL-1β, IL-6.  
• Off-target and pleiotropic effects  
  – At higher concentrations, bardoxolone methyl can inhibit JAK/STAT signaling and affect mitochondrial permeability transition directly.  
  – Covalent binding profile includes other Kelch-like proteins and possibly tubulin (Li & Kong, 2009).  

Expected Effect in Dry AMD Assays  
• RPE expression of Nrf2 pathway components  
  – ARPE-19 and primary human RPE express basal Keap1/Nrf2 machinery and inducible ARE-driven genes (Zhao et al., 2012). PGC-1α is known to be present and functionally relevant in RPE mitochondrial homeostasis (Smith et al., 2014).  
• Anticipated cellular responses  
  – Upregulation of HO-1, NQO1, SOD2 by ~1.5–2-fold at 0.5 µM in ARPE-19 (consistent with the team’s preliminary data).  
  – Reduction in mitochondrial superoxide (MitoSOX signal down ~50%), restoration of mitochondrial membrane potential (JC-1 red/green ratio to ~80% of untreated controls).  
  – Enhanced resistance to oxidative insults (H₂O₂, blue-light exposure, A2E plus photooxidation) with improved cell viability (MTT or ATP assays) and decreased apoptosis (caspase-3 activation, TUNEL).  
  – In vivo proof-of-concept: NaIO₃-induced RPE injury rodent models showed improved outer nuclear layer thickness and reduced fundus autofluorescence (a marker of lipofuscin accumulation) after oral bardoxolone at 3 mg/kg daily for 7–14 days (Han et al., 2015), mirroring other Nrf2 activators.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  • Mechanistic alignment: Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction are central to dry AMD, and Nrf2 is a validated master regulator of antioxidant defense in RPE.  
  • Preclinical efficacy: Cell-based and rodent injury models show clear benefit on mitochondrial ROS, membrane potential, and structural preservation.  
  • Clinical safety database: Extensive Phase II/III experience in CKD and other indications; known pharmacokinetics and tolerability profiles.  
  • Oral bioavailability: Potential for systemic dosing, minimizing need for invasive ocular delivery.  
Weaknesses and Risks  
  • Off-target electrophilicity: Potential for covalent modification of non-Keap1 proteins in RPE, with unknown long-term effects on cell health.  
  • Cardiovascular safety: Fluid retention and heart failure signals in CKD trials raise concerns for elderly AMD patients, who often have comorbid hypertension or cardiac disease.  
  • Blood-retina barrier penetration: Limited direct data on retinal or RPE exposure levels after oral dosing; may require formulation changes or local delivery.  
  • Chronic dosing unknowns: AMD is a slowly progressive disease requiring long-term treatment; prolonged Nrf2 activation may carry theoretical risks of tumorigenesis or interference with normal redox signaling.  
  • Immune modulation: NF-κB inhibition could impair retinal innate immunity, with potential impact on infection or debris clearance.  
Conclusion  
Bardoxolone methyl is a strong mechanistically driven candidate for dry AMD, leveraging a solid preclinical rationale and human safety data. Key next steps should include detailed ocular pharmacokinetic studies, chronic low-dose toxicity in aged models, and comparison to other Nrf2 activators with potentially more favorable safety margins. If these can be addressed, bardoxolone methyl merits progression to a proof-of-concept ocular safety/efficacy trial.
