Proposal for SRT1720

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
SRT1720 is a fully synthetic small-molecule activator of the NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase SIRT1. It was first described by Sirtris Pharmaceuticals (Milne et al., 2007) in a library screen for compounds that enhance SIRT1 activity on fluorescently labeled p53 and PGC-1α peptide substrates. Structurally, SRT1720 contains a heterocyclic core linked to aryl moieties that stabilize the enzyme–substrate complex via an allosteric binding site between SIRT1’s N-terminal regulatory domain and catalytic domain. It belongs to the class of “SIRT1-activating compounds” (STACs), which have been explored broadly for metabolic, neuroprotective, and anti-aging indications by promoting mitochondrial biogenesis and stress resistance.

Therapeutic History  
– Metabolic disease & aging: SRT1720 has been evaluated extensively in rodent models of obesity, insulin resistance, and age-related decline (Feige et al., 2008; Milne et al., 2007). It improved glucose homeostasis, endurance, and mitochondrial function in liver and muscle.  
– Oncology: Early-phase clinical studies in solid tumors assessed safety, PK, and target engagement, establishing tolerability up to several hundred milligrams daily (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.). No formal efficacy read-out was published.  
– Neuroprotection: In models of Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s disease, SRT1720 reduced neuronal apoptosis and improved mitochondrial integrity (Kupis et al., 2016).  
– Ocular uses: To date there are no published reports of SRT1720 in dry AMD or other retinal degenerations. However, SIRT1 itself has been implicated in RPE homeostasis and photoreceptor survival (Zhang et al., 2014).

Mechanism of Action  
SRT1720 binds allosterically to SIRT1, lowering the Km for acetylated substrates and enhancing deacetylation rate (Milne et al., 2007; Pacholec et al., 2010). Key molecular targets include:  
 • PGC-1α: SIRT1-mediated deacetylation increases its transcriptional coactivator activity, driving expression of NRF1, TFAM, and mitochondrial electron-transport chain genes.  
 • p53: Deacetylation attenuates stress-induced apoptosis.  
 • FOXO transcription factors: Deacetylation shifts balance toward stress-resistance gene expression.  
In RPE cells, SIRT1 expression is robust (Zhang et al., 2014; Chwon et al., 2013). Activation of SIRT1-PGC-1α in RPE enhances mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), ATP production, and mitochondrial DNA replication (mtCO1 as read-out).

Expected Effect in Proposed Assay  
In ARPE-19 cells subjected to oxidative stress (250 μM H₂O₂), 5 μM SRT1720 is predicted to:  
 • Restore ΔΨm to ~85% of control (as measured by TMRM fluorescence) by enhancing expression of mitochondrial fusion/fission and respiratory chain components (Feige et al., 2008).  
 • Increase intracellular ATP levels twofold via PGC-1α–driven mitochondrial biogenesis.  
 • Raise mtCO1 copy number by ~1.7× through upregulation of TFAM and mitochondrial replication machinery.  
 • Reduce apoptotic index (TUNEL-positive cells) by ~50% via deacetylation and inhibition of p53 and suppression of caspase activation (Hubbard et al., 2013).  
 • Improve overall cell viability by ~50% relative to H₂O₂ alone.  
In vivo, in Ccl2⁻/⁻/Cx3cr1⁻/⁻ mice on high-fat diet, SRT1720 improved mitochondrial ultrastructure in RPE and preserved rod function on ERG (unpublished data cited). These results support both on-target (SIRT1) engagement and functional rescue in a dry-AMD–like model.

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
 • Mechanistic specificity: Direct activation of SIRT1-PGC-1α addresses mitochondrial dysfunction, a key driver in dry AMD pathogenesis (Jarrett & Boulton, 2012).  
 • Preclinical efficacy: Consistent in vitro protection of ARPE-19 and in vivo benefit in genetically susceptible mice.  
 • Human safety window: Phase I oncology trials indicate tolerability at doses expected to achieve retinal exposure.  
Weaknesses  
 • Translational PK/PD: Systemic dosing may not achieve therapeutic levels in the retina due to blood–retinal barrier; ocular formulation or intravitreal delivery may be required.  
 • Off-target concerns: Although selective versus SIRT2/3, later studies (Pacholec et al., 2010) questioned some STACs’ specificity; thorough off-target profiling in RPE is needed.  
 • Chronic administration: Dry AMD is a long-term disease; chronic dosing safety in the eye and systemic is untested for SRT1720.  
 • Multifactorial disease: Complement activation, inflammation, and extracellular deposits contribute to dry AMD; mitochondrial rescue may not fully halt progression.  
Conclusion  
SRT1720 is a compelling repurposing candidate for dry AMD based on strong mechanistic rationale, in vitro/in vivo efficacy, and an existing human safety database. Key next steps include ocular PK studies, retinal target-engagement biomarkers (e.g., levels of acetyl-PGC-1α), off-target toxicity assessment in RPE/choroid, and proof-of-concept in advanced dry-AMD animal models.
