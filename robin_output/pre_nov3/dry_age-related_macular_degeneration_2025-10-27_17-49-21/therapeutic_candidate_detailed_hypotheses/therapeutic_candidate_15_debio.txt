Proposal for Debio-025 (Alisporivir)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Debio-025 (Alisporivir) is a synthetic cyclophilin inhibitor derived from cyclosporine A (CsA) by modification of the MeBmt^1 side chain and removal of the immunosuppressive moiety that confers calcineurin binding. It was first described by Debiopharm and collaborators in the early 2000s as “DEBIO-025” (or “DEB025”) and subsequently branded Alisporivir for clinical development. As a non-immunosuppressive cyclosporin analogue, it belongs to the class of cyclic peptide peptidomimetics targeting peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerases (PPIases or cyclophilins). Cyclophilin inhibitors have long been studied for antiviral, anti-fibrotic, and cytoprotective properties distinct from classical immunosuppression.

Therapeutic History  
• Antiviral host-targeted therapy in hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection. Multiple phase II and III trials showed that Alisporivir, by binding cyclophilin A (CypA), inhibits HCV replication, acting synergistically with direct-acting antivirals (Flisiak et al., 2012; Lawitz et al., 2013).  
• Preclinical evaluation in other RNA viruses (e.g., HIV-1, coronaviruses) based on cyclophilin dependence, though no late-stage clinical reports beyond HCV.  
• No documented clinical or preclinical use in ocular disease, and to date no reports directly testing Alisporivir in age-related macular degeneration (AMD) or other retinopathies.  
• Related cyclophilin D (CypD) inhibitors (e.g., NIM811, sanglifehrin A derivatives) have been explored in models of myocardial ischemia, neurodegeneration, and drug-induced mitochondrial injury but not extensively in RPE or AMD.

Mechanism of Action  
At the molecular level, Debio-025 binds with high affinity to cyclophilin family members, particularly CypA (Kd ~ 15 nM) and CypD (mitochondrial cyclophilin, PPID) (Watashi et al., 2005; Yamada et al., 2009). The key aspects of its mechanism include:  
1. PPIase Inhibition: Debio-025 blocks the peptidyl-prolyl isomerase activity of cyclophilins, preventing their chaperone-like facilitation of protein folding and protein–protein interactions.  
2. mPTP Regulation via CypD: CypD is a regulatory component of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP). Under stress (oxidative, Ca^2+ overload), CypD binds to the oligomycin-sensitive conferring ATP synthase (or adenine nucleotide translocase, depending on model) to trigger pore opening (Baines et al., 2005; Giorgio et al., 2013). Debio-025’s binding to CypD prevents its interaction with the pore complex, raising the threshold for pore opening.  
3. Downstream Effects: By stabilizing the inner mitochondrial membrane, Debio-025 preserves mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), prevents cytochrome c release, reduces reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, and limits activation of intrinsic apoptosis. These effects have been demonstrated in cardiomyocytes (Nazareth et al., 2007), neurons (Fournier et al., 2013), and hepatocytes (Colin et al., 2010).  

Mechanistic relevance to RPE and Dry AMD:  
• RPE cells express PPID/Cyclophilin D transcripts and protein (Messmer et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2017).  
• Mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and RPE apoptosis via mPTP opening are well-recognized in dry AMD pathology (Feher et al., 2006; Ferrington et al., 2017).  
• Genetic or pharmacologic inhibition of CypD (cyclosporine A, NIM811) protects RPE from NaIO₃-induced mitochondrial collapse and cell death in vitro (Jang & Kim, 2015; Shiu et al., 2018).

Expected Effect in Proposed Assay  
Assay Components: primary human RPE or ARPE-19 cultures treated with Debio-025, with readouts including:  
• Calcein–cobalt quenching assay of mPTP opening (Nicholls & Crompton, 1980): Debio-025 should reduce cobalt-mediated calcein leakage upon oxidative or Ca^2+ stress, indicating pore inhibition.  
• ΔΨm measurement (e.g., TMRM, JC-1): Debio-025–treated cells should better maintain ΔΨm after challenge (e.g., H₂O₂, NaIO₃), reflecting preserved mitochondrial integrity.  
• ROS assays (e.g., MitoSOX): Lower mitochondrial superoxide accumulation in Debio-025 groups.  
• Apoptosis markers (e.g., cytochrome c release, caspase-3 activation): Reduced induction upon stress.  

Mechanistic Rationale: Debio-025’s high affinity for CypD in mitochondria will raise the threshold for stress-induced mPTP opening in RPE, halting the cascade of ΔΨm collapse, ROS amplification, and apoptotic signaling known to contribute to RPE atrophy in dry AMD (Ferrington et al., 2017; Kowalczuk et al., 2020).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Mechanistically aligned with established AMD pathology: targeting mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress in RPE is strongly validated.  
• Proven safety and tolerability in humans at systemic doses (up to 1200 mg/day), reducing early-stage risk.  
• Non-immunosuppressive, minimizing risk of ocular or systemic immunomodulation.  
• Robust mPTP inhibition demonstrated across multiple cell types, suggesting high likelihood of target engagement in RPE.  

Weaknesses and Risks  
• Ocular pharmacokinetics: systemic dosing may not achieve therapeutic levels in the subretinal space; intravitreal or periocular delivery formulations will require development and safety evaluation.  
• Off-target cyclophilin inhibition (e.g., CypA, CypB) could affect protein folding homeostasis and ER stress—potentially deleterious in secretory RPE.  
• Long-term impact on mitochondrial dynamics and mitophagy in RPE is unknown; chronic mPTP suppression could impair quality control under physiological turnover.  
• Absence of in vivo AMD model data; translation from acute oxidative insults to the chronic, multifactorial environment in dry AMD remains to be demonstrated.  

Conclusion  
Debio-025 is a compelling repurposing candidate for dry AMD based on its well-defined CypD-dependent mPTP inhibition, favorable human safety profile, and mechanistic fit with RPE mitochondrial pathology. Key next steps include establishing ocular PK/PD, validating target engagement in primary RPE under AMD-relevant stress, and assessing efficacy in established in vivo dry AMD models (e.g., NaIO₃ or transgenic complement factor H–deficient rodents). If these hurdles are met, Debio-025 could progress swiftly into early-phase ocular trials.
