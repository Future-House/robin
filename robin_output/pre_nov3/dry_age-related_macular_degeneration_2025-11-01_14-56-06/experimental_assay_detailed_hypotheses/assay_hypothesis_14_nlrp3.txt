Assay Overview: The assay models AMD-relevant NLRP3 inflammasome signaling in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) by combining a priming step (NF-κB induction) with a lysosomal damage signal, and measures ASC specking, caspase‑1 activation, IL‑1β/IL‑18 secretion, and pyroptotic lysis as drug‑responsive endpoints using ARPE‑19 or primary human RPE (preferred) (tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 1-2, tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 7-9). Practical implementations include IL‑1α or complement C5a priming (physiologic for RPE) followed by A2E exposure, lysosomotropic Leu‑Leu‑OMe, or lipofuscin plus blue light (e.g., 448 nm, ~0.8 mW/cm2, 3–6 h); readouts comprise ASC speck microscopy, caspase‑1 FLICA (FAM‑YVAD‑FMK), ELISA for IL‑1β/IL‑18, LDH release, and cathepsin‑dependent pharmacology (Z‑FF‑FMK/CA‑074) (brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 80-85, brandstetter2015lightinducesnlrp3 pages 9-11, brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 47-51). Note that LPS is a poor primer for ARPE‑19 due to low TLR4; IL‑1α or C5a are more reliable in this context (tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 5-7, brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 99-102).

Biomedical Evidence: RPE from AMD patients demonstrates inflammasome activation and elevated IL‑1β/IL‑18 in ocular/systemic compartments, implicating this axis in disease biology (brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 80-85). Mechanistically, lipofuscin photooxidation drives lysosomal membrane permeabilization and cathepsin‑dependent NLRP3 activation in primed RPE, yielding caspase‑1 activation, polarized IL‑1 family cytokine release, and pyroptosis; C5a or IL‑1α provide physiologically plausible priming, with an IL‑1–dependent paracrine amplification loop (blocked by IL1R antagonism) (brandstetter2015lightinducesnlrp3 pages 9-11, brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 92-95). However, there are notable caveats: a rigorous study failed to detect NLRP3 protein in multiple human RPE systems and donor tissues, and in vivo data suggest CASP‑1 and downstream cytokines drive injury even when NLRP3’s role is variable, arguing for endpoint selection that emphasizes caspase‑1/IL‑1 biology and orthogonal validation (kosmidou2018issueswiththe pages 3-4, wooff2020caspase1dependentinflammasomesmediate pages 8-10).

Previous Use: In primed ARPE‑19, lysosomal destabilization (Leu‑Leu‑OMe) triggered caspase‑1 activation and mature IL‑1β release; DPP‑I, cathepsin B/L, and caspase‑1 inhibitors abrogated these readouts and pyroptotic cytotoxicity, establishing pathway specificity and pharmacologic tractability (tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 7-9, tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 1-2). Lipofuscin plus blue light elicited NLRP3‑dependent IL‑1β/IL‑18 release and pyroptosis; caspase‑1 and cathepsin inhibition reduced death in primed cells and defined assayable checkpoints (brandstetter2015lightinducesnlrp3 pages 9-11, brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 47-51). A2E stimulation (with IL‑1α priming) produced ASC specks, NLRP3‑dependent IL‑1β secretion, and caspase‑1‑sensitive IL‑1β maturation, with in vivo correlation in ABCA4−/− mice (anderson2013a2einducesil1ss pages 8-9). NLRP3 knockdown in stressed RPE reduced caspase‑1, IL‑1β, and IL‑18, further supporting target linkage (piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 47-49).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths include direct linkage to AMD‑relevant stimuli, orthogonal and quantifiable readouts (ASC specks, caspase‑1 activity, IL‑1β/IL‑18, LDH), and clear pharmacologic and genetic points of intervention, enabling screening and MoA confirmation; complement‑based priming increases physiological relevance (brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 80-85, brandstetter2015lightinducesnlrp3 pages 9-11). Weaknesses include cell‑line artifacts (LPS‑insensitivity in ARPE‑19), variable detectability of NLRP3 in human RPE, and mixed in vivo attribution to NLRP3 versus the broader CASP‑1 inflammasome network; assays should prioritize primary RPE, physiologic priming (C5a/IL‑1α), and include controls for NLRP3 dependence versus CASP‑1–centric effects (e.g., NLRP3 siRNA, ASC imaging, caspase‑1 and cathepsin inhibitors, IL1R blockade), with macrophage positive controls for benchmarking (tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 5-7, kosmidou2018issueswiththe pages 3-4, wooff2020caspase1dependentinflammasomesmediate pages 8-10, brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 92-95).

This assay is useful for screening modulators of inflammasome signaling in RPE provided priming/stimulation are physiologic and endpoints include caspase‑1 and cytokines alongside NLRP3‑specific measures. Given controversies around RPE NLRP3 expression, emphasize CASP‑1/IL‑1 readouts and orthogonal validation to ensure disease relevance. Integrating complement priming and lipofuscin‑photooxidative activation will improve translational fidelity.
