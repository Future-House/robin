Assay Overview: This assay quantifies mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) as an early readout of mitochondrial dysfunction central to dry AMD. Live RPE (primary human RPE from AMD and non-AMD donors and/or iPSC-RPE) are exposed to disease-relevant stressors (e.g., H2O2, cigarette smoke extract) with FCCP as a positive depolarizing control, then stained with JC-1 (red/green ratio) or TMRE/TMRM (normalized to mitochondrial mass, e.g., TOM20) for plate-based or high-content imaging readouts; optional co-measures include mitophagy markers and dynamics proteins to contextualize ΔΨm changes (DRP1, OPA1/MFN, LC3/Parkin) (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 11-13, lenin2023dysfunctionalautophagyproteostasis pages 2-4, fisher2022investigatingmitochondrialfission pages 9-10, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 10-11, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 2-3).

Biomedical Evidence: Mitochondrial dysfunction and decreased ΔΨm are repeatedly observed in aging RPE and are implicated in AMD pathogenesis, coinciding with lower ATP, increased ROS, and structural mitochondrial damage in AMD donor RPE and tissue (cristae loss, reduced number) (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 7-8, bilbaomalave2021mitochondrialdysfunctionand pages 2-4, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 3-5). Loss of ΔΨm triggers PINK1–Parkin mitophagy; AMD RPE show reduced basal PINK1 and impaired Parkin induction and mitophagy capacity after FCCP, directly linking depolarization sensing to a disease-defective quality-control axis (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 2-3, fisher2022investigatingmitochondrialfission pages 9-10). Multiple AMD-relevant insults produce membrane depolarization in RPE, with downstream bioenergetic failure, ROS, and apoptosis (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 11-13).

Previous Use: ΔΨm loss is a common endpoint in RPE stress models used to evaluate mitochondrial protectants; interventions that enhance mitochondrial quality control have increased ΔΨm in AMD-relevant cybrid systems, supporting ΔΨm as a drug-responsive efficacy marker (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 14-14). iPSC-RPE platforms are established for mitochondrial-targeted drug testing in AMD, providing disease-relevant cellular context for ΔΨm assays (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 15-17). Reviews recommend ΔΨm assays alongside ROS/ATP/mitophagy readouts to capture the RPE mitochondrial phenotype under different stressors (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 10-11, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 11-13).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—early, sensitive, scalable readout; mechanistically tied to mitophagy (PINK1–Parkin); discriminates AMD versus control RPE responses to depolarization; compatible with primary and iPSC-RPE; high-content imaging allows spatial analysis (fisher2022investigatingmitochondrialfission pages 9-10, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 2-3, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 11-13). Weaknesses—ΔΨm shifts are stressor- and context-dependent, and alone do not capture the full mitochondrial pathobiology; dye signals can be confounded by mitochondrial mass/dynamics, necessitating orthogonal measures (ROS, ATP, OCR/ECAR, TOM20, mitophagy flux) and imaging-based localization; in vitro models and aged-donor cultures introduce selection and interpretation biases (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 10-11, lenin2023dysfunctionalautophagyproteostasis pages 2-4, fisher2022investigatingmitochondrialfission pages 9-10).

Mitochondrial membrane potential assays are biologically aligned with dry AMD and are practical for screening mitochondrial protectants in RPE. They should be embedded in a multi-parametric mitochondrial panel to ensure mechanistic interpretability and translational relevance (fisher2022investigatingmitochondrialfission pages 9-10, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 10-11, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 11-13).
