Assay Overview: An H2O2-induced oxidative stress assay models the ROS-driven injury to retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) implicated in dry AMD by exposing ARPE-19 or patient-derived iPSC-RPE to defined H2O2 pulses and quantifying cell injury and stress responses in 96-well format (e.g., 0.3–1 mM for 1–2 h; 0.4–10 mM for 24–48 h; repeated short exposures) (arend2015idebenonepreventsoxidative pages 2-3, liu2010therapeuticeffectsand pages 1-2, aryan2016oxidativestressinduces pages 1-3, golestaneh2016repressedsirt1pgc1αpathway pages 4-5). Primary readouts include viability/metabolic activity (MTT/PrestoBlue), intracellular ROS (DCFH-DA), senescence (SA-β-Gal), apoptosis (BAX/Bcl-2, DNA fragmentation), antioxidant enzymes (e.g., catalase), and mitochondrial function (ATP), enabling both screening and mechanistic inference (arend2015idebenonepreventsoxidative pages 2-3, liu2010therapeuticeffectsand pages 1-2, arend2015idebenonepreventsoxidative pages 1-2, golestaneh2016repressedsirt1pgc1αpathway pages 4-5).

Biomedical Evidence: Oxidative stress is a core driver of AMD: RPE are highly susceptible due to light exposure, high oxygen tension, lipid load, and age-related declines in antioxidant defenses; patient iPSC-RPE exhibit elevated baseline ROS and reduced antioxidant capacity, with mitochondrial defects and SIRT1/PGC-1α repression, and oxidative stress promotes RPE senescence/SASP linked to disease progression (chang2014thegenerationof pages 1-2, chang2014thegenerationof pages 5-7, golestaneh2016repressedsirt1pgc1αpathway pages 1-2, golestaneh2016repressedsirt1pgc1αpathway pages 2-3, lazzarini2022oxidativestressin pages 1-2). Clinical and translational evidence (e.g., AREDS/AREDS2) supports antioxidant strategies, reinforcing assay relevance (kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 13-14).

Previous Use: In ARPE-19, H2O2 produces dose-dependent viability loss, ROS, lipid peroxidation, senescence, and apoptotic signaling that are attenuated by protective agents, demonstrating assay responsiveness and mechanistic reporting (e.g., idebenone stabilizing BAX/Bcl-2; mannitol upregulating catalase; curcumin protecting AMD iPSC-RPE) (arend2015idebenonepreventsoxidative pages 1-2, arend2015idebenonepreventsoxidative pages 2-3, liu2010therapeuticeffectsand pages 1-2, chang2014thegenerationof pages 1-2). The platform is widely applied for oxidative-stress compound screening with standardized readouts (subramaniam2022oxidativestressand pages 15-16).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: human-relevant, scalable, and mechanistically rich; compatible with patient iPSC-RPE to capture disease backgrounds and with NRF2/mitochondrial endpoints for target engagement (golestaneh2016repressedsirt1pgc1αpathway pages 4-5, webster2023nrf2associatedantioxidantstress pages 77-79). Weaknesses: ARPE-19 have maturational and functional limitations; H2O2 may not model iron-driven ferroptosis or necrosis and can miss cell-type differences observed with Fe-NTA; results vary with cell state, dose, and exposure paradigm; single-cell-type systems omit immune/complement and tissue context (voisin2018hrpecellsderived pages 12-15, voisin2018hrpecellsderived pages 8-12, aryan2016oxidativestressinduces pages 3-5, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 13-14). Best practice is to use AMD donor iPSC-RPE, include mitochondrial/NRF2/senescence readouts, and complement H2O2 with iron or lipid electrophiles plus in vivo validation (golestaneh2016repressedsirt1pgc1αpathway pages 1-2, webster2023nrf2associatedantioxidantstress pages 77-79, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 13-14).

An H2O2-induced RPE viability assay is a useful first-line screen for dry AMD therapeutics targeting oxidative injury, with established sensitivity to protective mechanisms and clear mechanistic readouts (arend2015idebenonepreventsoxidative pages 1-2, liu2010therapeuticeffectsand pages 1-2). To enhance disease relevance and translatability, prioritize iPSC-RPE from AMD donors and complement H2O2 with iron/lipid stressors and mitochondrial/NRF2/senescence endpoints (voisin2018hrpecellsderived pages 12-15, golestaneh2016repressedsirt1pgc1αpathway pages 1-2, webster2023nrf2associatedantioxidantstress pages 77-79).
