Assay Overview:  
The tandem RFP–GFP–LC3 reporter imaging assay employs a dual-fluorescence construct fused to LC3 to differentiate immature autophagosomes (GFP+RFP+, yellow) from mature autolysosomes (RFP+ only) in live retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells. Automated live-cell imaging quantifies vesicle number, size, and distribution in response to stress or drug treatment, with flux controls (e.g., chloroquine) validating alterations in autophagosome–lysosome fusion. This assay is implemented in primary RPE cell cultures, which recapitulate key aspects of autophagy dysfunction seen in dry AMD (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 31-35).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Autophagic dysregulation is central to the pathogenesis of dry AMD, where impaired clearance leads to lipofuscin accumulation, oxidative stress, and subsequent RPE degeneration. Disruption in autophagic flux is linked to the accumulation of damaged proteins and organelles, fueling chronic inflammation and cellular demise, all of which are evident in AMD pathology (kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3, liton2023autophagyinthe pages 23-24). Hence, the modulation and restoration of autophagic flux remain promising therapeutic targets in the clinical landscape of dry AMD (zhang2015protectiveeffectof pages 1-2).

Previous Use:  
The tandem reporter has been validated in RPE models to monitor autophagy dynamics, with studies demonstrating that pharmacologic blockade (chloroquine) increases GFP signal accumulation and that treatments such as trehalose enhance autophagic flux (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8, rao2022monitoringbasalautophagy pages 9-11). Its quantifiable outputs have thus been effectively leveraged in early drug discovery studies to correlate flux restoration with disease-relevant cellular responses.

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay’s strength lies in its capacity for real-time, quantitative analysis of autophagic flux, providing mechanistic insights into autophagosome maturation in RPE cells—a critical event in dry AMD pathogenesis. However, technical caveats such as potential interference from lipofuscin autofluorescence and artefacts from LC3 overexpression require rigorous optimization and complementary validation (rao2022monitoringbasalautophagy pages 9-11, rao2022monitoringbasalautophagy pages 2-4).

This assay is a valuable tool for early-stage screening of autophagy-modulating therapeutics in dry AMD. It offers precise mechanistic readouts essential for advancing drug discovery while demanding careful control of technical variables to ensure reliability.
