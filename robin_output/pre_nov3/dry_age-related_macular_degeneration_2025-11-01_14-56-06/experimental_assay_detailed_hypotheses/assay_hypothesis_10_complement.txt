Assay Overview:  
The assay employs human retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells, cultured on porous supports to facilitate the formation of sub‑RPE, drusen‐like deposits. Upon exposure to complement‐active human serum, these deposits initiate local generation and deposition of the membrane attack complex (MAC/C5b‑9), which is then detected and quantified by immunostaining and confocal microscopy. Additionally, cell lysis is measured using cytotoxicity assays (forest2015developmentofcell pages 41-46, forest2015developmentofcell pages 60-67).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Complement dysregulation and the resulting MAC deposition play pivotal roles in the pathogenesis of dry AMD by driving chronic, localized inflammatory responses and RPE injury. Several studies show that deposition of terminal complement complexes within drusen correlates with progressive RPE dysfunction, and genetic links with polymorphisms in complement regulators (e.g., CFH) emphasize the clinical relevance of this pathway (park2021complementsystemand pages 14-15, bora2015relationshipbetweenthe pages 1-2, piri2023roleofcomplement pages 4-6).

Previous Use:  
This cell culture model has been previously implemented to dissect the roles of complement pathways using serum depleted of specific components (e.g., C1q, C5, Factor B), and it has been instrumental in testing complement inhibitors such as compstatin analogs that effectively reduce MAC deposition and protect RPE cells (forest2015developmentofcell pages 46-50, forest2015developmentofcell pages 89-93, forest2015developmentofcell pages 12-17).

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay’s strength lies in its ability to recapitulate key aspects of dry AMD pathogenesis by modeling complement-mediated damage and providing quantifiable endpoints for both MAC deposition and cytotoxicity. However, limitations include potential differences between cultured RPE and the aged, stressed RPE in vivo, and challenges in fully replicating the complex regulation of complement in systemic circulation (forest2015developmentofcell pages 36-41).  

This in vitro model is a valuable platform for early-stage therapeutic screening in dry AMD, offering both mechanistic insights and quantitative assessments of complement inhibition efficacy. Its translational relevance underscores its potential for advancing drug discovery in AMD.
