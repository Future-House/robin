Assay Overview:  
This assay uses 3D RPE spheroids loaded with glycoxidized lipofuscin to mimic drusen accumulation, a hallmark of dry AMD. The model comprises primary or hiPSC-derived RPE forming a differentiated outer monolayer, which, along with an underlying Bruch’s membrane–like ECM, enables the deposition of lipofuscin-driven, autofluorescent aggregates. Readouts include live imaging of deposit formation, transmission electron microscopy for ultrastructural analysis, and quantification of drusen-associated markers (e.g., TIMP3, APOE) via immunostaining and gene expression assays (mesch2021agedhumanipscrpe pages 15-17, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 1-4).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Drusen formation, directly linked to RPE dysfunction, oxidative stress, and impaired phagocytosis, is central to the pathogenesis of dry AMD. The assay recapitulates key pathological events including ECM remodeling and complement pathway activation. Clinical studies corroborate that the accumulation of such deposits precedes RPE atrophy and vision loss, making this model highly relevant for understanding disease progression (geathers2023developmentofaa pages 20-24, geathers2023developmentofa pages 20-24).

Previous Use:  
Prior studies have successfully employed similar 3D RPE spheroid models to explore drusen biogenesis and identify molecular signatures of aging RPE. Usui et al. demonstrated that glycoxidized lipofuscin accelerates drusen-like deposit formation and alters the expression of key biomarkers, advancing our understanding of RPE-derived drusen formation (mesch2021agedhumanipscrpe pages 15-17, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 1-4).

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay’s strengths include physiological relevance through the recreation of a native-like RPE–Bruch’s membrane interface, multiparametric readouts, and the ability to monitor age-related, oxidative stress–induced changes in drusen formation. Weaknesses involve potential variability in RPE differentiation, challenges in quantifying deposits due to pigmentation, and the need for further validation against in vivo data (mesch2021…anin pages 74-77, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 1-4).

This 3D RPE spheroid drusen accumulation assay is a promising tool for early-stage drug discovery in dry AMD. It enables detailed mechanistic studies of RPE dysfunction and extracellular deposition while providing multiple endpoints for therapeutic evaluation.
