Overall Evaluation: Strengths and weaknesses of this assay for testing therapeutics for dry age-related macular degeneration.

Assay Overview: Seahorse extracellular flux profiling (XFe96) quantifies real-time oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) to interrogate mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OxPhos) and glycolysis in RPE, directly modeling the mitochondrial bioenergetic deficit implicated in dry AMD pathogenesis. OCR traces with sequential oligomycin, FCCP, and rotenone/antimycin A yield basal respiration, ATP-linked respiration, maximal respiration, and spare respiratory capacity; the ATP Rate assay partitions mitochondrial vs glycolytic ATP production, and post-assay Hoechst staining enables cell-number normalization. The assay is implemented in patient-specific iPSC-RPE and primary human adult RPE (haRPE), typically plated on Matrigel-coated XF96 plates with five technical replicates per condition and integrated with orthogonal RPE functional readouts (e.g., polarized PEDF/VEGF secretion, phagocytosis). (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 12-14, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionina pages 56-61, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionina pages 159-162)

Biomedical Evidence: Mitochondrial abnormalities in AMD RPE include reduced mitochondrial number/mass, disrupted ultrastructure, decreased ETC subunits and mitochondrial chaperones, and mtDNA damage that correlates with disease severity—changes predicted to reduce ATP output and precipitate a bioenergetic crisis. Seahorse analyses in cultured human RPE from AMD donors show reduced OxPhos and, in some reports, reduced glycolysis, with other studies observing compensatory glycolytic increases, consistent with model- and stage-dependent metabolic adaptations. These bioenergetic defects are mechanistically linked to impaired RPE–photoreceptor metabolic coupling and downstream inflammatory/cell death pathways, providing a clinically relevant axis for therapeutic intervention. (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionina pages 32-36, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 49-52, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 32-36, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionina pages 36-40, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 36-40)

Previous Use: In iPSC-RPE from AMD patients, CMST and ATP Rate assays detected drug-induced increases in maximal respiration, spare capacity, and basal respiration after AMPK activation or autophagy induction, with time-dependent signaling corroborated by pAMPK biochemistry. Primary haRPE screens using CMST reported AMD-dependent improvements in mitochondrial function in response to mitochondrial-protective compounds, demonstrating disease-selective pharmacodynamics. Seahorse profiling has also resolved genotype-stratified deficits (e.g., CFH Y402H carriers show broad mitochondrial impairments) and unmasked stress-sensitive phenotypes by applying mitochondrial inhibitors, supporting its use in precision and stress-challenge paradigms. (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionina pages 36-40, ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 14-15, fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4)

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—direct, quantitative, and high-throughput functional readout of OxPhos and glycolysis in patient-derived RPE; detects baseline disease phenotypes, genotype effects, and pharmacologic rescue; integrates with ATP partitioning and orthogonal RPE functions for mechanistic depth. Weaknesses—inter-donor and intra-model variability; iPSC-RPE may lack aging “metabolic memory,” requiring stress or environmental conditioning; OCR/ECAR are population averages sensitive to plating, substrate, and timing; divergent glycolytic findings across studies necessitate careful interpretation and complementary assays (mitophagy, mtDNA damage, ROS). This platform is well-suited as a primary functional assay for mitochondrial-targeted therapeutic testing in dry AMD, provided rigorous standardization, appropriate controls, and integration with disease-relevant co-endpoints. (fisher2022humanipscand pages 11-12, fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 32-36, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionina pages 32-36, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionina pages 159-162, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15)

Seahorse bioenergetic profiling in iPSC-RPE and primary haRPE directly measures the mitochondrial deficits central to dry AMD and sensitively detects pharmacologic rescue, genotype effects, and stress-induced vulnerabilities. Variability across models and the youthful state of iPSC-RPE are addressable by stress paradigms, normalization, and orthogonal readouts. As a core functional assay for mitochondrial therapeutics in dry AMD, it is highly useful when embedded in a standardized, multi-assay pipeline. (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionina pages 36-40, fisher2022humanipscand pages 11-12)
