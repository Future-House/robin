Biomedical Evidence: RPE mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired autophagy/lysosomal clearance are central to dry AMD: human donor RPE show mtDNA damage, reduced oxidative phosphorylation, and correlations with disease severity; aging RPE exhibit downregulated autophagy and proteostasis decline, leading to dysfunctional mitochondria, lipofuscin accumulation, and drusen formation (lenin2023dysfunctionalautophagyproteostasis pages 2-4, lenin2023dysfunctionalautophagyproteostasis pages 1-2). Clinical-translational links include transcriptomic downregulation of autophagy with age and proteomic ties between drusen composition and degenerating RPE, implicating disrupted clearance; restoring autophagy in RPE protects retina in vivo (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 1-2). Genetic and mechanistic models (NFE2L2/PGC‑1α dKO; early AMD perifoveal RPE with reduced PINK1) demonstrate mitophagy defects, lysosomal bottlenecks, and EMT/cell death pathways—validating mitochondrial–lysosomal endpoints as disease-relevant (gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 1-3, datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 9-12).

Previous Use: High-content, multiplexed imaging of mitochondrial and lysosomal features in RPE has been applied to model AMD-relevant stress, quantify mitophagy initiation versus completion (LC3/PINK1/Parkin vs LAMP2 colocalization), and assess flux with lysosomal inhibitors; NaIO3 and antimycin A paradigms produce robust, quantifiable phenotypes (skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 9-11, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 11-12). Patient iPSC-RPE platforms have been used for drug testing with integrated OCR and lysosome/autophagy readouts, demonstrating pathway- and patient-specific responses and supporting personalized screening (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 15-17, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 9-11). In vivo and ex vivo confirmation of imaging endpoints—LC3B turnover, p62 degradation, and RPE structural protection after autophagy enhancement—supports assay responsiveness (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 1-2). Standardized confocal quantification pipelines (e.g., ComDet) and ultrastructural validation (TEM) have been established in RPE models of incomplete mitophagy (gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 10-12, gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 7-10).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: strong mechanistic alignment to core AMD biology; rich, multiparametric single-cell quantitation; scalable to 96-well HCI; compatible with patient iPSC-RPE and genetic stratification; drug-responsive with orthogonal functional readouts for triage (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 15-17, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 17-18, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6). Weaknesses: steady-state morphology can confound induction vs blockage—flux controls and longitudinal designs are mandatory; late-stage autolysosomal failure is common and must be captured by LAMP2/mitochondria colocalization and degradation metrics; ARPE-19 has limitations; inter-patient heterogeneity requires line-specific baselining; in vitro monocultures lack complement/choroid interactions and chronicity, necessitating downstream validation (gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 7-10, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 11-12, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 11-12, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 9-11, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 15-17). Net, the assay is well-justified as a phenotypic primary/secondary screen when paired with flux and bioenergetic validation.

High-content imaging of mitochondrial and lysosomal morphology in RPE models directly measures central dry AMD mechanisms and yields drug-responsive, quantifiable endpoints. With flux controls and orthogonal functional assays, it is a strong screening tool, particularly in patient iPSC-RPE cohorts to address heterogeneity. Its predictive value increases when late-stage autophagy and bioenergetics are explicitly incorporated (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 1-2, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 9-11, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 15-17).
