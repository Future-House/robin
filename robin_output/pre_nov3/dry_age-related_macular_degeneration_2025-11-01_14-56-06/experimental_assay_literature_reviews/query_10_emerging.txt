Query: Emerging high-content imaging and machine learning analysis pipelines for quantifying multiple phenotypes in RPE dry AMD assays, such as changes in cell morphology, pigmentation, autofluorescence, mitochondrial dynamics, drusen-like deposit formation, label-free modalities, and multiplexed readouts

A practical HCI stack combines multiplex immunofluorescence with label‑free channels and volumetric imaging to capture heterogeneous RPE phenotypes. Multiplexed panels (e.g., ZO‑1, RPE65, PMEL, collagen IV, phospho‑ezrin, F‑actin) imaged on Zeiss LSM 980 Airyscan 2 in Multiplex Mode yield high‑SNR, low‑photodose acquisitions; far‑red secondaries mitigate strong ECM autofluorescence from collagen/elastin and can be paired in similar emission bands to improve SNR in autofluorescent tissues (useful for pigmentation and AF quantification) (daniele2024agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 84-87). Label‑free quantitative phase imaging adds refractive‑index–based morphology/mass/texture without dyes, while tissue optical clearing plus two‑photon microscopy enables 3D imaging of thick samples for drusen‑like deposit quantification; conical‑diffraction beam shaping with maximum‑likelihood reconstruction provides gentle super‑resolution for live mitochondrial/cytoskeletal features (blanco2022bookofabstracts pages 116-120). For live dynamics, spinning‑disk confocal supports fast time‑lapse; Mitometer quantifies mitochondrial morphology/motion, and temporal CNN+LSTM backbones capture dynamic events; ABANICCO supplies robust, unsupervised color segmentation/unmixing for multiplex and autofluorescence separation (blanco2022bookofabstracts pages 78-81).

Analysis pipelines couple deep learning segmentation with multiparametric profiling and supervised classification. Cellpose integrated into CellProfiler improves cell/TJ delineation relative to watershed, stabilizing morphology features (e.g., form factor, perimeter) (gao2024useofmachine pages 14-22). Broad feature sets (shape, intensity, texture, adjacency) from single cells and tight junctions feed supervised classifiers; human‑in‑the‑loop labeling with iterative AUC monitoring converges rapidly (AUC ≥ 0.7 ~10 iterations) (gao2024useofmachine pages 5-8, gao2024useofmachine pages 8-11). Classifier outputs as normalized per‑well positive counts deliver strong assay quality (robust Z’ ~0.75–0.81) and support dose–response and pharmacologic rescue readouts aligned with TER functional measures (gao2024useofmachine pages 22-24, gao2024useofmachine pages 5-8). Multiplexed profiling (Cell Painting) and supervised multi‑class models quantify enrichment of multiple phenotypes simultaneously; Random Forest/analyst tools enable interactive exploration and feature importance (gao2024useofmachine pages 22-24, gao2024useofmachine pages 11-14).

To address limited annotations in multiplexed images, Synplex can generate cohort‑like synthetic multiplex IF for data augmentation and in silico marker selection, improving classifier robustness across morphology, pigmentation, AF, and deposit contexts (blanco2022bookofabstracts pages 116-120). Orthogonal SEM/TEM provide ultrastructural validation alongside fluorescence and label‑free channels (daniele2024agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 84-87).

An end‑to‑end RPE AMD pipeline therefore pairs multiplex/label‑free, super‑resolution‑capable acquisition with Cellpose+CellProfiler feature extraction and supervised single‑cell classifiers, producing robust, scalable multi‑phenotype readouts. Synthetic multiplex data and volumetric clearing/2P imaging extend coverage to AF‑sensitive pigmentation and drusen‑like deposits while maintaining gentle live‑cell capability for mitochondrial dynamics (blanco2022bookofabstracts pages 116-120, gao2024useofmachine pages 22-24, blanco2022bookofabstracts pages 78-81, daniele2024agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 84-87, gao2024useofmachine pages 5-8).
