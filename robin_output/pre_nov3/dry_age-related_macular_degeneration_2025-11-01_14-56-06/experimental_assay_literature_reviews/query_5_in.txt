Query: In what ways do mitochondrial bioenergetics deficits and altered oxidative phosphorylation contribute to RPE and photoreceptor vulnerability in dry AMD, which mitochondrial quality control pathways (e.g., mitophagy) are disrupted, and what high-throughput assays can measure mitochondrial function and ROS generation in relevant cell models?

Mitochondrial OXPHOS failure in RPE lowers ATP supply needed for outer‑segment phagocytosis, ion/nutrient transport, and proteostasis, driving lipofuscin/drusen accumulation, Bruch’s membrane changes, and RPE loss that secondarily endangers photoreceptors (lenin2023dysfunctionalautophagyproteostasis pages 2-4, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 4-6). RPE bioenergetic collapse also uncouples the retinal metabolic ecosystem: damaged RPE mitochondria force a shift to glycolysis, diverting glucose from photoreceptors, reducing photoreceptor glycolysis/lactate output, and precipitating photoreceptor starvation and death (ferrington2020mitochondrialdefectsdrive pages 2-4, fisher2018perspectiveonamd pages 3-5). Structural and molecular lesions—mtDNA damage, reduced ETC subunits, loss of cristae/mitochondrial mass—directly impair OXPHOS capacity in human AMD RPE (ferrington2020mitochondrialdefectsdrive pages 4-6, brooks2023roleofmitophagy pages 5-6). Defective OXPHOS elevates mitochondrial ROS and redox stress, amplifying mtDNA/protein/lipid damage, triggering cytochrome c–mediated apoptosis and inflammasome activation, and further degrading mitochondrial performance (basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 7-8, fisher2018perspectiveonamd pages 2-2, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 1-3).

Multiple mitochondrial quality‑control axes are perturbed: biogenesis signaling (PGC‑1α/NRF/TFAM) is insufficient, fission–fusion is imbalanced (↑Drp1, ↓Mfn2) with consequent OXPHOS loss, and mitophagy/turnover is reduced (lower PINK1/Parkin activity), allowing dysfunctional mitochondria to accumulate; lysosomal/autophagy defects (including mTOR‑mediated autophagy inhibition) exacerbate this burden (basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 7-8, ferrington2020mitochondrialdefectsdrive pages 4-6, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12, si2023theroleof pages 7-9, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 9-10).

High‑throughput functional readouts include:
- Extracellular flux (Seahorse) assays of OCR/ECAR to quantify basal/ATP‑linked/maximal respiration and spare capacity; stress with FCCP or CoCl2 to unmask deficits (fisher2018perspectiveonamd pages 2-3, bhattacharya2022modelingofmitochondrial pages 15-18, fisher2022investigatingmitochondrialfission pages 10-10).
- Plate‑based ATP assays with glycolysis blockade (e.g., hexokinase inhibition) to isolate mitochondrial ATP output (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 2-5).
- High‑throughput ROS quantification under oxidative challenge (e.g., H2O2) to measure elevated cellular/mitochondrial ROS in AMD RPE (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 2-5, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 9-10).
- ΔΨm measurements to assess mitochondrial membrane potential loss as a surrogate of OXPHOS dysfunction (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 4-6, lenin2023dysfunctionalautophagyproteostasis pages 2-4).

Defective OXPHOS drives ATP shortfall, excess mtROS, and metabolic uncoupling, directly compromising RPE support functions and starving photoreceptors. MQC is disrupted at the levels of biogenesis, dynamics (Drp1/Mfn2), and mitophagy (PINK1/Parkin), with autophagy–lysosome failure compounding mitochondrial accumulation. Seahorse OCR/ECAR, ATP partitioning, ROS assays, and ΔΨm metrics provide scalable, cell‑model readouts of these mitochondrial defects.
