Query: Which complement components and regulatory factors are most critically implicated in the pathogenesis of dry AMD, how do C3 and C5 activation fragments contribute to retinal pigment epithelial dysfunction and drusen formation, and what in vitro assays can quantitatively measure complement-mediated damage or inhibition in cultured RPE cells?

Critically implicated complement elements in dry AMD center on the alternative pathway: C3/C3b and the downstream C5→C5b-9 (MAC) effector arm, with genetic and biochemical support for dysregulation of CFH/FHL-1 and CFI, and membrane regulators MCP/CD46, DAF/CD55, CR1/CD35, and CD59; risk and functional data repeatedly implicate CFH (Y402H), C3, CFI, CFB/C2, C9, and CFHR1/3 in disease susceptibility and local overactivation at Bruch’s membrane/choriocapillaris (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 1-2, clark2018theeyeas pages 2-4, desai2022complementcascadeinhibition pages 3-4, cao2016theroleof pages 27-31, wilke2024complementregulationin pages 5-6, armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 7-8, wilke2024complementregulationin pages 2-3).

C3a and C5a are detected in drusen, act as potent anaphylatoxins that drive chemotaxis and VEGF upregulation from RPE, and thereby promote a pro‑inflammatory, pro‑angiogenic milieu tied to drusen biogenesis (park2021complementsystemand pages 4-6, kassa2019complementinhibitionas pages 1-7, piri2023roleofcomplement pages 2-4). MAC deposits accumulate in Bruch’s membrane and the choriocapillaris, form membrane pores that cause lytic injury, and are linked to RPE/choriocapillaris degeneration and GA progression, especially under chronic alternative‑pathway “tick‑over” (desai2022complementcascadeinhibition pages 3-4, qin2021complementinhibitorsin pages 1-2). Local C3b deposition in BM/choriocapillaris and impaired FH/FHL‑1 regulation (e.g., Y402H) further support sustained complement activation within drusen‑forming sites (wilke2024complementregulationin pages 5-6, bora2015relationshipbetweenthe pages 2-3).

Quantitative RPE in vitro assays include: (i) C5b-9 (MAC) deposition on RPE surfaces by immunoassay as a direct terminal‑pathway injury readout (bora2015relationshipbetweenthe pages 2-3, desai2022complementcascadeinhibition pages 3-4); (ii) ELISA quantification of anaphylatoxins (C3a, C5a) and AP fragments (Ba/Bb) in supernatants to index activation magnitude (chrzanowska2018newinsightinto pages 1-2, qin2021complementinhibitorsin pages 2-4); (iii) surface C3b/iC3b deposition to monitor C3 convertase activity and FI/FH cofactor efficacy (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 1-2, wilke2024complementregulationin pages 2-3); and (iv) complement‑dependent cytotoxicity/permeabilization assays capturing MAC pore–mediated lysis (qin2021complementinhibitorsin pages 1-2). Inhibition is quantified as reductions in these metrics upon addition of regulators or inhibitors (e.g., CD59 augmentation, CFI supplementation, anti‑properdin), consistent with in vivo data that CD59 limits MAC and CFI lowers C3 cleavage products (patel2022targetingthecomplement pages 10-12, wilke2024complementregulationin pages 2-3).

The alternative pathway with C3/C3b and C5→MAC, regulated by CFH/FHL‑1, CFI, and membrane inhibitors (CD46/CD55/CR1/CD59), is most strongly linked to dry AMD. C3a/C5a in drusen drive inflammation and VEGF, while MAC causes RPE/choriocapillaris injury. In vitro, quantify MAC deposition, C3a/C5a/Ba/Bb release, C3b/iC3b surface load, and MAC‑dependent cytotoxicity to measure complement damage and its inhibition in RPE cultures (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 1-2, desai2022complementcascadeinhibition pages 3-4, bora2015relationshipbetweenthe pages 2-3, chrzanowska2018newinsightinto pages 1-2, qin2021complementinhibitorsin pages 2-4, wilke2024complementregulationin pages 2-3, qin2021complementinhibitorsin pages 1-2, patel2022targetingthecomplement pages 10-12).
