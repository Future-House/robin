Query: Evaluation of autophagy and lysosomal dysfunction in RPE cells in dry AMD, with search for assays measuring LC3 lipidation, p62 accumulation, lysotracker staining, cathepsin activity, and modulators of autophagic flux in vitro

Robust evaluation in RPE (ARPE‑19/iPSC‑RPE) combines LC3 lipidation, p62 turnover, lysosomal acidity/integrity, and cathepsin competence, interpreted with flux controls. LC3‑II is quantified by immunoblot of whole‑cell lysates normalized to housekeeping proteins; flux is resolved by clamping lysosomal degradation with CQ, NH4Cl, bafilomycin A1, or lysosomal protease inhibitors (E64d+leupeptin), and by imaging GFP‑LC3 puncta or tandem LC3 reporters (mRFP‑GFP‑LC3; GFP‑LC3‑RFP‑LC3ΔG) to distinguish autophagosomes versus autolysosomes and to obtain ratiometric degradation readouts (abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 137-141, chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 22-23, chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 23-24). In ARPE‑19, trehalose increases LC3‑II and converts diffuse GFP‑LC3 to puncta; free GFP fragments accumulate with CQ, indicating increased flux to autolysosomes (abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 137-141).

p62/SQSTM1 is measured by immunoblot (and IF) alongside LC3, with parallel qPCR to exclude transcription‑driven changes; accumulation with reduced LC3‑II clearance indicates impaired autophagic turnover and is observed in AMD RPE (abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 73-78, abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 106-111, abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 23-27). Proteasome inhibitor MG132 and CQ can be used to dissect proteasomal versus lysosomal contributions to p62/NRF2 turnover (abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 73-78).

Lysosomal function is profiled with LysoTracker/LysoSensor for acidity, complemented by integrity markers (LGALS3/galectin‑3 puncta, lysosomal‑METRIQ) and by enzyme activity assays; pH alone does not equate to flux (chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 22-23, chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 23-24, abokyi2021targetinglysosomesto pages 21-21). Cathepsin activity is measured with fluorogenic probes (e.g., MagicRed for cathepsins; substrate assays for CTSs), noting cathepsin D/L activities are reduced in AMD RPE; LAMP2 and cathepsin D protein levels are informative but do not substitute for activity readouts (chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 23-24, chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 22-23, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7, abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 137-141).

In vitro modulators include trehalose (TFEB/lysosome‑autophagy program inducer), rapamycin or metformin (AMPK/mTOR axis), lysosomal blockers (CQ, NH4Cl, bafilomycin A1), protease inhibitors (E64d+leupeptin), and genetic perturbations (ATG5/ATG7 knockdown/knockout; TFEB activation) to define flux dependence (abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 151-154, abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 129-133, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7, abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 23-27, chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 22-23, chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 23-24, abokyi2021targetinglysosomesto pages 10-13).

In RPE AMD models, quantify LC3‑II with lysosomal blockade, track p62 protein plus mRNA, and deploy GFP/tandem LC3 reporters to call flux. Pair lysosomal pH/integrity assays with cathepsin activity readouts, and use pharmacologic/genetic modulators (e.g., CQ/bafilomycin vs trehalose/rapamycin/TFEB) to mechanistically attribute defects to formation versus degradation steps (abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 137-141, chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 22-23, chen2024internationalconsensusguidelines pages 23-24, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7).
