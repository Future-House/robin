Query: What are the key molecular and cellular mechanisms driving dry age-related macular degeneration—specifically retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) dysfunction, complement cascade activation, mitochondrial and oxidative stress pathways, lipid metabolism dysregulation, impaired autophagy and inflammasome signaling, drusen biogenesis, and associated transcriptomic or proteomic alterations—that could be targeted or recapitulated in mechanistic cell culture models?

Dry AMD is initiated by RPE degeneration driven by convergent oxidative, mitochondrial, lipid, proteostatic, and innate immune insults that are experimentally tractable in cell models (primary or iPSC‑RPE, ARPE‑19) (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 59-62). Oxidative stress from POS phagocytosis, blue light, and mitochondria (ETC I/III) triggers mitochondrial depolarization, cytochrome c release, and caspase‑3/9 activation; A2E lipofuscin photo‑oxidation binds/inhibits COX, increases ROS, and promotes RPE apoptosis, while mtDNA damage with macula‑specific lesions, reduced repair, and proteomic loss of mtHsp70, ATP synthase subunits, and mitochondrial translation factors indicate bioenergetic failure (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 1-2). Lipid dysregulation is central: POS DHA-rich PUFAs yield MDA, 4‑HNE, and CEP adducts that are proteolysis‑resistant DAMPs, accumulate in RPE/drusen, and are practical inducers of AMD phenotypes in vitro (basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 4-5). Complement overactivation—driven by drusen constituents and CFH/CFB/CFI/C3 risk alleles—causes MAC‑mediated RPE injury in GA and pro‑angiogenic signaling via C3a/C5a‑induced VEGF/TGF‑β2 secretion (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, park2021complementsystemand pages 2-4). Chronic proteostasis failure (autophagy/lysosome and UPS) fosters lipofuscin, impaired flux (LC3, ATG5/ATG7), and inflammasome engagement; drusen‑associated C1q activates NLRP3 with IL‑1β/IL‑18 release, amplified by TLR4/CD36/RAGE–C5a crosstalk (chu2024therelationshipbetween pages 3-4, garciaquintanilla2022recentadvancesin pages 1-2, kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 16-23). Drusen biogenesis reflects extracellular deposition of oxidized lipids, complement, apolipoproteins, and amyloid‑β within Bruch’s membrane, accompanied by ECM/elastic changes and EMT that degrade RPE polarity and barrier function (hyttinen2023noncodingrnasregulating pages 2-4, garciaquintanilla2022recentadvancesin pages 1-2). Proteomics repeatedly detects complement components, lipid metabolism factors, oxidative‑stress proteins, angiogenesis/ECM markers, and mitochondrial protein losses; transcriptomics shows shifts toward senescence, complement dysregulation, and angiogenic programs (garciaquintanilla2022recentadvancesin pages 1-2, ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, hernandez2025roleofoxidative pages 4-6).

Model implementation: apply H2O2, A2E+blue light, cigarette smoke mimetics, oxidized lipoproteins, or CEP/MDA/4‑HNE; perturb mitophagy/mtDNA integrity; expose to C3a/C5a or inhibit complement; and measure autophagic flux/lysosomal function (LC3, ATG5/7), mitochondrial health (mtHsp70, ATP synthase), NLRP3–IL‑1β/IL‑18, VEGF/TGF‑β2, MMP2/9–TIMP1/3, complement/MAC, and drusen‑like deposits (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 16-23, chu2024therelationshipbetween pages 3-4, garciaquintanilla2022recentadvancesin pages 1-2, basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 5-7).

Dry AMD is a feed‑forward cascade centered on RPE oxidative–mitochondrial damage, lipid peroxidation, proteostasis/autophagy failure, and dysregulated complement/inflammasome signaling that drives drusen formation and cell loss (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 16-23). These axes can be recapitulated in vitro with defined oxidative/lipid/complement insults and quantified by mitochondrial, autophagic, inflammasome, complement, and secretome/drusen readouts (chu2024therelationshipbetween pages 3-4, garciaquintanilla2022recentadvancesin pages 1-2). Proteomic and genetic risk markers (complement and lipid genes; mitochondrial proteins) provide mechanistic endpoints and stratification handles (garciaquintanilla2022recentadvancesin pages 1-2, landowski2022targetinglipidmetabolism pages 1-2, ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19).
