Assay Overview:  
This assay utilizes ARPE-19 or iPSC‐RPE monolayers exposed to oxidative challenges such as H2O2 or A2E combined with blue light to induce mitochondrial depolarization, increased ROS generation, release of cytochrome c, and subsequent caspase‐3/9 activation. Readouts include ROS detection via H2DCFDA, mitochondrial membrane potential assessment using TMRM/TMRE, and cytochrome c release measured by ELISA, with downstream caspase activity assays to monitor apoptosis (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 11-13).  

Biomedical Evidence:  
Mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress are recognized as central to dry AMD pathogenesis. The RPE, due to its high exposure to oxidative insults from both light and metabolic activity, exhibits cumulative mitochondrial damage that triggers bioenergetic failure and apoptosis (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 1-2). Clinically, increased mitochondrial DNA damage and impaired oxidative phosphorylation are hallmarks of AMD, making this assay’s focus on mitochondrial rescue directly relevant to the disease (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 1-3, bhattacharya2022modelingofmitochondrial pages 19-20).  

Previous Use:  
Similar oxidative-stress paradigms in RPE models have been widely employed for drug discovery purposes, screening for agents capable of preserving mitochondrial membrane potential, reducing intracellular ROS, and inhibiting apoptotic cascades (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 14-16, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 10-11). Notable studies in these models have identified mitochondrial-targeted compounds, such as humanin and NMN, that restore bioenergetics and mitigate cell death, thereby demonstrating the assay’s predictive validity (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 11-13, bhattacharya2022modelingofmitochondrial pages 5-6).  

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay’s strengths lie in its mechanistic relevance, rapid turnaround, and utilization of well-established reporters to measure mitochondrial health. However, its reductionist nature may fail to capture the full complexity of the AMD microenvironment and the interplay of additional pathogenic factors. Nonetheless, as a robust platform for initial screening of mitochondria‐targeted therapeutics, it is highly valuable for early-stage drug discovery in dry AMD (bhattacharya2022modelingofmitochondrial pages 15-18, bhattacharya2022modelingofmitochondrial pages 18-19).

This assay effectively models the mitochondrial and oxidative stress aspects central to AMD pathogenesis. It provides quantifiable and reproducible endpoints that are critical for screening candidate drugs aimed at rescuing mitochondrial function in the RPE.
