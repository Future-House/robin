Assay Overview  
• Aim: Model RPE cholesterol overload—a key feature of drusen biogenesis in dry AMD—by disrupting ABCA1‐dependent efflux and then testing compounds for their ability to restore cholesterol homeostasis.  
• Cells: Human ARPE-19 cell line, engineered by CRISPR/Cas9 to knock out ABCA1.  
• Protocol steps:  
  1. Load ABCA1–/– and isogenic control ARPE-19 with fluorescent NBD–cholesterol (or TopFluor–cholesterol) for 6–12 h.  
  2. Wash and incubate cells with apoA-I as a cholesterol acceptor for 4 h.  
  3. Collect medium and measure NBD fluorescence to quantify cholesterol efflux (% of total cell fluorescence released).  
  4. Fix cells and stain lipid droplets with BODIPY 493/503 (or LipidTOX) to quantify intracellular neutral lipid accumulation by high‐content imaging.  
• Readouts:  
  – Cholesterol efflux efficiency (NBD–cholesterol in medium vs. total)  
  – Lipid droplet number/area per cell (BODIPY image analysis)  
  – Viability and morphology controls (e.g., Hoechst for cell count, propidium iodide).  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Drusen composition & RPE lipid overload  
   – Histological studies (Curcio et al., 2010; Wang et al., 2015) show drusen cores are rich in esterified cholesterol and apolipoproteins (ApoE/ApoA-I).  
   – Lipid‐laden RPE cells and sub‐RPE deposits correlate with progression from early soft drusen to geographic atrophy (GA) in clinical imaging (Schmitz-Valckenberg et al., 2016).  
2. ABCA1 in RPE physiology & AMD genetics  
   – ABCA1 mediates RPE cholesterol efflux to HDL‐like particles; its expression is downregulated by oxidative stress and aging (Johnson et al., 2003).  
   – GWAS link ABCA1 locus variants to increased AMD risk (Fritsche et al., 2013), and expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) analyses show reduced ABCA1 mRNA in AMD donor RPE (Ratnapriya et al., 2019).  
3. Pathogenic cascade  
   – Impaired efflux → intracellular cholesterol accumulation → RPE dysfunction, inflammasome activation, complement deposition, and eventual photoreceptor loss (Vavvas et al., 2018; Hirao et al., 2020).  
   – Modulation of lipid homeostasis via nuclear receptors (e.g., LXRα/β agonists) preserves RPE integrity in vitro and in animal models (Yates et al., 2014).  

Previous Use  
• Macrophage cholesterol efflux assays are well established in atherosclerosis drug discovery (e.g., to screen LXR/RXR agonists).  
• Johnson et al. (2003) characterized ABCA1 function in ARPE-19 but did not screen compounds.  
• Swaroop et al. (2012) proposed RPE lipid dysregulation as a target in AMD but did not deploy a high-throughput cholesterol efflux screen.  
• Recent pilot studies have used primary human RPE and NBD–cholesterol to show LXR agonists boost efflux and reduce lipid droplet load (Chang et al., 2019), suggesting feasibility for small‐molecule screening.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Directly addresses a genetically and pathologically validated node in dry AMD (ABCA1‐mediated efflux).  
• Quantitative, ratiometric fluorescence readouts (NBD efflux + BODIPY imaging) suitable for medium‐ to high‐throughput screening.  
• Dual measurement of extracellular efflux and intracellular lipid accumulation gives orthogonal endpoints, improving assay robustness.  
• Amenable to mechanistic follow-up (e.g., coupling to transcriptomics, nuclear‐receptor activation assays).  

Weaknesses  
• ARPE-19 cells lack some primary RPE features (e.g., polarization, apical microvilli, native lipoprotein secretion), potentially limiting in vivo translation.  
• Complete ABCA1 KO may be more severe than the partial loss–of–function seen in AMD risk alleles, possibly biasing hit selection toward exaggerated effects.  
• Does not capture other dry AMD features: complement dysregulation, sub‐RPE extracellular matrix alterations, local inflammation, or photoreceptor–RPE interactions.  
• Reliance on exogenous apoA-I may not fully model the complex lipoprotein milieu in Bruch’s membrane.  

Conclusion  
This ABCA1‐knockout/NBD–cholesterol efflux + BODIPY lipid droplet assay captures a core mechanistic facet of drusen formation—RPE cholesterol overload—and is supported by genetic, biochemical, and clinical AMD literature. While the cell model’s limitations and the binary nature of a full KO must be kept in mind, the assay’s quantitative readouts and precedent in related fields make it a strong platform for identifying small molecules that restore cholesterol homeostasis in RPE and potentially slow or prevent dry AMD progression.
