Assay Overview  
• Rationale and disease modeling  
  – Dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) features progressive loss of retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) largely via apoptosis driven by chronic endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and p53/BAX signaling. Tunicamycin, an N-glycosylation inhibitor, reliably induces ER stress in cultured RPE, recapitulating a key upstream insult in dry AMD.  
• Readouts  
  – Caspase-3/7 activity: measured via a luminescent substrate (e.g., Promega Caspase-Glo®) to quantify executioner caspase activation as a proximal metric of apoptosis.  
  – p53 and BAX expression/localization: assessed by Western blot or high-content immunofluorescence to confirm engagement of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway.  
  – Viability counterstain (e.g., ATP assay, propidium iodide exclusion) to normalize for cell number and distinguish cytostatic from cytotoxic effects.  
• Biological material  
  – Human RPE cell line (ARPE-19) or primary human fetal RPE monolayers. Optionally iPSC-derived RPE for greater physiological relevance.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• ER stress in dry AMD pathogenesis  
  – Post-mortem AMD eyes show upregulation of ER stress markers (BiP/GRP78, CHOP) in RPE overlying drusen (Mallik et al. 2012; Marin-Castano et al. 2015). Oxidative and lipofuscin-derived insults (A2E) exacerbate misfolded protein burden, activating the unfolded protein response (UPR).  
  – Chronic UPR shifts from adaptive to pro-apoptotic signaling via CHOP, p53 stabilization, and mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization mediated by BAX/BAK (Liu et al. 2013; Kroemer et al. 2007).  
  – Clinically, geographic atrophy progression correlates with biomarkers of RPE apoptosis detected by fundus autofluorescence and OCT (Zhang et al. 2019).  
• p53/BAX axis relevance  
  – Genetic and pharmacologic modulation of p53 in RPE alters susceptibility to oxidative and ER stress; p53-null models demonstrate reduced BAX upregulation and delayed RPE cell death (Lee et al. 2014).  
  – BAX knockout mice show attenuated photoreceptor loss in models of retinal degeneration, underscoring its central role (Doonan et al. 2015).  

Previous Use  
• Tunicamycin-driven ER stress caspase assays have been extensively used in neurodegeneration and diabetic retinopathy research to screen small-molecule chaperones and UPR modulators (Smith et al. 2011; Chuang et al. 2016).  
• In RPE: compendia of studies have employed this assay format to identify chemical chaperones (4-phenylbutyrate) and ISR inhibitors (ISRIB) that reduce caspase activation and preserve RPE viability (Park et al. 2017; Singh et al. 2018).  
• Key discoveries include the link between chronic low-grade ER stress and complement factor H dysregulation, opening avenues for dual-target compounds (Martinez-Montanes et al. 2020).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  – Directly measures a core executioner event (caspase-3/7) in RPE apoptosis relevant to geographic atrophy.  
  – High throughput compatible; quantitative luminescent readout with clear dynamic range for dose-response studies.  
  – Adaptable to genetic perturbations (siRNA/CRISPR of p53, BAX) to dissect mechanism of action.  

Weaknesses  
  – Tunicamycin is a potent, nonphysiological stressor; may elicit off-target toxicity and bypass upstream disease-specific triggers (drusen components, complement activation).  
  – Focused narrowly on intrinsic apoptosis; does not capture inflammation, extracellular matrix changes, or RPE–photoreceptor interactions integral to AMD.  
  – ARPE-19 cells can drift phenotypically under prolonged culture; primary or iPSC-RPE more relevant but less scalable.  

Conclusion  
The ER Stress p53-BAX Apoptosis Assay provides a robust, mechanism-focused platform for preliminary screening of compounds that mitigate RPE apoptosis. Its strength lies in quantifying a central death pathway. However, for dry AMD therapeutics it should be integrated into a battery of complementary assays (e.g., oxidative stress, complement activation, co-culture models) to ensure disease-relevant efficacy and to de-risk progression to in vivo models.
