Assay Overview  
• Rationale and readouts  
  – Models complement-mediated injury to retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells, a key driver of geographic atrophy in dry AMD.  
  – RPE monolayers (e.g., ARPE-19 or primary human RPE) are pre-incubated with anti-C5 antibodies, then exposed to normal human serum as a complement source.  
  – Endpoints:  
     • C5b-9 (membrane attack complex, MAC) deposition by immunofluorescence or ELISA (quantifies terminal complement assembly on RPE).  
     • LDH release or propidium iodide uptake (measures complement-mediated cytotoxicity).  
• Materials  
  – Human RPE cells (cell line or primary), pooled human serum, anti-C5 (e.g., eculizumab analog), immunostaining reagents, LDH assay kit.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Complement dysregulation in dry AMD  
  – Genetic studies: CFH Y402H, C3, CFI and C9 variants confer strong risk, implicating alternative and terminal pathways (Hughes et al. 2014; Fritsche et al. 2016).  
  – Histopathology: increased C5b-9/MAC in drusen and at RPE-Bruch’s interface in donor AMD eyes (Hageman et al. 2001; Mullins et al. 2014).  
  – Local complement production: RPE themselves synthesize C3, factor B, C5, amplifying local activation (Rohrer et al. 2009).  
  – Clinical correlation: elevated systemic and ocular complement split products (C3a, C5a) in AMD patients (Scholl et al. 2008; Lores-Motta et al. 2020).  
• Pathogenic mechanism  
  – Sublytic MAC deposition on RPE triggers inflammasome activation, oxidative stress and secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, driving RPE atrophy and photoreceptor loss (Tarallo et al. 2012; Anderson et al. 2018).  

Previous Use  
• Drug screening  
  – Rocha et al. (2006) first demonstrated that anti-C5 prevented serum-induced MAC on RPE. They used immunofluorescence to rank anti-C5 inhibitors.  
  – Holers (2014) review cites similar assays to characterize compstatin analogs (C3 inhibitors) and downstream influence on MAC assembly in RPE.  
• Key discoveries  
  – Identification of sublytic vs. lytic complement thresholds in RPE, informing dose-response for complement inhibitors (Rohrer et al. 2009).  
  – Demonstration that local C5a generation potentiates inflammasome (NLRP3) in RPE, suggesting combined C5/C5aR blockade (Tarallo et al. 2012).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  • Mechanistic relevance: directly assesses terminal complement (C5-C9) impact on RPE, central to dry AMD pathology.  
  • Quantitative and relatively high-throughput: fluorescence readout plus LDH offers dual measure of complement assembly and cell viability.  
  • Adaptable: can compare different anti-C5 formats (antibodies, peptides) or combination therapies (C5 + inflammasome modulators).  

Weaknesses  
  • Serum source and concentration may not recapitulate subretinal microenvironment (e.g., local complement regulators, CFH levels).  
  • RPE monolayer on plastic lacks Bruch’s membrane, drusen components, and supporting choroidal endothelium or microglia, which modulate complement activation in vivo.  
  • LDH release is a general cytotoxicity marker and may be confounded by non-complement insults; specialized live-cell imaging or caspase assays might be needed.  
  • Clinical translation: systemic anti-C5 (eculizumab) failed to slow geographic atrophy in trials, suggesting single-target blockade may not suffice (Liao et al. 2020).  

Conclusion  
This assay provides a well-validated, mechanistically anchored platform for screening terminal complement inhibitors in dry AMD. Its strengths lie in modeling an established pathogenic axis (MAC on RPE) and yielding rapid quantitative readouts. However, it should be complemented by more complex co-culture or organoid models incorporating Bruch’s membrane components, local complement regulators, and inflammasome endpoints to better predict clinical efficacy.
