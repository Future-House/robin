Assay Overview  
• Rationale and disease aspect modeled  
  – Dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is characterized in part by progressive dysfunction of the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), where mitochondrial bioenergetic failure leads to impaired phagocytosis, oxidative stress, inflammation and ultimately RPE atrophy.1,2  
  – Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator-1 alpha (PGC-1α) is a master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis and antioxidant defense in RPE.3,4 Genetic or pharmacologic upregulation of PGC-1α in RPE cells models restoration of mitochondrial capacity in a disease‐relevant context.  
• Readouts and methodology  
  – Mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) via tetramethylrhodamine ethyl ester (TMRE) or JC-1 fluorescence.  
  – ATP production quantified by luciferase‐based assays (e.g., CellTiter-Glo) or coupled bioluminescence.  
  – Optionally, oxygen consumption rate (OCR) by Seahorse XF assay and mitochondrial ROS (MitoSOX) can be multiplexed.  
  – PGC-1α expression manipulated by adenoviral/adeno-associated virus vectors, CRISPRa, or small molecules (e.g., ZLN005, SRT1720).  
• Cell types and culture conditions  
  – Human primary RPE cells or iPSC-derived RPE monolayers to best mimic native RPE metabolism.  
  – ARPE-19 can be used for higher‐throughput screening, though its baseline mitochondrial function is lower than primary RPE.  
  – Cells are stressed (e.g., sublethal H2O2 or drusen‐like lipofuscin71) to induce mitochondrial dysfunction, then treated with PGC-1α inducers.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Centrality of mitochondrial dysfunction in dry AMD  
  – Numerous clinical and post‐mortem studies tie RPE mitochondrial DNA damage, reduced respiratory chain enzyme activity, and diminished ATP output directly to AMD severity.5,6  
  – Early AMD features accumulation of drusen enriched in oxidized lipids and proteins that further compromise RPE mitochondrial health7; as disease progresses, impaired mitophagy amplifies oxidative injury.8  
• Role of PGC-1α in RPE homeostasis  
  – PGC-1α levels decline with age in human RPE9; animal models with PGC-1α knockdown recapitulate RPE degeneration, complement activation, and sub-RPE deposit formation10.  
  – Upregulation of PGC-1α enhances expression of mitochondrial antioxidant enzymes (SOD2, catalase) and respiratory chain components, reducing RPE apoptosis under oxidative and lipofuscin‐mediated stress4.  
• Clinical links  
  – Certain AMD-associated CFH and ARMS2 genotypes correlate with exacerbated mitochondrial dysfunction, suggesting patient subsets may benefit from bioenergetic rescue strategies.11  

Previous Use  
• In vitro drug screening  
  – Choksi et al. (2014) used TMRE and ATP assays in ARPE-19 and primary RPE to demonstrate that genetic overexpression of PGC-1α rescues ΔΨm and ATP levels under H2O2 challenge.3  
  – Merle et al. (2015) screened a small library of sirtuin activators on PGC-1α induction and measured mitochondrial respiration by Seahorse, identifying lead compounds that improved RPE survival.4  
• Key discoveries  
  – Demonstration that SRT1720 and related SIRT1 activators stimulate PGC-1α–mediated mitochondrial biogenesis in RPE, reducing lipofuscin‐induced ROS.4  
  – Evidence that CRISPRa targeting the endogenous PGC-1α promoter outperforms cDNA overexpression in sustaining mitochondrial function long term.12  
• Translational precedent  
  – PGC-1α modulators are in preclinical development for other age-related and neurodegenerative disorders, endorsing the assay’s value for lead identification.13  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  – Directly measures core bioenergetic parameters (ΔΨm, ATP) tightly linked to RPE viability in dry AMD.  
  – Amenable to medium-throughput screening of both genetic and pharmacologic modulators.  
  – Uses disease-relevant human RPE models that recapitulate age-related mitochondrial decline.  
  – Prior publications validate assay reproducibility and link results to downstream RPE survival and phagocytic function.  
Weaknesses  
  – Two-dimensional RPE monocultures lack outer retinal cell interactions, complement exposure, and Bruch’s membrane changes characteristic of AMD in vivo.  
  – PGC-1α upregulation affects broad metabolic networks; off-target systemic effects may complicate translation.  
  – Luciferase-based ATP readouts can be confounded by test-compound interference with the luminescent signal.  
  – Therapeutic benefit in restoring ΔΨm/ATP may not fully predict prevention of drusen formation, inflammation, or geographic atrophy.  
Conclusion  
  – The PGC-1α Activation Bioenergetics Assay is well supported by biomedical and clinical literature as a surrogate for RPE mitochondrial health in dry AMD. Its proven utility in lead discovery and mechanistic clarity make it a strong candidate for early‐stage therapeutic screening, provided complementarity with assays modeling inflammation, outer retinal interactions and long‐term RPE viability.
