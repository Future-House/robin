Assay Overview  
1. Disease aspect modeled  
   – Dry AMD is characterized by progressive dysfunction of retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) driven in large part by accumulation of photo-oxidized lipofuscin (e.g. A2E) and resultant lysosomal impairment. This assay models the lysosomal Ca2+ dysregulation and alkalinization seen in RPE burdened with oxidized lipofuscin.  
2. Key measurements  
   a. Lysosomal Ca2+ transients: recorded in live RPE (ARPE-19 or primary human RPE) loaded with a low-affinity, lysosome-targeted Ca2+ dye (e.g. Rhod-dextran or GCaMP3 fused to LAMP1). Upon addition of the TRPML1 agonist ML-SA1, cytoplasmic or lysosomal Ca2+ release kinetics and amplitude are quantified by time-lapse fluorescence imaging (Dong et al. 2010).  
   b. Lysosomal pH rescue: assessed with LysoSensor Yellow/Blue DND-160 ratiometric dye. The fluorescence excitation/emission ratio (340/380 nm excitation) shifts in response to lumenal pH changes, allowing quantification of acidification restored by TRPML1 activation.  
   c. Secondary endpoints (optional): autophagic flux (LC3-II turnover by Western blot or tandem mCherry-GFP-LC3), cathepsin D maturation (immunoblot of pro-/mature forms), and phagocytic clearance of isolated POS (photoreceptor outer segments) labeled with pHrodo.  
3. Biological material  
   – Human ARPE-19 cells or primary foetal RPE cultured to allow formation of mature lysosomal network. Cells are pre-loaded for 24–48 h with synthetic A2E or isolated human lipofuscin extracts. Oxidative photo-stress (blue-light exposure) can be applied to recapitulate in vivo oxidation. After stress, ML-SA1 (1–10 µM) is added and imaging performed over 5–30 min.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Centrality of lysosomal dysfunction in dry AMD  
   – Lipofuscin accumulation: A2E and other bisretinoids accumulate in RPE lysosomes with age; when photo-oxidized they impair proton-pumping and enzyme activities, driving pH elevation (Terluk et al. 2015; Sparrow et al. 2012).  
   – Autophagy/lysosomal decline: Age-related decline in cathepsin D maturation, LAMP2 expression and autophagic flux correlates with drusen formation and RPE atrophy in patient eyes (Mitter et al. 2014; Menzies et al. 2017).  
   – TRPML1’s role: TRPML1 is the principal endolysosomal Ca2+ release channel controlling membrane fusion, acidification and hydrolase activation. Loss-of-function (as in mucolipidosis IV) leads to lipofuscin-like storage, oxidative stress and RPE cell death (Venugopal et al. 2007).  
2. Clinical correlations  
   – Post-mortem AMD RPE show enlarged, under-acidified lysosomes with reduced protease activity (Wavre-Shapton et al. 2013). Lysosomal pH correlates with RPE viability in culture (Zhao et al. 2011).  
   – Genetic risk factors (e.g. C2/CFB, LAMP2 variants) point to compromised clearance pathways.  

Previous Use  
1. TRPML1 activation in lysosomal storage diseases  
   – Dong et al. (2010) used ML-SA1 to rescue Ca2+ release and lysosomal trafficking in fibroblasts from mucolipidosis IV and Niemann–Pick type C patients.  
   – Wang et al. (2018) demonstrated that ML-SA1 restored lysosomal acidification and autophagic flux in NPC1-deficient neuronal cultures.  
2. RPE lipofuscin models  
   – A2E-loaded ARPE-19 assays have been widely used to screen for small molecules that prevent lysosomal alkalinization or enhance degradation (La Cunza et al. 2020). However, specific targeting of TRPML1 in such RPE models has not yet been published, representing an unexplored niche.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
 • Mechanistic specificity: Directly probes a key ion channel regulating lysosomal homeostasis.  
 • Quantitative readouts: Ratiometric pH and Ca2+ kinetics provide robust, high-content metrics.  
 • Relevance: Models the lipid-oxidative stress central to dry AMD pathogenesis.  
 • Translational potential: Small-molecule TRPML1 agonists (e.g. ML-SA1 derivatives) could be advanced into RPE-targeted formulations.  

Weaknesses  
 • Biological complexity: Dry AMD involves complement activation, inflammation and Bruch’s membrane changes not captured here.  
 • Model limitations: ARPE-19 can differ from native RPE in lysosomal content and polarization; primary RPE are more physiologic but lower throughput.  
 • Off-target concerns: ML-SA1 has modest selectivity; confirmation with genetic activation or additional chemotypes is required.  
 • Throughput: Live-cell imaging at the temporal resolution needed is lower throughput than endpoint assays.  

Conclusion  
This TRPML1-dependent lysosomal rescue assay offers a highly focused, mechanistic platform to screen and characterize therapeutics aimed at restoring RPE lysosomal function in dry AMD. It captures a central pathogenic axis—lipofuscin-induced lysosomal alkalinization—supported by extensive biomedical and clinical literature, and builds on precedents in lysosomal storage disease models. To maximize translational relevance, it should be combined with complementary assays addressing inflammation, drusen component clearance, and RPE polarization.
