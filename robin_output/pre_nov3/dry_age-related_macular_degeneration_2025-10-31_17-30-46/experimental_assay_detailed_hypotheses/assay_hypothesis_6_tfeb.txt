Assay Overview  
The proposed assay tests the hypothesis that boosting TFEB-driven lysosomal biogenesis and autophagic flux can improve clearance of toxic bisretinoid (A2E) deposits in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells, a key pathogenic feature of dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD).  
•   Cell system: Cultured human RPE cells (e.g. ARPE-19 or primary donor RPE) pre-loaded with A2E to mimic lipofuscin accumulation.  
•   Intervention: Transduction with adenoviral vectors encoding human TFEB (versus empty-vector control).  
•   Readouts:  
    –  LC3-II and p62/SQSTM1 levels by Western blot, with and without lysosomal inhibitors (e.g. bafilomycin A1) to quantify autophagic flux.  
    –  LysoTracker or Magic Red cathepsin substrates and high-content fluorescence imaging to assess lysosomal mass, acidification, and proteolytic activity.  
    –  Immunofluorescence for nuclear TFEB translocation and lysosomal marker LAMP1.  
    –  Viability assays (e.g. ATP content, TUNEL) to test whether enhanced autophagy correlates with improved RPE survival under A2E stress.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1.   Central role of lipofuscin/A2E in dry AMD: Clinical and histopathological studies of AMD donor eyes demonstrate accumulation of A2E and other bisretinoids in RPE lysosomes, leading to oxidative stress, complement activation, and cell death (Boulton et al. 2012; Sparrow et al. 2017).  
2.   Lysosomal/autophagy dysfunction in AMD: Multiple studies report reduced autophagic flux and impaired lysosomal enzyme activity in RPE from AMD donors and animal models (Mitter et al. 2014; Zhou et al. 2018). Genetic risk loci (e.g. C3, CFH) and complement-driven inflammation further disrupt lysosomal homeostasis (Fritsche et al. 2016).  
3.   TFEB as master regulator: Settembre et al. (2011) first identified TFEB as a transcriptional regulator coordinating lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy. In RPE, TFEB activation enhances clearance of photoreceptor outer segment–derived debris and reduces lipofuscin burden (Ferrington et al. 2016; Yang et al. 2015).  
4.   Clinical relevance: Impaired proteostasis correlates with drusen formation and geographic atrophy progression (Kaarniranta et al. 2013). Restoring lysosomal function is thus a plausible therapeutic approach for the dry AMD phenotype, where no approved treatments currently exist.  

Previous Use  
•   Settembre et al. (2011) and Martina et al. (2014) established adenoviral TFEB overexpression in HeLa and neuronal cells to demonstrate enhanced lysosomal gene expression and autophagic flux.  
•   In RPE models, Ferrington et al. (2016) used TFEB overexpression to mitigate A2E-induced toxicity, showing reduced oxidative stress and improved cell viability.  
•   Small-molecule TFEB activators (e.g. genistein, trehalose) have been screened in RPE and neuronal assays, linking TFEB activation to clearance of aggregated proteins in neurodegeneration (Song et al. 2016).  
•   These studies confirm that TFEB modulation can serve both as a mechanistic probe and as a validation tool for candidate autophagy inducers.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
•   Disease relevance: Models the key pathogenic event in RPE—A2E accumulation and lysosomal failure—directly implicated in dry AMD.  
•   Mechanistic insight: Quantitative measures of autophagic flux and lysosomal biogenesis give clear, proximal readouts of target engagement.  
•   Screening potential: Amenable to medium-throughput formats with high-content imaging and automated Western or capillary-based immunoassays.  
•   Versatility: Can be paired with CRISPR or siRNA screens to identify co-factors that modulate TFEB efficacy.  

Weaknesses  
•   Overexpression artifact: Adenoviral TFEB levels may exceed physiological activation, obscuring dosage relationships and off-target effects.  
•   Model limitations: ARPE-19 cells and primary RPE in 2D culture lack full retinal architecture (polarization, Bruch’s membrane interactions, choroidal environment).  
•   Partial pathology: Does not capture complement activation, inflammation or drusen biogenesis, all contributors to dry AMD progression.  
•   Flux ambiguity: Reliance on static LC3-II/p62 measurements can be misleading without rigorous inhibitor controls and time-course studies.  

Conclusion  
This assay is well positioned to screen and mechanistically characterize autophagy/lysosome-targeted compounds for dry AMD. It provides direct, quantitative readouts of the TFEB-driven clearance pathway central to RPE proteostasis. However, integration with complementary assays (e.g. complement activation markers, polarized RPE-choroid co-culture) will be essential to capture the multifactorial nature of dry AMD and validate therapeutic candidates before in vivo studies.
