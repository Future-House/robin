A2E and blue light recapitulate AMD-relevant oxidative/lysosomal stress that activates NLRP3 in RPE, and hiPSC-RPE improve physiological relevance versus ARPE-19 (piippo2019inflammasomesignalinganda pages 91-95, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingandb pages 83-87, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 83-87).

Biomedical Evidence: NLRP3 engagement is documented in AMD: RPE lesions show increased NLRP3, pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18; IL-1β is elevated clinically; active caspase-1 is increased in GA RPE; and drusen extracts from AMD eyes evoke NLRP3 activation (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 76-79, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 47-49). AMD-relevant stimuli—including A2E/lipofuscin photo-oxidation, oxidized lipids (7-ketocholesterol, oxLDL), and blue light—activate inflammasomes/caspase-1 and drive IL-1β/IL-18 secretion in RPE, with ROS and lipid peroxidation as central mediators (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 86-87, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingandb pages 87-91). Photo-oxidative injury models in vivo link caspase-1/IL-1β to photoreceptor death, supporting the biological significance of these readouts (wooff2020caspase1dependentinflammasomesmediate pages 8-10).

Previous Use: RPE inflammasome assays have measured IL-1β (ELISA) and caspase-1 (FLICA) after IL-1α priming plus proteasome/autophagy inhibition, and pharmacologic modulation reduced both readouts, establishing tractability and assay feasibility (bhattarai2021effectsofresvega pages 2-4, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 91-95). A2E and lipofuscin-mediated blue light have been shown to activate NLRP3 in ARPE-19; selective NLRP3 inhibitors (e.g., MCC950/OLT1177/β-hydroxybutyrate) and RPE-active NLRP3 modulators (IFM-514, IFM-632, CRID3) suppress caspase-1 activation and IL-1β/LDH, providing positive controls and benchmark effects (piippo2019inflammasomesignalinganda pages 91-95, piippo2019inflammasomesignalinganda pages 47-49, piippo2019inflammasomesignalinganda pages 87-91, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 47-49).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: human-cell context (hiPSC-RPE), AMD-relevant trigger (A2E+blue light), mechanistically linked, quantitative endpoints (IL-1β, caspase-1), and established pharmacologic sensitivity; ROS/lipid peroxidation dependence supports construct validity (piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 83-87, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 86-87, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 87-91, piippo2019inflammasomesignalinganda pages 47-49). Weaknesses: priming is often required and IL-1β yields can be low; stimulus- and model-specific mechanisms vary; caspase-1 can be engaged by multiple sensors; IL-18 roles are variable; monoculture lacks complement/microglia/chronicity; and reagent specificity and NLRP3’s precise role in AMD remain debated—demanding orthogonal endpoints and genetic controls, plus validation across RPE models and in vivo (piippo2019inflammasomesignalingandb pages 83-87, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 76-79, wooff2020caspase1dependentinflammasomesmediate pages 17-18, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingandb pages 62-67).

This assay is a biologically grounded, pharmacologically tractable screen for NLRP3-pathway modulators in dry AMD. Include priming, orthogonal inflammasome/pyroptosis readouts, and specificity controls, and confirm across RPE models to address pathway heterogeneity and translation limits (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 86-87, piippo2019inflammasomesignalinganda pages 87-91).
