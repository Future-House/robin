Assay Overview: The proposed assay utilizes an ARE-luciferase reporter in ARPE-19 cells subjected to tert‐butyl hydroperoxide stress to model the oxidative damage observed in retinal pigment epithelium in dry AMD. Cells are transfected with an ARE-inducible firefly luciferase construct; subsequent luciferase activity is quantified from cell lysates, representing activation of the Nrf2-ARE signaling pathway (huang2021obacunoneprotectsretinal pages 11-13, huang2021obacunoneprotectsretinal pages 9-11).

Biomedical Evidence: Oxidative stress is a central factor in the pathogenesis of dry AMD, where depletion of antioxidant defenses in RPE cells leads to cellular degeneration. Dysregulation of Nrf2 signaling has been implicated in this redox imbalance, emphasizing the therapeutic value of restoring antioxidant responses (catanzaro2020eyelightonagerelated pages 1-2, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3, hyttinen2019theregulationof pages 10-12). Clinical and biochemical data support that enhancing Nrf2-mediated responses can counteract oxidative damage and improve RPE cell survival (hyttinen2019theregulationof pages 1-3, bellezza2018oxidativestressin pages 2-3).

Previous Use: ARE-luciferase reporter assays have been effectively employed in drug discovery to screen for compounds that activate Nrf2 and enhance cellular antioxidant defenses. Historic applications of this assay have led to the identification of agents, such as obacunone, that mitigate oxidative injury in ARPE-19 cells (huang2021obacunoneprotectsretinal pages 11-13, huang2021obacunoneprotectsretinal pages 9-11).

Overall Evaluation: The strengths of this assay lie in its specificity for a key pathogenic mechanism in dry AMD and its amenability to high-throughput screening. However, its reliance on the ARPE-19 immortalized cell line may limit direct translational interpretation compared to primary RPE models (chen2023oxidativestressin pages 1-1). In vitro conditions might not fully replicate the complex in vivo environment, necessitating further validation.

This ARE-luciferase reporter assay provides a robust early-stage platform for screening compounds capable of restoring redox homeostasis in dry AMD. Its design directly targets a critical pathogenic pathway and offers a quantifiable readout for therapeutic efficacy.
