Assay Overview:  
This assay utilizes ARPE‑19 cells to model ferroptosis—a regulated cell death process driven by iron‐dependent lipid peroxidation—as observed in dry age‑related macular degeneration. Erastin is employed to disrupt the cystine/glutathione axis, leading to labile iron accumulation and oxidative lipid damage that is quantified using lipid‑peroxidation sensors (e.g., Liperfluo) and complemented by viability measurements (MTS/CCK‑8) (tang2021ho1mediatedferroptosisas pages 2-4, zhao2021ironaccumulationand pages 11-12).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Ferroptosis, mediated by iron overload and lipid peroxidation, is central to RPE degeneration implicated in dry AMD. Studies demonstrate that disruptions in antioxidant defenses (e.g., GPX4 inactivation, system Xc– inhibition) result in RPE cell death, a phenomenon recapitulated both in vitro and in vivo. These molecular hallmarks directly correlate with clinical findings of increased retinal iron and lipid markers in AMD patients (tang2021ho1mediatedferroptosisas pages 1-2, dohl2025theintersectionof pages 7-8).

Previous Use:  
Similar platforms using ARPE‑19 cells challenged with ferroptosis inducers such as erastin, sodium iodate, or atRAL have successfully demonstrated the rescue of cell viability by ferroptosis inhibitors (e.g., Fer‑1) and iron chelators. These studies have advanced our understanding of the underlying molecular pathways and provided proof‑of‑concept data for therapeutic screening (shen2025ferrostatin1aferroptosis pages 1-2, shen2025ferrostatin1aferroptosis pages 4-6).

Overall Evaluation:  
This assay is robust and mechanistically specific, offering quantifiable endpoints that directly map onto key dry AMD pathologies. Its strengths include the ability to dissect iron‑driven lipid peroxidation and evaluate drug efficacy in a controlled in vitro setting. However, limitations include potential discrepancies between ARPE‑19 responses and primary RPE cell behavior or in vivo complexity, which necessitate subsequent validation in complementary models (zhao2025ferroptosisanenergetic pages 20-21, zhao2025ferroptosisanenergetic pages 13-14).

The ferroptosis protection assay in ARPE‑19 cells is a valuable model for testing dry AMD therapeutics because it effectively recapitulates iron-dependent lipid peroxidation and cell viability loss. It provides a strong mechanistic and screening platform for identifying potential ferroptosis inhibitors in the early stages of drug discovery.
