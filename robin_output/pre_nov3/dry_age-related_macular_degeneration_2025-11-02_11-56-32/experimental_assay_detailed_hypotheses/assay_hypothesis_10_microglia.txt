Assay Overview:  
This assay uses a transwell co‐culture system in which primary retinal microglia are cultured in conjunction with hiPSC‐derived retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells. It models the paracrine interactions whereby microglia, upon activation, release soluble factors that trigger inflammatory responses in RPE cells. The proposed measurements include quantification of TNF‐α and IL‐6 levels—using immunoassays such as ELISA—to serve as readouts for microglia‐driven inflammation related to RPE degeneration (brandli2024thecontributionof pages 12-14, zhao2024crosstalkbetweenmicroglia pages 6-7).

Biomedical Evidence:  
The model targets microglia‐driven para-inflammation, a key pathogenic process in dry age-related macular degeneration. Elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including TNF‐α and IL‐6, have been linked to RPE dysfunction and subsequent retinal degeneration. Clinical and preclinical data support the centrality of microglia–RPE crosstalk in disease progression, with evidence indicating that immune-mediated cytokine release creates a vicious cycle that exacerbates damage (chen2015parainflammationchronicinflammation pages 9-11, zhao2024crosstalkbetweenmicroglia pages 18-19).

Previous Use:  
Similar co-culture assays have been employed to characterize the effects of microglial activation on RPE inflammatory responses and to test pharmacological inhibitors targeting pathways such as A2AR and p38 MAPK. These studies demonstrated that modulating microglia activity could dampen cytokine production and protect RPE integrity (dietrich2020interactionofinflammatorily pages 6-7, madeira2018blockadeofmicroglial pages 9-10).

Overall Evaluation:  
The strengths of this assay include its human-cell basis and clear, quantitative cytokine endpoints that are highly relevant to dry AMD pathology. However, its limitations lie in the reductionist nature of the model. While TNF‐α and IL‐6 are valuable markers, they capture only part of the inflammatory network, omitting other key factors such as complement activation, inflammasome signaling, oxidative stress, and the chronicity found in vivo (dabouz2024neuroimmuneinteractionsin pages 80-84, dabouz2024neuroimmuneinteractionsin pages 9-15). Additionally, inter-donor variability in primary microglial preparations and the lack of full tissue architecture may restrict predictive accuracy.

This transwell co-culture assay is a promising platform for screening anti-inflammatory therapies in dry AMD and for dissecting microglia–RPE interactions. Nevertheless, it should be complemented with additional readouts and more complex models to fully capture the multifactorial nature of the disease.
