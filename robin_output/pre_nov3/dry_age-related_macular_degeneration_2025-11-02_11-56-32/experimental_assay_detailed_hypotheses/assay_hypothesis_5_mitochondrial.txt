Overall Evaluation: Strengths and weaknesses of this assay for testing therapeutics for dry age-related macular degeneration.

Assay Overview: The proposed assay uses the Seahorse XF Cell Mito Stress Test (CMST) in human iPSC-derived RPE (hiPSC-RPE) to quantify mitochondrial respiration (OCR) and glycolysis (ECAR), deriving basal and maximal respiration, ATP-linked respiration, and spare respiratory capacity, with optional ATP Rate analysis to separate mitochondrial versus glycolytic ATP production and normalization to cell count by Hoechst staining (oligomycin → FCCP → antimycin A/rotenone sequence) (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6). hiPSC-RPE from AMD and non-AMD donors would be assayed at baseline and after an acute mitochondrial challenge (e.g., low-dose rotenone to model complex I impairment), with rescue assessed by NAD+ precursors, read out as increased OCR parameters and favorable shifts on OCR–ECAR energy maps (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15, bhattacharya2022modelingofmitochondrial pages 15-18, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 61-67, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 36-40).

Biomedical Evidence: AMD RPE exhibits decreased oxidative phosphorylation, reduced ETC subunits, mtDNA damage, and altered mitochondrial architecture, with Seahorse-detected deficits in basal/ATP-linked/maximal respiration and glycolytic reserve in primary human RPE from AMD donors (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 61-67, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 32-36). Because RPE relies heavily on mitochondrial ATP and participates in a metabolic ecosystem supplying photoreceptors, RPE mitochondrial failure drives a pathological shift to glycolysis that reduces photoreceptor fuel and links to degeneration (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 36-40). Disease-relevant signaling changes co-occur with mitochondrial dysfunction in RPE (STAT3 activation, AMPK inhibition, PGC-1α changes, APOE upregulation), underscoring the centrality of mitochondrial impairment to AMD phenotypes (bhattacharya2022modelingofmitochondrial pages 15-18). NAD+-related pathways are implicated by repression of SIRT1/PGC‑1α in AMD iPSC-RPE and rescue of disease phenotypes by nicotinamide (golestaneh2016repressedsirt1pgc1αpathway pages 1-2, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 15-17).

Previous Use: Seahorse assays in human RPE models have repeatedly detected AMD-associated bioenergetic deficits and pharmacologic rescue, including AMD-selective improvement of mitochondrial function by the NAD+ precursor NMN in primary AMD RPE and Seahorse-based drug testing pipelines in patient iPSC-RPE (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 36-40, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15). In hiPSC-RPE, CMST readouts capture improvements in maximal and spare respiratory capacity with metformin and increases in basal respiration with trehalose, validating the platform for detecting mitochondrial rescue (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6). Studies emphasize patient-specific variability and differences between primary haRPE and iPSC-RPE, motivating prescreening and, when needed, addition of disease-mimetic stress to reveal AMD phenotypes (fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 1-2, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 140-144).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths include direct measurement of the core AMD pathology (RPE mitochondrial failure), quantitative and sensitive endpoints, capacity to partition mitochondrial versus glycolytic ATP, and precedent for NAD+ precursor–mediated rescue in AMD RPE, with translational support from mitochondrial-targeted interventions and photobiomodulation (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 36-40, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 61-67, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 159-162). Weaknesses include the acute nature of rotenone injury versus the chronic, multifactorial AMD milieu, potential need to add disease-relevant stressors to hiPSC-RPE to manifest phenotypes, and inter-donor variability requiring careful normalization and replication (fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 61-67, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 140-144).

Seahorse XF mitochondrial respiration in hiPSC-RPE is well-aligned with dry AMD pathobiology and has demonstrated ability to detect pharmacologic rescue, including by NAD+ precursors, in human RPE models. Incorporating appropriate stress paradigms and patient-specific replication will maximize disease relevance and assay sensitivity for therapeutic testing.
