Assay Overview:  
The proposed p62 degradation assay utilizes Western blotting or immunofluorescence to measure p62 protein levels in ARPE-19 cells exposed to H2O2-induced oxidative stress. This in vitro model simulates a key aspect of dry AMD pathogenesis—oxidative stress leading to autophagy impairment and disrupted proteostasis. The assay quantifies the accumulation of p62, a selective autophagy receptor, thereby serving as a surrogate readout for autophagic flux and inflammasome priming (govindaraju2017cigarettesmokeinduced pages 6-8, song2017oxidativestressmediatednfκb pages 20-21).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Autophagic dysfunction and impaired proteostasis are central to the progression of dry AMD. Elevated p62 levels have been associated with ineffective clearance of damaged proteins in retinal pigment epithelial cells, directly linking oxidative stress to disease pathogenesis. Clinical and preclinical studies emphasize that modulating autophagy may counteract RPE degeneration, making p62 a relevant biomarker in this context (song2017oxidativestressmediatednfκb pages 13-16, song2017oxidativestressmediatednfκb pages 2-4).

Previous Use:  
The p62 degradation assay has been employed in drug discovery to evaluate the efficacy of autophagy enhancers—including agents like cysteamine and fisetin—by demonstrating their potential to reduce p62 accumulation and restore proteostasis under oxidative conditions (govindaraju2017cigarettesmokeinduced pages 6-8, song2017oxidativestressmediatednfκb pages 20-21).

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths of the assay include its direct mechanistic relevance to dry AMD, its capacity to model oxidative stress-induced autophagic impairment, and its utility for screening autophagy-enhancing therapeutics. Weaknesses include potential variability in ARPE-19 responses and the limitation of interpreting p62 accumulation without complementary markers, necessitating further confirmation of autophagic flux (zhang2020alltransretinalinducesautophagic pages 12-15, song2017oxidativestressmediatednfκb pages 7-10).

This assay is a valuable tool for early-stage screening of therapeutics for dry AMD by linking drug effects to proteostasis and autophagy. Its combination with additional endpoints may improve the translational relevance of the findings.
