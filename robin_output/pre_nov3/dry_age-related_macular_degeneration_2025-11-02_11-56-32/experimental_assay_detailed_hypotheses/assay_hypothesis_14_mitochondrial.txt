Assay Overview: This live-cell assay quantifies mitochondrial superoxide in human retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) as a proximal readout of mitochondrial oxidative stress implicated in dry AMD. Primary adult donor RPE (haRPE) from AMD and non-AMD eyes are challenged with H2O2 (time/dose window guided by reports using 0.2–10 mM for 24–48 h) and treated with candidate antioxidants; mitochondrial superoxide is measured with MitoSOX, complemented by cytosolic ROS (H2DCF-DA), mitochondrial respiration (Seahorse Cell Mito Stress Test), mitochondrial dynamics/autophagy markers (Drp1 phosphorylation, LC3-II, TOM20), and viability (Annexin V/PI) to contextualize ROS changes and drug effects (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 2-5, chan2019reactiveoxygenspeciesdependent pages 1-2, fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4).

Biomedical Evidence: Oxidative stress is a central driver of RPE degeneration in dry AMD, with mitochondria a major ROS source; AMD RPE show reduced mitochondrial density, ATP production, cristae loss, mtDNA damage, and widespread alterations in mitochondrial proteins, linking mitochondrial dysfunction to disease (chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3). Clinical and donor-based studies show ROS-driven mitochondrial dysfunction and mtDNA damage correlate with AMD severity, and targeting mitochondrial oxidative pathways is a therapeutic focus (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 1-3, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3). Mechanistically, transcriptional control of mitochondrial biogenesis/antioxidant defenses (MITF→PGC1α) modulates RPE susceptibility to oxidant stress and ATP loss, underscoring the pathogenic role of mitochondrial oxidative imbalance (hua2018mitfactsas pages 6-8, hua2018mitfactsas pages 1-2).

Previous Use: RPE oxidative-stress assays using H2O2 or redox cyclers have been widely applied to test mitochondria-targeted interventions, with improvements in ROS, mitochondrial membrane potential, bioenergetics, and ultrastructure reported in ARPE-19 and patient-derived models (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 14-16, kim2021improvedeffectof pages 5-8). In primary haRPE, oxidant challenges reveal disease-dependent vulnerabilities and metabolic shifts (lower mitochondrial ATP, higher glycolytic ATP), with stronger AMD-control separation at longer H2O2 exposures (48 h) (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 2-5). Mitochondrial function assays under stress (e.g., Agilent phenotype test, menadione challenge) detect AMD-relevant differences more robustly than basal readouts, supporting stress-based designs for therapeutic testing (fisher2022humanipscand pages 12-14, fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4). Notably, NaIO3 can elevate cytosolic but not mitochondrial ROS in ARPE-19, and antioxidant scavengers (NAC, Trolox) unexpectedly increased cell death, highlighting interpretive pitfalls if relying on a single ROS compartment (chan2019reactiveoxygenspeciesdependent pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—Biologically anchored to a core AMD mechanism; MitoSOX provides a direct mitochondrial ROS readout; primary haRPE capture disease phenotypes and donor variability; combining mitoROS with respiration, dynamics/autophagy, and viability yields mechanistic and translationally relevant signatures for compound triage (chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3, fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 2-5). Weaknesses—MitoSOX can be negative for some oxidative injuries (e.g., NaIO3) despite cytotoxicity; ROS can drive both pro-death (MAPKs) and pro-survival (autophagy/mitodynamics) programs; oxidant choice and exposure time critically shape outcomes; iPSC-RPE often lack disease-specific responses unless conditioned; cell density and polarization alter ROS and stress susceptibility; antioxidants may worsen outcomes, necessitating multiplex endpoints and careful controls (chan2019reactiveoxygenspeciesdependent pages 1-2, fisher2022humanipscand pages 12-14, fisher2022humanipscand pages 14-15, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 2-5).

A mitochondrial superoxide assay in primary RPE is biologically relevant and useful for evaluating dry AMD therapeutics if embedded in a multiplexed panel that includes mitochondrial function, dynamics/mitophagy, and viability. Oxidant selection, exposure kinetics, donor stratification, and culture conditions must be controlled, and cytosolic ROS should be measured alongside mitoROS to avoid false negatives and misinterpretation (chan2019reactiveoxygenspeciesdependent pages 1-2, fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 2-5).
