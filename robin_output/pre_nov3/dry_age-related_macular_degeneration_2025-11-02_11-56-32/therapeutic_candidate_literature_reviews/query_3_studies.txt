Query: studies reporting small molecules that restore mitochondrial membrane potential and oxygen consumption rate in RPE cells under oxidative, complement, and lipid stress to model dry AMD, including preclinical efficacy in rodent or human-derived cell models and mechanistic validation by genetic or pharmacological target knockdown

Candidate small molecules such as metformin, PU-91, mitochondrial‐derived peptides (e.g., humanin), SkQ1, NMN, and zinc have been reported to improve mitochondrial health in preclinical models of dry AMD using human-derived RPE cells and rodent systems (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 20-21). These studies demonstrate improvements in parameters including autophagy, mitochondrial biogenesis, redox signaling, and cell viability; however, none of the excerpts provide explicit quantitative data demonstrating restoration of mitochondrial membrane potential or oxygen consumption rate under combined oxidative, complement, and lipid stress (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 20-21, zhuang2025decodingretinaldegeneration pages 7-8).

Furthermore, while mechanistic pathways involving AMPK, PGC-1α, Nrf2, STAT3, and mTOR are discussed and preclinical efficacy is evident, the reported work does not include genetic (e.g., siRNA or CRISPR-mediated knockdown) or pharmacological target knockdown/overexpression experiments to validate these pathways directly in RPE cell models (zhuang2025decodingretinaldegeneration pages 7-8, luu2024systemspharmacologyapproach pages 106-112). In addition, although complementary animal models are used, direct metabolic assays such as Seahorse analyses to monitor mitochondrial respiration and membrane integrity in stressed RPE cells are missing from the experimental design (bilbaomalave2021mitochondrialdysfunctionand pages 2-4).

These gaps call for future studies to incorporate explicit measurements of mitochondrial membrane potential and oxygen consumption rate, together with mechanism-driven target validation approaches.

The reported studies indicate promising preclinical efficacy but lack direct mitochondrial bioenergetic measurements and target knockdown validation. This represents a critical area for further investigation to substantiate the therapeutic potential of these small molecules in RPE models of dry AMD.
