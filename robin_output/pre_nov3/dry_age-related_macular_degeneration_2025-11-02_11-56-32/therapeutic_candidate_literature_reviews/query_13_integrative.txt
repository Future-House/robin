Query: integrative studies on the intersection of oxidative stress, lipid drusen accumulation, complement activation, and impaired mitochondrial respiration in RPE cells, identifying key nodes amenable to small-molecule intervention for dry AMD

Across integrative studies, RPE pathology in dry AMD converges on redox injury, lipid-derived debris, aberrant complement activity, and mitochondrial failure, defining druggable network nodes rather than isolated targets (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 1-2). ROS arise from phagocytic bursts and mitochondrial ETC leakage, while antioxidant defenses (NRF2 program, SOD/catalase) decline; small-molecule strategies include NOX2/4 inhibition and NRF2 activation to rebalance redox tone (basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 5-7, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 15-16). Lipofuscin/bisretinoids, particularly A2E, photo-oxidize, bind COX, suppress respiration, and trigger cytochrome c/caspase apoptosis, supporting approaches that limit A2E formation/phototoxicity and bolster lysosomal function (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12). Lipid peroxidation products (MDA, 4‑HNE, CEP) accumulate in PUFA-rich POS-derived material, act as DAMPs, impair proteostasis, and link to drusen; actionable levers include aldehyde scavengers, NOX inhibitors, and enhancement of cholesterol efflux via LXR agonists or HDL/ApoA‑I mimetics, with preclinical evidence for lipid removal and anti-inflammatory effects and a small clinical signal with high‑dose atorvastatin (basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 4-5, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 13-15, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 15-16, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 5-6). Alternative-pathway complement dysregulation (CFH Y402H, C3/CFI variants) drives MAC-mediated RPE injury and C3a/C5a signaling; selective complement blockade (e.g., CFD/C3) and upstream lipid–endosome interventions (ASM inhibition with desipramine to reduce intracellular C3a and mTOR activation) are supported (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 4-5, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 11-12, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 15-16). Mitochondrial nodes include mtDNA damage, impaired dynamics/mitophagy (Drp1, PINK1), and metabolic derailment (PARP‑2–NAD+, AMPK/SIRT1/PGC‑1α, mTOR), with tractable tools such as Drp1 inhibitors (Mdivi‑1), mitophagy enhancers, mTOR inhibitors, AMPK/SIRT1 activators, NAD+ restoration, and ferroptosis/iron modulators (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 7-8).

A rational small-molecule strategy combines redox control (NOX/NRF2), lipid efflux and endolysosomal repair (LXR, ASM inhibition), selective complement modulation, and mitochondrial quality control to interrupt the feed-forward loop linking oxidized lipids, complement, and respiratory failure in RPE (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 13-15, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 15-16, ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12).

Key integrative nodes include NOX–mitochondrial ROS, bisretinoid/COX inhibition, lipid peroxidation adducts and cholesterol efflux, alternative-pathway complement control (CFH/C3), ASM–ceramide–endosomal C3a–mTOR, and mitochondrial dynamics/mitophagy. Small-molecule levers with evidence span NOX inhibitors, NRF2 activators, LXR agonists, ASM inhibitors (desipramine), selective complement inhibitors, Drp1 inhibitors, AMPK/SIRT1/mTOR modulators, and NAD+ restoration, favoring combination regimens for dry AMD (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 11-12, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 13-15, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 15-16, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12).
