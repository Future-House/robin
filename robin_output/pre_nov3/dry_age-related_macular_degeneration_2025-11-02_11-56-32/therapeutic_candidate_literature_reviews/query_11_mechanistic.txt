Query: mechanistic studies elucidating how mitochondrial dysfunction in RPE cells contributes to dry AMD pathogenesis, including genetic, transcriptomic, proteomic, and functional evidence of causative links to RPE degeneration and geographic atrophy

Mitochondrial dysfunction in RPE cells is a central mechanism in the pathogenesis of dry AMD. Impaired electron transport leads to excessive reactive oxygen species production that damages mitochondrial DNA, proteins, and lipids, thereby compromising ATP generation and triggering apoptotic pathways (hernandez2025roleofoxidative pages 4-6, kim2021molecularmechanismsof pages 4-6). Transcriptomic studies reveal vast gene expression alterations in RPE cells, with over 3,000 differentially expressed genes affecting oxidative phosphorylation, metabolic regulation, and senescence pathways in geographic atrophy (senabouth2021transcriptomicandproteomic pages 14-17, senabouth2022transcriptomicandproteomic pages 5-6). Genetic analyses, including eQTL mapping, have linked mitochondrial gene variants and risk alleles—impacting proteins such as PYROXD2 and IDH2—to increased susceptibility for AMD (ferrington2020mitochondrialdefectsdrive pages 6-7, senabouth2022transcriptomicandproteomic pages 6-8). 

Proteomic profiling further supports these findings by demonstrating significant alterations in mitochondrial protein content, especially in components of the respiratory chain and metabolic enzymes, which indicate a metabolic reprogramming in affected RPE cells (ferrington2020mitochondrialdefectsdrive pages 26-26, senabouth2022transcriptomicandproteomic pages 10-11). Functional evidence confirms that diminished mitochondrial respiration, impaired mitophagy, and altered fission–fusion dynamics contribute directly to RPE degeneration and the development of geographic atrophy (ferrington2020mitochondrialdefectsdrive pages 4-6, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 6-7).

Mitochondrial dysfunction in RPE cells disrupts energy metabolism and escalates oxidative stress, ultimately driving RPE degeneration in dry AMD. This evidence from genetic, transcriptomic, proteomic, and functional studies highlights the causative links that underlie the progression of geographic atrophy.
