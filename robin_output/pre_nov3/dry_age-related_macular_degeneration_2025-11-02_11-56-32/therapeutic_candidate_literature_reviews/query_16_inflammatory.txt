Query: inflammatory cytokine signaling, particularly TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-18, and their downstream pathways, impacting mitochondrial respiration, reactive oxygen species generation, and metabolic reprogramming in RPE cells under dry AMD stress

Inflammatory cytokine signaling in RPE cells subjected to dry AMD stress appears to be largely mediated by inflammasome activation that upregulates TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-18. TNF-α exposure in RPE has been linked to inflammasome activation, which in turn drives the secretion of IL-1β and IL-18 (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 86-87). These cytokines are associated with mitochondrial dysfunction; their release correlates with decreased mitochondrial membrane potential, reduced ATP production, and increased ROS generation, suggesting that inflammasome-related signaling adversely affects mitochondrial respiration (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 168-171, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 11-13). In addition, emerging evidence indicates that the chronic inflammatory environment and impaired proteostasis lead to metabolic reprogramming in RPE cells, characterized by shifts away from oxidative phosphorylation toward increased glycolysis and altered NAD+/SIRT1 and PGC-1α signaling (garciagarcia2022pathophysiologyofagerelated pages 4-5, garciagarcia2022pathophysiologyofagerelated pages 8-9). However, the direct mechanistic pathways linking cytokine receptor activation—specifically for TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-18—to precise changes in mitochondrial electron transport and metabolic enzyme regulation remain insufficiently detailed within these sources (khan2023beyondthecomplement pages 3-5). 

Inflammatory cytokines in dry AMD may impair mitochondrial function and promote metabolic shifts that enhance ROS production and reduce bioenergetic efficiency. Further targeted studies are needed to elucidate the receptor-specific signaling cascades governing these effects.
