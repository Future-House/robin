Query: role of complement factors, including CFH variants, C3a, C5a, and terminal complement complexes, in modulating mitochondrial function, bioenergetics, and cell death pathways in RPE cells in dry AMD-like conditions, with mechanistic insights

Complement dysregulation in RPE directly impinges on mitochondria, bioenergetics, and cell fate. CFH loss or high‑risk variants (Y402H) reduce mitochondrial respiration and ATP, increase mitochondrial stress (e.g., Hsp60), and induce mitophagy gene programs (PINK1, PARKIN), consistent with primary bioenergetic compromise; CFH knockout reduces mtDNA content and ATP, and CFH knockdown under mild oxidative stress depresses glycolysis and OXPHOS while increasing lipid peroxidation, indicating CFH maintains redox and metabolic competence independently of MAC inhibition (ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 15-16, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, armento2020lossofcomplement pages 1-2, romerovazquez2021interlinkbetweeninflammation pages 16-17).

Anaphylatoxins reprogram intracellular clearance and mitochondrial signaling. Intracellular CFH facilitates cathepsin L–mediated C3→C3a/C3b; lysosomal C3aR signaling sustains mTOR and suppresses autophagy, while C3a also dampens proteasome activity, jointly hindering removal of damaged proteins/mitochondria. C5a signals via mitochondrial C5aR1 to elevate ROS and activates NF‑κB, primes RPE for inflammasome activation, and experimentally decreases mitochondrial respiration with increased ROS, potentially via altered Ca2+ handling. Oxidative stress can drive thrombin‑dependent C5a generation independent of C3, a process restrained by CFH, linking CFH dysfunction to C5a‑dominant inflammatory stress (ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 15-16, ebeling2022inflammasomeactivationin pages 17-18, chu2024therelationshipbetween pages 4-5, gurubaran2021oxidativestressand pages 1-2).

Terminal complexes add degradative pressure. C5b‑9/MAC forms pores that injure membranes; in CFH Y402H iPSC‑RPE, MAC is internalized to lysosomes, which become swollen with reduced cathepsin D and diminished autophagic flux, promoting accumulation of damaged mitochondria and mtDNA‑driven NF‑κB/NLRP3 signaling. Complement exposure induces mitochondrial injury and drusen‑associated protein aggregation; CFH Y402H associates with heightened C3d/MAC deposition (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 16-18, bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 16-17).

Redox–complement feedback amplifies dysfunction: oxidative stress increases surface C3 and reduces CD55/CD59, while C3 depletion restores autophagy and antioxidant tone, supporting a complement–metabolism–inflammasome axis that culminates in bioenergetic failure and inflammatory cell death in RPE (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 33-34, ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 19-20).

CFH variants and loss compromise mitochondrial respiration and antioxidant defenses in RPE, while C3a/C5a signaling and MAC deposition disrupt autophagy/proteasome function, increase ROS, and activate NF‑κB/inflammasome pathways. These convergent extracellular and intracellular complement mechanisms drive mitochondrial damage, bioenergetic decline, and inflammatory cell death under dry AMD–like stress. Therapeutically, restraining C3/C5 activation and restoring CFH-regulated intracellular complement balance may normalize mitochondrial quality control and metabolism (ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 15-16, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 16-18, chu2024therelationshipbetween pages 4-5, armento2020lossofcomplement pages 1-2, gurubaran2021oxidativestressand pages 1-2).
