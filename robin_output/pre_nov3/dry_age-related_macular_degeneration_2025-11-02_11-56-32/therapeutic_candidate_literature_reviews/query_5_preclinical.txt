Query: preclinical data on small molecules that preserve RPE cell viability, phagocytosis, barrier function, and mitochondrial function in rodent models of geographic atrophy or dry AMD, with accompanying pharmacokinetic profiles, ocular distribution studies, and safety data

The available literature provides preclinical evidence for two distinct clusters of data, but no single study integrates all of the requested parameters. On one hand, the eye drop candidate CK41016 has been characterized extensively for its pharmacokinetics, ocular tissue distribution, and safety in both rats and rabbits. These studies, using compartmental analyses and validated bioanalytical methods, reveal parameters such as a two‐compartment pharmacokinetic model, rapid absorption in ocular tissues, and acceptable safety as demonstrated in cytotoxicity assays (choi2020anoveleye pages 1-3, choi2020anoveleye pages 10-13, choi2020anoveleye pages 7-10, choi2020anoveleye pages 13-15, choi2020anoveleye pages 15-17, choi2020anoveleye pages 3-7).

On the other hand, several reports describe small molecules—such as quercetin, resveratrol, carnosine, sulforaphane, and metformin—that preserve retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cell viability, phagocytosis, barrier function, and mitochondrial function in models of geographic atrophy or dry age‐related macular degeneration. However, these studies do not include corresponding pharmacokinetic profiles, ocular distribution data, or comprehensive safety assessments (basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 19-21, basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 27-28, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 18-20, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 20-21, camelo2025repurposingdimethylfumarate pages 4-6).

The current preclinical evidence is therefore divided between compounds with robust pharmacokinetic and ocular distribution data and those demonstrating RPE functional preservation. There is insufficient integrated data combining these aspects into a single comprehensive dataset.
