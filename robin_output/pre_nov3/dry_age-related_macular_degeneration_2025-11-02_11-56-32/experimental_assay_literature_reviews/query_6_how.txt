Query: How do prevalent AMD genetic risk variants—such as CFH Y402H, ARMS2/HTRA1 SNPs, C3 R102G—modulate RPE complement regulation, inflammatory responses, oxidative damage susceptibility, and therapeutic responsiveness in in vitro models?

CFH Y402H. In patient-specific iPSC‑RPE, homozygous Y402H disrupts FH/FHL‑1 anchoring to Bruch’s membrane glycosaminoglycans, diminishing local C3b/C3 convertase control and yielding AMD‑like phenotypes despite secretion of FH, FI, and FHL‑1: heightened inflammatory/stress signaling, lipid droplet accumulation, impaired autophagy, and drusen-like deposits; intriguingly, intermittent UV improved morphology and function only in the high‑risk cells, indicating genotype‑dependent responsiveness (hallam2017aninducedpluripotent pages 1-5). In ARPE‑19 with the at‑risk background, oxidative challenge drives exaggerated cytokine induction and apoptosis; pre‑incubation with exogenous wild‑type CFH suppresses cytokines in a dose‑dependent fashion and prevents cell death, demonstrating a rescue of inflammatory/oxidative vulnerability in vitro (velazquezsoto2023exogenouscfhmodulates pages 1-2, velazquezsoto2023exogenouscfhmodulates pages 12-14). Complement exposure using AMD patient plasma elicits stronger Ca2+ signals and pro‑inflammatory transcription/secretion in RPE, with late Ca2+ dynamics differing by CFH 402H genotype, linking genotype to complement‑evoked responses (busch2023increasedplasmalevel pages 1-2). Consistent with these data, FH 402H is less effective than 402Y at mitigating oxidative injury in RPE contexts (merle2023thenoncanonicalimpact pages 21-26).

ARMS2/HTRA1. CRISPR isolation of rs10490924 in iPSC‑RPE shows reduced SOD2 activity, increased ROS, and preferential rescue of cell death by sodium phenylbutyrate, establishing allele‑specific oxidative susceptibility and pharmacologic rescue in vitro (chang2023crispreditingdemonstrates pages 1-2). Independently, recombinant HTRA1 reduces CD36 protein via proteolysis and alters oxidized‑lipid uptake, whereas CFH variants preserve CD36 and interfere with HTRA1–lipoprotein interactions, revealing antagonistic, complement‑independent control of oxPL handling and inflammatory potential in RPE (lin2019cfhandhtra1 pages 1-14, lin2019cfhandhtra1 pages 14-18).

C3 R102G. The provided sources do not report in vitro RPE data delineating how R102G modifies complement regulation, inflammatory outputs, oxidative susceptibility, or drug responsiveness (figueiredo2024nutritionalgenomicsimplications pages 5-7, hernandez2025roleofoxidative pages 6-8, merle2023thenoncanonicalimpact pages 21-26, bhumika2024geneticinsightsinto pages 4-6, lauwen2021agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 19-21).

CFH Y402H compromises RPE complement regulation and heightens inflammatory/oxidative stress phenotypes in vitro, with rescue by exogenous CFH and genotype‑specific responsiveness to UV (hallam2017aninducedpluripotent pages 1-5, velazquezsoto2023exogenouscfhmodulates pages 1-2, velazquezsoto2023exogenouscfhmodulates pages 12-14). ARMS2/HTRA1 risk alleles increase ROS via reduced SOD2 and alter oxPL/CD36 biology; sodium phenylbutyrate provides allele‑preferential rescue (chang2023crispreditingdemonstrates pages 1-2, lin2019cfhandhtra1 pages 1-14, lin2019cfhandhtra1 pages 14-18). No in vitro RPE data are provided for C3 R102G in these sources (figueiredo2024nutritionalgenomicsimplications pages 5-7, hernandez2025roleofoxidative pages 6-8, merle2023thenoncanonicalimpact pages 21-26, bhumika2024geneticinsightsinto pages 4-6, lauwen2021agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 19-21).
