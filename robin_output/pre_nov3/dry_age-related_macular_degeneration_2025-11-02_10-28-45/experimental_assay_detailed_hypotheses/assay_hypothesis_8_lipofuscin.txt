Assay Overview:  
This assay involves loading retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells—typically ARPE-19 or primary human RPE—with exogenous A2E to mimic lipofuscin accumulation. Subsequent lipofuscin‐like autofluorescence is quantified using a plate reader or high-content confocal imaging, providing a surrogate measure for intracellular accumulation and clearance. In addition to autofluorescence intensity, related endpoints such as lysosomal pH (using probes like pHrodo™) and autophagy markers (e.g., p62, LC3-II) can be monitored to assess cellular lysosomal function (choi2024protectiveeffectof pages 18-29, moody2018recombinantmanganeseperoxidase pages 17-19).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Lipofuscin accumulation, particularly A2E, has been strongly linked with RPE dysfunction in dry age-related macular degeneration via lysosomal alkalinization, impaired autophagic flux, and promotion of cytotoxicity. Clinically, increased fundus autofluorescence is associated with AMD progression, and numerous studies demonstrate that A2E and related bis-retinoids contribute to oxidative stress and inflammatory complement activation (guan2020a2edistributionin pages 10-11, rozanowska2023lipofuscinitsorigin pages 54-56).

Previous Use:  
Similar cell-based models have been employed to screen compounds that either prevent A2E accumulation or promote its clearance. For instance, interventions such as ClioQ and 1H10 were shown to reverse lysosomal pH disturbances and reduce both p62 levels and A2E-induced cell death in RPE (choi2024protectiveeffectof pages 18-29, moody2018recombinantmanganeseperoxidase pages 11-13). Additionally, enzyme-based approaches (e.g., recombinant manganese peroxidase) have utilized related autofluorescence readouts to monitor lipofuscin degradation (moody2018recombinantmanganeseperoxidase pages 11-13, guan2020a2edistributionin pages 8-10).

Overall Evaluation:  
This assay is advantageous for its scalability, quantitative output, and direct targeting of a key pathogenic process in AMD. However, limitations include the inherent differences between acute exogenous A2E loading versus the chronic accumulation observed in vivo, potential spectral interference from heterogeneous lipofuscin components, and the need for orthogonal functional validation (zhang2023improvedlipofuscinmodels pages 14-16, rozanowska2023lipofuscinitsorigin pages 1-2).

The A2E/lipofuscin autofluorescence assay is a promising surrogate model for evaluating dry AMD therapeutics. It captures critical aspects of lysosomal dysfunction though requires complementary assays to address its limitations.
