This assay employs fluorescent autophagy reporters (GFP‐LC3 or mCherry‐GFP‐LC3) in ARPE-19 cells combined with lysosomal inhibitors (E64d/pepstatin A) to block degradation. The approach quantitatively measures LC3‐II accumulation and p62 turnover as surrogates for autophagic flux, directly modeling the impaired clearance mechanisms central to lipofuscin deposition in dry AMD (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 1-2, nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 9-11, szatmaritoth2016clearanceofautophagyassociated pages 11-12). Measurements are obtained via fluorescence microscopy, flow cytometry, and biochemical readouts.  

Biomedical Evidence:  
Robust evidence supports that autophagy dysfunction in the retinal pigment epithelium is a key pathogenic driver in dry AMD, contributing to the accumulation of deleterious substrates such as lipofuscin and triggering downstream oxidative stress and inflammation (kaarniranta2017autophagyregulatesdeath pages 4-7). Clinical and biochemical studies show that restoring autophagic flux can ameliorate RPE degeneration (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 5-9, cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 1-2). This assay thus targets a central disease mechanism relevant to both early and late stages of AMD.  

Previous Use:  
GFP-LC3 reporter assays have been widely used in RPE cell models to measure autophagic flux and assess the efficacy of autophagy-modulating compounds (szatmaritoth2016clearanceofautophagyassociated pages 11-12, viiri2013autophagyactivationclears pages 1-2). Such assays have contributed to our understanding of lysosomal dysfunction and proteostasis imbalance in AMD and have served as preliminary screening tools in early-stage drug discovery (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 1-2).  

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay’s strengths lie in its direct measurement of autophagic flux using validated molecular markers and its quantitative, high-throughput potential. However, limitations include the use of ARPE-19 cells, which may not fully recapitulate primary RPE physiology, and the need for complementary endpoints given the complexity of autophagic regulation in AMD (liton2023autophagyinthe pages 23-24, si2023theroleof pages 9-10). Further validation in more physiologically relevant models is advisable.  

This assay is a useful tool for screening therapeutics aimed at restoring autophagic clearance in dry AMD. It provides critical mechanistic insights while highlighting the importance of secondary validations to account for the in vitro model's limitations.
