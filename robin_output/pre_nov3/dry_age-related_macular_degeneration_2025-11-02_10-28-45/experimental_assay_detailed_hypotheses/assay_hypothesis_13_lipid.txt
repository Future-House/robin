Assay Overview:  
This assay models a central aspect of dry AMD pathogenesis by inducing lipid overloading in RPE cells—typically ARPE-19—through exposure to exogenous fatty acids (e.g., oleic acid) or oxidized photoreceptor outer segments. Lipid droplets are stained with BODIPY and quantitatively measured via live-cell imaging platforms (for example, with the Incucyte) to assess droplet area, number, and fluorescence intensity normalized to cell confluence (zhang2023clearanceoflipid pages 10-11, yako2022lipiddropletaccumulation pages 2-5). Additional assays, such as ROS detection using CellROX/MitoSOX and phagocytic evaluation via pHrodo-labeled POS, further enhance the readout of RPE function within this system (yako2022lipiddropletaccumulation pages 5-7).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Defects in lipid handling and efflux in the RPE have been implicated in the pathogenesis of dry AMD, mirroring the lipid composition observed in human drusen where esterified cholesterol and retinyl esters accumulate (federica2019impairedabca1abcg1mediatedlipid pages 16-18, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 5-6). The buildup of these lipids promotes oxidative stress and inflammatory responses that lead to cellular dysfunction and eventual photoreceptor loss, thus linking this assay to clinically relevant disease mechanisms (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 4-5, zhang2023clearanceoflipid pages 13-14).

Previous Use:  
Similar in vitro models have been instrumental in drug discovery, notably where autophagy-targeting compounds (such as LD·ATTEC constructs) lowered lipid droplet accumulation and improved RPE functionality in both cell culture and animal studies (zhang2023clearanceoflipid pages 1-2, zhang2023clearanceoflipid pages 9-10). These prior studies have demonstrated that modulating lipid clearance can restore phagocytic capacity and diminish ROS production (yako2022lipiddropletaccumulation pages 5-7, yako2022lipiddropletaccumulation pages 2-5).

Overall Evaluation:  
The lipid droplet clearance assay provides a robust and quantitative platform for early-stage screening of therapeutics aimed at correcting lipid dysregulation in dry AMD. Its strengths lie in the simultaneous assessment of lipid burden, oxidative stress, and phagocytic function; however, reliance on immortalized RPE cell lines and the inherent limitations of in vitro systems in replicating the in vivo microenvironment remain challenges (ma2021adam17mediatesectodomain pages 25-30, yako2022lipiddropletaccumulation pages 5-7).

This assay offers a valuable tool for screening candidate drugs targeting impaired lipid clearance in dry AMD. Its multifaceted readouts align with key pathogenic processes, making it promising for early drug discovery.
