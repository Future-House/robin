Assay Overview:  
This assay employs an in vitro RPE cell system in which cells are exposed to a sublytic dose of the membrane attack complex (MAC) in the presence of normal human serum. The goal is to trigger NLRP3 inflammasome activation, leading to the release of IL-1β and the activation of caspase-1. Readouts include quantification of IL-1β by ELISA and measurement of caspase-1 enzymatic activity. This approach models the inflammatory response and RPE degeneration observed in dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) (wang2016nlrp3upregulationin pages 6-9, dabouz2020anallostericinterleukin1 pages 1-2).

Biomedical Evidence:  
NLRP3 inflammasome activation is a central mediator of sterile inflammation in dry AMD. In patients, increased IL-1β and caspase-1 activity have been linked with RPE dysfunction and photoreceptor cell death, key elements in disease progression. These inflammatory markers, when elevated, mirror clinical observations of chronic retinal inflammation and degeneration, making them highly relevant for assessing therapeutic interventions (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 76-79, wooff2019il1familymembers pages 3-4, piippo2019inflammasomesignalinganda pages 87-91).

Previous Use:  
Similar cell-based assays have been employed in drug discovery to evaluate compounds that inhibit inflammasome activation. Past studies have demonstrated that reducing IL-1β release and caspase-1 activity via specific inhibitors can mitigate RPE damage, thereby validating the use of these biomarkers in screening potential treatments (brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 92-95, wang2016nlrp3upregulationin pages 3-6, brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 99-102).

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay’s strength lies in its focused mimicry of a key inflammatory pathway in dry AMD and its quantifiable endpoints, making it suitable for early-stage therapeutic screening. However, limitations include potential variability in MAC-induced responses and the inability of a monoculture system to recapitulate the complex in vivo microenvironment of the retina. Thus, while promising for initial compound screening, complementary in vivo validation remains essential (korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 86-87, piippo2019inflammasomesignalingand pages 47-49).

This assay offers a robust platform for screening NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors in dry AMD. It effectively models critical inflammatory mechanisms but should be integrated into a broader validation strategy to address in vivo complexity.
