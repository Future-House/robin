Assay Overview: The proposed assay utilizes ARPE-19 cells that are stably transduced with an NF-κB-luciferase reporter. In this system, cells are stimulated with complement components C5a and C3a, and luciferase activity is measured as a quantitative indicator of NF-κB pathway activation. This approach models the complement‐driven inflammatory signaling observed in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) during dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) (brandstetter2015complementcomponentc5a pages 1-2, brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 77-80).

Biomedical Evidence: Dysregulation of the complement system is central to dry AMD pathogenesis, with complement products—especially C5a—being implicated in priming RPE cells for inflammasome and NF-κB activation (brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivationa pages 67-70, brandstetter2015complementcomponentc5a pages 2-3). The differential effects of C5a versus C3a on NF-κB activation have been documented, highlighting that C5a is a potent activator of pro‐inflammatory signaling in RPE, thus supporting the relevance of NF-κB as a readout in this context (cao2016theroleof pages 151-151).

Previous Use: ARPE-19 cells with NF-κB reporters have been employed to study complement-induced inflammatory responses and to screen potential inhibitors of this pathway (brandstetter2015complementcomponentc5a pages 7-8, cao2016cfhy402hpolymorphism pages 6-6). These studies have contributed to our understanding of how complement-driven signals modulate inflammatory cytokine secretion and cell death in RPE models (brandstetter2015complementcomponentc5a pages 5-6).

Overall Evaluation: The major strengths of this assay include its high-throughput, quantitative readout and its focus on a key pathogenic mechanism in dry AMD. However, limitations include its exclusive emphasis on NF-κB signaling, which may not capture the full spectrum of inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways involved in AMD. Moreover, while ARPE-19 cells are a useful model, they might not fully reflect primary RPE physiology and in vivo complexity (brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 77-80, cao2016cfhy402hpolymorphism pages 6-6).

The complement NF-κB reporter assay in ARPE-19 cells offers a robust and quantifiable platform for evaluating therapeutic candidates targeting complement-mediated inflammation in dry AMD. Its focused readout on NF-κB activation is valuable, though additional assays may be needed to fully mimic the multifactorial nature of the disease.
