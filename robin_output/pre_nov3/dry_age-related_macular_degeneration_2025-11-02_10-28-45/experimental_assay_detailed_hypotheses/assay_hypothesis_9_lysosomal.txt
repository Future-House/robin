Assay Overview:  
This assay employs ratiometric LysoSensor probes in ARPE-19 cells to monitor lysosomal pH changes under stress conditions that mimic the intracellular environment of dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD). The approach involves quantifying fluorescence ratios from the LysoSensor Yellow/Blue DND-160 probe, serving as a surrogate for lysosomal acidity, and correlating these measurements with enzyme activity and TFEB/mTOR modulation. ARPE-19 cells, widely used as a model for retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), will be utilized to assess whether TFEB activators and mTOR inhibitors restore proper lysosomal pH and enhance photoreceptor outer segment degradation (ng2024impairedcathepsind pages 5-6, li2025sustainedlysosomaldelivery pages 14-17).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Lysosomal dysfunction, characterized by elevated lysosomal pH and inefficient degradation of photoreceptor debris leading to lipofuscin accumulation, is central to dry AMD pathogenesis (boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 4-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6). Dysregulation of the mTOR/TFEB axis plays a pivotal role in perturbing lysosomal biogenesis and autophagic flux, ultimately contributing to drusen biogenesis and retinal degeneration (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 6-10, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 4-6).

Previous Use:  
Ratiometric LysoSensor assays in ARPE-19 cells have been successfully employed to evaluate lysosomal pH normalization and TFEB/mTOR pathway modulation in the context of retinal degeneration studies (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 10-13, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 13-18). These applications have provided quantitative insights into therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring lysosomal function and mitigating AMD-related pathology.

Overall Evaluation:  
The strength of this assay lies in its ability to directly quantify changes in lysosomal pH—a critical determinant of RPE cell health—while enabling the screening of modulators of the TFEB/mTOR axis. However, while ARPE-19 cells offer a convenient and relevant model, differences from primary RPE cells and the complex in vivo environment may limit translatability. Nonetheless, its quantitative nature and established use in lysosomal studies make it a valuable tool for early-stage drug discovery for dry AMD (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2).

This assay provides a robust, quantifiable method to evaluate compounds targeting lysosomal dysfunction in dry AMD. It directly addresses key mechanisms underlying the disease, although further validation in more physiologically relevant models is advised.
