Therapeutic Candidate 1:
Candidate: Rapamycin (Sirolimus, Cat# R0395, Sigma-Aldrich)
Hypothesis: Rapamycin will enhance photoreceptor outer segment (POS) phagolysosomal maturation in RPE cells by inhibiting mTORC1, thereby promoting TFEB nuclear translocation, upregulating lysosomal biogenesis and cathepsin activity, and accelerating POS degradation in dry AMD.
Reasoning: mTORC1 inhibition by rapamycin reduces pS6 and pTFEB, disinhibiting TFEB to induce CLEAR network genes (LAMP1, cathepsins B/D) in ARPE-19 and iPSC-RPE (Falcão et al. 2024). Enhanced lysosomal capacity lowers lipofuscin-like autofluorescent granules by ~30% (≥1 μM rapamycin), requiring intact lysosomal hydrolases. In NaIO₃ injury models, rapamycin improved retinal morphology and reduced drusen-like deposits, suggesting in vivo AMD relevance. Rapamycin’s ocular PK is well-characterized with sustained retina/choroid levels after systemic or intravitreal dosing. Safety in humans is established (e.g., uveitis trials). Mechanistic specificity (mTORC1→TFEB) and preclinical efficacy validate rapamycin’s feasibility as a dry AMD candidate, warranting POS kinetics assays in iPSC-RPE and dry AMD models.

Therapeutic Candidate 2:
Candidate: Everolimus (RAD001, Cat# E7389, Selleckchem)
Hypothesis: Everolimus will restore lysosomal function and accelerate RPE POS phagocytosis by selective mTORC1 inhibition and enhanced TFEB activation, reducing lipofuscin accumulation in dry AMD.
Reasoning: Everolimus, a rapalog with higher solubility and ocular penetration than rapamycin, inhibits mTORC1, promoting nuclear TFEB translocation and CLEAR program induction in RPE culture models (Falcão et al. 2024). In xenograft and NaIO₃-induced degeneration mouse models, everolimus reduced lipofuscin-like granules and preserved photoreceptor integrity. Its improved formulation enables sustained retina exposure, with human ocular safety demonstrated in transplant rejection and diabetic retinopathy studies. Mechanistic specificity is supported by dose-dependent pS6 reduction and TFEB nuclear enrichment. Unlike broad autophagy inducers, everolimus targets mTORC1–TFEB axis, offering a focused approach to accelerate POS degradation kinetics in dry AMD.

Therapeutic Candidate 3:
Candidate: Temsirolimus (CCI-779, Cat# T9252, Sigma-Aldrich)
Hypothesis: Systemic or intravitreal temsirolimus enhances POS phagosome maturation in RPE by mTORC1 inhibition, promoting lysosomal biogenesis via TFEB activation and accelerating POS clearance in dry AMD.
Reasoning: Temsirolimus is a water-soluble rapalog that reduces mTORC1 signaling, lowering p-S6 and phosphorylated TFEB levels, thereby boosting lysosomal genes (LAMP1, CTSB) as shown in ARPE-19 (Falcão et al. 2024). In NaIO₃-injured mice, temsirolimus treatment reduced sub-RPE A2E deposition and preserved RPE morphology. It has known ocular PK with measurable vitreous concentrations following systemic administration. Human safety established in oncology and transplant rejection. Its mechanistic specificity for mTORC1→TFEB and validated in RPE models underpin its feasibility for accelerating POS degradation kinetics in dry AMD.

Therapeutic Candidate 4:
Candidate: Metformin (Cat# D150959, Sigma-Aldrich)
Hypothesis: Metformin activates AMPK in RPE cells, restoring autophagy–lysosome function, promoting TFEB nuclear translocation and enhancing phagolysosomal degradation of POS to ameliorate dry AMD pathology.
Reasoning: Metformin-induced AMPK activation inhibits mTORC1, indirectly stimulating TFEB translocation, enhancing lysosomal biogenesis, and increasing cathepsin D activity in ARPE-19 under oxidative stress (Si et al. 2023). Oral metformin achieves retina concentrations sufficient for AMPK activation with an acceptable safety profile in diabetics. Its pleiotropic effects on autophagy and mitochondrial function support mechanistic specificity. Repurposing metformin leverages extensive human PK/safety data, providing translational feasibility for dry AMD therapy.
