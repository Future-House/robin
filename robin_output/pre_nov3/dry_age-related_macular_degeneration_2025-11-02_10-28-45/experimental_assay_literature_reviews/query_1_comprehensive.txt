Query: Comprehensive mechanistic insights into retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) oxidative stress pathways in dry AMD, focusing on mitochondrial dysfunction, ROS generation sources, key antioxidant defenses, and existing in vitro assays for quantifying RPE oxidative damage

Mitochondrial dysfunction is a central contributor to dry AMD pathogenesis, with RPE cells exhibiting significant mtDNA damage, decreased ATP production, and altered morphology such as reduced mitochondrial number and loss of cristae, particularly in vulnerable regions of the macula (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 3-5, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 1-2, domenech2020therelevanceof pages 9-11). Electron leakage from mitochondrial complexes I–IV generates superoxide radicals during oxidative phosphorylation; this is further exacerbated by the high metabolic load imposed by daily phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments and light-induced photo-oxidation (chen2023oxidativestressin pages 1-2, si2023theroleof pages 7-9, basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 4-5).

The RPE’s antioxidant defenses combine enzymatic and non-enzymatic systems. Enzymatic defenses include superoxide dismutase isoforms (SOD1, SOD2, SOD3), catalase, and glutathione peroxidase, which act in concert with regulatory factors such as NRF2 and PGC-1α to maintain redox balance and mitochondrial quality control (basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 7-8, domenech2020therelevanceof pages 18-19, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 18-18, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3). Non-enzymatic antioxidants like glutathione and macular pigments (lutein and zeaxanthin) provide additional protection against ROS (domenech2020therelevanceof pages 9-11, abukhaled2025reactiveoxygenspecies pages 4-6).

In vitro assays primarily utilize primary RPE cells, often isolated from donor eyes, with oxidative stress induced by agents such as hydrogen peroxide. Quantitative measures include evaluating biomarkers like 8‑hydroxy‑2′‑deoxyguanosine (8‑OHdG), malondialdehyde (MDA), and carboxyethyl pyrrole (CEP) via ELISA, alongside assessments of ATP production, mitochondrial respiration, and proteomics to monitor oxidative damage (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 3-5, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 7-8, si2023theroleof pages 16-17, basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 4-5).

Dry AMD results from a cascade triggered by mitochondrial dysfunction leading to excessive ROS generation that overwhelms antioxidant defenses. In vitro assays quantifying oxidative damage are critical for elucidating these processes and guiding targeted therapeutic development.
