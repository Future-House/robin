Query: Mechanistic data on drusen constituents and extracellular matrix remodeling in dry AMD, covering lipoprotein aggregates, amyloid-β deposition, metalloproteinase dysregulation, and in vitro assays for modeling drusen formation and ECM changes in RPE cultures

Drusen in dry AMD are sub-RPE deposits enriched in lipids, with esterified cholesterol and phosphatidylcholine dominating the extractable fraction, and contain frequent apolipoproteins (apoE, apoB, A-I/C variants) together with vitronectin, complement components, crystallins, zinc, and TIMP‑3 (curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 10-11, hu2021interactionsbetweenapolipoprotein pages 1-2). Ultrastructurally, basal deposits and soft drusen harbor solid, nonvesicular lipoprotein-derived particles and EC‑rich lipid pools consistent with an intraocular response‑to‑retention process analogous to atherosclerosis (curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 13-14, curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 10-11).

Amyloid-β (Aβ) is present at low levels in normal retina, accumulates with age at the RPE–Bruch’s membrane interface, and occurs in drusen as nonfibrillar toxic oligomers; human RPE expresses APP, α/β/γ‑secretases and the Aβ‑degrading metalloprotease neprilysin, and Aβ exposure shifts the RPE secretome toward pro‑angiogenic VEGF with reduced PEDF via RAGE/NF‑κB signaling, while neprilysin deficiency recapitulates this imbalance (fisichella2017tgfbeta1pathway pages 21-23). Aβ assemblies are pro‑inflammatory and can amplify complement activation in the sub‑RPE space, linking amyloid deposition to innate immune amplification (usui2019invitrodrusen pages 4-7, parthasarathy2015neprilysindependentreductionof pages 22-26).

ECM remodeling is marked by altered protease balance: drusen/BLamD contain MMP‑7 and TIMP‑3, drusen are “cold spots” for proteolysis, and oxidant injury to RPE reduces MMP‑2 activity, implicating impaired ECM turnover; conceptually, MMP/TIMP disequilibrium and matrikine generation contribute to chronic inflammation and deposit growth (curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 13-14, fernandezgodino2016extracellularmatrixalterations pages 6-6, fernandezgodino2016extracellularmatrixalterations pages 1-4, garciagarcia2022pathophysiologyofagerelated pages 6-7).

In vitro modeling spans: RPE (primary or ARPE‑19) exposure to oligomeric Aβ with readouts of VEGF/PEDF and NF‑κB, supported by ELISA and immunoelectron microscopy; lipid‑preserving EM methods to visualize lipoprotein aggregates; oxidant‑injury and collagen barrier assays to probe MMP‑2 activation; 3D RPE spheroids that secrete apo‑containing lipoproteins and form drusen‑/BLamD‑like deposits; and polarized RPE EV proteomics/lipidomics showing basal enrichment of HTRA1, clusterin, fibronectin, and GM3 gangliosides that can drive ECM proteolysis and accelerate Aβ assembly (fisichella2017tgfbeta1pathway pages 21-23, curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 10-11, fernandezgodino2016extracellularmatrixalterations pages 6-6, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 4-7, kurzawa‐akanbi2022retinalpigmentepithelium pages 12-13).

Drusen biogenesis reflects retention and aggregation of RPE‑derived lipoproteins, Aβ oligomer deposition, and impaired ECM proteolysis governed by MMP/TIMP imbalance (curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 10-11, fisichella2017tgfbeta1pathway pages 21-23, curcio2013structurefunctionand pages 13-14, fernandezgodino2016extracellularmatrixalterations pages 6-6). RPE culture systems—including Aβ exposure, oxidant/MMP assays, EV omics, and 3D spheroids—provide tractable in vitro platforms to mechanistically dissect and modulate these pathways (fisichella2017tgfbeta1pathway pages 21-23, fernandezgodino2016extracellularmatrixalterations pages 6-6, kurzawa‐akanbi2022retinalpigmentepithelium pages 12-13, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 4-7).
