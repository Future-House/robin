Query: Search for small molecule agonists or positive allosteric modulators of αvβ5 integrin in retinal pigment epithelial cells, focusing on studies that quantify increases in outer segment binding and ingestion rates in vitro, confirm target specificity, and provide proof-of-concept efficacy in dry AMD animal or patient-derived cellular models

No published studies have reported small molecule agonists or positive allosteric modulators of αvβ5 integrin that are specifically designed to enhance photoreceptor outer segment binding and ingestion in retinal pigment epithelial cells in vitro or demonstrated proof‐of‐concept efficacy in dry AMD models. A targeted search using clinical trial databases with terms encompassing αvβ5 integrin activation, outer segment binding and phagocytosis in RPE cells yielded no results, indicating that this therapeutic strategy has not yet advanced to clinical or even robust preclinical investigation (Clinical Trial Search: dc8b5d5c9812). Studies employing small molecule modulators, such as the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632, have shown increases in photoreceptor outer segment ingestion in RPE cell cultures; however, these effects are achieved via cytoskeletal modulation rather than direct positive modulation of αvβ5 integrin (baileysteinitz2021developmentofin pages 87-91, baileysteinitz2021developmentofin pages 76-81). Similarly, the existing literature is dominated by approaches employing integrin antagonists or anti-integrin therapies targeting various retinal diseases, with a specific focus on inhibition rather than activation of integrin pathways (bhatwadekar2020antiintegrintherapyfor pages 11-12, bhatwadekar2020antiintegrintherapyfor pages 12-14, bhatwadekar2020antiintegrintherapyfor pages 4-6). Hence, no studies to date have quantitatively confirmed increased POS binding or ingestion through selective activation of αvβ5 integrin with target specificity in dry AMD animal or patient-derived models (Clinical Trial Search: dc8b5d5c9812, bhatwadekar2020antiintegrintherapyfor pages 12-14).

There is currently no evidence of small molecule agonists or positive allosteric modulators of αvβ5 integrin enhancing phagocytosis in RPE cells, nor any proof-of-concept data in dry AMD models. This represents a significant gap and an opportunity for future research in integrin-based therapeutic strategies for retinal disease.
