The Ezrin inhibitor NSC668394 increases lysosome number and cathepsin activity in RPE and promotes autophagy with in vivo protection in a rhodopsin-mutant mouse, yet it is not a direct cathepsin activator and lacks RPE donor–line efficacy and PK/safety details here (boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 6-8). 

Indirect strategies that secondarily raise v-ATPase subunits/cathepsins include TFEB activation and mTORC1 inhibition: rapamycin reduces A2E/lipofuscin-like granules in RPE models via TFEB-dependent lysosomal competence, and TFEB activation upregulates ATP6VOA1 and cathepsins, but these do not constitute direct v-ATPase/cathepsin pharmacology and clinical efficacy has been limited with adverse effects; donor-RPE and PK/ADME specifics are not provided (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7).
