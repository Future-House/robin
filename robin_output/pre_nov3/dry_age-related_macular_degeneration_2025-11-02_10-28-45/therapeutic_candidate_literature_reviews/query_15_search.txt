Query: Search for studies on extracellular drusen components (complement complexes, lipids, proteins) that inhibit RPE phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments, including mechanistic characterization and reversal strategies tested in vitro

Multiple studies show that extracellular drusen constituents—complement complexes, oxidized lipids/bisretinoids, and modified proteins—impair RPE phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments (POS) by driving complement overactivation, lysosomal/autophagic dysfunction, and metabolic injury. Oxidative stress and OSE-enriched POS/drusen reduce CFH, CD46, CD55, and CD59, amplify C3 deposition, C3a/C5a generation, and MAC assembly, and induce NF-κB–dependent cytokines, collectively degrading RPE homeostasis and phagocytic competence (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 8-10). Lipofuscin/bisretinoids (e.g., A2E) both activate complement and inhibit lysosomal degradation of ingested cargo; MAC endocytosis further burdens endo‑lysosomes and requires caveolin‑1/dynamin‑2, linking complement deposition to degradative overload (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 21-22, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 10-11). Drusen‑associated lipid remodeling activates ASMase, elevates ceramide and tubulin acetylation, and disrupts microtubule‑dependent autophagosome/phagosome trafficking; intracellular C3a generated in enlarged endosomes activates mTOR and suppresses autophagy, directly impeding POS processing (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 11-12). CFH dysfunction (e.g., Y402H; CFHR1 competition on MDA‑rich surfaces) and OSE‑targeting natural IgM further bias complement toward pathogenic opsonization and MAC deposition; CFH loss also drives mitochondrial defects that limit ATP‑dependent phagocytosis (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 10-11, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15).

In vitro/ex vivo reversal strategies target both extracellular deposits and intracellular sequelae. Complement inhibition and modulation (including C3 blockade; pegcetacoplan) and strategies that restore opsonization balance (natural IgM augmentation) have reversed RPE risk‑variant phenotypes or improved complement‑driven dysfunction in cell models (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 21-22). Lipid‑directed approaches—short heparan sulfate oligosaccharides, ApoA‑I mimetics (5A, L‑4F), and LXR agonists—remove ApoB‑lipoproteins/MAC from BrM explants and reduce lipid deposition and RPE stress in ARPE‑19/RF/6A and explant systems (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 13-15). Inhibition of ASM with desipramine normalizes early endosomes, prevents intracellular C3 proteolysis/C3a–mTOR signaling, and restores trafficking in RPE models; anti‑CEP antibodies and antioxidants (e.g., quercetin) neutralize pro‑oxidative adducts (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 13-15). Enhancing MAC clearance via endocytosis/ectocytosis and bolstering autophagy/lysosome pathways are mechanistically supported targets in vitro (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 21-22).

Extracellular drusen components impede RPE POS phagocytosis by complement overactivation, OSE‑driven inflammation, lysosomal/autophagic blockade, and metabolic compromise. Multiple in vitro strategies—complement inhibitors/modulators, lipoprotein‑removing agents, lipid efflux agonists, ASM inhibition, and anti‑OSE/antioxidant tools—have shown efficacy in reversing these pathways in RPE and explant systems. These findings nominate combined extracellular and intracellular targeting to restore RPE phagocytic capacity (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 21-22, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 11-12, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 13-15, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 10-11).
