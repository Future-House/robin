Query: Review of lysosomal function and autophagy–phagocytosis crosstalk in RPE cells from dry AMD donors, with focus on lipofuscin accumulation, altered pH, cathepsin activity, and the impact on outer segment clearance kinetics in vitro and in animal models

The RPE relies on tightly coupled autophagy–lysosome and LC3‑associated phagocytosis (LAP) programs to clear the diurnal load of photoreceptor outer segments (POS). In RPE from AMD donors, autophagy is transcriptionally upregulated yet functionally blocked: cells fail to induce autophagy with starvation, accumulate p62, and display enlarged annular LAMP1-positive organelles, while donor RPE also exhibits reduced phagocytic capacity versus age‑matched controls (pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 6-9, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, nita2020interplaybetweenreactive pages 1-3, si2023theroleof pages 4-5).

Lipofuscin and its bis‑retinoid A2E raise lysosomal pH and impair flux, driving lipofuscin accrual, lysosomal membrane permeabilization (LMP), cathepsin leakage, and necroptotic death; these events are reproduced in Abca4−/−Rdh8−/− mice and are mitigated by necrostatin‑7 or by Hsp70 upregulation (arimoclomol), which stabilizes membranes via BMP binding and acid sphingomyelinase activity (pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 6-9, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 4-6, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 3-4). Perturbation of V‑ATPase/βA3/A1‑crystallin–Ragulator signaling suppresses TFEB/TFE3, limiting lysosomal biogenesis and degradative capacity; pharmacologic re‑acidification restores POS degradation in impaired RPE, linking acidity to clearance kinetics (boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 4-6, pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 6-9).

Cathepsin D (CTSD) is the principal lysosomal POS protease; genetic CTSD loss causes retinal degeneration, and βA3/A1‑crystallin deficiency diminishes lysosomal acidification, reduces CTSD, and impairs heterophagic POS degradation (si2023theroleof pages 4-5, pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 4-6). Oxidative injury models corroborate this axis: hydroquinone alkalinizes lysosomes, lowers LAMP2/CTSD, and blocks autophagy, while Ezrin inhibition increases lysosome number and cathepsin activity, enhancing autophagy (abokyi2021targetinglysosomesto pages 1-2, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 4-6). Direct cathepsin activity and lysosomal pH measurements in AMD donor RPE remain limited in the cited material (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 13-14).

Functionally, autophagy/LAP disruption slows POS processing in vivo and in vitro: Atg5/Atg7‑deficient RPE shows defective lysosomal POS processing, reduced photoreceptor light responses, and decreased chromophore; POS‑feeding models reveal that impaired lysosomal enzymes (e.g., CTSD) increase autofluorescent granules, whereas TFEB activation (via mTORC1 inhibition or constitutively active TFEB) reduces burden in a lysosome‑dependent manner; receptor‑mediated lysosomal re‑acidification accelerates POS degradation (pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 4-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 6-9, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 4-6, liton2023autophagyinthe pages 23-24).

RPE from AMD donors show blocked autophagic flux and reduced phagocytosis, consistent with impaired lysosomal handling of POS. Lipofuscin‑driven de‑acidification and LMP, suppressed TFEB signaling, and CTSD deficits converge to reduce cathepsin‑dependent degradation and slow POS clearance; restoring lysosomal acidity/biogenesis or stabilizing membranes improves degradation in models. Quantitative lysosomal pH, cathepsin activity, and POS‑clearance kinetics in AMD donor RPE are still sparsely defined in these sources (pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 6-9, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 4-6, pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 4-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 13-14).
