Assay Overview: This assay exposes mature, polarized iPSC‑derived RPE monolayers to normal human serum (NHS) to drive complement activation at the RPE surface and quantifies deposited fragments (e.g., C3b/iC3b and C5b‑9/MAC) by high‑content immunofluorescence or ELISA after ~24 h, with candidate inhibitors co‑incubated to assess blockade efficacy. The model targets apical/basal complement handling in human RPE, a key tissue interface for drusen/MAC deposition, and should be run with fully differentiated iPSC‑RPE (high TER, apical–basal polarity) rather than ARPE‑19 to ensure physiologic complement secretion/localization and assay sensitivity (include HI‑NHS and complement‑depleted serum controls) (lueck2011sublyticc5b9induces pages 1-2, ebrahimi2013decreasedmembranecomplement pages 1-2, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 32-35, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 12-16, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 26-29).

Biomedical Evidence: Complement dysregulation is genetically and pathologically central in dry AMD: risk variants in CFH, C3, CFB, and CFI, abundant complement components in drusen, and MAC on RPE underscore clinical relevance (dalvi2019pluripotentstemcells pages 11-13, lueck2011sublyticc5b9induces pages 1-2). Human RPE responds to complement with inflammatory and pro‑angiogenic outputs, and C5a from serum primes inflammasome pathways, supporting NHS‑based activation as biologically meaningful (lueck2011sublyticc5b9induces pages 1-2, brandstetter2015complementcomponentc5a pages 2-3). Disease‑relevant stressors (aged/modified ECM, oxLDL) amplify complement activation and regulator loss, mirroring sub‑RPE pathology (gong2020stemcell‐derivedretinal pages 8-8, ebrahimi2013decreasedmembranecomplement pages 1-2). iPSC‑RPE can model local complement production/processing, which interacts with serum‑derived complement in vitro (chinchilla2021tick‐over‐mediatedcomplementactivation pages 1-2, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 32-35).

Previous Use: Serum‑driven MAC deposition on RPE has been repeatedly modeled; NHS increases C5b‑9 and induces cytokines/viability loss, with HI‑NHS as a required control (ebrahimi2013decreasedmembranecomplement pages 1-2, lueck2011sublyticc5b9induces pages 1-2). iPSC‑RPE challenged with complement‑competent human serum (CC‑HS) reproduces AMD‑like phenotypes (TER drop, lipid/APOE deposits, NF‑κB activation), supports CFH genotype–dependent responses, and has enabled screening that identified agents reversing complement‑driven pathology; C5a depletion ameliorated phenotypes (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 1-2, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 12-13, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 13-14). Complement tick‑over models in serum‑free iPSC‑RPE link local C3(H2O)/C3a generation to basal deposit formation and show that down‑regulating C3 prevents deposits, offering orthogonal validation readouts and mechanistic controls (chinchilla2021tick‐over‐mediatedcomplementactivation pages 1-2, chinchilla2021tick‐over‐mediatedcomplementactivation pages 8-9).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: human, genotype‑resolvable iPSC‑RPE; rapid, quantitative, and multiplexable readouts of C3b and MAC; direct linkage to clinically validated complement targets; and well‑defined controls (HI‑NHS, complement‑depleted serum/reconstitution) (schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 32-35, lueck2011sublyticc5b9induces pages 1-2, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 35-38). Limitations: NHS can overwhelm or mask RPE‑autonomous complement; outcomes depend on RPE maturation/polarity; 96‑well formats lack apical/basal segregation compared with Transwells; and 24 h captures acute deposition but not chronic basal deposit biology that emerges over weeks on disease‑like ECM (mitigated by adding ECM stressors or running paired tick‑over assays) (schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 35-38, dalvi2019pluripotentstemcells pages 11-13, gong2020stemcell‐derivedretinal pages 8-8, chinchilla2021tick‐over‐mediatedcomplementactivation pages 1-2). Translationally, alternative‑pathway tick‑over may evade some C3 inhibitors, so including endogenous C3 controls (e.g., C3KO/shRNA) and isoform‑specific readouts is advisable (chinchilla2021tick‐over‐mediatedcomplementactivation pages 1-2, schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 35-38).

This NHS‑driven complement deposition assay is useful for ranking complement‑targeting therapeutics on human iPSC‑RPE with fast, quantitative readouts tied to AMD biology. To maximize predictive value, ensure mature polarized RPE, rigorous serum controls, genotype stratification, and incorporate complementary tick‑over/ECM‑stress assays that capture local, chronic complement dysregulation (schikora2025maturationdependentcomplementproduction pages 32-35, chinchilla2021tick‐over‐mediatedcomplementactivation pages 1-2, ebrahimi2013decreasedmembranecomplement pages 1-2).
