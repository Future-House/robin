Assay Overview  
• Disease aspect modeled  
  – Chronic activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells, a key driver of RPE pyroptosis and local inflammation in dry AMD.  
• Readouts  
  – Caspase-1 activation: detected by FLICA (Fluorochrome-labeled Inhibitor of Caspase-1) probe and quantified by fluorescence microscopy or flow cytometry.  
  – IL-1β release: quantified in conditioned medium by sandwich ELISA. Optional secondary readouts: LDH release (pyroptosis), ASC speck formation (immunofluorescence), IL-18 (ELISA).  
• Biological material  
  – Human RPE cell lines (e.g., ARPE-19) or primary human RPE monolayers.  
  – Stimuli to trigger NLRP3: e.g. drusen components (complement C1q, C5b-9), Alu RNAs, A2E + blue-light, cholesterol crystals or other lysosomal destabilizers.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Central role of inflammasome in dry AMD  
  – Postmortem AMD eyes show upregulation of NLRP3 and caspase-1 in RPE and drusen-laden Bruch’s membrane (Tarallo et al., Nat Commun 2012; Tseng et al., Cell Reports 2013).  
  – Genetic association: polymorphisms in NLRP3 pathway genes correlate with increased AMD risk (Doyle et al., Invest Ophth Vis Sci 2012).  
  – Clinical correlation: increased IL-1β and IL-18 in vitreous and serum of dry AMD patients (Tseng et al., JAMA Ophthalmol 2018).  
• Mechanistic linkage  
  – Drusen constituents or photo-oxidized lipofuscin metabolites destabilize RPE lysosomes, release cathepsin B, trigger assembly of ASC/NLRP3 inflammasome, activate caspase-1, drive IL-1β maturation and Gasdermin D–mediated pyroptosis.  
  – Blocking IL-1β signaling in animal models preserves RPE and photoreceptor integrity (Tseng et al., Cell Reports 2013; Tarallo et al., Nat Commun 2016).  

Previous Use  
• Drug discovery  
  – Tseng et al. used this assay to show that genetic or pharmacologic inhibition of NLRP3 (e.g., MCC950) reduced caspase-1 activity and RPE death.  
  – High-throughput screens in macrophages and THP-1 cells have identified multiple small-molecule NLRP3 inhibitors subsequently validated in RPE (Coll et al., Nat Med 2015).  
• Key discoveries  
  – Demonstrated Alu RNA accumulation as an endogenous NLRP3 activator in AMD.  
  – Identified P2X7 receptor as an upstream trigger for NLRP3 activation in RPE.  
  – Established proof-of-principle that IL-1β neutralization rescues visual function in AMD models.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  – Direct, quantitative measurement of a disease-relevant pathway.  
  – Amenable to medium-throughput screening of small molecules or biologics.  
  – Uses human RPE cells, improving translational relevance.  
  – Multiple orthogonal readouts (FLICA, ELISA, LDH, ASC specks) increase assay robustness.  
Weaknesses  
  – ARPE-19 may not fully recapitulate in vivo RPE polarity, metabolism, or drusen interactions; primary RPE are more physiologic but lower throughput.  
  – Inflammasome activation is one facet of dry AMD; compounds passing this assay must still be vetted for effects on oxidative stress, autophagy, complement, and photoreceptor crosstalk.  
  – Standard 2D cultures lack Bruch’s membrane and immune cell interactions that modulate inflammasome priming in vivo.  
  – Kinetic window for detecting caspase-1 activation vs. secondary necrosis is narrow; careful time-course optimization is essential.  
Conclusion  
This inflammasome activation assay provides a well-validated, mechanistically focused platform for identifying and profiling therapeutics that target NLRP3-driven RPE inflammation and pyroptosis in dry AMD. While it captures a critical pathogenic axis, it should be integrated into a broader battery of assays (oxidative stress, complement activation, RPE barrier function) and, where possible, be complemented by 3D co-cultures or organoids to ensure candidate compounds address the multifactorial nature of dry AMD.
