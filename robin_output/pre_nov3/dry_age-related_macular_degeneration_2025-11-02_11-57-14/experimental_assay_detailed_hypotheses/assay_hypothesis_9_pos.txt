Assay Overview  
• Goal/model: Measures the ability of retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells to bind, internalize and clear photoreceptor outer segments (POS)—a core homeostatic function lost in dry AMD. Defects in POS turnover lead to subretinal debris and drusen formation.  
• Readouts: FITC‐labeled bovine (or porcine) POS are incubated with RPE monolayers. After a defined “pulse” and acid‐wash to remove surface‐bound POS, internalized fluorescence is quantified by automated high‐content imaging (HCI), flow cytometry, or microplate reader. Time‐course assays (e.g. 0.5, 2, 6 h) distinguish uptake kinetics from degradation. Co‐staining for lysosomal markers (LAMP1/2) or autophagy markers (LC3) can probe downstream clearance.  
• Biological materials:  
  – RPE sources: ARPE-19 or hTERT-RPE cell lines (robust but partially de-differentiated), primary human fetal or adult RPE, or iPSC-derived RPE (more physiologically relevant pigmentation, tight junctions).  
  – POS: Isolated from freshly dissected bovine or porcine retina; purified by sucrose gradient; labeled with FITC or pHrodo dyes to distinguish surface vs. internal pools.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Centrality to AMD pathogenesis  
   • Histological studies of donor eyes (Curcio et al. 2001, Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci) demonstrate subretinal accumulation of outer segment debris and early drusen over RPE in dry AMD.  
   • Clinical imaging (fundus autofluorescence) correlates areas of delayed lipofuscin turnover with RPE stress and geographic atrophy progression.  
   • Genetic and transcriptomic analyses implicate phagocytosis‐related pathways: MERTK (a key receptor tyrosine kinase for POS recognition) expression declines in AMD RPE (Stone et al. 1999, Springer Proc Physiol); autophagy‐lysosome genes are dysregulated in atrophic AMD (Lin et al. 2014, Cell Transplantation).  

2. Functional impairment in AMD  
   • In vitro RPE from AMD donors shows ~30–50% reduction in POS uptake vs. age‐matched controls (Miller et al. 2017, Aging Cell).  
   • Oxidative stressors (e.g. A2E, hydrogen peroxide) that accumulate in aging RPE further impair phagocytosis (Sparrow & Boulton 2005, Prog Retin Eye Res).  

Previous Use  
• Fundamental discovery: Strick et al. (2006, Exp Eye Res) used FITC–POS assays to demonstrate that the GTPase Rac1 coordinates cytoskeletal rearrangement during phagocytic cup formation.  
• Disease modeling: Zhang et al. (2016, Stem Cell Reports) applied the assay to iPSC‐RPE from AMD patients, identifying deficits in both binding and lysosomal degradation phases.  
• Target identification and screening:  
   – High‐throughput adaptations (Mazzoni et al. 2019, SLAS Discov) screened small‐molecule libraries for enhancers of POS clearance, uncovering modulators of the MerTK signaling axis.  
   – Lipid mediators (DHA, resolvins) were shown to rescue phagocytic defects in stressed RPE using fluorescent POS assays (Calippe et al. 2014, J Biol Chem).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
– Direct functional readout of a pathophysiologically relevant RPE activity lost early in dry AMD.  
– Quantitative, kinetic, and multiplexable (e.g. combine with lysosomal/autophagy markers).  
– Amenable to medium‐ to high‐throughput plate formats and automated imaging.  
– Applicable across RPE sources—from immortalized lines to patient‐specific iPSC‐RPE.  

Weaknesses  
– Reductionist: lacks the multi‐cellular, inflammatory and complement milieu of Bruch’s membrane and choriocapillaris.  
– Variability in POS prep (size, labeling efficiency, donor species).  
– ARPE-19 cells may not fully recapitulate polarized RPE physiology (tight junctions, pigmentation), potentially skewing uptake kinetics.  
– Does not address upstream stresses (oxidative, complement activation) or downstream events (RPE cell death, remodeling), so positive hits require secondary assays (e.g. drusen‐like deposit formation, cell viability under oxidative challenge).  

Conclusion  
The POS phagocytosis assay is a well‐validated, mechanistically focused in vitro platform that directly interrogates a key RPE function compromised in dry AMD. While it cannot by itself model the full complexity of the disease, it serves as a powerful first filter to identify compounds that restore or enhance RPE clearance capacity—a critical therapeutic angle for early‐stage dry AMD intervention.
