Assay Overview  
The mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) assay uses potentiometric dyes (JC-1 or tetramethylrhodamine methyl ester, TMRM) to quantify early mitochondrial dysfunction in live retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells under oxidative stress. In practice:  
• Cells: Human RPE cell lines (ARPE-19) or primary RPE from donor eyes.  
• Stressor: Sublethal hydrogen peroxide (100–200 μM) or blue-light irradiation to mimic cumulative oxidative insult seen in dry AMD.  
• Measurement: After treatment with test compounds, cells are incubated with JC-1 (5 µg/mL) or TMRM (25 nM).  
  – JC-1 aggregates in healthy mitochondria (emits red fluorescence) vs. monomers in depolarized mitochondria (green). The red/green fluorescence ratio is quantified by flow cytometry or high-content imaging.  
  – TMRM accumulates in polarized mitochondria; absolute fluorescence intensity correlates with ΔΨm and is recorded by microplate reader or confocal microscopy.  

Biomedical Evidence  
Mitochondrial dysfunction is a hallmark of dry AMD pathogenesis:  
• Clinical correlation: Post-mortem RPE from AMD patients shows fragmented mitochondria, reduced mitochondrial DNA copy number, and decreased electron transport chain activity (Feher et al., Exp. Eye Res. 2006; Golestaneh et al., Invest. Ophth. Vis. Sci. 2017).  
• Oxidative stress link: Chronic light exposure and lipofuscin accumulation in RPE promote reactive oxygen species that impair ΔΨm, triggering RPE apoptosis and subretinal drusen formation (Ferrington et al., Prog. Retin. Eye Res. 2016).  
• Animal models: Mice with RPE-targeted deletion of mitochondrial DNA replication factor Twinkle develop RPE degeneration resembling geographic atrophy (Hanus et al., Free Radic. Biol. Med. 2015).  

Thus, stabilizing ΔΨm in RPE is directly relevant to interrupting RPE dysfunction that precipitates dry AMD.  

Previous Use  
• Early screens: MitoQ and SS-31, two mitochondria-targeted antioxidants, were first validated in ARPE-19 cells by monitoring JC-1 ratios under H₂O₂ stress (Liu et al., Redox Biol. 2017). Protection of ΔΨm correlated with reduced caspase-3 activation.  
• Mechanistic insights: Using TMRM, studies have shown that tauroursodeoxycholic acid (TUDCA) restores ΔΨm and reduces ER stress in RPE (Castro et al., Mitochondrion. 2013).  
• High-throughput adaptation: Automated high-content screening of small-molecule libraries in 384-well format has been reported, with ΔΨm as an early endpoint for compound selection (Lin et al., J. Biomol. Screen. 2018).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Pathophysiological relevance: Directly quantifies a central feature of RPE degeneration in dry AMD.  
• Early, sensitive readout: ΔΨm collapse precedes cell death, enabling detection of sub-lethal rescue.  
• Throughput and adaptability: Amenable to plate-based screening and multiplexing with ROS or cell-viability assays.  

Weaknesses  
• In vitro simplification: Lacks Bruch’s membrane, inflammatory milieu, and photoreceptor interactions integral to dry AMD.  
• Probe limitations: JC-1 aggregation can be affected by cell density and non-mitochondrial binding; TMRM requires careful calibration to avoid quenching.  
• Translational gap: Rescue of ΔΨm in RPE may not predict functional preservation of photoreceptors or clinical endpoints such as geographic atrophy progression.  

Conclusion  
A ΔΨm assay in RPE provides a mechanistically grounded, quantifiable platform to identify mitochondrial-stabilizing therapeutics for dry AMD. It is most powerful when combined with complementary assays (e.g., oxidative stress markers, inflammasome activation) and ultimately followed by co-culture or ex-vivo models to address tissue-level complexity.
