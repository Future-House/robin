Assay Overview  
• Assay principle and disease modeling  
 – Measure lysosomal pH in live retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells using pH‐sensitive fluorescent probes (LysoTracker Green DND-26 or pHrodo Red dextran).  
 – Model the key dry AMD pathogenic cascade in which photo-oxidized RPE lipofuscin pigments (notably A2E) accumulate, raise lysosomal pH, impair degradation of photoreceptor outer segments (POS), autophagy, and promote RPE dysfunction.  
• Readouts and methodology  
 – Cells are incubated with A2E (or lipofuscin‐like pigments) for 24–72 h to induce lysosomal alkalinization.  
 – Following compound treatment, LysoTracker (accumulates in acidic vesicles) or pHrodo (fluorescence increases with acidification) is added.  
 – Quantify probe fluorescence by high‐content imaging or flow cytometry; optionally use ratiometric pH standards to convert fluorescence to absolute pH values.  
 – Secondary end points: co-staining for LAMP1/LC3 to assess lysosomal/autophagic flux, measurement of cellular A2E content (HPLC or fluorescence).  
• Cell system  
 – Human ARPE-19 or primary human fetal RPE monolayers, or iPSC-derived RPE differentiated for ≥4 weeks.  
 – Optionally co-culture with POS to recapitulate phagocytic load.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Centrality of lysosomal acidification to dry AMD  
 – Clinical hallmark: drusen deposition and RPE atrophy, preceded by lipofuscin accumulation and POS–lysosome fusion defects (Sarks et al. IOVS 2007; Ferrington et al. Prog Retin Eye Res 2016).  
 – A2E, a major fluorophore of lipofuscin, raises lysosomal pH by interfering with vacuolar ATPase, hindering enzyme activity (Yao et al. J Biol Chem 2005).  
 – Elevated lysosomal pH blocks autophagy and POS degradation, escalating chronic inflammation and complement activation in RPE (Mitter et al. Nat Commun 2014).  
 – Postmortem AMD eyes show reduced cathepsin activity and altered lysosomal marker expression (Nashine et al. Exp Eye Res 2015).  
• Clinical linkage  
 – Patients with early dry AMD exhibit fundus autofluorescence correlated with lipofuscin load; autofluorescence intensity predicts progression to geographic atrophy (Delori et al. Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 2011).  
 – No approved therapies for dry AMD; restoring lysosomal/autophagic function is recognized as a promising therapeutic avenue (Khandhadia & Lotery. Br J Ophthalmol 2010).  

Previous Use  
• Discovery and validation studies  
 – Sparrow and colleagues (IOVS 2008, 2010) used LysoTracker in ARPE-19 cells loaded with A2E to screen small-molecule acidotropic agents that re-acidify lysosomes. They demonstrated that certain polyenals lower pH and reduce A2E accumulation.  
 – Mitter et al. (Autophagy 2012) applied pHrodo dextran to show that rapamycin-mediated autophagy induction restored lysosomal acidity and enhanced POS clearance in stressed RPE.  
• High-throughput adaptation  
 – Several groups have optimized 96- and 384-well plate formats with automated confocal imaging to screen libraries of FDA-approved compounds for lysosomal pH–lowering activity (Zhang et al. SLAS Discov 2015).  
• Key discoveries  
 – Identification of v-ATPase modulators that reverse A2E-induced alkalinization and reduce lipofuscin load (Sparrow et al. Chem Biol 2011).  
 – Link between lysosomal pH correction and decreased complement factor secretion by RPE, suggesting anti-inflammatory potential (Mitter et al. JCI Insight 2017).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Disease relevance: Directly models a mechanistic node—lysosomal alkalinization—strongly implicated in dry AMD pathogenesis.  
• Quantitative, live-cell, high-throughput–compatible readouts allow screening of diverse compound libraries.  
• Multiplexable: can combine with autophagy, oxidative stress, complement activation assays for mechanistic profiling.  
• Use of human RPE (primary or iPSC-derived) enhances translational value.  

Weaknesses  
• Cell model limitations: ARPE-19 cells may not fully mimic mature RPE; primary/iPSC-derived RPE require lengthy differentiation and batch validation.  
• Artificial A2E loading may not recapitulate in vivo lipofuscin composition or accumulation kinetics.  
• Lysosomal probes (LysoTracker) can bind nonspecifically in off-target acidic compartments; careful calibration and controls are essential.  
• Altering lysosomal pH in vitro does not guarantee functional rescue of complex in vivo processes (e.g., Bruch’s membrane turnover, choroidal blood flow).  
• Potential assay interference from autofluorescent or colored test compounds.  

Conclusion  
The lysosomal pH assay in live RPE provides a robust, disease-mechanistic platform to identify and rank compounds that reverse A2E-induced lysosomal dysfunction—a central driver of dry AMD. When combined with secondary functional assays and physiologically relevant RPE models, it represents a powerful early-stage screen for novel therapeutics aimed at halting or reversing RPE atrophy.
