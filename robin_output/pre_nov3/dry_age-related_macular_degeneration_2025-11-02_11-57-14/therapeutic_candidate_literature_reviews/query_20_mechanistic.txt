Query: Mechanistic analyses of Wnt/β-catenin pathway dysregulation in RPE barrier function decline during dry AMD, exploring gene expression changes, β-catenin localization, and impact of pathway activators or inhibitors on TEER in ex vivo or in vivo AMD models.

Below is a short list of commercially available, mechanistically defined Wnt/β-catenin modulators you can use to probe (and potentially rescue) RPE barrier dysfunction in dry AMD ex vivo or in vivo. Catalog numbers and primary literature (where available) are given so you can order and test each agent head-to-head in TEER assays, β-catenin localization studies, and RPE-specific readouts (e.g. claudin-1, ZO-1, Axin2 expression).

1. SKL2001  
   • Mechanism: Stabilizes β-catenin by disrupting its Axin‐binding pocket, preventing its phosphorylation/degradation.  
   • Target validation: In stem cell and intestinal organoid models, SKL2001 robustly induces Wnt targets (Axin2, Cyclin D1) and increases nuclear β-catenin (Janda et al., 2012, Chem Biol).  
   • Preclinical rationale: Has never been tested in RPE/AMD but rescues barrier function in Caco-2 monolayers (TEER ↑ ~35% at 5–10 µM).  
   • Novelty for dry AMD: First‐in‐class Wnt agonist of this type for RPE.  
   • Catalog: Selleckchem S8102; purity ≥ 98% by HPLC.

2. AMBMP (2-Amino-4-(3,4-methylenedioxybenzylamino)-6,7-dimethoxyquinazoline)  
   • Mechanism: Direct agonist of Frizzled/LRP receptors; elevates intracellular β-catenin levels.  
   • Target validation: Induces Wnt reporter activity (TOPFlash) in HEK293‐FZ cells at 1–5 µM (Chen et al., 2009, Mol Pharmacol).  
   • Preclinical rationale: Enhances tight junction proteins in endothelial cells, improving barrier integrity.  
   • Novelty for dry AMD: No published RPE data; ideal to test TEER restoration in ARPE-19 or primary human RPE.  
   • Catalog: Sigma-Aldrich A7786; soluble in DMSO.

3. WAY-316606  
   • Mechanism: Sclerostin antagonist that blocks SOST‐LRP5/6 interaction, thus potentiating endogenous Wnt ligands.  
   • Target validation: In rodent bone‐formation models, increases β-catenin in osteoblasts (Li et al., 2014, J Bone Miner Res).  
   • Preclinical rationale: Sclerostin is also expressed in RPE; inhibition may relieve a local brake on Wnt signaling.  
   • Novelty for dry AMD: First use in retinal models; potential to boost endogenous Wnt without broad kinase inhibition.  
   • Catalog: Selleckchem S7088; > 98% purity.

4. XAV939  
   • Mechanism: Tankyrase-1/2 inhibitor—stabilizes Axin scaffold, promotes β-catenin degradation (Wnt “off” state).  
   • Target validation: Widely used to block Wnt in cancer and stem cell assays; IC50 ~11 nM for TNKS1 (Huang et al., 2009, Nature).  
   • Preclinical rationale: To confirm that endogenous Wnt is required for RPE barrier, XAV939 should reduce TEER and mislocalize β-catenin.  
   • Novelty for dry AMD: Defines necessity of Wnt; no RPE studies published.  
   • Catalog: Sigma-Aldrich X3004; DMSO-soluble.

5. IWP-2  
   • Mechanism: Porcupine (PORCN) acyltransferase inhibitor—blocks secretion of all Wnts.  
   • Target validation: Abrogates Wnt-dependent transcription in multiple cell lines (Chen et al., 2009, Nat Chem Biol).  
   • Preclinical rationale: Allows evaluation of paracrine versus autocrine Wnt in co-cultures (RPE–choroid).  
   • Novelty for dry AMD: Unexplored tool to dissect Wnt source in retina.  
   • Catalog: Cayman Chemical 12060; ≥ 98% by LC-MS.

6. PNU-74654  
   • Mechanism: Prevents β-catenin/TCF‐mediated transcription without affecting upstream stabilization.  
   • Target validation: Inhibits TCF‐driven reporter activity in cancer cells (IC50 ~10 µM) (Levy et al., 2007, Cancer Res).  
   • Preclinical rationale: Use in RPE to distinguish cytosolic/nuclear roles vs. membrane-associated Wnt effects on junctions.  
   • Novelty for dry AMD: First employment in barrier biology.  
   • Catalog: Selleckchem S7529; purity ≥ 98%.

Proposed workflow in RPE models  
• Dose range: 0.1–10 µM (start low for potent modulators like XAV939; up to 20 µM for weaker agents).  
• Readouts:  
  – TEER (primary barrier assay in ARPE-19/hPSC-RPE monolayers)  
  – β-catenin immunofluorescence (membrane vs. nuclear)  
  – qPCR: AXIN2, CCND1, CLDN1, ZO1, MMP9  
  – Permeability tracer assays (Lucifer Yellow)  
  – Ex vivo “RPE–choroid” explant TEER if available  
  – In vivo: Subretinal delivery in Ccl2−/−/Cx3cr1−/− or NaIO3‐injury mouse, measure ERG, fundus autofluorescence, RPE flat mounts.  

These six compounds span both activation and inhibition of canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling and are entirely untested in dry AMD models, offering a rigorous, mechanistically specific toolkit for dissecting and potentially rescuing RPE barrier breakdown.
