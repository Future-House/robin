Query: Investigations of endoplasmic reticulum stress and unfolded protein response signaling in RPE under dry AMD-like conditions, delineating its effect on barrier integrity factors such as ZO-1 expression, and efficacy of chemical chaperones or UPR modulators in restoring TEER in vitro

Below is a focused list of five commercially-available small-molecule “chemical chaperones” or UPR modulators that you can test, one agent per arm of the UPR plus two broad ER stress reducers. All have defined mechanisms, some preliminary in non-RPE models, and none (to our knowledge) have been published in dry AMD/RPE barrier work. Catalog numbers are provided where available.

1. Tauroursodeoxycholic acid (TUDCA)  
   • Mechanism: a bile-acid–derived chemical chaperone that promotes protein folding and attenuates all three UPR branches (↓BiP, ↓CHOP).  
   • Rationale/Evidence: shown to reduce ER stress and cell death in neural and retinal cells; improves barrier integrity in gut and endothelial monolayers.  
   • Catalogue #: Sigma-Aldrich T0260  

2. 4-Phenylbutyric acid (4-PBA)  
   • Mechanism: low-affinity chemical chaperone that stabilizes folding intermediates; also a weak histone deacetylase inhibitor at higher doses.  
   • Rationale/Evidence: widely used to lower ER stress in cultured cells, reduces PERK-eIF2α–CHOP signaling; reported to restore tight junction proteins (ZO-1) in blood–brain barrier models.  
   • Catalogue #: Sigma-Aldrich P21005  

3. AA147 (N-(2-chloro-4-fluorophenyl)-N'-(furan-2-ylmethyl)urea)  
   • Mechanism: selective ATF6 activator via reversible targeting of protein disulfide isomerases; ramps up the adaptive ATF6 arm without triggering PERK/IRE1 pro-apoptotic outputs.  
   • Rationale/Evidence: Paxman et al. (Cell Chem Biol, 2018) demonstrated protection against ER stress in hepatocytes and neurons; has not yet been explored in RPE.  
   • Catalogue #: Cayman Chemical #21693 or Tocris #6452  

4. Sephin1 (IFB-088)  
   • Mechanism: selective inhibitor of the stress-induced eIF2α phosphatase regulatory subunit PPP1R15A; prolongs eIF2α phosphorylation, reducing protein load and favoring adaptive UPR signaling.  
   • Rationale/Evidence: shown to protect against ER stress in models of Charcot-Marie-Tooth and ALS, with minimal off-target kinase effects.  
   • Catalogue #: MedChemExpress HY-101008  

5. 4μ8C (4-methyl-5-(8-methylcarbazol-3-yl)-2-thiophenecarboxaldehyde oxime)  
   • Mechanism: potent, selective inhibitor of the IRE1α RNase domain; blocks XBP1 splicing and regulated IRE1-dependent decay (RIDD) transcripts linked to apoptosis.  
   • Rationale/Evidence: protects photoreceptors in light-damage models; reduces CHOP and JNK activation in stressed cells.  
   • Catalogue #: Sigma-Aldrich SML0141  

Suggested in vitro workflow:  
• Induce “dry AMD–like” ER stress in ARPE-19 or iPSC-derived RPE (e.g. A2E + blue light, hydroquinone)  
• Treat with each single agent at a dose–response range (e.g. 1, 10, 50 μM)  
• Endpoints: TEER measurements, ZO-1 immunofluorescence and Western blot, CHOP/ATF4/XBP1s levels by RT-qPCR or capillary Western  

All five compounds are readily soluble in DMSO (final ≤0.1 %) and have precedent for cell-based ER stress assays. This panel lets you dissect which arm or global chaperoning approach best restores RPE barrier integrity under dry AMD–mimetic ER stress.
