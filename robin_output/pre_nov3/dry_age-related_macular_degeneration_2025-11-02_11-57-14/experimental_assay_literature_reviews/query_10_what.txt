Query: What in vitro strategies for recapitulating Bruch’s membrane permeability, RPE cell polarity, and drug uptake/efflux have been implemented to assess pharmacokinetics, tissue penetration, and functional efficacy of candidate therapeutics in dry AMD cell culture systems?

Below is a summary of the main in vitro approaches that groups have developed to capture key features of Bruch’s membrane, preserve RPE polarity, and measure drug uptake/efflux and tissue penetration in dry-AMD–relevant cell systems.  Wherever possible I have included vendor/catalog references so you can order or reconstitute the same platforms.

1. Transwell-Based RPE Monolayers on ECM-Coated Membranes  
   • RPE cell types:  
     – Primary human RPE (hwRPE), or human-iPSC-derived RPE (e.g. Cellular Dynamics iCell-RPE, cat. RPE-100-010-001)  
     – ARPE-19 (ATCC® CRL-2302™) for higher throughput, though polarity and tight junctions are weaker.  
   • Membrane inserts:  
     – Corning® Transwell® 6-well, polyester (PET) or polycarbonate (PC), 0.4 µm pore, clear (cat. 3450 for PET, 3415 for PC)  
     – Corning Synthemax® II SC (CovEntry ID 6003, synthetic ECM) for improved cell attachment and basement-membrane mimicry  
   • ECM coatings to approximate Bruch’s membrane:  
     – Human collagen IV (Sigma C7521), human laminin-521 (BioLamina LN521), fibronectin (Sigma F0895) applied at 5–10 µg/cm²  
     – Optional 1:1 mixtures of collagen IV:laminin plus 50 µg/mL hyaluronic acid to simulate GAG content  
   • Readouts of barrier integrity and polarity:  
     – TEER (e.g. EVOM^2 voltohmmeter + STX2 electrodes) to monitor tight-junction formation (values >200 Ω·cm² for hiPSC-RPE)  
     – Paracellular flux assays using FITC-dextrans (4 kDa, 40 kDa) or Lucifer yellow (457 Da) dosed apically, sampled basally, quantified by plate reader.  

2. Decellularized/Explant Bruch’s Membrane Scaffolds  
   • Human donor Bruch’s membrane: cadaveric eye cups are decellularized with 0.1% SDS, washed, then mounted in custom diffusion chambers (e.g. Franz cells).  
   • Porcine Bruch’s membrane: similar protocol, more readily available.  
   • Advantages: native ultrastructure, collagen/elastin mesh preserved; allows RPE attachment on “real” Bruch’s.  
   • Readouts:  
     – Confocal or SEM to verify ECM integrity  
     – Drug permeability across Bruch’s alone (no cells) vs. cell-covered  

3. Synthetic Nanofibrous or Microporous Membranes  
   • Electrospun poly(ε-caprolactone) (PCL) or poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) with fiber diameters ~200–800 nm to mimic Bruch’s elastin/collagen network.  
   • Commercial example: GreyWolf PCL nanofiber inserts (custom order)  
   • PDMS membranes with etched nanopores (40–100 nm) to allow only diffusional transport, preserving RPE polarization.  
   • These substrates often yield higher RPE transepithelial resistance and more basal infoldings.  

4. Microfluidic “Retina-on-a-Chip” Systems  
   • Emulate “RPE-Chip” (Emulate Inc.) or Mimetas OrganoPlate®  
     – Two parallel microchannels separated by a porous ECM‐coated membrane; upper channel seeded with RPE, lower with choroidal endothelial cells.  
     – Continuous medium flow (~5 µL/min) provides shear stress to endothelium and dynamic supply/drain of drugs.  
   • Measurable outputs:  
     – Real-time TEER on-chip (integrated electrodes)  
     – In situ live/dead, phagocytosis assays (pHrodo®-labeled POS)  
     – Sampling of apical vs. basal effluents for LC-MS/MS quantification of drug or metabolite concentrations  

5. 3D Retinal Organoids with RPE-Underlayer  
   • hiPSC-derived retinal organoids matured for >60 days to generate photoreceptor outer segments.  
   • Organoids placed atop an RPE monolayer on ECM membrane: allows bidirectional crosstalk, builds sub-RPE ECM deposits (“drusen‐like”).  
   • Useful for assessing whether a compound penetrates through the photoreceptor layer and RPE to reach Bruch’s analog.  

6. Functional and Pharmacokinetic Readouts  
   a) Polarity markers:  
      – Immunostaining for ZO-1 (tight junctions), Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (apical), Bestrophin‐1 (basolateral), MERTK (phagocytosis receptor)  
      – Transmission EM for apical microvilli and basal infoldings  
   b) Drug permeability/permeation assays:  
      – Calculate apparent permeability coefficient (Papp) from concentration–time profiles:  
         Papp = (dQ/dt) / (A × C₀)  
      – Use model compounds (propranolol, atenolol, propranolol‐d7) to benchmark  
   c) Transporter activity:  
      – P-glycoprotein (ABCB1) assays with rhodamine 123 or calcein AM; inhibitors (verapamil, 50 µM) to confirm  
      – BCRP (ABCG2) with Hoechst 33342 and Ko143 (1 µM)  
      – MRPs with CM-FDA and MK-571 (20 µM)  
   d) Metabolic profiling:  
      – LC-MS/MS of apical/basal media to identify drug metabolites  
      – UGT/Phase II activity via 4-methylumbelliferone glucuronide assays  

7. Assessing Functional Efficacy in Dry AMD Models  
   • Oxidative stress induction: tert-butyl hydroperoxide or H₂O₂, measure cell viability (resazurin), ROS (DCFDA).  
   • Lipofuscin‐like deposit formation: feed cells with A2E precursor, quantify autofluorescence.  
   • Complement activation assays: add human serum, measure C3a/C5b-9 by ELISA; test complement inhibitors.  
   • Phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments (isolated porcine POS, pHrodo® labeled) to measure RPE function; quantify uptake by fluorescence.  

8. Putting It All Together  
   To evaluate a new small-molecule candidate for dry AMD:  
   1) Culture hiPSC-RPE for 4–6 weeks on collagen IV/laminin coated Transwells; confirm TEER >200 Ω·cm² and correct polarity markers.  
   2) Measure baseline permeability of marker compounds, then dose your molecule apically (physiologic route) or basolaterally (via choroid).  
   3) Sample apical & basal media over time, quantify compound and metabolites by LC-MS/MS.  
   4) In parallel channels on an RPE-Choroid microfluidic chip, confirm dynamic distribution and assess transporter involvement via specific inhibitors.  
   5) Challenge the same cultures with oxidative or complement stress, measure functional rescue (viability, ROS, phagocytosis) in the presence vs. absence of drug.  

These layered strategies—moving from simple Transwells to decellularized Bruch’s scaffolds to dynamic microfluidic co-cultures—allow you to interrogate not only whether your drug reaches the RPE from the apical or basal side, but also its mechanism of uptake/efflux, its ability to traverse Bruch’s analog, and its functional rescue of dry-AMD hallmarks.
