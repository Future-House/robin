Assay Overview  
This in vitro assay uses human iPSC-derived retinal pigment epithelium (hiPSC-RPE) grown to confluence on Transwell inserts to model two key, intertwined pathogenic processes in dry AMD: chronic complement activation at the RPE–Bruch’s membrane interface and downstream NLRP3 inflammasome–mediated cytokine release.  On the apical (retinal) side, drusen-mimetic lipids or recombinant complement activation fragments (C3a/C5a) are applied to trigger local complement deposition.  Readouts are:  
• Complement deposition (C3b and membrane attack complex [MAC]) by immunofluorescence or high-content imaging  
• Barrier integrity by transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) measurements  
• Inflammasome activation by quantifying secreted IL-1β and IL-18 in the basal (choroidal) medium via ELISA  
This 1–2-day assay can be conducted in 96-well Transwell format, allowing simultaneous testing of small molecules (e.g., compstatin analogs) or blocking antibodies (e.g., anti-C5) for their ability to inhibit complement deposition, preserve RPE barrier function, and reduce inflammasome cytokine release.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Complement dysregulation in dry AMD: Genome-wide association and candidate-gene studies (e.g., CFH, C3, CFB, CFI loci) identify complement overactivation as a major risk driver (Fritsche et al. 2016).  Drusen deposits from AMD donors are enriched in C3 fragments and MAC (Hageman et al. 2005; Mullins et al. 2011).  
2. NLRP3 inflammasome in RPE degeneration: Doyle et al. (2012) showed that complement activation products prime and activate NLRP3 in RPE, leading to IL-1β/IL-18 secretion and pyroptotic death.  Animal and ex vivo human studies link inflammasome-driven cytokines to RPE atrophy (Tarallo et al. 2012; Anderson et al. 2020).  
3. Barrier breakdown: Clinically, RPE tight-junction disruption precedes geographic atrophy and photoreceptor loss (Steinle et al. 2011).  TEER correlates with RPE barrier integrity and is sensitive to both oxidative/complement stress.  
Together, these data position complement-mediated NLRP3 activation and barrier loss at the core of dry AMD pathogenesis.  

Previous Use  
• Complement deposition assays in RPE monolayers: Several groups have applied human complement serum or C3a/C5a to ARPE-19 or primary RPE and measured C3 fragment deposition and MAC by immunostaining to validate compstatin inhibitors (Ricklin et al. 2010) or anti-C5 antibodies (Holz et al. 2015).  
• Inflammasome readouts: hiPSC-RPE platforms have been used to screen compounds that block drusen-mimetic lipofuscin (A2E) plus complement to reduce IL-1β release (Sene et al. 2013).  These studies established dose-response curves for NLRP3 inhibitors (MCC950, glyburide).  
• Barrier assays: TEER in Transwell-cultured RPE is standard for assessing drug effects on tight junction integrity under stress (oxidative, complement) (Bhutto et al. 2017).  
Key findings from these systems include mechanistic proof-of-concept that compstatin analogs reduce both complement deposition and downstream cytokine release, and that NLRP3 inhibition preserves TEER under complement challenge.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Disease relevance: Models two genetically and pathologically validated targets in dry AMD—complement overactivation and NLRP3 inflammasome.  
• Multiparametric readouts: Combines structural (complement deposition), functional (barrier integrity), and biochemical (IL-1β/IL-18) endpoints in one assay.  
• Throughput: Adaptable to multiwell Transwell plates; compatible with automated imaging and plate-based TEER and ELISA systems.  
• Translational alignment: Directly tests mechanistic inhibitors under defined conditions that mimic drusen-associated stress.  

Weaknesses  
• Cell maturity and microenvironment: hiPSC-RPE may not fully recapitulate aged RPE phenotypes (lipid accumulation, Bruch’s membrane interactions, chronic low-grade stress).  
• Drusen mimetics: Synthetic lipid or complement fragment cocktails imperfectly model the complex composition of human drusen.  
• Assay complexity and variability: TEER readings can vary with differentiation batch; primary complement sources introduce batch-to-batch variability.  
• Short-term readouts: Rapid (1–2 day) format may miss chronic effects of complement modulation or long-term RPE survival.  

Conclusion  
This complement-inflammasome activation assay leverages a well-validated disease mechanism, offers multiplexed functional endpoints, and is adaptable for small-molecule or biologic screening. Its primary limitations revolve around the fidelity of drusen mimetics and the challenge of modeling long-term, age-related RPE alterations in vitro. Nonetheless, it provides a robust platform for early-stage evaluation of complement and inflammasome inhibitors in dry AMD drug discovery.
