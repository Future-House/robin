Assay Overview  
This assay models two interlinked RPE dysfunctions central to dry AMD—impaired phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments (POS) and secondary lipofuscin/A2E accumulation—plus downstream barrier breakdown and oxidative stress. Human primary or hiPSC-derived RPE cells are cultured on permeable supports in a 96-well high-content format. Daily doses of pHrodo-labeled bovine POS allow automated fluorescence imaging of acidified phagolysosome formation (phagocytic uptake) within hours of feeding. After repeated feeding cycles (e.g. 7–14 days), intracellular lipofuscin-like autofluorescence is quantified (450–650 nm excitation/emission). Trans-epithelial electrical resistance (TEER) measurements monitor monolayer integrity, and ROS levels are assessed by CM-H2DCFDA fluorescence. Together, these endpoints—POS uptake rate, lipofuscin load, barrier function, and oxidative stress—provide a multi-parametric readout of RPE health and the ability of candidate therapies (e.g. MerTK agonists or nuclear receptor modulators) to restore key functions lost in dry AMD.

Biomedical Evidence  
1. RPE Phagocytosis Deficit in AMD: In vivo and histological studies (Mazzoni et al., 2018; Strauss 2005) demonstrate that aged or diseased RPE exhibit reduced MerTK-mediated clearance of shed POS. Failure to clear POS leads to accumulation of bisretinoid byproducts (notably A2E) within RPE lysosomes.  
2. Lipofuscin and Oxidative Injury: Bisretinoids are intrinsically phototoxic and generate reactive oxygen species upon light exposure (Sparrow et al., 2010; Kim et al., 2008). Elevated lipofuscin autofluorescence in patients correlates with geographic atrophy progression (Delori et al., 2006; Schmitz-Valckenberg et al., 2008).  
3. Barrier Dysfunction: RPE tight junction breakdown, measurable via TEER, is documented in AMD donor tissue and associated with drusen formation (Curcio & Johnson, 2013). Oxidative stress (ROS) exacerbates junctional disruption (Bhutto & Lutty, 2012).  
4. Clinical Relevance: Fundus autofluorescence imaging in patients serves as a non-invasive biomarker for RPE lipofuscin load and predicts lesion growth (Greenberg et al., 2008). Supporting this, in vitro assays that recapitulate the same fluorescence spectra validate mechanistic links.

Previous Use  
• Phagocytosis Readouts: ARPE-19 and primary RPE lines have been used extensively to quantify POS uptake using radiolabels or fluorescent tags (Levy et al., 1988; Kevany & Palczewski, 2010). The pHrodo approach adds specificity for phagolysosome formation and has been adapted to 96-well high-content screening (HCS) platforms to profile small-molecule enhancers of MerTK or integrin-driven clearance.  
• Lipofuscin Measurement: In vitro accumulation of A2E and related fluorophores in RPE cultures has been correlated with gene expression changes in complement factors and oxidative-stress pathways (Lieu et al., 2013). High-throughput analysis of autofluorescence has been used to identify modulators of bisretinoid synthesis in RPE.  
• Barrier and Oxidative Stress Endpoints: TEER is a standard quality control for RPE monolayers in drug permeability and toxicity screens (Teeling et al., 2017). CM-H2DCFDA assays are broadly employed to quantify intracellular ROS—often paired with lipofuscin assays to validate antioxidant or anti-inflammatory interventions.

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Pathology-relevant: Directly measures hallmarks of dry AMD RPE pathology—phagocytosis failure, lipofuscin burden, barrier dysfunction, and oxidative stress—in a human cell context.  
• Multiplexed, Quantitative: High-content imaging and automated TEER/ROS assays enable parallel readouts, reducing assay time and reagent use compared to separate endpoint assays.  
• Predictive Potential: Endpoints map onto clinical biomarkers (autofluorescence imaging, visual cycle integrity), improving translational confidence.  
• Flexible: Can be tuned for genetic backgrounds (patient-derived hiPSC lines) or co-culture variations (microglia or choroidal endothelial cells added).

Weaknesses  
• Complexity and Throughput: Differentiation of hiPSC-RPE and repeated POS feedings require 2–4 weeks, limiting throughput and increasing cost.  
• Variability: Primary and iPSC-derived RPE exhibit donor-to-donor and line-to-line heterogeneity in maturity and phagocytic capacity, complicating assay reproducibility.  
• Simplified Niche: Lacks components of the outer retina microenvironment (Bruch’s membrane changes, complement activation, immune cell interactions) that contribute to dry AMD progression.  
• Substrate Source: Bovine POS preparations can vary, introducing batch effects; efforts to standardize or use synthetic POS surrogates may be needed.

Conclusion  
This phagocytosis–lipofuscin assay provides a robust, mechanism-based platform to screen and characterize candidate therapeutics that aim to restore RPE clearance functions and reduce downstream oxidative damage. While it does not capture every facet of dry AMD pathology, its direct alignment with disease-relevant endpoints and compatibility with high-content workflows make it a valuable component of an integrated in vitro drug-discovery pipeline.
