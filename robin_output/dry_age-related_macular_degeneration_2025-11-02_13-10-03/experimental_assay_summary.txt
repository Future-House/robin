Assay Candidate 1:
Strategy: Mitochondrial function assay in RPE
Reasoning: Oxidative stress–induced mitochondrial damage is a key driver of RPE dysfunction in dry AMD (Golestaneh et al., 2017). By exposing hiPSC-derived or primary human RPE monolayers to a controlled H2O2 or blue-light insult, then rapidly measuring oxygen consumption rate (OCR) via a Seahorse analyzer and mitochondrial ROS with MitoSOX fluorescence, one obtains direct, quantitative endpoints of mitochondrial health. Testing small molecules such as nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) or the mitochondria-targeted antioxidant MitoQ in a 96-well format yields sub-day readouts of rescue in OCR, ATP production and ROS levels. This simple, biologically relevant assay directly links drug action to mitochondrial bioenergetics and oxidative status and has been validated in RPE models of AMD (Zhang et al., 2019).

Assay Candidate 2:
Strategy: Complement-inflammasome activation assay
Reasoning: Chronic complement dysregulation and NLRP3 inflammasome activation underlie RPE degeneration in dry AMD (Doyle et al., 2012). Using hiPSC-RPE cultured on Transwell inserts, one can deliver drusen-mimetic lipids or recombinant C3a/C5a to the apical side and then measure C3b and membrane attack complex deposition by immunofluorescence, transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) for barrier integrity, and IL-1β/IL-18 in the basal medium by ELISA. This workflow provides same-day to next-day functional endpoints of complement deposition, barrier breakdown and inflammasome cytokine release. It is amenable to small-molecule or antibody screening (e.g., compstatin analogs, anti-C5 drugs) and directly ties mechanistic inhibition of complement or NLRP3 to preservation of RPE barrier and reduced pyroptotic signaling.

Assay Candidate 3:
Strategy: Phagocytosis and lipofuscin accumulation assay
Reasoning: Deficient phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments (POS) by RPE leads to lipofuscin (A2E) build-up and secondary oxidative injury in dry AMD (Mazzoni et al., 2018). In a 96-well high-content format, primary or hiPSC-RPE cells on permeable supports are fed daily pHrodo-labeled bovine POS; uptake and acidified phagolysosome formation are quantified by automated fluorescence microscopy within hours. After repeated cycles, lipofuscin accumulation is measured via autofluorescence in the 450–650 nm range, and TEER and ROS (CM-H2DCFDA) provide functional confirmation of barrier status and oxidative stress. This streamlined assay tests MerTK agonists (e.g., Gas6 protein) or LXRα/β modulators for their ability to restore POS clearance, reduce lipofuscin and maintain RPE barrier—key functional endpoints directly relevant to dry AMD pathology.
