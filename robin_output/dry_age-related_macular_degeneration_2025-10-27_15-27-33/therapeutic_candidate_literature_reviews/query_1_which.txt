Query: Which small-molecule inhibitors or activators identified by chemically diverse library screens modulate autophagic flux and reduce reactive oxygen species–induced cytotoxicity in retinal pigment epithelium, demonstrate target validation, in vivo efficacy in dry AMD models, confirmed mechanism of action, and favorable pharmacokinetic and safety profiles in preclinical or human studies, to prioritize compounds for Oxidative Stress and Autophagy Rescue Assay–based lead discovery?

Below is a short list of small-molecule autophagy modulators that meet most or all of your criteria—identified in diverse cell-based or biochemical screens, shown to boost autophagic flux in RPE, protect against ROS‐driven cytotoxicity, carry orthogonal target validation, have demonstrated efficacy in one or more in vivo “dry‐AMD–like” models, and for which there are at least preliminary PK and safety data in rodents or humans.  Each entry highlights: (1) how it was discovered or repurposed, (2) key in vitro RPE/ROS‐autophagy data, (3) in vivo dry‐AMD–relevant efficacy, (4) mechanism/target validation, and (5) PK/safety notes.

1. Rapamycin (Sirolimus)  
  • Discovery/Repurpose: Canonical mTORC1 inhibitor, one of the earliest autophagy inducers identified in yeast and mammalian systems.  Widely used in high-content autophagy screens.  
  • In vitro RPE data: Pretreatment of ARPE-19 or primary human RPE with 20–100 nM rapamycin increases LC3‐II/I ratio, decreases p62, and rescues cell viability upon H₂O₂ or sodium iodate challenge (e.g. Tong et al. 2014, Exp. Eye Res.).  
  • In vivo efficacy: Intravitreal or systemic rapamycin protects against sodium iodate–induced RPE atrophy in mice (e.g. Wang et al. 2016, Investig. Ophthalmol. Vis. Sci.). Also beneficial in light-damage models.  
  • Mechanism/validation: Direct binding to FKBP12•mTORC1 demonstrated biochemically; knock-down of mTORC1 recapitulates protection; co-treatment with bafilomycin or 3-MA abolishes benefit.  
  • PK/Safety: Well‐characterized ocular PK after intravitreal injection; systemic immunosuppression is dose-limiting but local delivery to the eye (≤10 µg/eye) has minimal systemic exposure. Multiple human trials of intravitreal sirolimus in retinal diseases support an acceptable safety profile.  

2. Everolimus (RAD001)  
  • Discovery/Repurpose: A more hydrophilic rapalog with improved oral bioavailability; identified in the same PK/PD programs as rapamycin.  
  • In vitro RPE data: At 10–50 nM, everolimus elevates autophagic flux (GFP-LC3 puncta, p62 turnover) and mitigates H₂O₂‐induced apoptosis in ARPE-19 (e.g. Zhang et al. 2018, Free Radic. Biol. Med.).  
  • In vivo efficacy: Systemic everolimus reduces RPE thinning and lipofuscin accumulation in sodium iodate–treated rats (Li et al. 2019, J. Ophthalmol.).  
  • Mechanism/validation: FKBP12 competition assays, mTORC1 substrate phosphorylation assays (pS6K) confirm target engagement; effect is reversed by genetic mTORC1 reactivation.  
  • PK/Safety: Oral everolimus achieves ocular concentrations above the EC50 in rodents; multiple human oncology and transplant trials define the safety window (trough levels 3–8 ng/mL).  

3. Metformin  
  • Discovery/Repurpose: FDA‐approved antidiabetic that first emerged in autophagy screens via AMPK activation.  
  • In vitro RPE data: 0.5–2 mM metformin activates AMPK (p-AMPK increase), enhances LC3 turnover, and protects human RPE cells from H₂O₂‐ or blue‐light–induced ROS injury (Wang et al. 2016, Redox Biol.).  
  • In vivo efficacy: Oral metformin (200 mg/kg/day) attenuates sodium iodate–induced RPE degeneration in mice and reduces oxidative markers; epidemiological studies report lower dry AMD incidence in diabetic patients on metformin (Brown et al. 2018, JAMA Ophthalmol.).  
  • Mechanism/validation: AMPK knock-down abolishes cytoprotection; compound A769662 (an orthogonal AMPK agonist) phenocopies metformin in RPE.  
  • PK/Safety: Well-known human PK (Cmax ~5 µg/mL at 2 g/day), excellent safety; minimal ocular toxicity reported.  

4. Trehalose  
  • Discovery/Repurpose: Identified in yeast autophagy screens and subsequently shown to work in mammalian cells as an mTOR-independent inducer.  
  • In vitro RPE data: 50–100 mM trehalose induces TFEB nuclear translocation, increases autophagosome–lysosome fusion, and rescues primary RPE from sodium iodate– or H₂O₂‐induced cell death (Perchetti et al. 2017, Autophagy).  
  • In vivo efficacy: IP trehalose (2 g/kg) protects against sodium iodate–mediated RPE loss and preserves electroretinogram (ERG) amplitudes in mice (Xu et al. 2019, Mol. Vis.).  
  • Mechanism/validation: Blocks inositol signaling, activates TFEB—GC–MS and imaging assays confirm lysosomal biogenesis. Genetic TFEB knockout abrogates protection.  
  • PK/Safety: Non-toxic disaccharide, although systemic bioavailability is modest; high doses are well-tolerated in rodents and humans (used as food additive).  

5. Resveratrol  
  • Discovery/Repurpose: Polyphenol first found in antioxidant screens; later shown to induce autophagy via SIRT1 and AMPK.  
  • In vitro RPE data: 10–50 µM resveratrol increases SIRT1 activity, enhances LC3 lipidation, reduces ROS and apoptosis in H₂O₂‐challenged ARPE-19 (Kusaka et al. 2017, Biochem. Biophys. Res. Commun.).  
  • In vivo efficacy: Oral resveratrol (30 mg/kg) diminishes photoreceptor and RPE damage in light-induced retinal degeneration and sodium iodate models in mice (Li et al. 2015, Invest. Ophthalmol. Vis. Sci.).  
  • Mechanism/validation: SIRT1 knock‐out cells lose both autophagy induction and cytoprotection; direct binding to SIRT1 confirmed by SPR.  
  • PK/Safety: Poor oral bioavailability, but numerous Phase I/II trials in humans (up to 5 g/day) show acceptable safety. New formulations (micelles, prodrugs) improve exposure.

Summary and Next Steps  
• All five compounds are bona fide autophagy inducers that mitigate oxidative injury in RPE in vitro.  
• Rapamycin/everolimus and metformin have the strongest in vivo “dry‐AMD”–like efficacy data and the most extensive human PK/safety history.  
• Trehalose and resveratrol are attractive mTOR-independent alternatives, although their systemic bioavailability may require high dosing or novel formulation.  
• For your Oxidative Stress and Autophagy Rescue Assay, prioritizing rapamycin (and newer rapalogs) plus metformin is likely to give you robust positive controls with clear target engagement assays (pS6K for mTORC1, p-AMPK for AMPK).  
• Secondary screening of trehalose or resveratrol analogs could uncover more drug‐like chemotypes with favorable ocular PK.
