Below is a consolidated overview of in-vitro assay strategies, organized by key mechanistic pathways implicated in dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD), together with recommended read-outs, reporter tools, high-content methods and viability assays. These platforms are readily adapted to moderate- or high-throughput formats for screening small molecules or biologics.

1. CELL MODELS  
  • Primary human retinal pigment epithelium (hRPE) isolated from donor eyes – gold standard for physiological relevance but limited expansion.  
  • ARPE-19 – widely used RPE-like cell line; exhibits many RPE phenotypes when fully differentiated (serum-free, low-calcium medium, polarized on Transwells).  
  • iPSC-derived RPE – patient‐specific genetic background, better expression of RPE markers (RPE65, BEST1), functional polarity.  
  • Co-culture systems – RPE co-cultured with choroidal endothelial cells or microglia to model complement, inflammation and ECM remodeling cross-talk.  
  • 3D organoids or Transwell, microfluidic (“organ-on-chip”) platforms for barrier and phagocytosis assays.

2. OXIDATIVE STRESS & MITOCHONDRIAL DYSFUNCTION  
  
  Induction  
   – Acute: H2O2 (100–500 µM), tert-butyl hydroperoxide (tBH), paraquat.  
   – Chronic/lipofuscin model: A2E loading + blue-light exposure (430 nm) to generate photooxidative stress.  
   – High-glucose or iron loading to exacerbate mitochondrial ROS.  
  
  Read-outs  
   • Intracellular ROS: DCFDA (general ROS), MitoSOX™ (mitochondrial superoxide).  
   • Mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm): JC-1, TMRE/TMRM fluorescence.  
   • ATP levels: luminescent ATP-Glo assay.  
   • Bioenergetics profiling: Seahorse XF analysis of OCR/ECAR.  
   • Lipid peroxidation: BODIPY 581/591‐C11 assay.  
  
  Reporters  
   • roGFP2‐Orp1 targeted to mitochondria or cytosol for dynamic redox sensing.  
  • Nrf2-ARE luciferase constructs to track antioxidant response element activation.  

3. COMPLEMENT ACTIVATION & DRUSEN COMPONENT ACCUMULATION  
  
  Induction  
   – Exogenous addition of complement proteins (C3, C5b-6, C5b-9) ± human serum.  
   – Drusen mimetics: purified Aβ1–42 fibrils, advanced glycation end‐products (AGEs), oxidized lipid extracts, ApoE4 lipoprotein particles, cholesterol crystals.  
  
  Read-outs  
   • C3a/C5a quantification by ELISA in supernatants.  
   • Membrane attack complex (MAC/C5b-9) deposition by immunostaining.  
   • Co-localization of drusen proteins (Aβ, ApoE) via immunofluorescence.  
  • qPCR/Western for upregulation of complement regulators (CFH, CD59).  

4. INFLAMMASOME INDUCTION  
  
  Induction  
   – Priming: low-dose LPS or TNF-α to induce pro-IL-1β/NLRP3.  
   – Activation: ATP (5 mM), nigericin, silica crystals or drusen components to trigger NLRP3 inflammasome assembly.  
  
  Read-outs  
   • IL-1β, IL-18 release by ELISA or multiplex bead arrays.  
   • Caspase-1 activity assays (e.g. FLICA™).  
   • ASC speck formation via immunofluorescence or fluorescent ASC‐GFP reporters.  
  • Western blot for cleaved caspase-1 (p20) and pro-IL-1β.  

5. AUTOPHAGY IMPAIRMENT  
  
  Induction  
   – Chloroquine, bafilomycin A1 to block lysosomal acidification.  
   – Lipofuscin/A2E loading, proteasome inhibitors.  
  
  Read-outs  
   • LC3-II/LC3-I ratio and p62/SQSTM1 accumulation by Western blot.  
   • mCherry-EGFP-LC3 tandem reporter: yellow puncta = autophagosomes; red only = autolysosomes.  
  • Lysotracker or Lysosensor dyes to assess lysosomal mass and pH.  
  • High-content quantitation of autophagosome number and size.  

6. BARRIER INTEGRITY & PHAGOCYTIC DEFECTS  
  
  Barrier function (polarized RPE on Transwells)  
   • Transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) measurement.  
   • Paracellular flux of FITC-dextran or lucifer yellow.  
   • Immunostaining for tight-junction proteins (ZO-1, occludin).  
  
  Photoreceptor outer segment (POS) phagocytosis  
   • Fluorescently labeled bovine or human POS; quantify uptake by high-content imaging or flow cytometry.  
  • Pulse-chase assays to measure binding vs. internalization vs. degradation.  
  • qPCR/protein for MerTK, αvβ5 integrin receptors.  

7. INFLAMMATORY CYTOKINE RELEASE & ECM REMODELING  
  
  Read-outs  
   • Multiplex cytokine panels (IL-6, IL-8, MCP-1, VEGF) in supernatants.  
   • NF-κB reporter assays (luciferase or fluorescent).  
   • MMP activity assays (fluorogenic substrates) and gelatin zymography for MMP-2/-9.  
  • Collagen I/IV deposition: Sircol soluble collagen assay or immunostaining.  
  • Fibronectin, laminin expression by ELISA or Western blot.  

8. VIABILITY AND CYTOTOXICITY ASSAYS  
  
  • Metabolic: MTT, resazurin (AlamarBlue®), WST-1.  
  • ATP‐based: CellTiter-Glo®.  
  • Membrane integrity: LDH release, PI uptake.  
  • Apoptosis: Annexin V/PI flow cytometry, caspase-3/7 activity assays.  

9. HIGH-CONTENT SCREENING (HCS) PLATFORMS  
  
  • Multi-parameter imaging combining nuclear morphology, ROS, mitochondrial health, autophagosomes, phagocytic vacuoles in 96- or 384-well plates.  
  • Automated analysis pipelines (e.g. CellProfiler, Harmony) for per-cell feature extraction.  
  • Assay miniaturization to 1,536-well for large library screens.  

10. ASSAY SELECTION & COMBINATION STRATEGY  
  • Dry AMD is multifactorial; single-endpoint readouts risk missing off-target or pathway-selective effects.  
  • A tiered approach is recommended:  
   – Primary screen: viability + one disease-relevant stress (e.g. oxidative) in ARPE-19 or iPSC-RPE.  
   – Secondary counterscreens:  
     · Mitochondrial function (Seahorse or ΔΨm dye)  
     · Autophagy flux (LC3 reporter)  
     · Inflammasome activation (IL-1β ELISA, ASC specks)  
   – Tertiary validation: polarized RPE Transwell assays for barrier, phagocytosis and complement susceptibility; co-culture with microglia/endothelial cells for inflammatory cross-talk.  
  • Orthogonal read-outs (imaging + biochemical) increase confidence in mechanism.  

Summary  
A robust drug‐discovery cascade for dry AMD in vitro should integrate RPE‐relevant cell systems, stress paradigms that recapitulate hallmark pathogenic processes, and a panel of complementary read-outs spanning oxidative stress, mitochondrial health, complement/inflammasome activation, autophagy, barrier function, phagocytosis and inflammatory/ECM remodeling. Early use of high-content and multiplexed assays boosts throughput while preserving mechanistic depth; focused follow-up in polarized and co-culture models strengthens translational relevance prior to in vivo or clinical evaluation.
