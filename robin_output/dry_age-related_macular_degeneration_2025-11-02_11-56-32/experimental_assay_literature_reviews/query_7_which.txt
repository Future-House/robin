Query: Which multi-omics approaches (transcriptomics, proteomics, lipidomics, metabolomics) have been applied to characterize disease progression in dry AMD cell culture models, identify novel therapeutic targets, and guide selection of mechanistically relevant assay endpoints?

- Transcriptomics has been applied longitudinally in primary human RPE cultures maintained up to ~25 weeks to model dry AMD progression, using regression modeling and DESeq2 to define 3,435 time‑correlated genes and thousands of enriched GO terms (lipid metabolism, EMT, calcium deposition), with strong overlap to in vivo AMD/aging signatures—thereby nominating disease‑relevant pathways and transcriptomic endpoints for assays (e.g., lipid, EMT, calcium genes) (alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 2-4, gonzaleziglesias2024untargetedmetabolomicsanalysis pages 2-4).

- Metabolomics and lipidomics (untargeted LC–MS in positive/negative modes) were performed across time and integrated with transcriptomics via MetaboAnalyst/KEGG, identifying glycerophospholipid and sphingolipid metabolism as significantly perturbed; cultures showed phospholipid declines and ceramide accumulation, with specific temporal patterns (e.g., GlcCer(d16:1/18:0) increase), directly informing mechanistic hypotheses and lipid endpoints (alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 7-10, alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 1-2). Targeted LC–MS quantified 18 of 23 culture-identified molecules in AMD patient serum, with four lipids significantly altered (e.g., GlcCer up; PE/PC species down), supporting biomarker selection and translational assay prioritization (alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 7-10, alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 1-2).

- Targeted proteomic readouts in the culture model—immunolocalization of ZO‑1/CLDN19 and detection of extracellular APOE and hydroxyapatite—were coupled to barrier function (TEER) and deposit formation, providing mechanistically anchored assay endpoints linked to the lipid‑pathway disturbances seen in the omics layers (alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 2-4, gonzaleziglesias2024untargetedmetabolomicsanalysis pages 2-4, alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 1-2). Complementary RPE multi‑omics with RNA‑seq, cellular/secretome proteomics, and pathway/GWAS overlays further demonstrate proteomics applied in AMD‑relevant RPE systems to nominate targets and endpoints, with sample workflows enabling concurrent metabolomics/lipidomics from the same preparations (emri2020amultiomicsapproach pages 5-8, emri2020amultiomicsapproach pages 3-5).

Transcriptomics, metabolomics including lipidomics, and targeted protein analyses have been integrated in long‑term RPE culture models to map dry AMD progression, highlight lipid‑centric pathways, and nominate biomarkers and targets. These omics layers guided selection of mechanistically relevant endpoints, including lipid species, transcriptomic signatures, TEER, tight‑junction integrity, and extracellular APOE/HAP deposits, with serum validation reinforcing translational relevance (alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 1-2, alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 7-10, alvarezbarrios2025dysregulatedlipidmetabolism pages 2-4, gonzaleziglesias2024untargetedmetabolomicsanalysis pages 2-4, emri2020amultiomicsapproach pages 5-8, emri2020amultiomicsapproach pages 3-5).
