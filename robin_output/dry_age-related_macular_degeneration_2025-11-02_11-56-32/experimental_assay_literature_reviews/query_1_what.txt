Query: What are the known molecular pathways and biochemical processes leading to RPE cell dysfunction and drusen formation in dry AMD, including roles of oxidative stress, complement activation, lipid metabolism alterations, mitochondrial impairment, and intracellular inflammasome activation?

Dry AMD arises from interlocking oxidative, lipid, mitochondrial, proteostatic, complement, and inflammasome pathways that converge on RPE dysfunction and sub-RPE drusen deposition (algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 3-4, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 6-10). Drusen are lipid-rich extracellular deposits—dominated by esterified cholesterol and phosphatidylcholine—likely derived in part from RPE lipoprotein secretion; they and related basal deposits impede diffusion across Bruch’s membrane and compromise RPE metabolism (algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 3-4, algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 2-3). Chronic photo-oxidative stress (blue light, A2E/lipofuscin) and exogenous insults drive ROS, lipid peroxidation (MDA, 4‑HNE, CEP), and neoantigen formation, damaging mitochondria and promoting RPE apoptosis and waste accumulation (kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 6-10, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15). Oxidative stress also suppresses complement inhibitors (CD46/55/59, CFH/CFI) and upregulates C3/CFB/C5, predisposing to alternative-pathway activation, anaphylatoxin signaling (C3a/C5a→NF‑κB), and MAC deposition at Bruch’s membrane/choriocapillaris (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15). Genetic risk in CFH (e.g., Y402H) and other complement/lipid loci reinforces lipid accumulation and dysregulated complement in drusen (algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 3-4, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15).

Mitochondrial dysfunction—ETC-derived ROS, mtDNA damage, ATP loss, defective mitophagy—both results from and amplifies oxidative and complement stress; CFH deficiency shifts RPE toward increased complement activators and impaired mitochondrial bioenergetics, and complement activation disrupts autophagic flux that can be restored by C3 depletion in models (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, si2023theroleof pages 7-9). Autophagy/lysosomal decline, driven by impaired Nrf2–p62 signaling and lipofuscin-mediated lysosomal inhibition, causes proteostasis failure with exocytosis of oxidized cargo that seeds drusen and fuels innate immune activation (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 9-10, kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 16-23, kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 6-10). Altered lipid handling—oxidized LDL uptake via CD36, high cholesterol in Bruch’s membrane/drusen—further increases oxidative stress, barrier dysfunction, and inflammasome signaling (kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 6-10, algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 3-4).

Inflammasome pathways link intracellular stress to inflammatory death: mtROS, ox‑LDL, aldehydic peroxidation products, Aβ, and lipofuscin/photo-oxidation prime/activate NLRP3→caspase‑1→IL‑1β/IL‑18 and GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis; complement C5a and C1q act as priming/activating cues, and a cGAS–type I IFN–caspase‑4 noncanonical route is also engaged by mitochondrial damage and Alu RNA (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 9-10, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 86-87). Iron-driven lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis add to RPE loss in this self-amplifying network (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 9-10, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12).

Dry AMD reflects a feed-forward loop: ROS and lipid peroxidation → impaired mitochondria/autophagy → complement and inflammasome activation → chronic para-inflammation and extracellular lipid–protein deposition as drusen (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 3-4). Drusen then further impede exchange at Bruch’s membrane and sustain complement-mediated injury at the chorioretinal interface (algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 3-4).

Answer: In dry AMD, oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation damage mitochondria and lysosomes, disrupt autophagy, and generate oxidized lipid–protein adducts that accumulate sub-RPE as drusen (kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 6-10, kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 16-23). Complement dysregulation (CFH pathway) and NLRP3 inflammasome activation—triggered by mtROS, oxidized lipids, Aβ/lipofuscin, and C5a/C1q—drive chronic inflammation, pyroptosis, and MAC injury, closing self-amplifying loops that worsen RPE dysfunction and deposit formation (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 86-87, algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 3-4). Altered lipid handling (cholesterol-rich deposits, oxLDL–CD36 signaling) and impaired Bruch’s membrane transport reinforce this pathogenic cycle (algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 3-4, kaarniranta2019fattyacidsand pages 6-10).
