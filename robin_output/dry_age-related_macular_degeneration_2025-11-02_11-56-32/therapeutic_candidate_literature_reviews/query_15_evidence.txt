Query: evidence of autophagy and mitophagy pathway dysregulation in RPE cells from dry AMD patients or models, pinpointing molecular targets such as LC3, p62, PINK1, and Parkin and linking these to mitochondrial quality control failure

Across human AMD RPE and AMD-relevant models, autophagy and mitophagy markers point to initiation with defective flux and lysosomal clearance, consistent with mitochondrial quality-control (MQC) failure. Primary AMD RPE exhibit increased LC3-II and autophagosome accumulation but reduced autophagic efficiency: p62/SQSTM1 fails to decrease during starvation and LAMP-1–positive organelles are enlarged/annular, indicating a late-lysosomal block; ultrastructurally, APs and disintegrated mitochondria are increased, mitochondrial ATP output is reduced with a glycolytic shift, and oxidative stress is elevated, linking stalled autophagy to mitochondrial dysfunction (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 10-11, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 2-5).

In the NFE2L2/PGC-1α double knockout RPE, canonical PINK1–Parkin mitophagy is engaged but fails late: PINK1/PRKN colocalization and pS65-PRKN puncta are increased, and LC3B colocalizes more with mitochondria, yet LAMP2–mitochondria colocalization is not meaningfully increased and autolysosomes are markedly fewer relative to autophagosomes; TEM shows fewer viable mitochondria with higher damage, Rab7 accumulates perinuclearly, and lipofuscin-like autofluorescent aggregates appear, consistent with impaired autophagosome–lysosome fusion/clearance and MQC collapse (gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 3-7, gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 1-3). A broader synthesis concurs: early PINK1/PRKN and LC3 signals rise without proportional lysosome recruitment, indicating either upstream activation or stalled late mitophagy (skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 11-12).

Patient data implicate deficient PINK1 in early AMD RPE, with impaired mitophagy driving EMT programs, strengthening the case that compromised PINK1 signaling undermines MQC in vivo (datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 1-2). Functionally, in ARPE‑19, mitochondrial injury (antimycin A) elevates LC3B and p62 and reduces mitochondrial counts; blocking lysosomal degradation (bafilomycin A1) exacerbates death, showing mitophagy is protective (skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 11-12). Therapeutically, Urolithin A restores flux, triggers PINK1/PRKN-dependent mitophagy, and requires p62 for lysophagy and viability rescue under lysosomal injury, directly positioning LC3–p62–PINK1/PRKN as actionable MQC nodes (jimenezloygorri2024urolithinapromotes pages 1-3).

Evidence from AMD RPE and models shows LC3 accumulation with p62 persistence and lysosomal pathology, increased PINK1/PRKN marking of mitochondria, and defective autolysosomal clearance, leading to accumulation of damaged mitochondria and bioenergetic failure. Interventions that reinstate PINK1/PRKN-dependent mitophagy and p62-mediated lysophagy restore flux and improve RPE survival, underscoring these proteins as mechanistic targets in dry AMD (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 10-11, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 3-7, gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 1-3, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 11-12, jimenezloygorri2024urolithinapromotes pages 1-3, datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 1-2).
