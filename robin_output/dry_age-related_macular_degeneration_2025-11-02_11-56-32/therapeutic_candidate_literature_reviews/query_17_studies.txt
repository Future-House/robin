Query: studies on calcium homeostasis disruptions leading to mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening, cytochrome c release, and RPE cell apoptosis in dry AMD, with kinetic, structural, and mechanistic evidence of key regulatory proteins

Disruptions in calcium homeostasis in retinal cells lead to excessive mitochondrial Ca2+ uptake and subsequently trigger the opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP). Elevated intracellular Ca2+ promotes overactivation of CaMKII, which phosphorylates Drp1 at Ser616, inducing mitochondrial fission and ROS generation. These alterations compromise mitochondrial integrity, facilitating mPTP opening and resulting in cytochrome c release and activation of the caspase cascade, ultimately driving RPE apoptosis (sun2024unveilingtherole pages 8-9, he2000leadandcalcium pages 1-1).

Kinetic studies in retinal cells reveal that Ca2+ exposure produces rapid mitochondrial depolarization, as evidenced by a shift in JC-1 dye fluorescence from aggregates to monomers, and electron microscopy confirms mitochondrial swelling and outer membrane rupture. The early kinetics—with significant cytochrome c efflux observed within minutes—are further supported by pharmacological experiments using cyclosporin A to inhibit mPTP opening and restore mitochondrial potential (he2000leadandcalcium pages 5-6, he2000leadandcalcium pages 8-9). Moreover, the involvement of key regulatory proteins, including cyclophilin D, is underscored by the sensitivity of these events to CsA, while Ca2+-dependent protease activation (e.g., calpain-mediated AIF cleavage) adds an additional layer to the apoptotic cascade (murakami2011ripkinasemediatednecrosis pages 6-8, morciano2021themitochondrialpermeability pages 1-2).

Calcium overload in RPE cells initiates mPTP opening, cytochrome c release, and apoptosis via coordinated kinetic, structural, and mechanistic events involving CaMKII, Drp1, and cyclophilin D. This cascade is a significant contributor to cell death in dry AMD.
