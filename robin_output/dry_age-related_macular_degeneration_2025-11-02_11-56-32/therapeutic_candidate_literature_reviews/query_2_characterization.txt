Query: characterization and screening of targeted small-molecule libraries enriched for mitochondrial biogenesis activators in RPE cells exposed to dry AMD mimetics, including mechanistic confirmation studies demonstrating PGC-1α induction, mtDNA copy number increases, and functional mitochondrial network restoration

No study in the provided corpus reports a targeted small‑molecule library screen in RPE exposed to dry‑AMD mimetics that, within the same system, demonstrates PGC‑1α induction together with increased mtDNA copy number and restoration of mitochondrial network function (brown2018mitochondriapotentialtargets pages 4-6, brown2018mitochondriapotentialtargets pages 1-4, zhang2020dysregulatedmetabolicpathways pages 12-13, bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19). Nonetheless, the literature establishes mitochondrial pathology in human AMD RPE and a validated assay toolbox for mechanistic confirmation: long‑extension PCR/qPCR for mtDNA lesions or content, extracellular flux for OCR/ECAR, mitochondrial mass and network imaging (TOMM20, Mitotracker; EM), citrate synthase/TFAM as mass proxies, and mito‑ROS normalized to mass (MitoSOX/MTG) (brown2018mitochondriapotentialtargets pages 4-6, zhang2020dysregulatedmetabolicpathways pages 12-13, bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19, jimenezloygorri2024mitophagycurtailscytosolic pages 8-8, djouadi2019mitochondrialgeneticdisorders pages 15-18). AMD RPE exhibits reduced AMPK activity, lower SIRT1 with hyperacetylated (inactive) PGC‑1α, and hyperactive mTORC1, nominating AMPK–SIRT1–PGC‑1α as the priority axis for biogenesis activation (zhang2020dysregulatedmetabolicpathways pages 9-10, zhang2020dysregulatedmetabolicpathways pages 4-7).

Compound exemplars support feasibility but fall short of the full triad: urolithin A increased mitochondrial mass, improved respiration, and lowered mito‑ROS in ARPE‑19 and in vivo, without explicit PGC‑1α induction or mtDNA copy‑number data in the excerpt; systemic metformin increased retinal mtDNA copy number and biogenesis proteins and protected RPE against sodium iodate; sulforaphane, AMPK activators, and astragaloside‑IV modulated PGC‑1α/Nrf2/Sirt1 pathways in RPE models (jimenezloygorri2024mitophagycurtailscytosolic pages 8-8, brown2018mitochondriapotentialtargets pages 4-6, hu2024regulatoryfactorsof pages 5-6). A pragmatic workflow is therefore: screen targeted libraries (AMPK agonists, NAD+/SIRT boosters, MITF→PGC‑1α inducers, TSPO ligands) in iPSC‑RPE/ARPE‑19 under dry‑AMD mimetics (cigarette smoke/hydroquinone; complement/C3a), with primary OCR/ECAR, MitoSOX/MTG, and TOMM20/Mitotracker morphometrics, followed by confirmation of PGC‑1α induction/activation, mtDNA copy number by long‑extension PCR/qPCR, and quantitative network restoration via imaging/EM (brown2018mitochondriapotentialtargets pages 1-4, bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19, hu2024regulatoryfactorsof pages 9-10, djouadi2019mitochondrialgeneticdisorders pages 44-46, jimenezloygorri2024mitophagycurtailscytosolic pages 8-8, brown2018mitochondriapotentialtargets pages 4-6, zhang2020dysregulatedmetabolicpathways pages 12-13).

There is no published RPE dry‑AMD mimetic library screen that confirms the triad of PGC‑1α induction, mtDNA copy‑number increase, and mitochondrial network restoration. Existing data define the target axis (AMPK–SIRT1–PGC‑1α), candidate chemotypes, and validated assays to execute such a screen. Implementing these endpoints in RPE models under AMD‑relevant stressors would close the current gap (brown2018mitochondriapotentialtargets pages 4-6, zhang2020dysregulatedmetabolicpathways pages 9-10, jimenezloygorri2024mitophagycurtailscytosolic pages 8-8, bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19).
