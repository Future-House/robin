Query: genome-wide association studies and functional validation of mitochondrial gene variants, such as those in mtDNA or nuclear-encoded mitochondrial genes, linked to dry AMD, demonstrating altered RPE bioenergetics and susceptibility in cellular or animal models

Genome-wide joint analyses that explicitly modeled mitochondrial–nuclear interactions (2-df test) in the IAMDGC cohort identified two novel nuclear loci for AMD—TRPM1 and a region near ABHD2/RLBP1—driven by interaction with a rare mtSNP in Complex I (MT-ND5 G12771A); functional annotation showed RPE-relevant regulatory features (DHS, enhancer/promoter marks, TF binding) at both loci, and the joint test provided substantially more power than main-effect scans to detect these signals (persad2017mitochondrialdna(mtdna) pages 62-68, persad2017mitochondrialdna(mtdna) pages 32-37, persad2017mitochondrialdna(mtdna) pages 134-139). The authors explicitly note that these statistical interactions require follow-up to determine whether they reflect biological interactions affecting RPE mitochondria (persad2017mitochondrialdna(mtdna) pages 134-139).

Human-donor data demonstrate AMD-associated mitochondrial pathology in RPE—disrupted ultrastructure, reduced mitochondrial mass, and mtDNA damage that scales with disease severity—consistent with an RPE bioenergetic crisis and suggesting a therapeutic window as damage is detectable early (fisher2018perspectiveonamd pages 3-5, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 32-36). Transmitochondrial cybrids isolating mtDNA effects show that AMD-derived mitochondria alter cellular phenotypes: reduced viability, lower mtDNA copy number, dysregulated mitochondrial replication/transcription and antioxidant genes, increased mtDNA fragmentation, and heightened sensitivity to stressors; mtDNA background also shifts complement regulator/activator expression independent of nuclear DNA, indicating retrograde signaling effects on disease-relevant pathways (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 7-8, nashine2016differentialexpressionof pages 1-2). Nuclear risk can impinge on RPE mitochondria: the CFH Y402H genotype reduces mitochondrial function in iPSC-RPE, connecting inherited risk to mitochondrial dysfunction (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 6-7). Mechanistic validation in models supports mitochondrial control of RPE susceptibility: combined loss of PGC-1α and NRF-2 causes age-dependent RPE degeneration, SOD2 deficiency increases RPE oxidative damage, and pharmacologic or antioxidant approaches (PU-91, SkQ1) improve mitochondrial programs and retinal phenotypes in AMD-relevant systems (nashine2019pu91drugrescues pages 10-12, nashine2019pu91drugrescues pages 16-17).

In sum, GWIS implicate TRPM1 and ABHD2/RLBP1 via interactions with an MT-ND5 variant, but direct variant-level functional validation in RPE is still needed (persad2017mitochondrialdna(mtdna) pages 62-68, persad2017mitochondrialdna(mtdna) pages 134-139). Multiple cellular and animal studies demonstrate that mitochondrial gene programs and mtDNA background alter RPE bioenergetics and susceptibility in dry AMD–relevant contexts (fisher2018perspectiveonamd pages 3-5, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 7-8, nashine2019pu91drugrescues pages 10-12).
