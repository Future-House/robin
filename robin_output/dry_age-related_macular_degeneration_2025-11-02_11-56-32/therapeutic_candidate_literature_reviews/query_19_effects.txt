Query: effects of drusen components, including cholesterol crystals, amyloid β, advanced glycation end-products, and lipofuscin, on RPE mitochondrial bioenergetics, oxidative phosphorylation defects, and cell dysfunction in AMD

Drusen components contribute to RPE mitochondrial dysfunction in AMD via both direct and indirect mechanisms. Amyloid β has been shown to damage mitochondrial membranes, deactivate electron transport chain complexes, and induce mitochondrial fragmentation through disrupted fission‐fusion dynamics. These effects impair oxidative phosphorylation, lower ATP production, and increase ROS generation, directly leading to RPE cell apoptosis (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 7-8, wang2023potentialregulatoryroles pages 5-7). Lipofuscin, particularly through its bisretinoid A2E, interferes with cytochrome oxidase activity, thereby hindering electron transport and ATP synthase function. In addition, lipofuscin accumulation disrupts lysosomal degradation and mitophagy, exacerbating mitochondrial quality control deficits and bioenergetic failure (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, dontsov2024retinalpigmentepithelium pages 3-4, rozanowska2023lipofuscinitsorigin pages 15-16). Advanced glycation end-products, though not directly linked with specific OXPHOS defects in the excerpts, promote oxidative stress and inflammatory signaling via RAGE activation, indirectly compromising mitochondrial function (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 2-4). Cholesterol crystals are recognized for their role in complement activation and inflammation; however, their specific direct impact on mitochondrial bioenergetics remains less well defined (ao2018retinalpigmentepithelium pages 14-19, basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 2-4). Overall, the combined actions of these drusen components lead to structural mitochondrial damage, impaired oxidative phosphorylation, and a metabolic shift toward glycolysis, resulting in RPE dysfunction and degeneration.

Amyloid β and lipofuscin directly impair mitochondrial energy production, while AGEs and cholesterol promote oxidative and inflammatory stress that further damages mitochondrial integrity. These convergent mechanisms drive the bioenergetic collapse and cell dysfunction observed in AMD.
