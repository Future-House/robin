Overall Evaluation: Strengths and weaknesses of this assay for testing therapeutics for dry age-related macular degeneration.

Assay Overview: This assay models RPE lipid handling defects that underlie sub-RPE/Bruch’s membrane lipid deposition in dry AMD by challenging primary or iPSC-derived RPE with oxidized LDL (oxLDL) and quantifying intracellular neutral lipids (BODIPY) and unesterified cholesterol (filipin) by microscopy, with optional plate-based quantification of lipid-positive area/intensity per cell (normalized to nuclear count) (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19). Uptake/localization controls include DiI-oxLDL and LAMP1 co-staining to verify lysosomal delivery, and CD36-blocking to confirm receptor-mediated entry (gnanaguru2016oxidizedlipoproteinuptake pages 2-3). Orthogonal readouts increase mechanistic depth: cholesterol efflux (3H- or fluorescent/enzymatic assays), ABCA1 expression, transepithelial resistance (TEER), cytotoxicity (LDH), and inflammasome activation (e.g., caspase-1), all feasible in polarized RPE on transwells (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19, gnanaguru2016oxidizedlipoproteinuptake pages 1-2, richards2024apoeimpactslipid pages 10-12). Complement-competent serum can be added to probe complement–lipid crosstalk and APOE-positive deposit formation (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19).

Biomedical Evidence: Drusen and sub-RPE deposits are enriched in esterified cholesterol/triglycerides and accumulate within Bruch’s membrane and beneath RPE in AMD, directly implicating RPE lipid handling in pathogenesis (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19, sreekumar2022oxidativestressand pages 13-14). LDLR/CD36-mediated lipoprotein uptake drives intracellular lipid burden; LDLR deficiency increases lipid droplets, esterified cholesterol at the RPE base, apoptosis, and retinal dysfunction, while oxidative stress downregulates LDLR and exacerbates these phenotypes (sreekumar2022oxidativestressand pages 12-13, sreekumar2022oxidativestressand pages 13-14, sreekumar2022oxidativestressand pages 9-12). ABCA1 is required for RPE lipid export, and LXR agonists prevent cholesterol accumulation in primary RPE, supporting efflux as a therapeutic target measured by this assay (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19). OxLDL triggers NLRP3 activation, barrier loss, and cytotoxicity in primary human RPE, linking lipid uptake to inflammasome-mediated degeneration (gnanaguru2016oxidizedlipoproteinuptake pages 1-2, gnanaguru2016oxidizedlipoproteinuptake pages 2-3). Beyond droplets, cholesterol/ceramide overload impairs trafficking, autophagy, and mitochondria, and promotes ApoE phase separation that correlates with AMD donor pathology, providing additional mechanistic endpoints to layer onto lipid staining (cunza2021mitochondriadependentphaseseparation pages 11-13). Lipid overload also drives IL-1β release and gasdermin cleavage with reduced oxidative capacity, all reversible by metabolic modulators in RPE models (dhingra2021assessmentofa pages 1-2).

Previous Use: OxLDL uptake with BODIPY/filipin imaging, cholesterol efflux assays, and ABCA1 modulation are standard in RPE studies and have identified efflux enhancement (via LXR) as a means to prevent cholesterol accumulation (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19). In primary RPE, oxLDL induces CD36-dependent uptake, lysosomal localization, NLRP3 activation, TER decline, and cytotoxicity, which are mitigated by inflammasome inhibition—demonstrating pharmacologic tractability of this readout panel (gnanaguru2016oxidizedlipoproteinuptake pages 1-2, gnanaguru2016oxidizedlipoproteinuptake pages 2-3). iPSC-RPE cholesterol challenges with apical/basal quantitation of free/esterified cholesterol and TEER provide scalable biochemical complements to imaging (richards2024apoeimpactslipid pages 10-12). Lipid droplet–centric paradigms show that droplet accumulation impairs POS phagocytosis and elevates ROS, both reversible by inhibiting droplet biogenesis, linking lipid readouts to core RPE functions (yako2022lipiddropletaccumulation pages 2-5, yako2022lipiddropletaccumulation pages 5-7). In vivo, LD-targeting autophagy tethers clear LDs, reduce oxidative/inflammatory markers, and rescue retinal function, supporting translational relevance of LD-reduction endpoints measured by BODIPY (zhang2023clearanceoflipid pages 9-10, zhang2023clearanceoflipid pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: strong construct validity to AMD lipid pathology; quantitative, multiplexable readouts (BODIPY/filipin, efflux, TEER/LDH, inflammasome, phagocytosis) and clear mechanism controls (CD36 blockade, ABCA1/LXR) (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19, gnanaguru2016oxidizedlipoproteinuptake pages 1-2, gnanaguru2016oxidizedlipoproteinuptake pages 2-3, richards2024apoeimpactslipid pages 10-12, yako2022lipiddropletaccumulation pages 2-5). Weaknesses: oxLDL is a simplified, potentially supra-physiologic stimulus; donor and maturation variability in primary/iPSC-RPE; limited modeling of Bruch’s membrane and complement unless explicitly included (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19). Mitigations include polarized transwells, defined oxLDL dosing, complementary efflux assays, and adding complement or mitochondrial stress modules to capture APOE condensates and deposit biogenesis features (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 17-19, cunza2021mitochondriadependentphaseseparation pages 11-13, richards2024apoeimpactslipid pages 10-12).

This BODIPY/filipin oxLDL-challenge assay models a central AMD mechanism—RPE lipid handling defects that drive sub-RPE lipid deposition—using validated readouts with pharmacologic responsiveness. With orthogonal efflux, barrier, inflammasome, and phagocytosis endpoints, it is well-suited for screening lipid-modulating interventions and triaging hits for in vivo validation. Incorporating complement and polarization improves disease fidelity and translational relevance.
