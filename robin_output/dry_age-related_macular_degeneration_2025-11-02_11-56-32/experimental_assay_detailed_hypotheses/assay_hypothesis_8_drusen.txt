Assay Overview:  
The assay utilizes hiPSC‐derived RPE cells cultured on transwell membranes to model RPE‐derived drusen formation. Prolonged culture with AMD serum induces the formation of basal extracellular deposits enriched in apolipoprotein E (APOE) and collagen IV, with concurrent activation of complement components such as C5b‐9. Quantification will be achieved by immunostaining and proteomic methods to measure deposit formation and assess complement augmentation (forest2015developmentofcell pages 36-41, bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 12-14).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Drusen deposition is a hallmark of dry age‐related macular degeneration and directly contributes to disease pathogenesis by promoting chronic inflammation, complement dysregulation, and subsequent RPE/photoreceptor degeneration. The local synthesis and accumulation of APOE and ECM proteins by RPE, combined with complement activation, are central to early AMD pathology as demonstrated by multiple studies (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 16-17, forest2015developmentofcell pages 17-21). This model reflects these pathogenic processes and supports its relevance in elucidating disease mechanisms and therapeutic intervention points (hageman2001anintegratedhypothesis pages 10-13).

Previous Use:  
Previous investigations have successfully employed hiPSC‐RPE transwell assays to recapitulate drusen‐like deposit formation, demonstrating genotype‐dependent responses and the capability to screen compounds that inhibit deposit accumulation and complement activation. These studies have highlighted both the mechanistic insights and proof‐of-concept in drug discovery for AMD (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 12-14, mesch2021towardsanina pages 24-27).

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths of the assay include its mechanistic relevance, ability to quantify specific RPE-derived deposit components, and its suitability for early-stage screening of deposit inhibitors. Weaknesses include the reductionist nature of a monoculture system, which does not fully capture the complexity of Bruch’s membrane, choroidal, and immune cell interactions, and the need for prolonged culture durations to achieve mature deposit formation (mesch2021towardsanin pages 24-27, mesch2021towardsanina pages 77-79).

This assay is a promising platform for screening therapeutics aimed at modulating RPE-driven basal deposit formation in dry AMD. However, complementary models will be necessary to address the full spectrum of disease complexity and to validate translational potential.
