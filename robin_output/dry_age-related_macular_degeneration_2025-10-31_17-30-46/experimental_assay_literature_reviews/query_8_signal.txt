Query: Signal transduction pathways mediating RPE cell apoptosis in dry AMD: roles of p53, BAX/BAK, JNK and ER stress UPR components in cell culture models and caspase activity assays for mechanistic evaluation of potential protective compounds

Below are 15 distinct mechanistic‐hypothesis/assay ideas, each testable in cultured human RPE cells (e.g. ARPE-19 or iPSC-derived RPE) using ER‐stress or oxidative‐stress triggers (tunicamycin, thapsigargin, H₂O₂, blue light, etc.) and readouts including caspase-3/7 activity, mitochondrial assays, Westerns, qPCR, immunofluorescence, viability assays, etc. Each also proposes a “protective” intervention.  

1. p53 Acetylation Drives BAX-Mediated Apoptosis  
   • Hypothesis: ER stress increases p53 acetylation, boosting BAX transcription and apoptosis.  
   • Assay: Treat RPE with tunicamycin ± SIRT1 activator (resveratrol) or inhibitor (EX-527).  
   • Readouts: p53 acetyl-Lys382 (WB), BAX mRNA/protein, caspase-3/7 luminescence, Annexin V.  

2. JNK-Dependent p53 Phosphorylation at Ser15  
   • Hypothesis: Thapsigargin induces JNK→p53(Ser15) phosphorylation, upregulating pro-apoptotic genes.  
   • Assay: Pre-treat with SP600125 (JNK inhibitor) then thapsigargin.  
   • Readouts: p-JNK, p-p53(Ser15), BAX/BAK by WB; caspase-9/3 activity; cell viability (MTT).  

3. BAX/BAK Double Knockdown Rescues from Oxidative Stress  
   • Hypothesis: Silencing BAX and BAK abrogates mitochondrial apoptosis under H₂O₂.  
   • Assay: Generate stable shRNA (or CRISPRi) RPE lines targeting BAX and BAK; expose to H₂O₂.  
   • Readouts: JC-1 mitochondrial membrane potential; cytochrome-c release (IF); caspase-3 activity.  

4. Chemical Chaperone 4-PBA Attenuates CHOP-Mediated BAX Induction  
   • Hypothesis: 4-phenylbutyrate (4-PBA) reduces UPR-CHOP→BAX axis.  
   • Assay: Co-treat tunicamycin ± 4-PBA.  
   • Readouts: GRP78, CHOP, BAX (WB/qPCR); caspase-3/7; LDH release.  

5. XBP1s Overexpression Enhances ER Homeostasis & Blocks Apoptosis  
   • Hypothesis: Spliced XBP1 (XBP1s) boosts folding capacity and suppresses p53/BAX.  
   • Assay: Transfect XBP1s-GFP plasmid; induce ER stress.  
   • Readouts: XBP1s target genes (EDEM1); p53/BAX levels; caspase activity; cell survival.  

6. PERK–eIF2α–ATF4 Axis Links ER Stress to p53 Activation  
   • Hypothesis: PERK-mediated eIF2α phosphorylation elevates ATF4/CHOP, crosstalking with p53.  
   • Assay: Use GSK2606414 (PERK inhibitor) prior to thapsigargin.  
   • Readouts: p-eIF2α, ATF4, CHOP, p-p53; caspase-3; Annexin-V/PI.  

7. IRE1α RNase Inhibition Suppresses JNK–p53 Pro-apoptotic Crosstalk  
   • Hypothesis: Blocking IRE1α RNase (STF-083010) reduces downstream JNK activation and apoptosis.  
   • Assay: Thapsigargin with/without STF-083010.  
   • Readouts: XBP1 splicing (RT–PCR); p-JNK, p-p53; caspase-3/7; TUNEL.  

8. CHOP Knockdown Prevents BAX Upregulation  
   • Hypothesis: CHOP is required for ER stress–induced BAX transcription.  
   • Assay: Transfect CHOP siRNA then stress cells.  
   • Readouts: CHOP, BAX protein; caspase-3 activity; mitochondrial potential.  

9. Dual JNK Inhibitor + 4-PBA Yields Synergistic Protection  
   • Hypothesis: Combining SP600125 and 4-PBA more fully abrogates UPR- and JNK-driven apoptosis.  
   • Assay: Tunicamycin ± SP600125 ± 4-PBA in factorial design.  
   • Readouts: Caspase-3/7; cell viability (ATP-Glo); p-JNK, CHOP, BAX.  

10. ATF6 Modulation by Ceapin-A7 Alters p53‐Driven Apoptosis  
    • Hypothesis: Pharmacological ATF6 inhibition (Ceapin-A7) shifts UPR balance away from PERK/IRE1 and downregulates p53 activation.  
    • Assay: Pre-treat with Ceapin-A7, induce ER stress.  
    • Readouts: ATF6 cleavage (WB), p-p53, caspase-3, cell viability.  

11. Hsp90 Induction Stabilizes p53’s Folding State & Reduces Apoptosis  
    • Hypothesis: Low-dose 17-AAG increases Hsp90 client stabilization, preventing p53 pro-apoptotic conformations.  
    • Assay: Pre-treat with 17-AAG then thapsigargin.  
    • Readouts: Hsp90, p53 (conformational‐specific Ab), caspase activation, MTT assay.  

12. Anti-miR-34a Mitigates p53/BAX Axis  
    • Hypothesis: miR-34a amplifies p53 signaling; its inhibition reduces BAX induction.  
    • Assay: Transfect locked‐nucleic‐acid anti-miR-34a, then apply tunicamycin.  
    • Readouts: miR-34a levels (qPCR), p53 targets (BAX, PUMA), caspase-3.  

13. BCL-2 Overexpression Blocks Mitochondrial Apoptosis  
    • Hypothesis: Ectopic BCL-2 protects RPE from ER/oxidative stress by sequestering BAX/BAK.  
    • Assay: Lentiviral BCL-2 vs. control; stress with H₂O₂ or tunicamycin.  
    • Readouts: Cytochrome-c release (fractionation/WB), caspase-9/3, viability.  

14. Antioxidant NAC Suppresses ROS-Driven JNK–p53 Activation  
    • Hypothesis: N-acetylcysteine reduces ROS, blunting JNK and subsequent p53/BAX pro-apoptotic signaling.  
    • Assay: Co-treat H₂O₂ ± NAC.  
    • Readouts: ROS (DCF-DA), p-JNK, p-p53, caspase-3, Annexin V.  

15. Small-Molecule PUMA Inhibitor Blocks p53/BAX Axis  
    • Hypothesis: Targeting downstream BH3-only PUMA interrupts p53-mediated mitochondrial apoptosis.  
    • Assay: Pre-treat with a PUMA inhibitor (e.g. compound 16) before ER stress.  
    • Readouts: PUMA expression, BAX activation (conformation‐specific IF), caspase-3/7, cell survival.
