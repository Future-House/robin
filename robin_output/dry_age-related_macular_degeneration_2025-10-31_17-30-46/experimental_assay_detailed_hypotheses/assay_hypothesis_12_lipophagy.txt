Assay Overview  
The proposed lipophagy‐enhancement assay models a key feature of dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD): the impaired clearance of lipid deposits in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells. Primary or iPSC‐derived human RPE (or ARPE-19) are first loaded with neutral lipids to mimic sub‐RPE lipid accumulation seen in drusen, using oxidized low‐density lipoprotein (oxLDL) or photo-oxidized photoreceptor outer segments. Cells are then treated with rapamycin analogs (rapalogs) or other mTOR inhibitors to induce autophagy. Lipid droplet clearance is quantified by staining with BODIPY 493/503 and measuring fluorescence by high-content imaging or flow cytometry at baseline and after treatment. Concurrently, autophagic flux is assessed by LC3 conversion assays: immunoblotting for LC3-I to LC3-II ratio in the presence and absence of lysosomal inhibitors (e.g., bafilomycin A1) and monitoring p62/SQSTM1 degradation. This dual readout provides a functional measure of lipophagy-mediated lipid removal and general autophagic activity in RPE under disease-relevant conditions.  

Biomedical Evidence  
Lipid accumulation in RPE and Bruch’s membrane is a hallmark of early dry AMD, with drusen containing neutral lipids, cholesterol esters and bisretinoid fluorophores. Pathological studies (Curcio et al., 2005) link excessive lipid deposition to RPE dysfunction and photoreceptor loss. Autophagy declines with age and in AMD patients: Ferrington et al. (2016) reported reduced autophagic flux and lysosomal capacity in AMD donor RPE, correlating with increased lipofuscin and lipid inclusions. Kaushik & Cuervo (2015) describe “lipophagy,” a selective autophagy pathway crucial for mobilizing lipid droplets in non-ocular cells; dysfunction of this pathway in RPE likely contributes to drusen biogenesis. Clinically, polymorphisms in autophagy-related genes (e.g., ATG5, MTOR) have been associated with AMD risk. Animal models in which autophagy is genetically enhanced (ATG7 overexpression) show reduced lipofuscin and drusen-like deposits, supporting lipophagy as a therapeutic target.  

Previous Use  
While lipophagy assays are well established in hepatocytes and macrophages for atherosclerosis research, their application in RPE for AMD is emerging but not yet routine. Ferrington’s group has applied general autophagy flux assays in ARPE-19 to evaluate proteostasis modulators. Mitter et al. (2014) used LC3 assays to study oxidative stress in RPE, but without specific lipid clearance readouts. To date, no high‐throughput screens for lipophagy enhancers in RPE have been published, though rapamycin’s capacity to reduce drusen in animal models (Zhang et al., 2019) provides proof-of-concept for this approach. The combination of BODIPY droplet quantification with validated autophagy flux markers positions this assay as a novel platform for early‐stage drug discovery in dry AMD.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths:  
• Directly models a central pathogenic event in dry AMD—lipid droplet accumulation in RPE—and links it to autophagic clearance.  
• Employs quantitative, multiplexed readouts (BODIPY imaging plus LC3 flux) adaptable to medium‐throughput screening.  
• Leverages well-validated reagents (BODIPY, rapalogs, LC3 antibodies) and orthogonal measurements to reduce false positives.  

Weaknesses:  
• RPE cell lines (e.g., ARPE-19) and acute lipid loading may not fully recapitulate the chronic, multifactorial environment of AMD in vivo (complement activation, ECM changes).  
• Rapalogs affect multiple cellular pathways beyond autophagy; hits will require rigorous secondary assays to confirm lipophagy specificity.  
• Autophagy flux assays can be confounded by lysosomal pH alterations or off-target effects of lysosomal inhibitors.  

In summary, the lipophagy enhancement assay provides a disease‐mechanism–driven, functional platform for identifying compounds that restore lipid homeostasis in RPE—an appealing strategy for dry AMD drug discovery—while recognizing the need for complementary models to capture the full complexity of the disease.
