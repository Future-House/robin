Query: What are the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying dry age-related macular degeneration, including RPE oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, complement activation, drusen biogenesis, lipofuscin accumulation, autophagy dysregulation, inflammasome activation, and associated gene expression and proteomic alterations in human RPE cells?

Dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) is driven by an intricate network of molecular and cellular alterations in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells. Chronic oxidative stress arises from increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) predominantly due to mitochondrial dysfunction, which leads to mitochondrial DNA damage and compromised ATP production, further exacerbating ROS generation (bilbaomalave2021mitochondrialdysfunctionand pages 2-4, markitantova2025retinalpigmentepithelium pages 2-4). This oxidative burden disrupts cellular homeostasis and drives the accumulation of oxidized proteins and lipids, significantly contributing to lipofuscin build‐up and drusen biogenesis at the RPE–Bruch’s membrane interface (chen2023oxidativestressin pages 1-2, basyal2024antioxidantsandmechanistic pages 2-4).

Inadequate clearance of damaged cellular components due to dysregulated autophagy and an inefficient ubiquitin-proteasome system further fuels oxidative stress, resulting in the accumulation of misfolded proteins and compromised RPE function (blasiak2019interplaybetweenautophagy pages 1-4, blasiak2019interplaybetweenautophagy pages 9-10). Concomitantly, complement activation—often linked to specific genetic variations such as those affecting CFH and other complement factors—triggers inflammatory cascades, thereby amplifying RPE cell injury (OpenTargets Search: dry age-related macular degeneration, bilbaomalave2021mitochondrialdysfunctionand pages 1-2). Inflammasome activation and the senescence-associated secretory phenotype contribute additional pro-inflammatory signals that exacerbate tissue degeneration (kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 3-5, wang2023potentialregulatoryroles pages 1-3). These processes pave the way for marked alterations in gene expression and proteomic profiles in human RPE cells, underscoring the multifactorial pathological landscape of dry AMD (pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 9-11, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 1-2).

Dry AMD results from a convergence of oxidative stress, mitochondrial impairment, autophagy failure, and inflammatory complement activation within RPE cells. These interlinked alterations drive drusen formation, lipofuscin accumulation, and proteomic changes that contribute to progressive vision loss.
