Query: What in vitro cell culture systems are most physiologically relevant for modeling dry AMD pathogenesis in the laboratory, such as primary human RPE, ARPE-19, hiPSC-derived RPE, co-culture with photoreceptors, 3D organoids or decellularized Bruch’s membrane scaffolds, and what are their advantages or limitations for recapitulating disease phenotypes?

In vitro AMD models utilize several cell culture systems that vary in their physiological relevance and complexity. Primary human RPE cells recapitulate native gene expression and functional characteristics, but their use is limited by donor variability and finite availability (molins2024biomaterialengineeringstrategies pages 4-5, rizzolo2022retinalcelltransplantation pages 10-11). ARPE-19 cells, although straightforward to culture, fail to fully mimic native RPE properties and lack the intricate cellular interactions present in vivo (molins2024biomaterialengineeringstrategies pages 4-5, rizzolo2022retinalcelltransplantation pages 7-8). hiPSC-derived RPE offer improved physiological accuracy by more closely matching the native transcriptomic profile and functionality; however, they require rigorous differentiation protocols and quality control to maintain long-term stability (rizzolo2022retinalcelltransplantation pages 7-8, rizzolo2022retinalcelltransplantation pages 10-11). Co-culture systems incorporating photoreceptors enhance cellular maturation and recapitulate critical cell–cell interactions such as those governing drusen formation and complement activation, key features of dry AMD (ghareeb2020coculturetechniquesfor pages 2-5, wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 1-2). Advanced three-dimensional models, including retinal organoids and decellularized Bruch’s membrane scaffolds, recreate the native microarchitecture and barrier properties, thereby better modeling phenotypes such as RPE atrophy and deposit accumulation; yet these models face challenges related to reproducibility, scaffold biocompatibility, and optimal cell adhesion (song2023bioprinted3douter pages 19-23, wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 4-6, molins2024biomaterialengineeringstrategies pages 8-9, wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 7-9).

In vitro models combining hiPSC-derived RPE, co-cultures with photoreceptors, and 3D organoids or decellularized scaffolds provide the most physiologically relevant platforms for studying dry AMD pathogenesis. These systems balance enhanced biological fidelity with practical limitations, offering valuable insight into key disease mechanisms while highlighting areas for further development.
