Assay Overview: The proposed mitochondrial function rescue assay uses hiPSC-derived retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells challenged with an acute oxidative insult (e.g., H2O2 or t-butyl hydroperoxide) to simulate the cellular stress encountered in dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD). Candidate drugs are co‐administered to assess their ability to restore mitochondrial parameters. Readouts are obtained via Seahorse Cell Mito Stress Test to quantify basal and maximal oxygen consumption rates, spare respiratory capacity, and ATP production, while high-content fluorescent ROS probes (such as CellROX) provide a direct measure of reactive oxygen species levels (cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 9-11, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6).

Biomedical Evidence: Mitochondrial dysfunction and enhanced oxidative stress are central to AMD pathology, as compromised bioenergetic function in RPE has been correlated with disease progression and clinical phenotypes (ferrington2017alteredbioenergeticsand pages 7-7, cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 1-2). Both biomedical and clinical data consistently demonstrate that improving mitochondrial health can mitigate the oxidative damage responsible for RPE degeneration, establishing this pathway as a critical therapeutic target (ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 14-15, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 2-4).

Previous Use: Prior studies have successfully employed similar assays in hiPSC-RPE models, demonstrating that compounds like ciclopirox olamine can significantly improve mitochondrial metrics and cell survival under oxidative stress (cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 9-11, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6). These investigations have provided key insights into mitochondrial rescue mechanisms and supported the assay’s utility in early-stage drug discovery (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 9-11, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: This assay is a robust, high-throughput method that directly links mitochondrial performance with RPE viability and oxidative stress responses, aligning well with AMD pathogenesis. Its strengths lie in the integration of sensitive metabolic and ROS measurements and the use of patient-specific hiPSC-RPE cells, which enhance clinical relevance (fisher2022humanipscand pages 11-12, fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4). However, limitations include the potential mismatches between acute oxidative stress conditions and the chronic pathology seen in AMD, as well as variability associated with hiPSC differentiation protocols (ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 5-7, ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 14-15).

This assay provides a mechanistically relevant, high-throughput platform to evaluate therapeutic candidates for dry AMD by directly assessing mitochondrial respiration and oxidative stress. Its application to patient-specific hiPSC-RPE models paves the way for precision medicine approaches in AMD treatment.
