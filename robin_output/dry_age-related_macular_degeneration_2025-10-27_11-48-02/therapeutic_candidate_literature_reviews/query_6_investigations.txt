Query: investigations of how chronic complement activation, inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1β), and drusen components modulate autophagy and lysosomal function in RPE cells, and how these interactions drive lipofuscin accumulation and RPE cell death in dry AMD pathophysiology

The pathophysiology of dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) involves a complex interplay between chronic complement activation, inflammatory cytokines, and drusen components that modulate autophagy and lysosomal function in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells. Chronic complement activation, notably through components such as C3 and C5, promotes a sustained inflammatory milieu that directly hinders lysosomal acidification and function (boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 3-4, chu2024therelationshipbetween pages 4-5). Elevated levels of pro‐inflammatory cytokines, including TNF-α and IL-1β, further exacerbate cellular stress by impairing the efficacy of autophagic clearance mechanisms, thereby allowing the accumulation of undegraded substrates (chu2024therelationshipbetween pages 4-5, pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 9-11).

Drusen components contribute to the dysregulation of these pathways by activating complement cascades and inflammatory responses that disrupt cellular homeostasis (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 10-11, chu2024therelationshipbetween pages 3-4). As autophagy and lysosomal degradation become compromised, toxic byproducts, such as oxidized lipids and misfolded proteins, accumulate to form lipofuscin granules (boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 3-4, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 2-4, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 14-15). This lipofuscin buildup not only serves as a marker of RPE cellular aging but also exacerbates oxidative stress and chronic inflammation, creating a self‐reinforcing cycle that culminates in RPE cell death (pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 9-11, pinelli2020areappraisalof pages 4-6, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 12-14).

Chronic complement activation, inflammatory cytokines, and drusen components jointly impair autophagy and lysosomal function, leading to the accumulation of lipofuscin and ultimately driving RPE cell death in dry AMD (chu2024therelationshipbetween pages 4-5, wang2023potentialregulatoryroles pages 1-3). Targeting these interconnected pathways offers a promising strategy for mitigating disease progression.
