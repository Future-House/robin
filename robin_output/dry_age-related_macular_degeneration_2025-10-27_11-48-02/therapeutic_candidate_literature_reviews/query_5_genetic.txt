Query: genetic and functional studies linking dysregulation of autophagy‐related genes and pathways (e.g., ATG5, Beclin1, ULK1, TFEB, mTORC1/2) to dry AMD susceptibility and progression, including GWAS data, animal knockout/knock‐in models, and patient‐derived RPE cell phenotypes

Dysregulation of autophagy‐related pathways is tightly linked to the pathogenesis of dry AMD through both genetic and functional mechanisms. Reduced autophagic activity in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) is one of the earliest events observed in dry AMD, characterized by increased lipid and protein deposits as well as lipofuscin accumulation leading to oxidative stress (liton2023autophagyinthe pages 23-24). Animal models, including Ambra1+/gt mice, have demonstrated that impaired autophagy in the RPE heightens sensitivity to AMD-related stressors, and targeted depletion of autophagy genes such as ATG5 in murine RPE cells produces AMD-like phenotypes, such as RPE thickening and pigmentary abnormalities (liton2023autophagyinthe pages 23-24, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3).

Functional studies in patient-derived RPE cells further underscore the impact of autophagic dysregulation; inhibition of autophagy results in enhanced lipofuscin accumulation and reactive oxygen species production (liton2023autophagyinthe pages 23-24). Concurrently, dysregulated mTOR signaling, particularly hyperactive mTORC1, has been consistently observed in AMD RPE cells, leading to impaired autophagic flux and metabolic dysfunction (OpenTargets Search: age-related macular degeneration-ATG5,BECN1,ULK1,TFEB,MTOR). Although current GWAS data do not provide definitive direct links between mutations in core autophagy genes (ATG5, Beclin1, ULK1, TFEB) and dry AMD susceptibility, they suggest that polymorphisms in these pathways may contribute to the condition’s progression (nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 9-11, pinelli2025retinalautophagyfor pages 15-16).

Dry AMD is marked by defective autophagy and mTOR pathway dysregulation leading to oxidative damage and RPE deterioration. Therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring autophagic balance and modulating mTOR activity hold promise in mitigating disease progression.
