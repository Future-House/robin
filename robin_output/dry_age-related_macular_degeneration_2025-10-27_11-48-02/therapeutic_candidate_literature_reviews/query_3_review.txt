Query: review repurposing efforts and preclinical/clinical trials of compounds targeting key autophagy regulators (e.g., mTOR inhibitors, AMPK activators, TFEB agonists) for dry AMD, focusing on pharmacokinetics, ocular bioavailability, safety data, and impact on RPE autophagy and lipofuscin clearance endpoints

Repurposing efforts targeting autophagy regulators for dry AMD have focused on modulating the mTOR/TFEB axis and activating AMPK to restore lysosomal function in RPE cells. Preclinical studies with mTOR inhibitors such as rapamycin have demonstrated enhanced TFEB activation, which drives lysosomal biogenesis and improves autophagy, thereby facilitating the clearance of lipofuscin-like granules accumulated due to impaired photoreceptor outer segment digestion (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7). Several investigations report that rapamycin exhibits a favorable ocular pharmacokinetic profile, and innovative delivery systems such as synthetic high-density lipoprotein nanoparticles are being explored to optimize its bioavailability while minimizing systemic toxicity (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7). In addition, overexpression of unphosphorylated TFEB in RPE cells has been shown to reduce lipofuscin accumulation, highlighting the therapeutic potential of TFEB agonists in enhancing autophagic flux (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 6-10). Although data on AMPK activators are less extensive, initial reports—exemplified by studies on metformin—suggest these agents might improve cellular redox balance and lysosomal degradation via AMPK/Nrf2 signaling (wu2022recentdevelopmentsof pages 6-7, wang2022mtorsignallingpathway pages 9-10). Clinical trials focusing on rapamycin report a satisfactory safety profile and encouraging, albeit preliminary, evidence of efficacy; however, further large-scale evaluations are needed to assess long-term impacts on RPE autophagy, lipofuscin clearance, and overall ocular bioavailability (wang2022mtorsignallingpathway pages 13-14, Clinical Trial Search: 638d0316e4af).

Rapamycin-based interventions have shown promise in modulating the mTOR/TFEB pathway and reducing toxic lipofuscin deposits in RPE cells. Further rigorous clinical investigations will be critical to validate the efficacy and safety of these repurposed compounds in dry AMD management.
