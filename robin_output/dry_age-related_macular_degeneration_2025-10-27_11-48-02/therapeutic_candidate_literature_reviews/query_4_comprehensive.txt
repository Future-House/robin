Query: comprehensive analysis of molecular mechanisms underlying lipofuscin accumulation in RPE cells during aging and dry AMD, including the role of bisretinoid byproducts (A2E), impaired autophagy–lysosomal degradation pathways, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and their contributions to RPE degeneration

Lipofuscin accumulation in RPE cells is governed by a multifactorial network of mechanisms that converge during aging and in dry AMD. Age‐related decline in lysosomal degradation, exacerbated by environmental stressors, impairs autophagy and leads to the persistent buildup of undigested cellular components (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 2-4, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 1-2). A central contributor is the formation of bisretinoid byproducts such as A2E, which originates from the incomplete processing of photoreceptor outer segments; A2E disrupts lysosomal enzyme function, notably by inhibiting V-ATPase activity and elevating the lysosomal pH, thereby further compromising degradative capacity (wang2023potentialregulatoryroles pages 11-13, rozanowska2023lipofuscinitsorigin pages 10-11, rozanowska2023lipofuscinitsorigin pages 8-10).  

Chronic oxidative stress, generated through photooxidative reactions and lipid peroxidation, exacerbates RPE cell damage by producing reactive oxygen species that impair lysosomal function and promote further oxidation of lipids and proteins (nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 5-7, rozanowska2023lipofuscinitsorigin pages 7-8, nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 9-11). In parallel, mitochondrial dysfunction—induced by oxidative injury—reduces ATP production required for effective autophagy, creating a vicious cycle that accelerates the accumulation of toxic aggregates (rozanowska2023lipofuscinitsorigin pages 4-5, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 3-4). The interplay among impaired autophagy–lysosomal degradation, bisretinoid accumulation, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial deficits ultimately drives RPE degeneration and contributes to the progression of dry AMD (nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 16-18, nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 3-5, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 4-5).

Lipofuscin build-up in RPE cells results from interconnected failures in degradation, oxidative balance, and mitochondrial function. Addressing these interrelated pathways is critical for the development of effective therapeutic strategies to mitigate RPE degeneration in dry AMD.
