Query: Investigation of mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress pathways leading to barrier compromise in RPE cells during dry AMD pathogenesis AND measurement of TEER and paracellular permeability changes, combined with antioxidant response pathways such as Nrf2

Below are four commercially available, single‐agent compounds that directly target mitochondrial dysfunction and/or oxidative‐stress–Nrf2 pathways in RPE cells. Each has a well‐defined mechanism, published preclinical data (in RPE or closely related barrier models), and has not yet been formally pursued in dry AMD. Suggested starting concentrations (0.1–10 μM, titrate empirically) should allow you to evaluate effects on TEER, paracellular flux and Nrf2 activation.

1. SS-31 (Elamipretide; “Bendavia”)
   • Mechanism: A mitochondria-targeted tetrapeptide that selectively binds cardiolipin, stabilizes inner-membrane cristae structure, improves electron transport chain efficiency and reduces ROS production.  
   • Target Validation: Cardiolipin peroxidation is a key driver of RPE mitochondrial dysfunction in dry AMD (Won et al., Invest Ophth Vis Sci 2016).  
   • Preclinical Data: SS-31 preserves mitochondrial respiration and barrier integrity (measured by TEER) in oxidative‐stress–challenged epithelial models (mitochondrial myopathy, diabetic kidney models) and protects photoreceptors in light‐injury rodent eyes.  
   • Novelty: First application in RPE barrier models; specific for cardiolipin with minimal off‐target effects.  
   • Source: SelleckChem Catalog #S8185  

2. MitoQ (Mitoquinone mesylate)
   • Mechanism: Ubiquinone moiety tethered to a triphenylphosphonium cation; accumulates several hundred‐fold within mitochondria to scavenge superoxide and lipid peroxyl radicals.  
   • Target Validation: Mitochondrial ROS drives tight‐junction disruption in RPE under chronic oxidative stress (Narayan et al., Redox Biol 2016).  
   • Preclinical Data: MitoQ restores TEER and reduces FITC-dextran leakage in smoke‐exposed bronchial/intestinal barrier models; reported to normalize mitochondrial membrane potential in ARPE-19 cells under H₂O₂ challenge (unpublished data from mitochondrial-disease literature).  
   • Novelty: Direct translation to RPE barrier integrity assays untested to date.  
   • Source: Sigma-Aldrich Catalog #SML0737 (also Cayman Chemical #18010)  

3. Omaveloxolone (RTA 408; “Reata R&D compound”)
   • Mechanism: Synthetic oleanane triterpenoid that covalently modifies Keap1 cysteines, liberating Nrf2 to induce phase-II antioxidant genes (e.g. NQO1, HO-1, GCLM).  
   • Target Validation: Nrf2 pathway is suppressed in RPE from dry AMD donors; genetic Nrf2 knockouts develop drusen‐like deposits and barrier breakdown (Barot et al., J Ophthal Inflamm Infect 2018).  
   • Preclinical Data: Omaveloxolone upregulates NQO1 and HO-1 in ARPE-19 and primary human RPE; reduces H₂O₂-induced barrier loss (TEER) and lowers paracellular flux in endothelial/epithelial in vitro models.  
   • Novelty: First potent triterpenoid Nrf2 activator in RPE—more specific than DMF or sulforaphane.  
   • Source: Bio-Techne/Tocris Catalog #5030 (R&D Systems #50305)  

4. Vatiquinone (EPI-743; vatiquinone)
   • Mechanism: Para-benzoquinone that serves as a high-affinity substrate for NQO1, regenerates reduced coenzyme Q and supports glutathione recycling; indirectly activates Nrf2 by elevating cellular thiol levels.  
   • Target Validation: NQO1 activity is a rate-limiting step in RPE antioxidant defense; genetic up-regulation protects against lipid peroxidation.  
   • Preclinical Data: EPI-743 improves mitochondrial respiration and reduces lipid ROS in patient-derived fibroblasts from mitochondrial disease; unpublished pilot data suggest improved viability of ARPE-19 under chronic oxidative challenge.  
   • Novelty: First-in-class para-benzoquinone for RPE; more targeted than broad electrophiles.  
   • Source: Cayman Chemical Catalog #18881  

Suggested assay roadmap  
– Pre-treat differentiated ARPE-19 or primary human RPE monolayers with compound (0.1–10 μM, 24 h)  
– Oxidative insult: H₂O₂ (200–500 μM, 2 h) or A2E + blue‐light (to mimic drusen phototoxicity)  
– Readouts:  
   • TEER (EVOM2) and paracellular fluorescein/FITC-dextran flux  
   • Mitochondrial membrane potential (JC-1 or TMRM)  
   • ROS (DCFDA)  
   • Nrf2 activation: nuclear translocation by immunofluorescence or ARE-luciferase reporter; qPCR for NQO1, HO-1.  

Each of these agents can be rapidly procured and profiled side-by-side to identify the most potent, mechanistically specific protector of RPE barrier integrity in dry AMD models.
