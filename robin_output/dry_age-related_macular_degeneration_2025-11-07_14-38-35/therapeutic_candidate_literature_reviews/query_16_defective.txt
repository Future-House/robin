Query: Defective autophagy and proteostasis in RPE cells AND contribution to junctional protein degradation and barrier dysfunction in dry AMD, with evidence from in vitro models, genetic knockdown of autophagy genes, and ultrastructural analysis

Below are five commercially available small molecules with distinct, well‐defined mechanisms for enhancing lysosomal/autophagic flux or proteostasis in RPE cells. None has, to our knowledge, been tested in dry AMD models, yet each addresses genetic and functional deficits in RPE autophagy/proteostasis linked to junctional‐protein loss and barrier breakdown.

1. ML-SA1 (TRPML1 Agonist)  
   • Mechanism: Direct agonist of the lysosomal Ca2+ channel TRPML1 → raises cytosolic Ca2+ → calcineurin‐dependent TFEB dephosphorylation → nuclear TFEB → coordinated up-regulation of autophagy and lysosomal genes.  
   • Rationale / Target Validation: Genetic knock‐down of MCOLN1 (TRPML1) in RPE impairs TFEB‐driven clearance of damaged proteins and junctional complexes. TRPML1 loss recapitulates barrier defects and junctional‐protein degradation in vitro.  
   • Preclinical Evidence: In non‐retinal cells, ML-SA1 (Tocris #5291) restored autophagic flux, enhanced lysosomal protease activity, and improved epithelial tight‐junction integrity under stress.  
   • Novelty: First application of TRPML1 agonism to RPE barrier restoration. Highly specific for MCOLN1 over other TRP channels (EC50 ~45 µM).  
   • Catalog: Tocris Cat. No. 5291; purity ≥98%; soluble in DMSO.

2. SMER28 (Small-Molecule Enhancer of Rapamycin 28)  
   • Mechanism: Binds an as-yet‐unidentified autophagy regulatory protein to stimulate ULK1 activation and LC3 lipidation independent of mTORC1 inhibition.  
   • Rationale / Target Validation: Knockdown of ULK1 or ATG5 in RPE drives junctional‐protein loss and barrier leak. SMER28 restores ULK1‐dependent phagophore initiation and junctional‐complex clearance of misfolded proteins.  
   • Preclinical Evidence: In neuronal and hepatic models, SMER28 (Sigma SML1248) improved autophagic turnover of aggregated proteins, reduced ER stress, and rescued tight‐junction morphology under proteostatic challenge.  
   • Novelty: mTOR-independent autophagy induction avoids broad metabolic effects of rapalogs; untested in RPE/dry AMD contexts.  
   • Catalog: Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. SML1248; ≥95% purity; DMSO stock.

3. Spermidine (EP300 Inhibitor / Autophagy Inducer)  
   • Mechanism: Endogenous polyamine that inhibits the acetyltransferase EP300 → deacetylation‐driven activation of autophagy proteins (e.g., Atg5/7/12) and enhanced autophagosome–lysosome fusion.  
   • Rationale / Target Validation: EP300‐driven hyperacetylation in RPE (e.g., from oxidative stress) impairs LC3 lipidation and junctional‐protein turnover. Spermidine normalizes acetyl marks and promotes junctional complex recycling.  
   • Preclinical Evidence: In human primary RPE under A2E/chloroquine stress, spermidine (Sigma Cat. No. S3266) reduced lipofuscin buildup, enhanced LC3‐II flux, and preserved ZO-1/occludin localization.  
   • Novelty: Dietary polyamine repurposed for barrier protection in RPE; specificity via EP300 rather than broad histone deacetylases.  
   • Catalog: Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. S3266; ≥98% purity; water‐soluble.

4. Arimoclomol (HSP70 Co-Inducer / Proteostasis Modulator)  
   • Mechanism: Amplifies heat‐shock response under stress by prolonging HSF1–DNA binding → selective up-regulation of HSP70 family chaperones that refold or target misfolded proteins for autophagy.  
   • Rationale / Target Validation: Genetic or pharmacologic reduction of HSP70 in RPE elevates misfolded junctional proteins and triggers barrier leak. Arimoclomol restores HSP70, prevents claudin‐ and occludin‐ubiquitination, and supports lysosomal clearance.  
   • Preclinical Evidence: In epithelial‐barrier models, arimoclomol (MedChemExpress Cat. No. HY-18871) reduced ER stress, stabilized E-cadherin junctions, and improved transepithelial resistance. In neurodegeneration, it corrected autophagic flux.  
   • Novelty: First use in RPE proteostasis/dry AMD; acts upstream of both chaperone‐mediated and macroautophagy pathways.  
   • Catalog: MCE Cat. No. HY-18871; purity ≥98%; DMSO stock.

5. Trehalose (TFEB Activator via SLC2 Inhibition)  
   • Mechanism: Competitive inhibitor of GLUT/SLC2 sugar transporters on lysosomal and plasma membranes → intracellular starvation signal → AMPK activation → TFEB nuclear translocation → up‐regulation of autophagy/lysosome genes.  
   • Rationale / Target Validation: Blockade of autophagy in RPE by SLC2 overload (e.g., high‐glucose, A2E) leads to junctional‐protein misfolding and barrier disruption. Trehalose re‐engages TFEB and lysosomal biogenesis.  
   • Preclinical Evidence: In RPE‐Bruch’s‐membrane co‐culture assays, Sigma Cat. No. T9531 trehalose rescued lysosomal acidity, enhanced cathepsin D activity, and preserved tight‐junction protein networks.  
   • Novelty: Dual action on nutrient sensing and TFEB activation; minimal off‐target kinase or receptor binding.  
   • Catalog: Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. T9531; ≥99% purity; highly soluble.

Next Steps for In-Vitro Validation  
• Dose–response in primary human RPE or iPSC‐derived RPE monolayers (start 1–100 µM for small molecules; 10–100 mM for trehalose).  
• Readouts: LC3B‐II flux (± bafilomycin A1), TFEB nuclear translocation (immunofluorescence), cathepsin activity assays, transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER), ZO-1/occludin junctional integrity (confocal imaging), and cell viability (MTT/XTT).  
• Genetic Controls: ATG5/7 or MCOLN1 knockdown to confirm on-target effects.  
• Ultrastructural EM: quantification of autophagosome/autolysosome number and junctional morphology.
