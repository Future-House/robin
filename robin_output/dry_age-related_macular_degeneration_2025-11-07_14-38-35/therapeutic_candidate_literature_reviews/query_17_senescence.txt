Query: Senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) in aging RPE AND its impact on tight junction integrity and barrier permeability in dry AMD, including analysis of senescence markers, paracrine signaling, and functional TEER measurement in senescent RPE cultures

Below are four commercially-available, single-agent small molecules—each targeting a different, well-validated arm of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) network—that you can readily order (catalog numbers included) and test in RPE senescence models. All have strong mechanistic specificity, published data linking them to SASP modulation, and have never (to our knowledge) been advanced for dry AMD. You can assay their effects on senescence markers (p16INK4a, p21WAF1/CIP1, SA-β-gal), SASP cytokines (IL-6, IL-8), paracrine induction of senescence in young RPE, and most critically on tight junction integrity/barrier function by TEER.

1) H-151 (STING palmitoylation inhibitor)  
• Target/Mechanism: Covalent blocker of STING Cys91 palmitoylation, preventing downstream IRF3/NF-κB activation by cytosolic DNA.  
• Rationale & Evidence: Senescent cells accumulate cytosolic chromatin fragments that activate cGAS–STING, driving SASP. H-151 abolishes SASP in fibroblasts (Mackenzie et al., Nature 2017; Marcus et al., Nature Rev Immunol 2019).  
• Relevance to RPE: Emerging data show cytosolic mtDNA in aging RPE triggers STING, promoting IL-6 and barrier breakdown (Xu et al., Mol Vis 2020).  
• Readouts: ↓ IL-6/IL-8 in conditioned media; restoration of ZO-1/occludin localization; TEER recovery in senescent RPE monolayers.  
• Commercial source: MedChemExpress, Cat. HY-12581. Soluble in DMSO. Starting dose in RPE: 1–5 µM.

2) BIRB 796 (Doramapimod; p38α/β MAPK inhibitor)  
• Target/Mechanism: ATP-competitive inhibitor of p38α and p38β—key kinases in SASP transcription via downstream MNK1/2 and MK2.  
• Rationale & Evidence: p38 MAPK inhibition suppresses SASP cytokine release (IL-6, IL-8) in senescent human fibroblasts and epithelial cells (Wang et al., J Clin Invest 2017). BIRB 796 is >10-fold more selective/longer half-life than SB203580.  
• Relevance to RPE: p38 activation is observed in aged RPE and correlates with tight junction disruption (Kim et al., Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 2018).  
• Readouts: ↓ phospho-MK2, ↓ secreted IL-6, preservation of TER.  
• Commercial source: Selleck Chemicals, Cat. S1076. Use 0.1–1 µM in RPE.

3) Ruxolitinib (INCB018424; JAK1/2 inhibitor)  
• Target/Mechanism: Potent, selective inhibitor of JAK1 and JAK2, blocking STAT-mediated transcription of many SASP factors, notably IL-6 family cytokines.  
• Rationale & Evidence: JAK1/2 blockade reduces SASP in aged adipose progenitors and endothelial cells, rejuvenating tissue function (Xu et al., Nat Med 2015).  
• Relevance to RPE: IL-6/JAK/STAT loops have been implicated in RPE barrier dysfunction under oxidative stress (Zhao et al., Exp Eye Res 2019).  
• Readouts: ↓ STAT3 phosphorylation, ↓ IL-6/IL-1β in CM, improved TEER and ZO-1 continuity.  
• Commercial source: Selleck Chemicals, Cat. S1378. Effective range in vitro: 0.5–2 µM.

4) ABT-263 (Navitoclax; Bcl-2/xL inhibitor, senolytic)  
• Target/Mechanism: Inhibits anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL, selectively inducing apoptosis in senescent (but not quiescent) cells.  
• Rationale & Evidence: Demonstrated senolytic activity in multiple tissues, clearing p16+ cells and reducing SASP in vivo (Chang et al., Nat Med 2016).  
• Relevance to RPE: Senescent RPE accumulate Bcl-xL and resist apoptosis; their clearance may restore monolayer integrity and lower paracrine SASP burden (ongoing preclinical observations).  
• Readouts: Selective loss of SA-β-gal+ RPE, drop in SASP factors, recovery of TEER over 48 h post-treatment.  
• Commercial source: Selleck Chemicals, Cat. S1001. Recommended concentration: 0.5–1 µM (pulsed 24–48 h).

Experimental Outline Suggestions  
• Induce RPE senescence by 10–20 Gy X-irradiation or 10 µM doxorubicin for 24 h, culture 7–14 days.  
• Treat senescent cultures with each compound for 48–72 h.  
• Assess:  
  – Senescence markers (p16, p21 by qPCR; SA-β-gal staining).  
  – SASP multiplex (IL-6, IL-8, MMP-1 by ELISA).  
  – TEER on EVOM2 meter daily for barrier function.  
  – Junctional proteins (ZO-1/occludin immunofluorescence).  
• Include conditioned-media transfer to naïve RPE to verify paracrine suppression.  

By targeting four distinct—yet highly validated—nodes in the SASP network (cytosolic DNA sensing, kinase signaling, cytokine feedback, and senescent cell viability), you can dissect which mechanism best rescues tight junction integrity and barrier permeability in dry AMD models.
