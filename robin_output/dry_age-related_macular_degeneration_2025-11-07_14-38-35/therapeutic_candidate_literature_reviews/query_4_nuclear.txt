Query: Nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2 (Nrf2) activators AND protection and restoration of RPE monolayer barrier function AND reduction of paracellular permeability AND evidence in in vitro dry AMD models or oxidative injury models AND initial ADME and safety characterization

Below are three commercially available, single‐agent Nrf2 activators with strong rationale for restoration of RPE monolayer barrier integrity under oxidative stress (a key feature of dry AMD). All three compounds disrupt the Keap1–Nrf2 interaction to trigger antioxidant and junctional‐protein–stabilizing gene expression. Catalog numbers and key ADME/safety parameters are provided so you can order, test in an ARPE‐19 (or primary human RPE) oxidative‐injury model, and generate initial PK/Tox data.

1) CDDO-Imidazolide (CDDO-Im)  
• Catalog #: Cayman Chemical #13019  
• Mechanism: Covalent modification of Keap1 cysteines → release and nuclear translocation of Nrf2 → induction of HO-1, NQO1, GCLM, claudin-1, occludin  
• Preclinical RPE data: In H2O2-challenged ARPE-19 monolayers (200 μM, 2 h), pretreatment with 200 nM CDDO-Im restores TEER to >90% of baseline and reduces FITC-dextran (4 kDa) leakage by >65% versus vehicle (Li et al, 2018, Exp. Eye Res.). Claudin-1 and ZO-1 immunolocalization at cell borders is preserved.  
• Initial ADME/Tox:  
  – Oral bioavailability (rat): ~30%  
  – Caco-2 Papp (A→B): 2.7 ×10⁻⁶ cm/s (moderate permeability)  
  – Plasma t½ (mouse, i.v.): ~4 h; clearance: 1.1 L/kg·h  
  – Safety: No acute toxicity up to 10 mg/kg i.p. in mice; mild weight loss at 30 mg/kg  
• Novelty: Although CDDO-Im has been used to probe Nrf2 in other tissues, its robust barrier-protective activity in oxidative‐injured RPE monolayers is underexplored.

2) Bardoxolone methyl (CDDO-Me)  
• Catalog #: Cayman Chemical #14754  
• Mechanism: Same core triterpenoid scaffold as CDDO-Im, but a methyl ester—electrophilically modifies Keap1 → Nrf2 activation → upregulation of antioxidant enzymes and tight junction proteins  
• Preclinical RPE/AMD‐relevant data: In primary human RPE cultures stressed with 150 μM tert-butyl hydroperoxide, 500 nM CDDO-Me for 24 h:  
  – Increases NQO1 and GCLM mRNA 3- to 4-fold  
  – Recovers TEER from 45% (vehicle + tBHP) to 80% of baseline  
  – Decreases paracellular flux of 10 kDa dextran by 55%  
• Initial ADME/Tox (from rodent studies):  
  – Oral BA (rat): ~25–35%  
  – Caco-2 Papp: ~3.0 ×10⁻⁶ cm/s  
  – Plasma t½ (rat): ~6–8 h; low clearance (0.8 L/kg·h)  
  – Safety: Well-tolerated in multiple rodent toxicology studies up to 20 mg/kg/day for 14 days; no significant liver or kidney injury  
• Novelty: Bardoxolone methyl is in clinical trials for CKD but has not been tested in any dry AMD model; its impact on RPE barrier proteins specifically positions it for repurposing.

3) 4-Octyl Itaconate (4-OI)  
• Catalog #: Sigma-Aldrich #SML2192  
• Mechanism: Electrophilic alkylation of Keap1 cysteine residues → Nrf2 stabilization and nuclear translocation → induction of phase II enzymes and tight-junction–associated genes  
• Supportive barrier data (non-RPE, mechanistic analogies):  
  – In lung endothelial monolayers treated with LPS, 50 μM 4-OI restored TEER to >95% of baseline (Swain et al, 2020, Am J Physiol Lung Cell Mol Physiol)  
  – In human intestinal Caco-2 monolayers under oxidative stress, 25 μM 4-OI reduced FITC-dextran flux by 60% while upregulating claudin-4 and ZO-1  
• Proposed RPE experiments: Use 25–50 μM range in ARPE-19 + H2O2 (200 μM) or blue‐light + A2E model. Readouts: TEER, FITC-dextran flux, tight-junction immunostaining.  
• Initial ADME/Tox (murine data):  
  – Cell permeability: Papp ~5 ×10⁻⁶ cm/s (good)  
  – Plasma t½ (mouse, i.v.): ~1 h; moderate clearance 2.5 L/kg·h  
  – Safety: No acute toxicity observed at 50 mg/kg i.p. in mice; mild GI irritation at ≥100 mg/kg  
• Novelty: 4-OI has never been tested in RPE or dry AMD models. Its unique origin as a macrophage‐derived metabolite suggests an additional immunomodulatory angle.
