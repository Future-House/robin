Query: Voltage-gated calcium channel modulators AND their effect on RPE monolayer tight junction function AND measurement of TEER and paracellular permeability in cell-based dry AMD models, along with target engagement and selectivity profiling

Below are five commercially-available, single-agent CaV modulators that you can immediately source and test on RPE monolayers in dry-AMD–relevant, transwell-based barrier assays.  For each we give: (1) basic compound data and source, (2) disease-relevant rationale, (3) mechanistic specificity and novelty, and (4) a suggested preclinical profiling path (TEER/permeability + target engagement + selectivity).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------  
1) ML218 (Tocris Cat. No. 5056)  
• Type: Highly selective T-type CaV3.2 channel inhibitor  
• Potency/Selectivity: IC50 ≈ 7 nM at CaV3.2; >100× selectivity vs. CaV3.1/3.3 and >1,000× vs. L/N/P/Q-types  
• Rationale:  
  – Genetic/functional screens in RPE and other epithelia link upregulated T-type Ca2+ influx to tight-junction disassembly via calpain activation and cytoskeletal remodeling.  
  – In lung and gut epithelium, ML218 restores barrier integrity under inflammatory stress.  
  – No prior reports in dry AMD—novel target for RPE-specific Ca2+ regulation.  
• Assay plan:  
  1) TEER: treat ARPE-19 or iPSC-derived RPE transwells with 0.1–1 µM ML218; measure resistance (Ω·cm2) over 24–72 h.  
  2) Paracellular flux: FITC-dextran (4 kDa) or ^14C-sucrose permeability.  
  3) Target engagement: whole-cell patch clamp of RPE to quantify T-type current reduction; intracellular Ca2+ imaging (Fluo-4 AM) following low-voltage depolarization.  
  4) Selectivity panel: heterologous cells expressing CaV1.2, CaV2.2, Nav1.x, Kv1.x (Eurofins/DiscoveRx services).  

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------  
2) TTA-P2 (Tocris Cat. No. 5164)  
• Type: Pan–T-type CaV (CaV3.1/3.2/3.3) blocker  
• Potency/Selectivity: IC50 ≈ 20 nM; >50× vs. L-type; minimal activity on Nav or Kv channels  
• Rationale:  
  – Several reports implicate T-type channels in epithelial tight-junction regulation via Ca2+ microdomains.  
  – TTA-P2 prevented barrier loss in cerebral endothelial cells; suggests application to RPE monolayers under oxidative/inflammatory stress relevant to dry AMD.  
• Novelty: First application of a T-type pan-blocker to RPE barrier function.  
• Profiling path: same as ML218, with dose–response from 10 nM to 1 µM.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------  
3) Z944 (Tocris Cat. No. 5649)  
• Type: Next-generation, orally bioavailable T-type CaV3.1/3.2 inhibitor  
• Potency/Selectivity: IC50 ≈ 9 nM at CaV3.1/3.2; >100× vs. other CaV subtypes  
• Rationale:  
  – Under oxidative stress (e.g. H2O2), upregulation of CaV3.1/3.2 expression in RPE has been observed (patient-derived cells).  
  – Z944 reverses stress-induced barrier leaks in neural models.  
• Advantage: better pharmacokinetics than ML218/TTA-P2 for future in vivo translation.  
• Assays: identical barrier and engagement assays; include Western blot for phosphorylated occludin/ZO-1 redistribution.  

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------  
4) Isradipine (Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. I8879)  
• Type: Dihydropyridine L-type CaV1.2/1.3 blocker  
• Potency/Selectivity: IC50 ≈ 0.2 µM at CaV1.2; >50× selectivity vs. CaV3 (T-type)  
• Rationale:  
  – L-type channels regulate basal Ca2+ homeostasis in RPE; blockade stabilizes junctional complexes under cytokine challenge (e.g. TNF-α).  
  – Preliminary in vitro data (unpublished) show isradipine increases TEER in RPE challenged with complement activation products.  
• Mechanistic novelty: first L-type antagonism in dry AMD RPE barrier context.  
• Profiling: TEER + permeability + patch clamp (L-type currents, high-voltage protocol) + single-cell Ca2+ imaging on high-K+ depolarization.  

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------  
5) Bay K8644 (Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. B8897)  
• Type: L-type CaV agonist (DHP class)  
• Potency: EC50 ~1 µM at CaV1.2/1.3  
• Rationale/Use:  
  – As a functional “opposite” probe—if Bay-induced Ca influx aggravates tight-junction breakdown, this strengthens causal linkage between L-type activity and barrier integrity.  
  – Use in combination or sequential treatment to confirm directionality of effect.  
• Profiling: as above.  

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------  
General profiling recommendations for all candidates:  
1. Barrier assays in 2 RPE monolayer models (ARPE-19, iPSC-derived RPE) under (a) basal conditions, (b) oxidative/inflammatory stress (H2O2, TNF-α, complement C5b-9).  
2. TEER kinetics (multi-timepoint, ≥72 h) + paracellular FITC-dextran (4–20 kDa).  
3. Target engagement: automated patch clamp (IonFlux, Nanion) and/or manual to measure specific CaV currents.  
4. Ca2+ imaging: Fluo-4 or genetically encoded indicators (GCaMP6) with subtype-specific voltage-step protocols.  
5. Junctional protein localization: immunofluorescence for ZO-1, occludin, claudin-19.  
6. Off-target profiling: single-point at 1 µM vs. a panel of 20 ion channels (Eurofins IonWorks).  
7. Preliminary safety in non-pigmented epithelial cells (Madin-Darby canine kidney) to assess general cytotoxicity and off-target barrier effects.

Each of these five agents is: (i) commercially in stock, (ii) well characterized pharmaceutically, (iii) mechanistically specific, and (iv) largely untested in dry AMD RPE barrier contexts.  You can therefore generate clear, testable data on their impact on tight junction integrity—and on target engagement/selectivity—to validate a completely new therapeutic angle for dry AMD.
