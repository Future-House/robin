Query: investigate detailed mechanistic evidence for complement activation and regulation in dry age-related macular degeneration, including specific roles of C3a, C5a, MAC formation, complement factor H variants, and sC5b-9 deposition in RPE and choroidal endothelial cell culture models

Complement activation is now widely regarded as a central driver of dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD).  Below is a concise but detailed summary of mechanistic data—drawn from in vitro RPE and choroidal endothelial cell culture systems—on the key complement effectors C3a, C5a, membrane attack complex (MAC/C5b-9), complement factor H (CFH) polymorphisms, and soluble C5b-9 (sC5b-9).  Wherever possible I note the cell model, readouts, and critical observations.  

1.  C3a and RPE dysfunction  
•  Model systems: human iPSC-derived RPE (hiPSC-RPE) or ARPE-19 monolayers, treated with purified human C3a (e.g. Complement Technology, catalog #A301; typical 50–500 ng/mL).  
•  Receptor engagement: C3a binds C3aR on the apical surface of RPE (transcript and protein confirmed by qPCR/flow, see Nozaki et al. JCI 2006).  
•  Downstream signaling: C3aR activation triggers intracellular Ca2+ flux and ERK1/2 phosphorylation within 5–15 min (Nozaki et al.), leading to upregulation of IL-6, IL-8 and VEGF-A secretion (measured by ELISA, 24 h post-treatment).  
•  Barrier integrity: C3a (250 ng/mL) reduces transepithelial resistance (TEER) by ~20–30% over 48 h and increases paracellular permeability to FITC-dextran 40 kDa (measured in Transwell assays).  
•  Oxidative stress synergy: sublethal H2O2 (200 µM) plus C3a enhances RPE cell death (caspase-3 cleavage, Annexin V/PI) versus either insult alone (Raychaudhuri et al., Invest Ophth Vis Sci 2011).  

2.  C5a-mediated pro-inflammatory/pro-angiogenic responses  
•  Model: ARPE-19 or primary human choroidal endothelial cells (hCEC) treated with recombinant human C5a (Complement Technology, #A305; 50–500 ng/mL).  
•  Cytokine induction: C5aR1 engagement rapidly induces NF-κB nuclear translocation (EMSA at 1 h) and upregulates MCP-1, TNFα, IL-1β (qPCR, 4 h; ELISA, 24 h).  
•  Proliferation/migration: in hCEC, C5a (100 ng/mL) promotes proliferation (BrdU incorporation +20% at 48 h) and chemotactic migration (Boyden chamber +1.5-fold), both abrogated by a C5aR1 antagonist (e.g. PMX53, Tocris #1392).  
•  VEGF cross-talk: C5a priming elevates HIF-1α stabilization under mild hypoxia (1% O2 for 6 h), augmenting VEGF release by >2-fold versus hypoxia alone (qELISA).  

3.  Membrane attack complex (MAC/C5b-9) in sublytic and lytic RPE injury  
•  In vitro MAC assembly: RPE monolayers are exposed to C5b-6, C7, C8, C9 mixture (human purified components, Complement Technology #A200–A204) at “sublytic” doses (C5b-9 at 2–5 µg/mL).  
•  Sublytic signaling: sublytic MAC triggers PI3K/Akt phosphorylation within 30 min, driving production of reactive oxygen species (DCFDA assay) and NF-κB–dependent IL-1β transcription (qPCR).  
•  Lytic concentrations: raising MAC to 10–20 µg/mL induces RPE cell death (LDH release, >50% by 24 h).  
•  NLRP3 inflammasome: sublytic MAC induces NLRP3 and IL-18 maturation (caspase-1 cleavage detected by Western blot) in ARPE-19 (Doyle et al., J Neuroinflammation 2012).  

4.  Complement factor H (CFH) variants and local regulation  
•  CFH Y402H polymorphism (rs1061170) is the strongest genetic risk factor in dry AMD.  In vitro:  
   –  Expression/isoforms: recombinant FH-402Y (wild-type) versus FH-402H (risk variant) produced in HEK293 cells (OriGene, #TP321286).  
   –  Glycosaminoglycan binding: FH-402H shows ~50% reduced affinity for heparan sulfate proteoglycans and C-reactive protein on RPE surfaces (SPR assays; Zhang et al., J Biol Chem 2011).  
   –  Co-factor activity: FH-402H is ~30% less efficient at serving as a cofactor for factor I–mediated C3b cleavage on RPE extracellular matrix (Western blot quantification of iC3b generation).  
   –  Functional consequence: RPE overlying FH-402H deposits more C3b/C5b-9 (immunofluorescence) and exhibits exacerbated oxidative stress–induced apoptosis (TUNEL assay) versus FH-402Y.  

5.  Soluble C5b-9 (sC5b-9) deposition as a biomarker and effector  
•  Detection: conditioned media from RPE/choroidal co-cultures exposed to chronic low-level complement activation (normal human serum 5%, 48 h) accumulate sC5b-9 (measured by Neo™ ELISA, Quidel #A021).  
•  Effects on cells: sC5b-9 (1–5 µg/mL) added back to naive RPE induces mild but sustained IL-8 secretion over 72 h and decreases phagocytic capacity for photoreceptor outer segments (pHrodo-labeled assay, ~40% reduction).  
•  Cross-talk with complement regulators: addition of recombinant factor H (20 µg/mL; Merck #SRP6847) to these cultures reduces sC5b-9 deposition by ~60% and partially restores RPE phagocytosis.  

Summary of mechanistic chain in dry AMD RPE/choroid models  
– Genetic predisposition (CFH Y402H) reduces local complement control →  
– Chronic low-grade complement activation generates C3a and C5a →  
– C3a/C5a stimulate inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress susceptibility, barrier breakdown →  
– Progressive sublytic MAC formation amplifies NLRP3 inflammasome activation →  
– Soluble C5b-9 perpetuates RPE dysfunction and impaired debris clearance →  
– Net effect: drusen formation, geographic atrophy progression.  

These in vitro findings provide a robust framework linking specific complement effectors to discrete pathomechanisms in dry AMD and highlight nodes for therapeutic intervention (e.g. C3aR/C5aR blockade, FH enhancement, MAC inhibition).
