Query: search for the key molecular pathways that mediate oxidative stress-induced retinal pigment epithelium dysfunction in dry age-related macular degeneration, including roles of mitochondrial ROS production, NRF2 signaling, lipid peroxidation, and antioxidant defense mechanisms in cultured RPE cells

Below is a concise overview of the principal molecular cascades by which oxidative stress drives RPE dysfunction in dry AMD, organized under the four headings you requested. Wherever possible, I’ve included key proteins, read-outs commonly used in cultured-RPE models (primarily ARPE-19 or primary human RPE), and cross-links between pathways.  

1. Mitochondrial ROS Production  
   • Electron‐transport chain (ETC) “leak” at Complex I and III  
     – Pathogenic drivers: age-related cardiolipin oxidation, mtDNA mutations, reduced cardiolipin synthase  
     – Readouts: MitoSOX Red for superoxide; JC-1 or TMRE for ΔΨm  
   • Mitochondrial dynamics/fission–fusion  
     – Elevated Drp1 phosphorylation → excessive fission → depolarization → more ROS  
     – Mitofusin-2 (Mfn2) downregulation impairs mitochondrial networking and mitophagy  
   • Mitophagy impairment  
     – PINK1/Parkin pathway is overwhelmed → accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria  
     – Lysosomal pH dysregulation worsens clearance  
   • Downstream effects  
     – Oxidized mtDNA released into cytosol → cGAS-STING–NLRP3 inflammasome activation → IL-1β/IL-18  
     – ATP depletion → AMPK activation (initially protective) but chronic activation triggers autophagic block  

2. NRF2-KEAP1 Antioxidant Signaling  
   • Basal state: NRF2 bound to KEAP1 in cytosol, targeted for proteasomal degradation  
   • Oxidative/electrophilic stress: modification of critical KEAP1 cysteines → NRF2 stabilization and nuclear translocation  
     – Upregulates ~200 genes via the Antioxidant Response Element (ARE)  
     – Key targets in RPE: HMOX1 (HO-1), NQO1, GCLC/GCLM (rate-limiting for GSH synthesis), SOD1/2, GPX2  
   • Regulatory cross-talk  
     – GSK-3β phosphorylates NRF2 (β-TrCP–mediated turnover)  
     – p62/SQSTM1 competes with NRF2 for KEAP1 binding → feed-forward amplification  
     – MicroRNAs (e.g. miR-144, miR-153) can blunt NRF2 expression in aged RPE  
   • Model readouts  
     – ARE-luciferase reporter assays, nuclear vs. cytosolic NRF2 immunoblots, glutathione (GSH/GSSG) ratios  

3. Lipid Peroxidation and Ferroptotic Signaling  
   • PUFA-rich Bruch’s membrane lipids and RPE photoreceptor outer segment processing are especially vulnerable  
     – Enzymatic peroxidation: 15-LOX, 12-LOX upregulation in stressed RPE  
     – Non-enzymatic: hydroxyl radical attack yields 4-HNE, malondialdehyde (MDA) adducts on proteins  
   • A2E (lipofuscin chromophore) + blue light → singlet oxygen → additional lipid radicals  
   • Ferroptosis hallmarks in RPE  
     – Downregulation or inactivation of GPX4 (glutathione peroxidase 4)  
     – Accumulation of Fe2+ via upregulated transferrin receptor CD71/CD98 or ferritinophagy (NCOA4)  
     – ACSL4-mediated enrichment of long‐chain PUFAs in membranes  
     – Rescue by lipophilic antioxidants (e.g. ferrostatin-1) or iron chelators (e.g. deferoxamine)  
   • Assays: BODIPY-C11 ratiometric lipid peroxidation; cell viability ± ferrostatin-1; western for GPX4, ACSL4  

4. Core Antioxidant Defense Enzymes & Redox Sensors  
   • Superoxide dismutases  
     – SOD1 (cytosolic), SOD2 (mitochondrial); reduced expression/activity with age in RPE  
   • Catalase (peroxisomal) and peroxiredoxins (Prx1-6)  
     – Prx3 (mitochondrial) particularly critical in RPE for H₂O₂ clearance  
   • Glutathione system  
     – GCL (glutamate–cysteine ligase), GSS (glutathione synthase), GSR (glutathione reductase)  
   • Thioredoxin–thioredoxin reductase axis  
   • Redox‐sensitive kinases/transcription factors  
     – ASK1/p38 MAPK/JNK cascade downstream of excessive H₂O₂  
     – FOXO family (e.g. FOXO3a) regulates antioxidant gene set  
     – SIRT3–PGC-1α axis in mitochondria enhances biogenesis and antioxidant enzyme expression  

Integration and Pathophysiologic Consequences in RPE  
   • Chronic ROS overwhelms NRF2 and the classical enzymatic defenses → progressive lipid and protein adduct formation  
   • Dysfunctional mitochondria accumulate → energy crisis, inflammasome activation, senescence (p16^INK4a, SA-β-gal)  
   • Impaired phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments, Bruch’s‐membrane thickening, complement activation  
   • Transition to a pro‐inflammatory, pro‐fibrotic milieu culminating in geographic atrophy  

Common In Vitro Stress Models of RPE Dysfunction  
   • H₂O₂ pulse or chronic exposure (150–500 µM)  
   • tert-Butyl hydroperoxide (t-BHP, 50–200 µM)  
   • A2E loading followed by blue-light irradiation (430 nm, 4–10 J/cm²)  
   • Iron overload (ferric ammonium citrate, 100–500 µM) to mimic ferroptotic cues  

This network of mitochondrial ROS overload, insufficient NRF2 buffering, unchecked lipid peroxidation/ferroptosis, and decline in core antioxidant systems collectively underlies RPE demise in dry AMD. Targeting nodal points in these interwoven pathways—especially those unique to RPE lipid handling or ferroptotic execution—offers promising avenues for novel single-agent therapies.
