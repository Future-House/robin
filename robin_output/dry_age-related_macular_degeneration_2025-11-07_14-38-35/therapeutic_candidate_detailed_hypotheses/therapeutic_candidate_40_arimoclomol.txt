Proposal for Arimoclomol (MCE HY-18871)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Arimoclomol (also known as BRX-345) is a synthetic hydroxylamine/hydroxamic-acid–derived small molecule developed as a heat-shock response (HSR) co-inducer that amplifies stress-inducible transcription of heat shock genes under conditions of proteotoxic stress, principally through prolongation and potentiation of heat shock factor-1 (HSF1) activation on heat shock elements (HSEs) in target promoters (Penke et al., 2018; Parfitt et al., 2014; O’Leary, 2019). In the hydroximic-acid derivative family (bimoclomol, arimoclomol, NG-094, BGP-15), several members synergistically enhance heat-induced Hsp expression; arimoclomol and bimoclomol have been shown to increase HSF1 activity via hyperphosphorylation and to amplify heat-shock gene expression in eukaryotic cells while sparing basal Hsp expression in unstressed cells, thereby acting as bona fide co-inducers rather than primary HSF1 agonists (Penke et al., 2018). The compound was originally advanced from the bimoclomol chemotype as a more drug-like analogue with similar HSR-potentiating properties and improved pharmacology, and it falls within a broader class of pharmacologic HSP70/HSF1 modulators investigated for neurodegenerative protein-misfolding diseases (e.g., ALS, NPC, tauopathies), as well as select retinal degeneration models (rhodopsin retinitis pigmentosa) (Parfitt et al., 2014; Venediktov et al., 2023; Penke et al., 2018). At the biophysical level, hydroximic-acid derivatives can intercalate into cellular membranes, a property hypothesized to contribute to regulation of HSF1 activation/attenuation kinetics and restoration of a protective Hsp profile under stress (Penke et al., 2018). In neuronal cell systems, arimoclomol has also been used in combination with primary HSF1 activators (e.g., celastrol) to achieve robust induction of inducible Hsp70s (HSPA1A, HSPA6) and other stress response proteins at lower inducer doses, consistent with its role as a co-inducer that amplifies pre-existing HSF1 activation (Deane, 2019).

Within the retina, arimoclomol has been evaluated in rod photoreceptor models of misfolded rhodopsin (P23H transgenic rats), where it enhanced Hsp70/Hsp90 expression and improved proteostasis, photoreceptor structure, and visual function; however, there is no published evidence in dry AMD models or direct demonstrations of RPE barrier rescue by arimoclomol, and at least one PRPF31-mutant RPE (RP11) cell system screening showed no aggregate-reducing effect of arimoclomol while an mTOR inhibitor (rapamycin) reduced aggregate burden via autophagy induction (Parfitt et al., 2014; Georgiou, 2021). The broader Hsp70/Hsc70 system, chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA), and macroautophagy are mechanistically central to RPE proteostasis and AMD pathobiology, establishing a strong rationale for HSF1–HSP70–targeted therapy, but translation from photoreceptor models and exogenous Hsp70 delivery to endogenous induction in RPE remains to be validated for this agent (Kumar et al., 2020; Chang et al., 2015; Subrizi et al., 2015).

Therapeutic History  
Arimoclomol’s development history is anchored in neurodegeneration and protein-misfolding indications. In SOD1-mutant ALS mice, arimoclomol increased Hsp70 expression and improved motor neuron survival and function, extending lifespan; it progressed to human ALS trials (including a phase 3 program), with mixed outcomes across adult and pediatric indications (O’Leary, 2019; Venediktov et al., 2023). Mechanistically consistent with HSF1 co-induction, arimoclomol is positioned to upregulate a suite of inducible chaperones and components of the proteostasis network rather than single chaperone overexpression, a strategy considered preferable for matching the endogenous disaggregation/refolding machinery and preserving feedback attenuation (O’Leary, 2019).

In the retina, arimoclomol has been tested extensively in rhodopsin retinitis pigmentosa (RP) models driven by the P23H misfolding mutation. In P23H-GFP cell models, arimoclomol reduced inclusion incidence and insoluble rod opsin, decreased higher-molecular-weight rhodopsin species, and improved viability, with contemporaneous increases in Hsp70/Hsp90 implying potentiation of the HSR in stressed photoreceptors (Parfitt et al., 2014). In P23H transgenic rats, daily intraperitoneal dosing (10 mg/kg) improved scotopic ERG a- and b-waves, preserved outer nuclear layer (ONL) thickness, increased outer segment (OS) length, and improved ultrastructure, consistent with disease modification through HSR and unfolded protein response (UPR) potentiation; Hsp70, Hsp90, and HSF1 were assayed directly in these studies (Parfitt et al., 2014). The authors further observed increased eIF2α phosphorylation and discussed enhanced IRE1/XBP1 activity, indicating crosstalk with ER stress responses (Parfitt et al., 2014). Of note, arimoclomol’s activity appeared contingent on the presence of misfolded-protein stress (i.e., stress-selective action), potentially limiting off-target effects in healthy tissue (Parfitt et al., 2014).

Beyond photoreceptors, the Hsp co-inducer class has retinal neuroprotective precedents: bimoclomol improved early electrophysiologic signs in diabetic rat retina, and the Hsp inducer geranylgeranylacetone protected retinal ganglion cells in a rat glaucoma model, underscoring that Hsp70 induction can be protective across retinal cell types and diseases of proteostasis and stress, although these are class exemplars rather than arimoclomol-specific RPE data (Hesselink, 2016). Separately, exogenous Hsp70 protein delivery to RPE has shown diffusion to the retina and RPE after intravitreal administration, intracellular uptake, lysosomal localization, and protection from oxidative stress and inflammation in ARPE-19 cells, supporting the concept that increasing Hsp70 activity in RPE can be beneficial, albeit via protein pharmacology rather than endogenous induction (Subrizi et al., 2015; Kumar et al., 2020).

Crucially for dry AMD translation, the current literature set provides no in vivo animal or clinical study of arimoclomol in AMD/dry AMD, and no direct measurements of RPE barrier integrity (tight-junction protein preservation or transepithelial electrical resistance [TEER]) following arimoclomol in RPE or AMD models; an RPE disease model of PRPF31 mutation with progressive aggregate accumulation reported that arimoclomol failed to reduce aggregates, whereas rapamycin decreased aggregate load and induced LC3 processing consistent with autophagy activation (LAMP1 and p62 remained unchanged), highlighting context-dependent efficacy and suggesting autophagy induction may be more decisive than Hsp co-induction in some RPE aggregate pathologies (Georgiou, 2021). Thus, while arimoclomol has a positive retinal track record in photoreceptor proteotoxicity and a robust extraocular development history, there is no direct evidence yet of arimoclomol ameliorating dry AMD phenotypes or preserving RPE barrier function in disease-relevant systems (Parfitt et al., 2014; Georgiou, 2021).

Mechanism of Action  
Arimoclomol is an HSR co-inducer that amplifies HSF1-driven transcription of inducible heat shock proteins under pre-existing cellular stress. Mechanistically, arimoclomol prolongs HSF1 binding to HSEs, increases HSF1 activity (e.g., hyperphosphorylation), and thereby increases transcription of Hsp70/Hsp90 and related chaperones, with limited impact in unstressed cells (i.e., stressor-dependent co-induction) (Penke et al., 2018; O’Leary, 2019; Parfitt et al., 2014). In neuronal cells, arimoclomol enhanced celastrol-induced HSF1 activation to elevate HSPA6, HSPA1A, HSPB1, DNAJB1, and HO-1 at doses that preserved viability and differentiated morphology, supporting efficient co-induction when paired with a primary HSF1 activator (Deane, 2019). In P23H photoreceptor models, arimoclomol reduced insoluble rhodopsin and inclusion incidence, decreased high-molecular-weight species, increased retinal Hsp70/Hsp90, and improved ERG/ONL/OS outcomes; the study observed increased eIF2α phosphorylation and discussed potentiation of the UPR (BiP/IRE1/XBP1), consistent with arimoclomol acting at the intersection of cytosolic HSR and ER homeostasis (Parfitt et al., 2014).

Hsp70 Biology in RPE and AMD. Inducible Hsp70 (HSPA1A/Hsp70-1) is a central, evolutionarily conserved ATPase chaperone that assists folding of nascent chains, refolds damaged/misfolded proteins, buffers aggregation, facilitates “protein triage” toward degradation pathways, and protects cells from apoptosis and inflammatory signaling (e.g., NF-κB pathway), with expression controlled by HSF1 (Ryhänen, 2011; Kivinen, 2018). In the RPE, Hsp70/Hsc70 function intersects both the ubiquitin–proteasome system (UPS) and lysosome-dependent proteolysis. Hsp70 can present misfolded proteins to the UPS via CHIP E3 ligase, regulate disaggregation with co-chaperones HSP40/HSP90/HIP/HOP, and is required for aggregate handling; in proteasome failure, protein aggregates in ARPE-19 cells co-localize with Hsp70 and p62, with p62 undergoing autophagic clearance while Hsp70 binds reversibly and evades autophagic degradation (Kumar et al., 2020; Kivinen et al., 2014; Ryhänen, 2011). Hsp70 is also integral to chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA): cytosolic Hsc70/Hsp70 recognizes KFERQ-containing substrates, delivers them to LAMP-2A at the lysosomal membrane, and ATPase-driven “entropic pulling” and luminal chaperones enable translocation and degradation; oxidative stress reduces HSP70 expression in RPE, impairs CMA, and can dysregulate macroautophagy cross-talk, contributing to RPE death (Chang et al., 2015; Kumar et al., 2020). Extracellular Hsp70 roles in RPE are complex: exogenous rhHsp70 injections protected against subretinal fibrosis via TLR2/TLR4 and IL-10 induction, while oxidized-lipid stress (4-HNE) can increase extracellular Hsp70 efflux and pro-inflammatory cytokines, indicating that compartmental localization of Hsp70 critically determines immunomodulatory outcomes (Kumar et al., 2020).

Evidence for arimoclomol in RPE cells. Direct mechanistic evidence for arimoclomol action in RPE is limited. A cell-culture report in ARPE-19 asserts that Hsp70 overexpression “with the help of a co-inducer like arimoclomol” suppressed IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α and increased IL-10 and TGF-β1, consistent with Hsp70-mediated IκK/NF-κB inhibition; however, the study details are summarized in low-quality review literature, and direct demonstration of arimoclomol-driven HSF1 activation in RPE is not provided (Kumar et al., 2020). Another ARPE-19-focused review excerpt states that arimoclomol acts on HSF1 to increase cytosolic Hsp70 without increasing extracellular Hsp70 and improved resistance to 4-HNE–induced oxidative stress (relative to paeoniflorin); again, the primary data are not provided here and the review is of limited quality, so these findings should be treated as provisional until replicated (Kumar et al., 2020). Importantly, in an isogenic patient-derived PRPF31-mutant RPE (RP11) model with progressive cytoplasmic aggregate accumulation, a 7-day treatment screen across HSR/UPR/autophagy modulators showed that rapamycin (mTORC1 inhibitor) reduced aggregate load and shifted LC3-I to LC3-II, whereas arimoclomol did not reduce aggregates under the conditions tested, arguing that in this RPE disease context autophagy activation via mTOR inhibition outperformed Hsp co-induction (Georgiou, 2021).

Non-pharmacological Hsp70 induction and autophagy in RPE inform plausibility. Subthreshold retinal thermal stimulation in pig and mouse RPE in vivo induced HSF1 and Hsp70, increased LC3B-II and decreased p62 (consistent with autophagy activation), and increased LAMP2, suggesting potential CMA upregulation, without apoptosis at ~44 °C, confirming that RPE supports coordinated HSR–autophagy programs with an exploitable therapeutic window (but autophagic flux was not directly quantified in vivo) (Amirkavei et al., 2022; Amirkavei et al., 2020). These data reinforce the therapeutic logic for HSF1–Hsp70 upregulation in RPE to restore proteostasis but do not directly validate arimoclomol’s efficacy in RPE (Amirkavei et al., 2022; Amirkavei et al., 2020). Exogenous rhHsp70 trafficking to lysosomes and protection against oxidative stress in ARPE-19 further supports the notion that increasing Hsp70 activity, whether by protein delivery or endogenous induction, can be beneficial to RPE survival under AMD-relevant stressors (Subrizi et al., 2015).

Autophagy and barrier integrity under AMD-relevant stressors. AMD-relevant toxicities (Aβ, lipofuscin/A2E, oxidants) perturb autophagy/lysosomes in RPE, with Aβ decreasing occludin and ZO-1 in ARPE-19 and activating autophagy markers in vitro and in vivo, and A2E promoting lysosomal membrane permeabilization and cell death; impaired UPS and autophagy with aging lead to aggregate accumulation (including p62-positive aggregates) in RPE and drusen, highlighting the importance of restoring both proteasomal and autophagic clearance to protect RPE function and barrier integrity (Wang et al., 2023; Viiri, 2019; Ryhänen, 2011). Hsp70/p62 roles in this axis are mechanistically established: Hsp70 prevents aggregation and participates in aggregate handling, while p62 is an autophagic receptor degraded by autophagy and accumulates when autophagy is impaired; pharmacologic AMPK activation can accelerate p62 clearance during proteasome inhibition and improve RPE survival (Viiri, 2019). To date, arimoclomol has not been shown to modulate CMA or macroautophagy in RPE, nor to preserve tight junctions or TEER in AMD-relevant models (Georgiou, 2021; Kumar et al., 2020).

Expected Effect  
Assay hypothesis and rationale. The working hypothesis is that arimoclomol will potentiate stress-triggered HSF1 activity in RPE, elevating inducible Hsp70 and coordinated chaperone networks to stabilize junctional proteostasis, reduce misfolded protein burden, and normalize autophagy (including potentially CMA), thereby preventing degradation of occludin/claudin and restoring TEER under oxidative or drusen-like stress (e.g., H2O2/4-HNE, A2E/lipofuscin, Aβ) (Kumar et al., 2020; Chang et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2023). This mechanism is biologically plausible: Hsp70 is abundantly expressed in RPE under stress and supports folding and refolding, substrate triage to proteasome via CHIP, and delivery of specific substrates to CMA via Hsc70/LAMP-2A; its induction suppresses NF-κB–driven pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) and can raise IL-10/TGF-β1, which together could indirectly protect junctional complexes from cytokine- and stress-mediated degradation (Kumar et al., 2020). Reducing intracellular aggregate load and restoring proteostasis would be expected to mitigate the downstream cascade of mTORC1 activation, p62/LC3-I accumulation, and tight-junction disruption observed in stressed RPE models (e.g., PRPF31-RPE), although in that system arimoclomol was ineffective while rapamycin reduced aggregates, underscoring that Hsp co-induction alone may be insufficient when autophagy is the principal bottleneck (Georgiou, 2021).

Evidence supporting expected effects in RPE assays:  
- Hsp70 induction in retinal tissues has been achieved via HSF1 activation by heat or pharmacologic inducers; in photoreceptor P23H models, arimoclomol increased Hsp70/Hsp90 and reduced aggregation/toxicity, consistent with the proximal arm of the proposed mechanism (HSF1→Hsp70→improved proteostasis) (Parfitt et al., 2014; Amirkavei et al., 2020).  
- In ARPE-19 summaries, arimoclomol increased intracellular (not extracellular) Hsp70 and improved resistance to 4-HNE oxidative stress, and Hsp70 overexpression with help of a co-inducer like arimoclomol suppressed pro-inflammatory cytokines, supporting cell-autonomous proteostasis and anti-inflammatory contributions that would be favorable for junctional stability (Kumar et al., 2020).  
- Exogenous rhHsp70 delivery to RPE localizes to lysosomes and reduces oxidative toxicity and inflammation, demonstrating that raising functional Hsp70 in RPE is sufficient to afford cytoprotection, a downstream functional goal of HSF1 co-induction (Subrizi et al., 2015; Kumar et al., 2020).

Evidence arguing for tempered expectations and the need for combination strategies:  
- In PRPF31-mutant RPE, arimoclomol did not reduce aggregates in a 7-day screen, whereas rapamycin shifted LC3 processing and reduced aggregate markers, suggesting that when autophagy is the limiting axis, direct autophagy activation (e.g., mTORC1 inhibition) is required; Hsp co-induction alone may not clear established aggregates or repair autophagic flux (Georgiou, 2021).  
- In primary neuronal ALS models, arimoclomol often failed to induce measurable HSPA1A/HSPA8 and its protective actions occurred without detectable Hsp70 upregulation; HDAC inhibition enhanced Hsp induction by other inducers, arguing that Hsp co-induction efficacy is cell-type and chromatin-context dependent and may require combination therapy to overcome induction thresholds in some cells (potentially relevant to differentiated RPE) (Comaduran et al., 2024).  
- AMD-relevant stressors directly disrupt tight junctions (Aβ reduces occludin and ZO-1 in ARPE-19) and invoke autophagy/lysosomal dysfunction; because arimoclomol’s effects on CMA/macroautophagy in RPE are unproven, barrier rescue may not occur unless autophagic flux is also corrected (Wang et al., 2023).

Assay implementation guidance for decision-making:  
- In vitro human RPE models (primary/donor iPSC-RPE monolayers with high TEER) should be stressed with 4-HNE, H2O2, A2E, or Aβ to recapitulate AMD-relevant proteotoxic and inflammatory conditions, and arimoclomol tested across concentrations paralleling prior cell work (e.g., 10–100 µM) with and without a priming HSF1 activator (celastrol ≤0.3 µM) to evaluate requirement for co-induction, keeping in mind the neuronal data identifying 50 µM arimoclomol + 0.3 µM celastrol as an optimized low-toxicity combination (Deane, 2019).  
- Primary readouts: HSF1 activation (nuclear translocation, Ser phosphorylation), Hsp70/Hsp90 induction (immunoblot, qPCR), autophagic flux (LC3-II turnover with/without bafilomycin, p62 dynamics), CMA markers (LAMP-2A levels, lysosomal Hsc70/Hsp70 substrate colocalization), proteasome activity, aggregate load (FK1, ubiquitin, p62 bodies), and barrier integrity (TEER, ZO-1/occludin/claudin-19 immunostaining continuity) (Kivinen et al., 2014; Chang et al., 2015; Viiri, 2019; Wang et al., 2023).  
- Cytokine profiling (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-10, TGF-β1) to detect Hsp70-linked NF-κB effects reported in ARPE-19 (Kumar et al., 2020).  
- Positive comparators: rapamycin for autophagy induction; exogenous rhHsp70 as a proteostasis rescue control; heat-based Hsp70 induction within non-damaging windows to confirm pathway responsiveness (Georgiou, 2021; Subrizi et al., 2015; Amirkavei et al., 2020).

Given the aggregate of evidence, the most realistic expectation is that arimoclomol will increase inducible Hsp70 in stressed RPE if HSF1 can be productively co-induced, reduce misfolded protein accumulation and inflammatory signaling, and potentially improve barrier metrics indirectly by attenuating stressors that degrade junctional complexes; however, without concurrent normalization of autophagic flux and CMA, tight-junction rescue and TEER restoration are uncertain, particularly in models where autophagy is the primary deficit (Georgiou, 2021; Kumar et al., 2020; Chang et al., 2015).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths supporting arimoclomol for dry AMD RPE barrier rescue:  
- Mechanistic fit: Hsp70/Hsc70 are central “gatekeepers” of RPE proteostasis across the UPS and lysosomal systems, including CMA; restoring inducible Hsp70 via HSF1 co-induction directly targets key failure nodes in RPE aging and AMD pathogenesis (aggregation, impaired clearance, inflammation), aligning with known disease biology (Kumar et al., 2020; Chang et al., 2015; Kivinen, 2018).  
- Demonstrated retinal efficacy in proteostasis-driven degeneration: arimoclomol increased Hsp70/Hsp90, reduced mutant rhodopsin aggregation, and preserved photoreceptor structure/function in P23H rats, with evidence of concurrent UPR potentiation; this validates the platform concept that HSF1 co-induction plus ER stress modulation can modify retinal degeneration, albeit in photoreceptors rather than RPE (Parfitt et al., 2014).  
- RPE permissiveness to Hsp70-based cytoprotection: exogenous rhHsp70 diffuses to RPE, is internalized, localizes to endolysosomal compartments, reduces IL-6 and cytolysis, and improves viability in oxidative stress paradigms; thus, augmenting Hsp70 in RPE is sufficient to produce protective effects, supporting the feasibility of endogenous induction with a co-inducer if pharmacology is adequate (Subrizi et al., 2015).  
- Stress-selective amplification: arimoclomol preferentially acts in stressed cells, limiting risks of unwanted Hsp70 induction in healthy tissue; this attribute is advantageous for chronic ocular therapy where avoiding constitutive pathway activation is desirable (Parfitt et al., 2014).  
- Clinical development and safety experience: extensive non-ocular development in ALS/NPC provides informative safety and pharmacology context; while ocular distribution is not established in these sources, the systemic safety window is a favorable starting point for repurposing (Venediktov et al., 2023; O’Leary, 2019).

Key weaknesses and uncertainties:  
- Lack of direct evidence in dry AMD/RPE barrier endpoints: no published data demonstrate arimoclomol preserves RPE tight junctions (ZO-1/occludin/claudin), restores TEER, or improves drusen-like pathology in vivo; ARPE-19 reports of arimoclomol-induced Hsp70 and anti-inflammatory effects are secondary summaries in low-quality reviews without primary detail (Kumar et al., 2020). In contrast, direct AMD-relevant in vitro data show that Aβ disrupts tight junction proteins, and autophagy failure correlates with p62 accumulation and aggregate burden, but the ability of arimoclomol to reverse these specific defects in RPE is untested (Wang et al., 2023; Viiri, 2019).  
- Negative/neutral result in PRPF31-RPE aggregation screen: arimoclomol did not reduce cytoplasmic aggregates in a rigorous 7-day screen in RP11-RPE; rapamycin reduced aggregates and shifted LC3 processing, suggesting that in certain RPE aggregate pathologies, autophagy activators outperform Hsp co-induction; whether arimoclomol could complement autophagy induction to achieve barrier rescue remains to be determined (Georgiou, 2021).  
- Uncertain induction competence in differentiated RPE: Primary neurons display high thresholds for inducible Hsp70 (HSPA1A) induction, with arimoclomol often failing to raise Hsp70; Hsp induction can be enhanced by HDAC inhibition, implying that chromatin accessibility and cell state are important variables; differentiated RPE monolayers may similarly require co-induction strategies (e.g., low-dose celastrol or epigenetic modulators) to achieve robust Hsp70 upregulation (Comaduran et al., 2024; Deane, 2019). If arimoclomol cannot reliably induce Hsp70 in mature RPE under clinically relevant stress, the strategy will falter.  
- CMA/macroautophagy modulation by arimoclomol in RPE not established: despite Hsp70’s mechanistic role in CMA, no data show arimoclomol improves CMA or autophagic flux in RPE, which are crucial to clearing damaged proteins/lipids under AMD stress; relying on Hsp70 alone without restoring autophagic flux may be insufficient to protect tight junctions (Chang et al., 2015; Georgiou, 2021).  
- Translational pharmacology: Retinal/RPE exposure of arimoclomol following systemic dosing is not presented in this corpus; ocular delivery strategies and RPE pharmacokinetics for arimoclomol are undefined here, in contrast to exogenous Hsp70 delivery where diffusion and tissue uptake were measured (Subrizi et al., 2015). Without ocular PK/PD and RPE Hsp70 induction data, dosing strategies for barrier rescue remain speculative.

Recommendation and development path:  
- Proceed to targeted preclinical validation in human iPSC-RPE monolayers and AMD-relevant stress paradigms, with rigorous endpoint panels including HSF1–Hsp70 induction, autophagic flux/CMA markers, aggregate clearance, cytokine modulation, and barrier integrity (TEER, ZO-1/occludin continuity), using rapamycin and rhHsp70 as comparators and benchmarking against known disruptions by Aβ and A2E (Georgiou, 2021; Subrizi et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2023).  
- Explore co-induction strategies: combine arimoclomol with low-dose celastrol to lower the induction threshold, and/or with autophagy activators (rapalogs/AMPK agonists) to correct flux while elevating chaperone capacity; justify combinations by neuronal data demonstrating enhanced Hsp induction and by RPE data showing autophagy dependence of aggregate clearance (Deane, 2019; Viiri, 2019; Georgiou, 2021).  
- If cell data are positive (robust Hsp70 induction, reduced aggregate burden, TEER protection), advance to ex vivo retina or small-animal models of RPE oxidative/drusen-like stress, with ocular pharmacokinetic measurements to confirm RPE exposure and on-target Hsp70 induction; consider local ocular delivery if systemic exposure is inadequate (Subrizi et al., 2015).  
- Maintain a realistic risk view: if arimoclomol fails to induce Hsp70 adequately in differentiated RPE or fails to impact autophagic flux, pivot to alternative HSF1 activators or to exogenous Hsp70 delivery or autophagy-centric therapies, where evidence in RPE is stronger (Subrizi et al., 2015; Georgiou, 2021).

In sum, arimoclomol is a mechanistically coherent candidate for restoring RPE proteostasis via HSF1–Hsp70 co-induction, supported by strong photoreceptor data and broad neurodegeneration experience, but it lacks direct evidence in dry AMD models and has an RPE aggregation screen result that favors autophagy induction over Hsp co-induction; rigorous, well-controlled RPE studies with barrier readouts are required before committing to translational development for dry AMD (Parfitt et al., 2014; Georgiou, 2021).
