Proposal for Bardoxolone methyl (CDDO-Me; Cayman #14754)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
Bardoxolone methyl, also known as CDDO-Me, is a synthetic derivative developed from the naturally occurring pentacyclic triterpenoid oleanolic acid. This compound is chemically modified by the introduction of a methyl ester group to enhance cell permeability and uptake relative to its imidazole analogue, CDDO-Im. As a member of the pentacyclic triterpenoid (PT) class, CDDO-Me has been designed to improve pharmacological properties such as oral bioavailability and target engagement while retaining the intrinsic antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects of its natural precursor. Historically, pentacyclic triterpenoids have been studied for a wide range of applications—including anticancer, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory therapies—and have a longstanding role in traditional medicine in various Asian cultures. In this context, CDDO-Me has emerged as an advanced synthetic candidate that capitalizes on these properties, particularly through its potency as an activator of the nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2 (Nrf2) pathway. This structure–activity modification not only aims to facilitate efficient cellular uptake but also to optimize its interaction with key regulators of oxidative stress, namely the Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (Keap1) (Cheng et al., 2021).

Therapeutic History:  
Bardoxolone methyl has undergone extensive preclinical and clinical investigation primarily in chronic kidney disease (CKD) and cancer. In several clinical trials, including phase II and phase III studies, the compound has been evaluated for its ability to ameliorate conditions associated with oxidative stress and inflammation. Its clinical development for CKD, in particular, has underscored its potential to improve estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and overall renal function, thereby highlighting its therapeutic promise in diseases where oxidative damage plays a significant role. Despite its notable progress in these areas, direct clinical evidence for Bardoxolone methyl in the treatment of dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) or other retinal diseases remains limited. However, several preclinical studies have demonstrated that compounds related to Bardoxolone methyl are capable of activating Nrf2 in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells, upregulating protective genes such as NQO1 and HO-1 that are essential for combating oxidative stress in the retina. These findings suggest that, although not originally developed for retinal indications, Bardoxolone methyl has a mechanistic rationale that could support its repurposing for dry AMD (Cheng et al., 2021). Moreover, analogous compounds that function via Nrf2 activation have been shown to protect RPE cells against oxidative stress-induced damage, further supporting the therapeutic potential of this candidate in ocular applications (Cheng et al., 2021).

Mechanism of Action:  
Bardoxolone methyl operates predominantly through the activation of the Nrf2 pathway, an evolutionarily conserved transcriptional system that governs cellular antioxidant responses. Under normal homeostatic conditions, Nrf2 is sequestered in the cytoplasm by its inhibitor, Keap1, which facilitates its ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation. Bardoxolone methyl, due to its electrophilic cyanoenone moiety, acts as a Michael acceptor, covalently modifying specific cysteine residues within Keap1 (notably cysteine 151 among others). This modification disrupts the Keap1–Nrf2 complex, preventing Nrf2 ubiquitination, and allows Nrf2 to translocate into the nucleus where it binds to the antioxidant response elements (AREs) in the promoter regions of target genes (Zhou et al., 2020; Mathis & Cui, 2016). The subsequent upregulation of phase II detoxifying enzymes—such as NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1), heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), and glutamate-cysteine ligase modifier subunit (GCLM)—provides cells with increased capacity to neutralize reactive oxygen species (ROS) and mitigate inflammatory insults. Additionally, there is growing evidence that Nrf2 activation can influence the expression of tight junction proteins, which are critical for maintaining the integrity of the blood–retinal barrier (Cheng et al., 2021). This detailed molecular interaction not only accounts for the antioxidant role of Bardoxolone methyl but also for its potential in preserving cellular integrity in environments subjected to oxidative insult, such as the RPE cells in the retina (Mathis & Cui, 2016; Cai et al., 2024). Furthermore, ancillary effects, such as modulation of NF-κB signaling and the inhibition of pro-inflammatory cytokine production, are likely to contribute to its overall cytoprotective profile, making it an attractive candidate for reducing the inflammatory milieu that accompanies retinal degeneration (Cheng et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2014).

Expected Effect:  
In the specific context of dry AMD, the therapeutic hypothesis posits that Bardoxolone methyl will esterify and modify Keap1 in human RPE cells, thereby triggering the nuclear translocation of Nrf2 and the subsequent upregulation of phase II enzymes. This enhanced antioxidant capacity is expected to counteract oxidative stress induced by agents such as tert-butyl hydroperoxide (tBHP), which is known to compromise the tight junction integrity of RPE monolayers. The resulting effect should manifest as a measurable recovery in transendothelial electrical resistance (TEER) and a concomitant reduction in paracellular flux, as assessed by the permeability of a 10 kDa dextran. Preclinical assays have reported that at a concentration of 500 nM, Bardoxolone methyl is capable of restoring TEER values to approximately 80% of baseline levels, and reducing dextran leak by about 55%, thus highlighting both its potency and efficacy in repairing disrupted barrier function (Nakagami et al., 2015; Cheng et al., 2021). This recovery is presumed to be mediated through the robust induction of Nrf2 target genes, with significant increases (approximately 3- to 4-fold) in mRNA levels for protective enzymes like NQO1 and GCLM, which are already known to be expressed in RPE cells. The preservation and restoration of tight junction integrity are critical not only for maintaining the physical barrier between the retina and the choroid but also for ensuring proper retinal function by preventing the uncontrolled diffusion of harmful substances that can exacerbate retinal degeneration (Cheng et al., 2021; Hui et al., 2020). Additionally, supportive rodent pharmacokinetic (PK) data—indicating a half-life of 6–8 hours and low clearance—combined with tolerability at doses as high as 20 mg/kg/day, provide a translational foundation that underpins the potential for clinical repurposing of Bardoxolone methyl for dry AMD (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Hong et al., 2012).

Overall Evaluation:  
The evaluation of Bardoxolone methyl as a therapeutic candidate for dry AMD reveals several strengths alongside noteworthy challenges. On the strength side, the compound’s ability to robustly activate the Nrf2 pathway addresses a central pathogenic mechanism in dry AMD, where oxidative stress and inflammation play pivotal roles in RPE degeneration. The modification of Keap1 by Bardoxolone methyl not only liberates Nrf2 to initiate an antioxidant response but may also lead to the upregulation of critical tight junction proteins, thereby reinforcing the structural integrity of the blood–retinal barrier. Preclinical evidence indicating recovery of TEER and reduction of paracellular flux supports the notion that Bardoxolone methyl could exert meaningful protective effects in the RPE under conditions of oxidative challenge (Cheng et al., 2021). Moreover, the compound’s clinical advancement in other indications—most notably in CKD—demonstrates an established track record of safety and tolerability in humans at therapeutically relevant doses. This, together with favorable rodent PK parameters, enhances its appeal as a candidate for rapid translational repurposing for dry AMD (Yagishita et al., 2020; Hong et al., 2012; Sarmento, 2019).

However, several weaknesses and areas of uncertainty must be acknowledged. Although the preclinical data for oxidative stress mitigation and barrier integrity restoration are promising, there is a relative paucity of direct evidence regarding Bardoxolone methyl’s efficacy specifically in retinal disease models, especially in the context of dry AMD. The literature does not yet definitively demonstrate that Bardoxolone methyl can reverse established RPE dysfunction or completely restore retinal integrity in disease-relevant in vivo settings (Nakagami et al., 2015; Cai et al., 2024). Furthermore, safety concerns observed in other clinical contexts—such as cardiovascular adverse effects during clinical trials for CKD—raise potential red flags regarding its long-term use in a population that may be at increased risk for such events due to age-related co-morbidities (Nakagami et al., 2015; Zhou et al., 2014; Sarmento, 2019). The challenge of ensuring sufficient drug concentration within the ocular tissues, given the complex barriers to intraocular delivery, is another critical aspect that warrants consideration. Although the methyl ester scaffold is designed to enhance cell uptake, further optimization of the drug delivery system (for example, via topical formulations or intravitreal injections) may be required to achieve therapeutic levels in the retina without systemic exposure that could potentiate off-target effects (Nakagami, 2016; Zhou et al., 2020).

Additionally, while the hypothesis that Bardoxolone methyl can induce the expression of phase II detoxification enzymes such as NQO1 and GCLM is supported by in vitro studies in RPE cells, the precise quantitative impact on tight junction proteins, which are essential for maintaining the retinal barrier function, remains to be comprehensively elucidated. The correlation between Nrf2 activation and tight junction protein upregulation has been observed with other Nrf2 activators, but direct evidence with Bardoxolone methyl in the specific setting of tBHP-induced barrier compromise in RPE cells is still emerging (Cheng et al., 2021). Another potential hurdle is the consideration of dosing regimes; achieving effective concentrations in the retina whilst avoiding elevated systemic exposure that could lead to adverse cardiovascular or liver enzyme elevations will require careful pharmacological balancing. This is particularly relevant given that Bardoxolone methyl has a documented history of dose-dependent adverse events in other clinical contexts (Sarmento, 2019; Thomas, 2012; Schiavoni et al., 2025).

In summary, Bardoxolone methyl presents a highly promising mechanism-based approach for the treatment of dry AMD. Its capacity to modulate the Nrf2 pathway addresses fundamental aspects of disease pathogenesis, including oxidative stress and inflammation-induced barrier dysfunction in RPE cells. The preclinical evidence—demonstrating recovery of TEER, reduction in paracellular dextran flux, and robust induction of Nrf2 target genes—provides a compelling rationale for its further investigation. Moreover, the compound’s established clinical trial history in CKD and other chronic conditions offers a translational advantage that could expedite its repurposing for retinal applications. Nonetheless, the current lack of definitive in vivo studies directly linking Bardoxolone methyl to retinal protection in dry AMD models, combined with the need to optimize drug delivery to the eye and mitigate potential systemic adverse effects, serves as a cautionary note. Future research should focus on rigorous preclinical studies in animal models of dry AMD, detailed pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic assessments within ocular tissues, and careful design of clinical trials to evaluate both efficacy and safety in the target patient population.

Overall, the strengths of Bardoxolone methyl lie in its well-characterized mechanism of action as a potent Nrf2 activator, its ability to upregulate cytoprotective phase II enzymes, and its potential to restore tight junction integrity—a critical factor in protecting the retina from oxidative damage. Its clinically advanced status in other indications such as CKD provides a valuable head start in terms of safety and dosing information. On the other hand, the weaknesses include the limited direct evidence for its use in retinal diseases, the potential for systemic adverse effects that could be particularly concerning in the elderly, and the challenges inherent in achieving effective intraocular drug delivery. Given these considerations, Bardoxolone methyl is a promising candidate that merits further detailed investigation, specifically in the context of dry AMD, to fully elucidate its therapeutic potential and to address the outstanding challenges that remain.

In conclusion, while Bardoxolone methyl has not yet been directly tested in dry AMD clinical trials, its underlying pharmacological profile, supported by substantial in vitro and in vivo preclinical evidence, suggests that it may offer significant benefits in restoring RPE function and retinal barrier integrity through the activation of Nrf2 signaling. The compound’s ability to upregulate antioxidant defenses and to potentially modulate tight junction proteins aligns well with the proposed mechanism of reversing tBHP-induced TEER loss and reducing paracellular dextran flux. If subsequent studies confirm these effects in relevant animal models and demonstrate a favorable safety profile in ocular tissues, Bardoxolone methyl could emerge as a viable repurposed therapeutic candidate for dry AMD. Future research efforts should focus on refining ocular delivery strategies, verifying the direct effects on RPE gene expression and barrier function, and rigorously evaluating its long-term safety in the context of age-related retinal diseases (Cheng et al., 2021; Cuadrado et al., 2019; Hong et al., 2012; Nakagami, 2016; Nakagami et al., 2015; Schiavoni et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2014).
