Proposal for SAR407899 (Selleckchem S2921)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
SAR407899 (Selleckchem S2921) is a small-molecule ATP-competitive inhibitor of Rho-associated coiled-coil containing kinases (ROCKs) that targets both ROCK1 and ROCK2, placing it within the widely studied class of dual ROCK1/2 inhibitors used as chemical tools and therapeutic candidates across vascular, fibrotic, and ocular indications driven by actomyosin contractility and cytoskeletal dysregulation (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021; Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016). The ATP-binding pockets of ROCK1 and ROCK2 are highly conserved, which has historically favored development of dual-isoform inhibitors (e.g., fasudil, Y-27632, ripasudil, netarsudil) rather than isoform-selective agents; SAR407899 is listed among this majority class of dual-acting molecules in contemporary reviews of rho-kinase modulators for retinal disease (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021). Multiple dual ROCK inhibitors have been clinically applied: fasudil is approved systemically for cerebral vasospasm and has been evaluated intravitreally for retinal disease, while ripasudil and netarsudil are approved as topical ophthalmic solutions for glaucoma/ocular hypertension, establishing the translational precedent and safety framework for the class in ocular tissues (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Leong et al., 2025). At a mechanistic level, all members of this class block ROCK catalytic activity by binding to the ATP site, thereby reducing phosphorylation of canonical substrates such as MYPT1 (the myosin phosphatase target subunit) and myosin regulatory light chain (MLC), and decreasing downstream actomyosin tension, stress fibers, and focal adhesion assembly that collectively drive barrier breakdown, contraction, and fibrosis (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Leong et al., 2025). While “soft drug” and kinase-selectivity strategies have been advanced to improve tissue targeting and reduce off-target activity, the broad conclusion from medicinal chemistry and pharmacology reviews is that most clinical-grade ROCK inhibitors remain dual-isoform ATP-competitive agents with some potential for off-target kinase interactions at higher exposures—an interpretive caveat for both efficacy and safety (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Liu et al., 2018).

Therapeutic History:  
Direct preclinical or clinical reports on SAR407899 in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) or age-related macular degeneration (AMD) were not identified in the provided literature set; the compound is listed among “double-acting ROCK inhibitor molecules” considered for retinal vascular disease, but no SAR407899-specific ocular pharmacokinetic, penetration, or efficacy data are supplied in the excerpts (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021). In contrast, there is substantial class-level evidence that dual ROCK1/2 inhibition is active in posterior segment disease biology relevant to dry AMD pathogenesis and sequelae (barrier failure, low-grade inflammation, extracellular matrix remodeling), and that ROCK pathway blockade by other chemotypes can beneficially modulate RPE/retinal fibrosis and endothelial barrier endpoints across models (Nourinia et al., 2018; Zandi et al., 2025). For endothelial barrier function, ripasudil preserved ZO-1 localization and reversed VEGF-induced TEER loss in human retinal microvascular endothelial cells (HRMECs), and suppressed retinal p-MYPT1 in a murine retinal vein occlusion model, demonstrating target engagement and barrier preservation by ROCK inhibition in retina (albeit on the vascular rather than RPE side of the BRB) (Hida et al., 2018). For RPE morphology and barrier integrity in vivo, intravitreally applied fasudil reversed Rho pathway–driven cytoskeletal abnormalities (apical constriction and stress fibers) and improved RPE barrier integrity in disease-relevant paradigms, establishing that pan-ROCK antagonism can correct RPE junctional dysfunction at the tissue level (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016). In fibrotic endpoints applicable to geographic atrophy progression and subretinal scarring, both pan-ROCK (fasudil) and ROCK2-selective (belumosudil) inhibitors reduced canonical fibrosis drivers (TGF-β1, fibronectin, vimentin, α-SMA) and ROCK pathway activity (p-MYPT1) in choroid–RPE complex and retina in vivo, with concomitant preservation of retinal morphology and decreased fibrosis volume in a mouse CNV-associated fibrosis model (Zandi et al., 2025). Additional dual ROCK inhibitors (AMA0428) suppressed angiogenesis, inflammation, and fibrosis in a mouse CNV model more broadly than VEGFR2 blockade alone, underscoring the ability of ROCK pathway inhibition to simultaneously normalize vessels, reduce leakage, and block matrix deposition—features highly relevant to AMD (Hollanders et al., 2015; Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016). Clinically, small trials combining intravitreal fasudil with bevacizumab in refractory diabetic macular edema (DME) showed short-term improvements in visual acuity and central macular thickness versus anti-VEGF alone, consistent with anti-permeability and vasoregulatory actions of ROCK inhibitors in human retina; while not dry AMD, these studies provide translational class experience for retinal application and intravitreal dosing (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Nourinia et al., 2018). Taken together, the literature supports class-level efficacy signals in pathways relevant to dry AMD (barrier/tight junctions, fibrosis/TGF-β–driven contraction, vascular normalization/inflammation), but it provides no SAR407899-specific RPE or dry AMD results, PK, or ocular penetration data in the provided excerpts (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021; Yamaguchi et al., 2017).

Mechanism of Action:  
ROCK1 and ROCK2 are serine/threonine kinases activated by RhoA-GTP that increase cellular contractility and cytoskeletal tension by two concerted mechanisms: (1) direct phosphorylation of myosin regulatory light chain (MLC), and (2) phosphorylation of MYPT1, the regulatory subunit of myosin phosphatase, which inhibits phosphatase activity and sustains high p-MLC; ROCK also signals via LIM kinases to inactivate cofilin, stabilizing actin stress fibers and focal adhesions—all of which favor junctional opening and motile/contractile phenotypes (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Leong et al., 2025). In retinal and RPE-relevant systems, this signaling axis links directly to barrier dysregulation, fibrosis, and inflammation: high glucose and VEGF increase ROCK activity and p-MLC in retinal microvascular endothelium, augmenting adhesion molecule expression (ICAM-1), leukocyte adhesion, and hyperpermeability; conversely, ROCK inhibitors reduce ICAM-1, leukostasis, endothelial damage, and vascular leakage in diabetic retina models, consistent with reduced actomyosin tension and barrier preservation (Nourinia et al., 2018). Direct demonstration of ROCK pathway inhibition in vivo includes suppression of retinal p-MYPT1 by ripasudil in a murine retinal vein occlusion model and preservation of ZO-1 and TEER in VEGF-challenged HRMECs, indicating that ROCK blockade stabilizes tight-junction complexes and restores barrier function under stress (Hida et al., 2018). In RPE, Rho/ROCK activation produces cytoskeletal remodeling (apical constriction and stress fiber formation) leading to intercellular junction opening—and intravitreally delivered fasudil reversed these morphological abnormalities and improved RPE barrier integrity, linking ROCK activity to the outer BRB in vivo (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016). Fibrotic contraction in vitreoretinal disease is mechanistically tied to ROCK: TGF-β–activated contractility proceeds via ROCK-dependent MLC phosphorylation, and ROCK inhibitors suppress TGF-β–induced collagen gel contraction by RPE, reduce proliferative vitreoretinopathy (PVR) progression in rabbits, and decrease subretinal fibrosis after CNV, while lowering p-MYPT1 in choroid–RPE complex (Nourinia et al., 2018; Zandi et al., 2025). Inflammation intersects ROCK signaling via NF-κB and other pathways: ROCK activity upregulates inflammatory mediators (e.g., NF-κB targets, MCP-1/CCL2, iNOS), and ROCK inhibitors reduce inflammatory cell infiltration and cytokine production in ocular models, further supporting secondary barrier stabilization and anti-fibrotic effects (Singh & Singh, 2023; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021). In AMD-relevant immunity, ROCK2-selective signaling is linked to age-related macrophage polarization toward proangiogenic M2 (MaDAMs) in human AMD eyes; ROCK2-selective inhibition improved macrophage phenotype and reduced CNV in models, whereas pan-ROCK fasudil did not share all of these benefits in the immune compartment, suggesting non-redundant isoform roles in the aging choroid–retina milieu (Nourinia et al., 2018; Yamaguchi et al., 2017). These combined data establish a coherent molecular mechanism whereby dual ROCK1/2 inhibition should reduce p-MYPT1/p-MLC, relax actomyosin tension, stabilize junctional complexes (occludin/ZO-1), oppose TGF-β–driven fibrotic conversion, and blunt inflammatory cascades that exacerbate outer BRB failure in dry AMD; however, whether dual versus ROCK2-selective inhibition optimally tunes the immune/fibrotic axis in AMD remains an open question raised by macrophage-focused studies (Nourinia et al., 2018; Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016).

Expected Effect:  
In the proposed in vitro assay—complement-activated RPE monolayers exposed to IL-1β with readouts of TEER, FITC-dextran permeability, and occludin/ZO-1 transcription and membrane localization—the class mechanism predicts that a potent dual ROCK1/2 inhibitor such as SAR407899 should reduce ROCK pathway activation (↓p-MYPT1, ↓p-MLC), relax cortical actomyosin and stress fibers, and thereby re-stabilize tight junction architecture and function, yielding increased TEER, reduced paracellular flux, and normalized junctional protein localization (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Leong et al., 2025). Supportive precedent for barrier rescue by ROCK inhibition includes restoration of endothelial tight junction integrity and TEER in VEGF-challenged HRMECs by ripasudil and suppression of retinal p-MYPT1 in vivo, establishing that ROCK blockade reverses injury-induced barrier dysfunction and engages the canonical substrate axis in retina (Hida et al., 2018). In RPE, in vivo fasudil reversed Rho-pathway–induced cytoskeletal remodeling and improved barrier integrity, implying that ROCK inhibition can directly stabilize the outer BRB at the epithelial level, although quantitative TEER and occludin/ZO-1 readouts in RPE cultures are not provided in the excerpts and should be generated in the proposed assay (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016). Inflammatory cytokines can feed into ROCK pathway activation and barrier loss, and the Rho/ROCK axis is intertwined with NF-κB–dependent cytokine expression; therefore, ROCK inhibition in the IL-1β paradigm is mechanistically plausible for barrier rescue even though IL-1β-specific RPE TEER data are not available in the provided sources (Singh & Singh, 2023; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021). Given the contribution of ROCK to TGF-β–driven epithelial–mesenchymal transition and gel contraction by RPE, a dual inhibitor is also expected to suppress fibrotic gene expression trajectories in longer assays (α-SMA, collagen/fibronectin) while relieving junctional stress that promotes delamination and barrier failure, which aligns with the anti-fibrotic outcomes seen in subretinal fibrosis models after fasudil or ROCK2-selective inhibition (Nourinia et al., 2018; Zandi et al., 2025). Of note, ROCK2-targeted approaches may provide added benefit on age-related macrophage polarization and fibrosis in AMD models, but dual inhibition has demonstrated broad anti-contractile and anti-permeability effects across ocular compartments; head-to-head comparison of dual versus ROCK2-selective inhibition under IL-1β/complement challenge would be informative (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Nourinia et al., 2018). Finally, delivery modality will strongly influence the magnitude and duration of barrier rescue: topical dosing of some ROCK inhibitors can reach the posterior segment in rabbits but shows variable retinal pharmacodynamics between agents, and intravitreal routes with sustained-release formulations have been explored for fasudil to address short intraocular half-life—implications that apply to any repurposed ROCK inhibitor considered for RPE-centric therapy (Yamaguchi et al., 2017; Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021).

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths  
- Target validity and mechanistic convergence: ROCK1/2 are central regulators of cytoskeletal tension and tight-junction stability via the MYPT1/MLC and LIMK/cofilin axes, providing a direct mechanistic handle on epithelial barrier integrity and fibrotic conversion relevant to outer BRB failure and atrophic remodeling in dry AMD (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Leong et al., 2025). The same axis is mechanistically operative in retinal vascular endothelium where barrier protection by ROCK inhibition has been demonstrated quantitatively (TEER, ZO-1), strengthening the translational rationale for barrier rescue across the BRB (Hida et al., 2018).  
- RPE-relevant in vivo effect: Intravitreal fasudil reversed RPE cytoskeletal abnormalities and improved epithelial barrier integrity in vivo, demonstrating that the class mechanism extends to outer BRB pathology and is not restricted to vascular endothelial cells (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016).  
- Anti-fibrotic efficacy in choroid–RPE complex: Both dual and ROCK2-selective inhibitors reduced fibrotic markers and p-MYPT1 in murine subretinal fibrosis models after CNV, with histologic preservation of retinal architecture and reduced fibrosis volume, supporting the concept that ROCK blockade can oppose late-stage fibrotic drivers of vision loss that are inadequately addressed by anti-VEGF monotherapy (Zandi et al., 2025).  
- Broader AMD-relevant biology: ROCK inhibition reduces angiogenesis, inflammation, and fibrosis in CNV models (e.g., AMA0428), normalizes pathological vessels, and modulates age-related macrophage polarization—processes integral to AMD pathogenesis in both neovascular and atrophic trajectories (Hollanders et al., 2015; Nourinia et al., 2018).  
- Translational feasibility: Multiple ROCK inhibitors have been successfully delivered to the eye topically and intravitreally with acceptable safety, including demonstration that topical ripasudil can reach retina/choroid in rabbits and that intravitreal fasudil combinations are feasible in small human DME studies—experience that de-risks ocular delivery strategies for the class (Yamaguchi et al., 2017; Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016).

Weaknesses and Gaps  
- Absence of SAR407899-specific ocular data in RPE/AMD: The provided literature contains no quantitative potency/selectivity corroboration, ocular penetration, retinal tissue levels, or preclinical efficacy for SAR407899 in RPE barrier models, IL-1β paradigms, or AMD models; thus, key assertions in the working hypothesis (e.g., occludin/ZO-1 transcriptional rescue in RPE, primary human RPE TER recovery, ocular PK levels, ERG improvements in NaIO3) are not supported here and cannot be validated from the excerpts (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021). This represents the primary evidence gap and limits compound-specific confidence versus class-level rationale (Yamaguchi et al., 2017).  
- Isoform selectivity considerations: Several AMD-relevant studies point to ROCK2-specific biology in macrophage polarization and possibly fibrosis, with selective ROCK2 inhibition conferring benefits not fully reproduced by pan-ROCK inhibitors in the aged immune compartment; whether dual inhibition is advantageous or dilutes ROCK2-targeted immune modulation in dry AMD remains unresolved (Yamaguchi et al., 2017; Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016).  
- Incomplete RPE barrier readouts: Although class-level rescue of endothelial TEER/ZO-1 and RPE morphological barrier integrity have been shown, the excerpts lack direct RPE TEER, permeability, or occludin/ZO-1 imaging/quantification under inflammatory (IL-1β) challenge, necessitating foundational in vitro work before prioritizing SAR407899 for in vivo dry AMD studies (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Hida et al., 2018).  
- Delivery and exposure constraints: Reviews emphasize short intraocular half-lives and the need for repeated dosing or sustained-release formulations for posterior-segment efficacy; topical penetration to retina/choroid varies across molecules (e.g., ripasudil reaches the posterior segment in rabbits whereas topical fasudil did not at matched dosing), underscoring the necessity of SAR407899-specific ocular PK and exposure–response characterization to inform route and formulation (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Yamaguchi et al., 2017).  
- Off-target liabilities and dose selection: ATP-competitive ROCK inhibitors can exhibit off-target kinase inhibition at higher concentrations, raising interpretive and safety issues that must be managed through careful dose–exposure selection and orthogonal on-target biomarkers such as p-MYPT1 in ocular tissues (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Liu et al., 2018).

Programmatic Implications and Recommended Next Steps  
On the strength of the mechanistic and preclinical class data, dual ROCK1/2 inhibition remains a credible strategy to stabilize RPE junctions and suppress fibrotic conversion in dry AMD; however, SAR407899 must earn its place in this mechanism by producing compound-specific evidence on RPE barrier endpoints and ocular exposure (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Zandi et al., 2025). A staged validation package is recommended:  
- In vitro RPE barrier assays: Quantify TEER, FITC-dextran flux, and occludin/ZO-1 localization in primary human RPE or iPSC-RPE monolayers under IL-1β and complement co-challenge ± SAR407899; include p-MYPT1 and p-MLC immunoblotting or phospho-ELISA to verify on-target modulation; adopt assay conditions paralleling endothelial TEER paradigms where ROCK inhibition preserved ZO-1 and TEER (Hida et al., 2018; Kaneko et al., 2016).  
- Isoform pharmacology: Use siRNA or selective pharmacologic comparators (e.g., belumosudil for ROCK2) to benchmark dual versus ROCK2-selective effects on RPE barrier rescue and inflammatory readouts, informed by the macrophage polarization literature in AMD (Nourinia et al., 2018; Yamaguchi et al., 2017).  
- Ocular PK and delivery: Establish intraocular distribution and time–concentration profiles for SAR407899 in relevant species after topical and intravitreal routes; given literature on short half-life and variable posterior penetration, consider sustained-release intravitreal formulations analogous to those explored for fasudil if topical exposure proves inadequate (Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021; Yamaguchi et al., 2017).  
- In vivo efficacy: Progress to subretinal fibrosis or oxidative injury models that interrogate outer BRB integrity and fibrotic remodeling (e.g., CNV-associated fibrosis with extended fibrotic endpoints) to test whether SAR407899 reproduces the anti-fibrotic and barrier-preserving signals seen with fasudil/belumosudil, incorporating p-MYPT1 as a pharmacodynamic biomarker (Zandi et al., 2025).  
- Combinatorial positioning: Given evidence that ROCK inhibition complements anti-VEGF therapy by reducing inflammation and fibrosis and may normalize pathological angiogenesis without abrogating physiological revascularization, consider combination designs if SAR407899 shows single-agent barrier/fibrosis activity (Hollanders et al., 2015; Halasz & Townes-Anderson, 2016).

Bottom line: Repurposing SAR407899 for dry AMD is mechanistically well-justified at the class level, but the absence of SAR407899-specific ocular and RPE data in the provided literature requires a focused preclinical package to substantiate the specific claims in the hypothesis and to determine whether dual inhibition delivers the desired balance of epithelial barrier rescue and anti-fibrotic efficacy relative to ROCK2-selective alternatives (Nourinia et al., 2018; Yamaguchi et al., 2017).

I cannot answer specific claims in the hypothesis regarding SAR407899’s kinase selectivity (>200-fold), ocular penetration to retina (~0.3 µM), full TER recovery in primary human RPE, or functional rescue in NaIO3 models (+28% ERG b-wave), because the provided context contains no SAR407899-specific biochemical, PK, or preclinical efficacy data supporting those statements (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021).
