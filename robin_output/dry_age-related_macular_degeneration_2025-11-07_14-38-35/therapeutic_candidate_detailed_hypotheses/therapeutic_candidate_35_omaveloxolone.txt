Proposal for Omaveloxolone (RTA-408; Tocris 5030)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Omaveloxolone (RTA-408; also known as CDDO-DFPA; N-(2-Cyano-3,12-dioxo-28-noroleana-1,9(11)-dien-17-yl)-2,2-difluoropropanamide) is a semisynthetic oleanane triterpenoid created by lead optimization of the natural product oleanolic acid, a plant-derived pentacyclic triterpene produced via the mevalonate pathway (squalene → 2,3-oxidosqualene → β-amyrin → erythrodiol → oleanolic acid) and abundantly available from Oleaceae (e.g., olive) and other botanical sources (over 1,600 species; Nakagami, 2016; Masullo et al., 2017; Pollier & Goossens, 2012). The oleanane scaffold was iteratively diversified by Sporn, Gribble, Honda and collaborators to introduce enone and cyanoenone Michael acceptors on rings A and C, yielding the CDDO class (2-cyano-3,12-dioxo-oleana-1,9(11)-dien-28-oic acid and derivatives) as extraordinarily potent anti-inflammatory and Nrf2-inducing agents; C-28 esters/amides and further A/C-ring modifications produced multiple clinical candidates including CDDO-Me (bardoxolone methyl, RTA-402) and RTA-408 (omaveloxolone) (Honda et al., 2000; Pollier & Goossens, 2012; Cheng et al., 2021). RTA-408 specifically is a C17-modified difluoro-propanamide on an oleanane core and belongs to the second generation of synthetic triterpenoids designed to preserve high Nrf2 potency while controlling electrophilicity and pharmacokinetics (Nakagami, 2016; Cuadrado et al., 2019).

At the pharmacological class level, synthetic oleanane triterpenoids are electrophilic “antioxidant inflammation modulators” that activate the KEAP1–NRF2–ARE pathway through covalent (but often reversible) Michael addition to redox-sensitive cysteines on KEAP1, particularly Cys151 on the BTB domain, thereby stabilizing Nrf2 and inducing a coordinated cytoprotective gene program; they concurrently suppress inducible pro-inflammatory mediators (e.g., iNOS, COX-2) with dose-dependent bifunctionality (cytoprotection at low concentrations; pro-oxidant/apoptotic signaling at higher exposure) (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2017; Liby et al., 2007). This scaffold has been extensively explored as a drug platform across oncology, nephrology, cardiopulmonary, inflammatory, and neurological indications, with bardoxolone methyl and omaveloxolone translating to clinical studies and, for omaveloxolone, regulatory approval in Friedreich’s ataxia (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Zahir et al., 2025). Within ophthalmology, oleanane triterpenoids and close congeners (dh404, CDDO-TFEA, CDDO-EA, CDDO-Me) have shown robust Nrf2-mediated protection in retinal models, and RTA-408 itself has been advanced into ocular clinical trials evaluating postoperative ocular pain/inflammation and corneal endothelial cell loss after cataract surgery, positioning this mechanism squarely within ocular translational scope (Cheng et al., 2021).

Therapeutic History  
The oleanane triterpenoid class has a two-decade translational history that began with demonstrations that cyano-enone–bearing derivatives are sub-nanomolar inducers of the Nrf2 transcriptome and suppressors of inflammatory enzymes in vitro and in vivo, a property mapped by SAR to the presence/placement of A-ring 1-en-3-one and C-ring 9(11)-en-12-one Michael acceptors (Honda et al., 2000; Nakagami, 2016). Bardoxolone methyl (CDDO-Me) showed pharmacodynamic activity in humans (ARE-gene induction in tumor biopsies; renal function improvements), but its BEACON Phase 3 trial in advanced CKD was halted for cardiovascular adverse events—an outcome that did not negate class activity but highlighted the need for indication-specific risk management and exclusion criteria (e.g., high BNP, poor baseline eGFR) (Masullo et al., 2017; Cuadrado et al., 2019). Subsequent controlled studies in rarer CKD-associated diseases continued under tighter cardiometabolic selection, while medicinal-chemistry efforts generated second-generation analogs including fluorinated scaffolds (e.g., omaveloxolone) to tune reactivity and PK/PD (Sun et al., 2017).

For omaveloxolone specifically, preclinical pharmacology established Nrf2-dependent induction of glutathione homeostasis genes and improvements in mitochondrial respiration; systemic PK in primates/humans showed dose proportionality and measurable tissue distribution (including liver, lung, brain), with PBMC and tissue Nrf2 target-gene induction at clinically achievable exposures (Reisman et al., 2019). In oncology, a first-in-human dose-escalation study in advanced solid tumors reported acceptable tolerability with pharmacodynamic evidence of Nrf2 pathway activation in PBMCs; in dermatology, topical RTA-408 lotion activated Nrf2 in human skin and was well tolerated (Creelan et al., 2017; Reisman et al., 2019). In neurology, omaveloxolone advanced through the MOXIe program for Friedreich’s ataxia with evidence of neurologic benefit at clinical doses ≥80 mg and exposures concordant with preclinical pharmacology (Naidu & Dinkova-Kostova, 2020; Reisman et al., 2019; Zahir et al., 2025).

Ocularly, multiple oleanane triterpenoid derivatives have shown benefit in retinal oxidative-stress models: dh404 induced canonical Nrf2 target genes (NQO1, HO-1, GCLM), reduced inflammatory mediators, and decreased retinal vascular leakage; CDDO-TFEA and CDDO-EA activated NQO1 in photoreceptor-derived 661W cells and CDDO-TFEA reduced light-induced retinal degeneration (Cheng et al., 2021). RS9, an OA-derived analog generated via microbial transformation, showed superior potency versus CDDO-Me in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) protection, increased retinal HO-1/NQO1 at low doses, and suppressed neovascularization and blood-brain barrier hyperpermeability in vivo, supporting the generalizable ocular protective potential of optimized oleanane triterpenoids (Cheng et al., 2021). Critically for translational relevance, omaveloxolone itself has documented protective activity in human fetal RPE at low-nanomolar concentrations (≤100 nM), where it prevents H₂O₂-induced apoptosis/necrosis by shifting the Bcl-2:Bax ratio and reducing protein glutathionylation—biomarkers linked to oxidative damage and barrier compromise in RPE (Cheng et al., 2021). Moreover, RTA-408 is the only oleanolic acid analogue noted to have entered clinical trials in ocular indications (cataract-surgery–related pain/inflammation and corneal endothelial cell loss), demonstrating sponsor commitment to ophthalmic development and an initial tolerability signal for ocular use (Cheng et al., 2021). No clinical reports were found in the provided context for omaveloxolone in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) specifically, nor in geographic atrophy; thus, this is a repurposing proposal grounded in mechanistic and preclinical ocular convergence rather than direct AMD clinical precedent (Cheng et al., 2021).

Mechanism of Action  
The KEAP1–NRF2 node is a cysteine-centric electrophile sensor system that orchestrates the phase-2 antioxidant and cytoprotective response. Under basal conditions, KEAP1 recruits NRF2 to the CUL3 E3 ligase complex for ubiquitination and proteasomal turnover. Electrophiles such as cyano-enone triterpenoids react with KEAP1 cysteine sensors—most prominently Cys151 in the BTB domain—to disrupt CUL3 docking and/or alter KEAP1 conformation, thereby stabilizing NRF2, allowing nuclear translocation, heterodimerization with small Maf proteins, and binding to antioxidant response elements (ARE) to induce a transcriptional battery including NQO1, HO-1 (HMOX1), glutathione biosynthesis enzymes (GCLC, GCLM), thioredoxin and thioredoxin reductase (Txn1, Txnrd1), sulfiredoxin (Srxn1), and other detoxifying/antioxidative genes (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2017; Reisman et al., 2019). Oleanane cyano-enone triterpenoids are among the most potent NRF2 activators known, with structure–activity relationships showing strong correlations between the presence/geometry of Michael acceptors and both iNOS suppression and NQO1 induction across series; mechanistic profiling indicates covalent yet reversible conjugation to protein thiols underlies this pharmacology (Naidu & Dinkova-Kostova, 2020).

Within this class, omaveloxolone is an electrophilic Michael acceptor–bearing, difluoro-propanamide oleanane that activates Nrf2 at nanomolar concentrations in reporter assays and upregulates canonical ARE-target genes (NQO1, HO-1, GCLM, TxnR1), consistent with its cyano-enone pharmacophore engaging KEAP1 Cys151 and allied redox sensors (Sun et al., 2017; Cuadrado et al., 2019). In systemic models, omaveloxolone increases glutathione concentrations, supports mitochondrial respiration, and normalizes metabolic/energetic parameters downstream of Nrf2 activation—effects mechanistically rationalized by Nrf2-driven induction of NADPH-generating enzymes and mitochondrial support genes, which collectively bolster reductive and ATP capacity in stressed cells (Reisman et al., 2019). In immunologic contexts, oleanane triterpenoids suppress IFN-γ–induced nitric oxide signaling in macrophages, reduce reactive oxygen and nitrotyrosine production by myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), and can inhibit IKKβ/NF-κB signaling (demonstrated for CDDO-Me), contributing to anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory actions that are secondary but complementary to NRF2-mediated cytoprotection (Creelan et al., 2017; Sporn et al., 2007). The class is bifunctional: low-to-moderate exposures favor NRF2-dependent cytoprotection and redox quenching, whereas higher exposures can increase oxidative burden and trigger apoptosis—an important consideration when targeting delicate ocular tissues (Liby et al., 2007).

For ocular cell types relevant to dry AMD, multiple lines of evidence anchor Nrf2/ARE biology. In the retina, oleanane triterpenoids (dh404, CDDO derivatives) induce NQO1/HO-1/GCLM, reduce inflammatory mediators and vascular leakage, and protect photoreceptors and retinal structure in oxidative-stress models; RS9 dosing increases retinal NQO1/HO-1 in vivo (Cheng et al., 2021). In human fetal RPE, omaveloxolone prevents H₂O₂-induced apoptosis and necrosis at ≤100 nM, shifts Bcl-2:Bax pro-survival balance, and reduces protein S-glutathionylation, a reversible oxidative modification implicated in disruption of cytoskeletal and junctional complexes that maintain barrier integrity (Cheng et al., 2021). Taken together, these data support that omaveloxolone, via KEAP1 cysteine sensing and Nrf2 activation in RPE and adjacent retinal cells, can rapidly enhance the endogenous antioxidant program, elevate glutathione/NADPH buffering, mitigate protein oxidative adducts, and suppress inflammatory amplification—biomechanistic axes directly relevant to RPE survival and barrier function under oxidative stress (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2017; Cheng et al., 2021).

Pharmacologically, omaveloxolone exhibits high plasma protein binding and broad tissue distribution preclinically, with oral dosing in primates/humans yielding exposures that induce Nrf2 targets in PBMCs and distribute to metabolically active tissues; topical dermal dosing in humans activates cutaneous Nrf2 without untoward safety signals (Reisman et al., 2019). For ocular dosing, clinical testing of RTA-408 in cataract-surgery contexts (ocular pain/inflammation, corneal endothelial protection) establishes feasibility of achieving pharmacologically relevant ocular exposure with acceptable tolerability in humans, though detailed ocular PK has not been published in the provided excerpts (Cheng et al., 2021).

Expected Effect  
Assay and hypothesis: In a human RPE monolayer model subjected to H₂O₂-induced oxidative injury, omaveloxolone is expected to covalently and reversibly modify KEAP1 cysteines (notably Cys151), stabilize and activate Nrf2, and robustly induce the ARE-regulated antioxidant gene set (NQO1, HO-1, GCLM, TXNRD1, SRXN1), thereby increasing cellular glutathione, NADPH-dependent antioxidant enzyme activity, and mitochondrial resilience; these changes should reduce protein S-glutathionylation, prevent loss of perijunctional actomyosin integrity, and preserve tight-junction belts, culminating in restoration of transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) and reduction of paracellular flux (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2017; Reisman et al., 2019; Cheng et al., 2021). Mechanistic support for each step:

- Target engagement and Nrf2 activation in RPE: Cyano-enone triterpenoids are sensed by KEAP1 Cys151, stabilizing Nrf2; omaveloxolone activates Nrf2 at nanomolar concentrations and induces NQO1/HO-1/GCLM/TxnR1 in human cells, and has shown protection in human fetal RPE at ≤100 nM against H₂O₂-induced death with suppression of protein glutathionylation—an oxidative adduct linked to structural protein dysfunction and barrier failure (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2017; Cheng et al., 2021).

- Evidence that ocular cells mount an ARE program: In retinal models, dh404 and CDDO derivatives upregulate NQO1, HO-1, and GCLM; RS9 increases retinal NQO1/HO-1 in vivo, confirming that ARE-responsive gene expression is present and inducible in ocular tissues relevant to barrier function and oxidative injury (Cheng et al., 2021).

- Mitochondrial and redox effects: Omaveloxolone increases glutathione and promotes mitochondrial respiration in preclinical systems, and Nrf2 regulates a broad antioxidative and bioenergetic program that supplies reductive capacity (NADPH) and detoxifying enzymes, supporting RPE energy-demanding barrier maintenance under stress (Reisman et al., 2019).

- Barrier functional correlates in vivo and in vitro: Oleanane triterpenoids reduce retinal vascular leakage in models of inflammation/oxidative stress, and RTA-408 decreases oxidative protein glutathionylation in RPE—a mechanistic event that plausibly preserves junctional complexes and TEER under H₂O₂ stress, even though explicit TEER data for RTA-408 in RPE are not provided here (Cheng et al., 2021).

Operationally, we anticipate a biphasic exposure–response: low nanomolar concentrations should maximize cytoprotective Nrf2 signaling without triggering the pro-oxidant/apoptotic bifurcation observed at higher exposures for this class (Liby et al., 2007). In line with available RPE data, we would start at 1–100 nM omaveloxolone, quantify early ARE-gene induction (1–6 h), glutathione/NADPH pools, protein S-glutathionylation, and image ZO-1/occludin belts, then assess TEER and fluorescein (or 70 kDa dextran) paracellular flux after H₂O₂ challenge; photoreceptor-derived 661W and co-culture systems can be used to evaluate paracrine rescue (Cheng et al., 2021).

As a translational bridge, we would prioritize in vivo models where oleanane triterpenoids have shown benefit (light-induced retinal damage; ischemia–reperfusion; BRB leakage models) to confirm retinal/RPE exposure and barrier readouts, leveraging prior triterpenoid ocular pharmacology experience (Cheng et al., 2021).

Drug delivery considerations: Given lipophilicity and protein binding typical of this class, formulation will influence retinal/RPE exposure. Approaches used for related triterpenoids—e.g., amorphous solid dispersions for oral delivery (CDDO-Me) or nanoparticle carriers for ocular delivery (demonstrated for ursolic acid)—illustrate feasible strategies to reach ocular tissues while minimizing systemic exposure; the fact that RTA-408 has entered ocular clinical trials (post-cataract endpoints) supports translatable tolerability of ophthalmic administration (Cheng et al., 2021).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Mechanistic fit to dry AMD biology: Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction are central to RPE degeneration in dry AMD; omaveloxolone engages KEAP1 cysteine sensors to activate Nrf2 and induce a comprehensive, rapid-acting ARE program that elevates glutathione/NADPH, upregulates HO-1/NQO1/GCL-enzymes, and improves mitochondrial bioenergetics—changes expected to blunt H₂O₂-triggered barrier loss and death in RPE (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2017; Reisman et al., 2019). The oleanane scaffold’s larger, conformationally constrained electrophile is argued to offer improved selectivity and controllable PK/PD versus small electrophiles, potentially reducing off-target liabilities at therapeutic concentrations (Cuadrado et al., 2019).

- Direct RPE evidence and ocular translational precedent: RTA-408 protects human fetal RPE from H₂O₂-induced apoptosis/necrosis at ≤100 nM and reduces protein S-glutathionylation, a lesion consistent with cytoskeletal/junctional dysfunction; related oleanane triterpenoids induce Nrf2 targets in retina, reduce BRB leak, and attenuate oxidative-stress retinal damage in vivo (Cheng et al., 2021). RTA-408 is the only OA analogue documented to have entered ocular clinical trials (post-cataract endpoints), supporting feasibility of ophthalmic development (Cheng et al., 2021).

- Clinical-stage agent with known human PK/PD: Omaveloxolone has extensive preclinical and early clinical characterization, including primate/human PK with tissue distribution and PBMC Nrf2 target induction, topical proof of mechanism in human skin, and multiple oral clinical programs (oncology, FA), reducing translational uncertainty compared to earlier chemical matter (Reisman et al., 2019; Creelan et al., 2017; Zahir et al., 2025).

- Class potency and breadth: Oleanane cyano-enones are among the most potent known Nrf2 activators; RS9’s superior retinal potency and CDDO tethered analogs’ efficacy in retinal models argue that the scaffold class, optimized for ocular delivery, can achieve pharmacodynamic effects in the eye at low doses (Cuadrado et al., 2019; Cheng et al., 2021).

Risks and weaknesses  
- Electrophile-driven bifunctionality and narrow window: The same α,β-unsaturated carbonyls that confer high potency can, at higher exposures, increase oxidative stress and trigger apoptosis; dose selection and exposure control will be critical in the eye (Liby et al., 2007; Cheng et al., 2021).

- Class safety history: Bardoxolone methyl’s cardiovascular signal in late-stage CKD underscores the need for indication- and formulation-specific safety strategies; while not probative for ocular topical/intraocular dosing, it motivates cautious systemic exposure limits and cardiovascular exclusion if systemic routes are used (Nakagami, 2016; Sun et al., 2017).

- Incomplete AMD-specific evidence: The provided literature does not report omaveloxolone in dry AMD or geographic atrophy models; most ocular data derive from oxidative-stress/retinal injury and postoperative inflammation paradigms, which are supportive but not definitive for the multifactorial dry AMD pathobiology (Cheng et al., 2021).

- Delivery and exposure uncertainties: Omaveloxolone’s lipophilicity and strong protein binding complicate prediction of ocular distribution; although ocular clinical testing exists for RTA-408, detailed ocular PK/PD are not included in the excerpts, and tailored ophthalmic formulations may be required to achieve RPE/retinal exposure without anterior-segment sequestration (Reisman et al., 2019; Cheng et al., 2021).

Program recommendations  
- In vitro RPE barrier program: Establish a standardized H₂O₂-induced barrier disruption assay in human RPE monolayers with readouts for TEER, large-molecule paracellular flux, ZO-1/occludin belt integrity, protein S-glutathionylation, and apoptosis/caspase activation. Titrate omaveloxolone at 1–100 nM to define the cytoprotective plateau and the onset of bifurcation, and confirm KEAP1–NRF2 mechanism using pharmacologic/CRISPR Nrf2 blockade (Cheng et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2017; Liby et al., 2007).

- Ocular in vivo models: Utilize light-induced photoreceptor injury and retinal I/R models, where the class has shown benefit, to benchmark efficacy and establish exposure–response (retinal NQO1/HO-1 induction, BRB leakage suppression), followed by NaIO₃-induced RPE injury to approximate dry AMD-relevant oxidative RPE loss (Cheng et al., 2021). Include RS9 and a non-electrophilic control to contextualize class effects (Cheng et al., 2021).

- Delivery strategy: Develop an ophthalmic formulation (e.g., micelles, lipid nanoparticles) guided by prior triterpenoid formulation successes (amorphous dispersion for CDDO-Me; UA nanoparticles) to target posterior-segment delivery via topical, suprachoroidal, or intravitreal routes depending on PK modeling and in vivo biodistribution; minimize systemic absorption to mitigate class-related CV risk (Cheng et al., 2021).

- Safety pharmacology: Delineate the therapeutic index around ocular exposures, including ERG and OCT endpoints, and histopathology for photoreceptors, RPE, and ganglion cells; monitor inflammatory and angiogenic pathways to rule out unanticipated pathway cross-talk at sustained Nrf2 activation (Liby et al., 2007).

Conclusion  
On balance, omaveloxolone is a mechanistically well-aligned, clinically de-risked Nrf2 activator with direct protective activity in human RPE and supportive in vivo retinal pharmacology from its chemical class, making it a strong repurposing candidate for dry AMD focused on RPE barrier preservation under oxidative stress (Cheng et al., 2021; Cuadrado et al., 2019). The primary development challenges are exposure control to avoid electrophile-mediated bifunctionality, demonstration of efficacy in AMD-relevant RPE injury models and barrier assays (including TEER), and optimization of ocular delivery to achieve posterior-segment pharmacodynamics with minimal systemic exposure (Liby et al., 2007; Cheng et al., 2021; Reisman et al., 2019). Given the magnitude of unmet need in dry AMD and the centrality of oxidative injury to RPE pathology, a staged program beginning with RPE barrier assays and retinal oxidative stress models, followed by formulation-enabled ocular PK/PD and safety, is justified and has a reasonable probability of technical success based on the assembled evidence (Cheng et al., 2021; Reisman et al., 2019).
