Proposal for JQ1 (Selleck S7110)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
JQ1 (Selleck S7110) is a synthetic small-molecule thienotriazolodiazepine developed as a first-in-class, highly selective competitive inhibitor of the bromodomain and extra-terminal (BET) acetyl-lysine reader family (BRD2/3/4, BRDT) that binds the acetyl-lysine pocket of BET bromodomains (BD1 and BD2) to displace BET proteins from chromatin and suppress transcription at dependent enhancers and promoters (pan-BET, BD1/BD2 non-selective) (Zhao et al., 2017; Zhu et al., 2023). BET bromodomains recognize acetylated histone tails at active enhancers/super-enhancers and recruit coactivator assemblies that couple to RNA polymerase II to drive high-amplitude transcription of inflammatory, immune, and stress-response genes; small-molecule BET inhibition collapses these assemblies and blunts stimulus-induced transcriptional programs in immune and neural cells (Zhao et al., 2017). Within ophthalmology, JQ1 has been deployed intravitreally or systemically in multiple preclinical models, including inherited photoreceptor degeneration (rd10 retinitis pigmentosa), oxidative-stress retinal injury (sodium iodate), light-damage photoreceptor injury, experimental autoimmune uveitis (with other BET inhibitors), corneal fibrosis, and proliferative vitreoretinopathy, where it has shown neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, and anti-fibrotic activity (Zhao et al., 2017; Zou et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2023; Eichenbaum, 2020; Liao et al., 2024). The BET modality also includes domain-selective ligands (e.g., BD2-preferring apabetalone/RVX-208) and targeted protein degraders (e.g., dBET6, a cereblon-recruiting JQ1-based PROTAC) that achieve complementary pharmacology by either sparing BD1 or depleting BRD2/3/4 protein, respectively (Zhu et al., 2023; Shahid et al., 2022).

Therapeutic History  
- Inherited photoreceptor degeneration (rd10, Pde6b mutant): A single intravitreal injection of JQ1 (2 µl of 0.1 mM at PN14) preserved photoreceptor number through PN24 (>90% of baseline), improved ERG a- and b-wave amplitudes, reduced TUNEL-positive photoreceptor apoptosis and caspase-3/7 activity, and markedly suppressed retinal microglial activation/infiltration (IBA1, TSPO, CD68) with cytokine mRNAs (TNFα, MCP-1, IL-1β, IL-6, RANTES) reduced to WT levels in whole retinas and in sorted retinal microglia (MCP-1 also lowered by ELISA) (Zhao et al., 2017). Complementary in vitro work showed JQ1 pre-treatment abolished LPS-induced upregulation of TNFα, MCP-1, IL-1β, IL-6 and RANTES and reduced microglial proliferation and migration by ~70%, with mechanistic dissection implicating BET2 (BRD2) and the BD2 (Brom2) domain in microglial activation; a BD2-preferring ligand (RVX208) suppressed cytokine induction more effectively than a BD1-preferring ligand (Olinone) (Zhao et al., 2017). In vivo, BET2 protein is increased at PN24 in rd10 retinas, correlating with peak microglial activation and photoreceptor degeneration (Zhao et al., 2017).  
- Oxidative-stress retinal/RPE injury (sodium iodate, SI): JQ1 suppressed BRD4-induced STING transcription by decreasing chromatin accessibility (reduced H3 acetylation, RNA Pol II recruitment) at the STING locus, curtailed cGAS–STING activation, reduced microglia/macrophage accumulation (IBA1+) in retina/subretinal spaces, prevented RPE/retinal degeneration, and preserved retinal integrity without detectable adverse retinal morphology when used alone (in vitro ARPE cells and in vivo SI models across ages) (Zou et al., 2022). Mechanistically, BRD4 upregulation under oxidative stress promotes STING and inflammatory outputs; JQ1 or BRD4 depletion enhances autophagic flux (LC3II), accelerates autophagic enclosure/clearance of cytosolic DNA, reduces dsDNA accumulation, and thereby attenuates STING-mediated inflammation; autophagy inhibitors (BafA1, chloroquine) block these effects, indicating an autophagy-dependent mechanism (Zou et al., 2022).  
- Light-damage photoreceptor injury: The JQ1-based PROTAC dBET6 delivered systemically degraded BRD2/3/4 in mouse retina, improved retinal responsiveness and visual acuity after light damage, suppressed macrophage/microglia activation and Müller gliosis, reduced photoreceptor death, and inhibited LD-induced cGAS–STING activation in retinal microglia; importantly, the PROTAC strategy is motivated by compensatory BRD4 accumulation observed with JQ1 (Zhu et al., 2023).  
- RPE cell inflammation (ARPE-19): Multiple BET inhibitors (including JQ1) reduced etoposide-induced IL-6 and IL-8 secretion, decreased NF-κB p65 activity, and reduced acetylation of p53 at K382 in human RPE without major cytotoxicity; the anti-inflammatory effects appeared SIRT1-independent (SIRT1 inhibition or knockdown did not reverse IL-8 lowering), although SIRT1 pharmacology altered the p53 acetylation response (Hytti et al., 2016). BET inhibitors slightly decreased baseline MTT conversion but reduced etoposide cytotoxicity (lower LDH release, higher viability), consistent with modest cytoprotection under this stressor (Hytti et al., 2016).  
- Endothelial barrier inflammation: In HUVEC transwell monolayers, TNF-α decreased TEER, whereas pretreatment with JQ1 or BD2-selective apabetalone prevented the TEER drop, maintaining monolayer integrity; BRD4 inhibition reduced inflammatory marker expression and midkine, and the authors concluded BRD4 inhibition preserved barrier function under inflammatory challenge; while promising for barrier biology, these data were generated in vascular endothelial cells, not RPE (Shahid et al., 2022).  
- Other ophthalmic signals: BET blockade has downregulated Th17-associated genes in experimental autoimmune uveitis, ameliorated acute RGC loss after NMDA by suppressing retinal cytokines (~50% reduction in TNFα, IL-1β, MCP-1, RANTES) and preserving RGC number/gene expression (~2×), and reduced/reversed corneal scarring via BRD4 inhibition and Nrf2-dependent antioxidant induction without impairing epithelial regeneration (Eichenbaum, 2020). A separate RPE-centric multi-omics PVR study used intraocular eNano-JQ1 to show potent anti-fibrotic efficacy (reduced αSMA, thinner membranes) with favorable ocular safety (ERG, histology, TUNEL) over seven days, supporting feasibility of sustained intraocular JQ1 delivery (Liao et al., 2024).  
- Application to AMD: JQ1 has not been tested clinically for dry AMD; however, in vivo SI oxidative-injury studies model RPE/retinal degeneration relevant to geographic atrophy and identify cGAS–STING repression and autophagy-mediated cytosolic DNA clearance as protective mechanisms; moreover, cGAS/STING promoter accessibility is increased in macular retinas from GA patients, strengthening translational plausibility for dry AMD (Zou et al., 2022).

Mechanism of Action  
BET chromatin reader displacement and inflammatory super-enhancer suppression  
- BET proteins, particularly BRD4, bind acetylated histone tails via BD1 and BD2 to stabilize enhancer/super-enhancer coactivator complexes and recruit Mediator and RNA Pol II, enabling rapid, high-output transcription of stimulus-responsive genes (e.g., NF-κB target programs and innate immune effectors) (Zhao et al., 2017). JQ1 competitively occupies the acetyl-lysine pocket in BD1/BD2, displacing BET proteins and causing collapse of the transcriptional assembly with consequent repression of inflammatory/immune gene expression; this has been demonstrated across macrophages, microglia, brain, and retina (Zhao et al., 2017). NF-κB drives dynamic super-enhancer formation during inflammation, providing a mechanistic intersection whereby BRD4 coactivation of NF-κB can be neutralized by BET inhibition to broadly downregulate cytokine programs and adhesion molecules (Zhao et al., 2017).  
- In retinal microglia and degenerating retina, BET2 (BRD2) and the BD2 (Brom2) domain appear to dominate cytokine induction and activation phenotypes; pharmacologic BD2 targeting (RVX208) and siRNA to BET2 blunt expression of TNFα, IL-1β, MCP-1, IL-6, and RANTES and curb microglial proliferation/migration, whereas BD1 targeting (Olinone) exerts narrower effects (notably migration and RANTES) (Zhao et al., 2017). In vivo, intravitreal JQ1 suppresses microglial infiltration into ONL/subretinal spaces and lowers retinal cytokine mRNAs and MCP-1 protein, consistent with an immunomodulatory mechanism downstream of BET displacement (Zhao et al., 2017).  
- In ARPE-19 cells, BET inhibition reduces etoposide-induced IL-6/IL-8 secretion and NF-κB activity without major cytotoxicity; the anti-inflammatory effect persists when SIRT1 is inhibited or knocked down, indicating SIRT1 independence for cytokine suppression in these conditions, even though BET inhibition reduces acetyl-p53 (Lys382) via a SIRT1-interacting axis (Hytti et al., 2016). These data collectively establish BET blockade as a chromatin-level anti-inflammatory mechanism across retinal microglia and RPE contexts (Hytti et al., 2016).

BRD4–cGAS–STING repression and autophagy-dependent cytosolic DNA clearance in oxidative stress  
- Under oxidative stress in RPE and retina, BRD4 is induced and directly promotes STING transcription; JQ1 counteracts this by reducing histone acetylation (e.g., H3 acetyl marks) and phosphorylated Pol II occupancy at the STING locus, thereby reducing chromatin accessibility and transcriptional output (Zou et al., 2022). This leads to reduced cGAS–STING activation, dampened downstream NF-κB/IRF-mediated inflammatory gene induction, decreased retinal microglia/macrophage recruitment, and protection against RPE/retina degeneration in vivo (Zou et al., 2022).  
- Beyond transcriptional repression, BRD4 inhibition potentiates autophagy: JQ1 or BRD4 knockdown increases LC3II, accelerates enclosure of cytosolic dsDNA by autophagosomes (visualized with GFP-LC3 and Cy3-DNA), reduces accumulation of oxidized cytosolic DNA (a potent innate immune agonist), and these effects require intact autophagic flux (blocked by chloroquine/BafA1), directly linking BET inhibition to enhanced clearance of DNA debris that drives cGAS activation (Zou et al., 2022). This dual mechanism—STING transcriptional silencing and autophagic DNA clearance—aligns with decreased innate immune activation in SI-injured retinas and provides a mechanistic bridge to geographic atrophy in which cGAS/STING promoters exhibit increased accessibility (Zou et al., 2022).

Barrier biology and tight junctions: what is and is not known  
- In vascular endothelium, BRD4 inhibition with JQ1 or BD2-selective apabetalone preserved TEER and monolayer integrity during TNF-α stimulation, reduced NF-κB activation, decreased pro-inflammatory markers, and lowered midkine expression, indicating a direct barrier-protective effect in inflamed endothelium (Shahid et al., 2022). While the cell type differs, these results are proof-of-concept that BET inhibition can functionally maintain barrier resistance under cytokine challenge.  
- Critically, across the RPE literature excerpts provided, no direct measurements of RPE barrier function (TEER), tight-junction proteins (ZO-1/TJP1, claudin-19/CLDN19), or junctional continuity were reported for JQ1 or any BET inhibitor in ARPE-19 or primary RPE under inflammatory/oxidative stress; multiple excerpts explicitly note the absence of tight-junction/barrier metrics and recommend further study in advanced models (Hytti et al., 2016; Eichenbaum, 2020; Zhu et al., 2023). Therefore, a mechanistic claim that JQ1 increases H3K27ac at TJP1/CLDN19 enhancers and directly raises ZO-1/claudin-19 transcription in RPE is not supported by existing evidence in the provided corpus.

Pharmacology, delivery, and safety considerations  
- JQ1 exhibits a short systemic half-life (~1 h reported elsewhere and noted in the retinal context), and in rd10, photoreceptor protection after a single intravitreal dose waned as drug dissociated from targets and diffused, with higher intravitreal concentrations not improving efficacy, motivating sustained ocular delivery strategies (e.g., nanoparticles, osmotic pumps) or alternative modalities (e.g., BET PROTACs) (Zhao et al., 2017; Zhu et al., 2023). Local formulation advances such as eNano-JQ1 achieved seven-day intraocular release with favorable safety and efficacy in a PVR model (Liao et al., 2024).  
- BET competitive inhibitors can trigger cell-cycle arrest and modestly reduce metabolic activity in vitro; ARPE-19 studies showed slight decreases in MTT conversion at baseline but no major viability loss, and in some assays BET inhibitors reduced etoposide cytotoxicity (Hytti et al., 2016). In SI models, JQ1 alone exhibited no overt adverse retinal morphology/viability, and systemic dBET6 achieved retinal BET degradation “without detectable toxicity” at the reported regimen (Zou et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2023). Notably, compensatory BRD4 upregulation was observed with JQ1 in certain contexts, potentially blunting efficacy—a liability that targeted degradation (dBET6) may overcome (Zhu et al., 2023).

Therapeutic implications for dry AMD  
Collectively, the ocular data support an anti-inflammatory/innate-immunity–modulating, neuroprotective role for BET inhibition in retina/RPE that is directly relevant to dry AMD pathobiology: oxidative stress-driven cytosolic DNA leakage, cGAS–STING activation, NF-κB–dependent cytokine induction, microglia/macrophage recruitment, and downstream neurodegeneration are all attenuated by JQ1 or dBET6 in preclinical models (Zou et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2023). However, there is no direct evidence that JQ1 improves RPE tight-junction expression or TEER; any barrier benefit inferred for RPE is, at present, extrapolated from endothelial TEER preservation and from general anti-inflammatory rescue in RPE/retina (Shahid et al., 2022; Hytti et al., 2016).

Expected Effect  
The proposed hypothesis states that JQ1 will relieve repression of tight-junction enhancers in RPE under inflammatory/oxidative stress, increasing H3K27ac at TJP1/CLDN19 loci, boosting ZO-1/claudin-19 transcription, and enhancing TEER; mechanistically, it posits that BRD4 occupancy at inflammatory loci displaces transcriptional machinery from tight-junction enhancers, and BET displacement by JQ1 restores enhancer-promoter function. Based on the available literature, this precise enhancer-activation model is not supported; instead, JQ1 typically represses transcription at BET-dependent loci and reduces histone acetylation and Pol II recruitment at targeted promoters (e.g., STING), thereby closing chromatin accessibility rather than increasing it (Zou et al., 2022). No data in the provided corpus demonstrate increased H3K27ac or enhanced transcription at TJP1/CLDN19 in RPE after JQ1, nor any RPE TEER restoration with JQ1 in IL-1β or oxidative-stress paradigms; multiple sources explicitly note the absence of RPE barrier metrics and tight-junction readouts in prior BET/RPE work (Hytti et al., 2016).

A more evidence-grounded expected effect in IL-1β- or TNF-α-treated RPE is indirect barrier preservation via upstream anti-inflammatory mechanisms:  
- JQ1 should reduce NF-κB–driven cytokine induction (e.g., IL-6, IL-8) in ARPE-19 based on prior findings with etoposide models, and suppress cGAS–STING signaling under oxidative stress by repressing STING transcription and promoting autophagic clearance of cytosolic DNA (Hytti et al., 2016; Zou et al., 2022). These actions plausibly decrease paracrine/autocrine junction-disrupting cues, which could secondarily stabilize tight-junction structure and TEER, as supported by endothelial TEER preservation in HUVECs during TNF-α challenge (Shahid et al., 2022).  
- In microglia-RPE co-culture or in retinal tissue, JQ1 would be expected to reduce the load of microglia-derived cytokines/chemokines that exacerbate epithelial barrier dysfunction and photoreceptor stress, via demonstrated suppression of retinal microglial activation and cytokine production (Zhao et al., 2017).  

Given the lack of published RPE barrier data, the appropriate experimental expectations and measurements for the proposed IL-1β RPE assay are:  
- Primary outcome: TEER rescue and paracellular permeability under IL-1β with and without JQ1 (0.1–1 µM), benchmarking against endothelial TEER precedent and aligning with ARPE-19 cytokine suppression (Shahid et al., 2022; Hytti et al., 2016). Secondary outcomes: immunofluorescence continuity and membrane localization of ZO-1 and CLDN19, and western/RT-qPCR quantification of TJP1/CLDN19 transcripts/proteins to test whether barrier maintenance arises from preserved junctional organization versus increased transcription.  
- Mechanistic chromatin assays to test the stated enhancer hypothesis: ChIP-seq/ChIP-qPCR for H3K27ac and BRD4 at TJP1/CLDN19 promoters/enhancers in IL-1β±JQ1, plus Pol II occupancy; based on STING-locus data, we predict decreased rather than increased H3 acetylation and Pol II occupancy at direct BRD4-dependent loci after JQ1 (Zou et al., 2022). A finding of increased H3K27ac at tight-junction loci with JQ1 would represent a novel and unanticipated RPE-specific BET effect that would require rigorous validation and mechanistic clarification.  
- Parallel readouts to anchor known mechanisms: NF-κB p65 nuclear activity (TransAM), STING expression, cGAS–STING pathway markers, LC3 flux with/without chloroquine, and cytokine ELISAs (IL-6, IL-8), all of which are supported in prior RPE/retina work and will clarify whether barrier effects track with established anti-inflammatory and autophagic actions of BET inhibition (Hytti et al., 2016; Zou et al., 2022).

Mechanistic expression support in the assayed cell type  
- BET target genes relevant to inflammation (e.g., IL-6, IL-8, STING) are expressed and modulated in ARPE-19 and in mouse retina; BRD4 is induced by oxidative stress in RPE and retina, and cGAS–STING components are present in retinal microglia (Zou et al., 2022; Hytti et al., 2016; Zhu et al., 2023). The corpus does not provide explicit documentation of TJP1/CLDN19 expression modulation by JQ1 in RPE, underscoring the need for primary measurement within the proposed assay (Hytti et al., 2016).

Therapeutic plausibility in dry AMD models  
- RPE oxidative stress, cytosolic DNA leakage, cGAS–STING activation, microglia/macrophage recruitment, and chronic NF-κB activity are central to dry AMD pathogenesis; JQ1 has demonstrably suppressed cGAS–STING signaling and protected RPE/retina in SI-injury models, and PROMOTER accessibility of cGAS/STING is increased in GA patients, providing a mechanistic and translational bridge (Zou et al., 2022). In light-damage injury, BET degradation with dBET6 improved functional outcomes and repressed microglial STING expression, suggesting that BET-targeted therapies are effective across different sterile-injury paradigms relevant to atrophic disease (Zhu et al., 2023).  
- Therefore, for dry AMD, the most defensible expectation is that JQ1 will reduce innate-immune and NF-κB–driven inflammatory signaling in RPE/retina and microglia, attenuate microglia recruitment/activation, and protect photoreceptors/RPE via transcriptional repression of STING and enhanced autophagic clearance of cytosolic DNA, rather than directly upregulating tight-junction genes; any barrier rescue measured by TEER would likely be an indirect consequence of reduced inflammatory burden (Zou et al., 2022; Hytti et al., 2016; Zhao et al., 2017).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Strong anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective efficacy across ocular models that capture key elements of dry AMD biology:  
  • In vivo oxidative-stress (SI) models demonstrate that JQ1 represses BRD4-driven STING transcription, reduces cGAS–STING signaling, limits immune cell accumulation, and prevents RPE/retinal degeneration with favorable safety in the absence of stressors (Zou et al., 2022).  
  • Inflammatory microglia suppression is consistent and robust: intravitreal JQ1 reduces microglia infiltration and cytokines in rd10 retina and abolishes LPS-induced cytokine induction and activation phenotypes in vitro; BET2/BD2 targeting emerges as a key effector axis, aligning with innate-immune pathophysiology in atrophic AMD (Zhao et al., 2017).  
  • dBET6 (systemic) degrades retinal BRD2/3/4, improves retinal function/acuity after light damage, and suppresses microglial STING and inflammatory responses, offering a mechanistically stronger alternative to JQ1 where compensatory BRD4 upregulation occurs (Zhu et al., 2023).  
- Mechanistic depth linking BET inhibition to innate immunity and autophagy:  
  • JQ1 reduces histone acetylation and Pol II engagement at the STING locus, silencing a central innate immune amplifier in RPE/retina (Zou et al., 2022).  
  • BRD4 inhibition increases LC3-dependent enclosure/clearance of cytosolic DNA; this autophagy-dependent mechanism is integral to lowering cGAS activation (Zou et al., 2022).  
- Feasibility of ocular delivery and safety:  
  • Intravitreal JQ1 achieved durable rescue for at least 10 days in rd10 despite short systemic half-life; higher doses did not improve efficacy, highlighting the need for sustained delivery rather than dose escalation (Zhao et al., 2017).  
  • eNano-JQ1 provides a practical intraocular slow-release platform with favorable ERG/histology safety and anti-fibrotic efficacy in PVR, supporting translatability of sustained local BET blockade (Liao et al., 2024).  
  • dBET6 showed retinal target engagement and no detectable retinal toxicity under the tested regimen (Zhu et al., 2023).  
- Ancillary barrier-preserving precedent:  
  • In HUVECs, BRD4 inhibition (JQ1/apabetalone) preserved TEER and monolayer integrity during TNF-α challenge, lending plausibility that BET blockade can maintain barrier function under inflammatory stress, though cell-type differences warrant caution (Shahid et al., 2022).

Weaknesses, risks, and key unknowns relative to the current hypothesis  
- No direct evidence that JQ1 increases H3K27ac at TJP1/CLDN19 or upregulates ZO-1/claudin-19 transcription in RPE; the available mechanistic data actually show decreased histone acetylation and Pol II recruitment at target inflammatory loci with JQ1, arguing against a generalizable H3K27ac-increasing effect at tight-junction enhancers (Zou et al., 2022). Multiple sources explicitly note the absence of RPE barrier (TEER) or tight-junction data in BET-RPE studies to date (Hytti et al., 2016).  
- BET inhibitors can cause cell-cycle arrest and slightly depress metabolic activity in vitro; while ARPE-19 viability was largely preserved and etoposide toxicity was reduced by BET pretreatment, these on-target cytostatic effects will require dose/formulation tuning for chronic intraocular use (Hytti et al., 2016).  
- Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics: single-dose intravitreal JQ1 protection wanes over time, and systemic administration risks off-target tissue exposure; local sustained delivery or newer modalities (e.g., dBET6) are likely needed for translational development (Zhao et al., 2017; Zhu et al., 2023; Liao et al., 2024).  
- Feedback and compensation: JQ1 can increase BRD4 protein levels in some contexts, potentially compromising effect; PROTAC degradation (dBET6) may mitigate this but brings its own safety and distribution questions (Zhu et al., 2023).  
- Cell-type selectivity: The strongest in vivo efficacy signals (rd10, light damage) include microglial suppression and photoreceptor rescue; dry AMD pathogenesis is centered on RPE/Bruch’s membrane/choriocapillaris with secondary photoreceptor involvement; while SI and oxidative-stress data in RPE support relevance, dedicated chronic AMD-like models (aging, complement dysregulation, diet-induced) and RPE barrier endpoints remain to be tested (Zou et al., 2022).

Go/no-go assessment and recommendations  
- Go decision for mechanism-driven validation in dry AMD-relevant preclinical systems, but with a revised primary hypothesis and fit-for-purpose endpoints. The most compelling AMD-relevant mechanism for JQ1 is epigenetic suppression of cGAS–STING and NF-κB inflammatory programs in RPE and resident microglia, plus autophagy-mediated clearance of cytosolic DNA, not direct activation of tight-junction gene enhancers (Zou et al., 2022; Hytti et al., 2016; Zhao et al., 2017).  
- Replace the current primary mechanistic claim (increased H3K27ac at TJP1/CLDN19) with a testable, literature-anchored hypothesis: JQ1 prevents inflammation- and oxidative-stress–induced RPE barrier breakdown by (i) repressing STING transcription and dampening cGAS–STING signaling; (ii) promoting autophagic clearance of cytosolic DNA; and (iii) suppressing NF-κB–driven cytokines in RPE and microglia that secondarily disturb junctional integrity. TEER and junctional continuity should be primary outcomes, but mechanistic attribution should center on cGAS–STING/NF-κB and autophagy rather than TJ enhancer activation (Zou et al., 2022; Hytti et al., 2016; Shahid et al., 2022).  
- Rapid experimental plan:  
  • ARPE-19 and/or primary human RPE on transwells: TEER, paracellular flux, ZO-1/CLDN19 localization under IL-1β or TNF-α ± JQ1 (0.1–1 µM), with NF-κB activity, cytokine ELISAs, STING expression, LC3 flux, and dsDNA imaging to anchor mechanism (Hytti et al., 2016; Shahid et al., 2022; Zou et al., 2022).  
  • Oxidative stress model (H2O2 or SI-conditioned medium) ± JQ1: replicate cGAS–STING repression and autophagy findings in RPE; use autophagy inhibitors to confirm flux-dependence (Zou et al., 2022).  
  • ChIP-qPCR/ATAC at STING and at TJP1/CLDN19 enhancers/promoters to directly test the tight-junction enhancer hypothesis; set expectation that STING chromatin accessibility/acetylation will decline with JQ1, while TJ loci changes are unknown and likely neutral or reduced (Zou et al., 2022).  
  • Microglia–RPE co-culture: test whether microglial cytokine suppression by JQ1 protects RPE TEER/barrier vs IL-1β, isolating paracrine contributions (Zhao et al., 2017).  
  • In vivo oxidative-injury model (SI) with intraocular slow-release JQ1 (eNano-JQ1) to assess RPE barrier integrity (flat-mount ZO-1 continuity), microglial recruitment (IBA1), and function, leveraging prior safety/feasibility data (Liao et al., 2024; Zou et al., 2022).  
- Modality strategy: Given compensatory BRD4 upregulation with JQ1 and BD2 dominance in microglial activation, parallel exploration of BD2-selective ligands (e.g., apabetalone analogs) or BET degraders (dBET6-like) for retina-directed delivery is warranted to optimize therapeutic index while maintaining target engagement (Zhu et al., 2023; Shahid et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2017).

Conclusion  
JQ1 is a mechanistically well-grounded epigenetic modulator that suppresses inflammatory transcriptional programs and innate immune activation in retina/RPE and microglia, protects photoreceptors and RPE from oxidative and inflammatory injury in vivo, and has feasible ocular delivery paths with encouraging preclinical safety signals (Zou et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2017; Liao et al., 2024). These features position BET inhibition as a credible therapeutic strategy for dry AMD, targeting core disease drivers (oxidative stress, cytosolic DNA sensing via cGAS–STING, chronic NF-κB activation, microglial recruitment) rather than exclusively downstream consequences (Zou et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2023). However, the specific hypothesis that JQ1 restores RPE barrier function by increasing H3K27ac at tight-junction loci (TJP1/CLDN19) is not supported by available evidence; on the contrary, BET inhibition generally reduces histone acetylation/accessibility at target loci like STING, and RPE barrier metrics after JQ1 remain unreported (Zou et al., 2022; Hytti et al., 2016). We therefore recommend proceeding with JQ1 (or improved BET-modality variants) in dry AMD-relevant models with primary endpoints focused on cGAS–STING/NF-κB suppression, autophagy-dependent cytosolic DNA clearance, microglial modulation, and functional RPE barrier preservation (TEER, ZO-1/CLDN19 continuity), while explicitly testing and likely revising the proposed tight-junction enhancer mechanism based on direct chromatin measurements (Zou et al., 2022; Shahid et al., 2022).
