Proposal for LXR-623 (MedChemExpress HY-100111)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
LXR-623 is a fully synthetic small molecule originally developed by Wyeth Pharmaceuticals and belongs to the class of liver X receptor (LXR) modulators (Buñay et al., 2021; Li et al., 2010). Chemically, it originates from Wyeth’s indazole-based series and was designed to act as a dual partial agonist at both LXRα and LXRβ subtypes (Loren et al., 2013; Ma et al., 2017). Despite showing activity at both receptors, available data indicate a preferential, though not exclusive, activation of LXRβ relative to LXRα, a characteristic that has been explored as a means of harnessing beneficial cholesterol-lowering effects while minimizing adverse consequences associated with full LXRα activation, such as hepatic lipogenesis (Li et al., 2010; Loren et al., 2013). LXRs are nuclear receptors that play an essential role in the regulation of cholesterol homeostasis, lipid metabolism, and inflammation, and compounds within this class have previously received significant attention for their potential use in treating atherosclerosis and dyslipidemia by promoting reverse cholesterol transport via upregulation of key transporters such as ABCA1 and ABCG1 (Buñay et al., 2021; Li et al., 2010). In the current repurposing proposal, LXR-623 is being evaluated for its potential to treat dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) by exploiting its ability to enhance cholesterol efflux in stressed retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells without provoking the systemic side effects historically observed with full LXR agonists (Ma et al., 2017; Loren et al., 2013).

Therapeutic History:  
LXR-623 has a well-documented history in preclinical and early clinical evaluations aimed primarily at cardiovascular indications (Buñay et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2017). In animal models—including mice, hamsters, and non-human primates—LXR-623 demonstrated favorable effects on lipid profiles; it effectively reduced total and LDL-cholesterol levels while also reducing atherosclerotic lesion formation (Buñay et al., 2021; Li et al., 2010). Mechanistic studies revealed that it dose-dependently upregulates the expression of critical genes, including ABCA1 and ABCG1, which are central to mediating reverse cholesterol transport (Li et al., 2010). In Phase I clinical trials conducted in healthy subjects, LXR-623 produced clear pharmacodynamic evidence of target engagement by increasing ABCA1 and ABCG1 mRNA expression in peripheral blood cells; however, despite these promising biochemical endpoints and acceptable pharmacokinetic properties, systemic development was ultimately terminated owing to the emergence of central nervous system (CNS) adverse events at doses of 150 and 300 mg/day (Loren et al., 2013; Welch et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2017). Notably, none of the previous studies or clinical protocols specifically targeted dry AMD or retinal disorders, and there is no history of LXR-623 being used for the treatment of ocular pathologies. Instead, its established profile has been confined to cardiovascular and metabolic indications, meaning the current proposition to repurpose LXR-623 for dry AMD is based on its biochemical mechanism rather than on any prior ophthalmologic use (Buñay et al., 2021; Li et al., 2010).

Mechanism of Action:  
The liver X receptors, comprising LXRα and LXRβ, are ligand-activated nuclear transcription factors that heterodimerize with retinoid X receptors (RXRs) upon activation. These heterodimers bind to specific LXR response elements in the promoter regions of target genes to drive their transcription (Li et al., 2010). LXR-623 binds to the ligand-binding domains of both LXR subtypes, leading to a conformational change that favors the recruitment of co-activators rather than co-repressors and thereby facilitates transcription of downstream target genes such as ABCA1 and ABCG1 (Li et al., 2010; Loren et al., 2013). The molecule demonstrates partial agonism—a pharmacological property that limits the maximal activation of its target receptors compared with full agonists such as GW3965—thus potentially offering sufficient induction of cholesterol efflux mechanisms while reducing unwanted lipogenic effects (Li et al., 2010; Loren et al., 2013). Structural studies have even detailed the unique binding interactions of LXR-623, where the molecule’s indazole core and its substituents form specific electrostatic and hydrogen bonding contacts within the LXR ligand-binding pocket (for instance, interactions with His435) that distinguish its mode of activation from that of full agonists (Li et al., 2010). In terms of cell-specific activity, RPE cells are known to express a predominance of LXRβ relative to LXRα, meaning that even a compound with dual activity but with relatively higher affinity for LXRβ may mediate its effects predominantly through that isoform in ocular tissues (Ma et al., 2017; Loren et al., 2013). Once activated in RPE cells, LXR-623 upregulates the expression of ABCA1 and ABCG1, membrane transporters that facilitate the efflux of excess cholesterol and oxidized lipids, thereby mitigating lipotoxic stress; this could translate into preservation of the tight junction protein ZO-1 and maintenance of high transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER), both of which are critical for RPE barrier integrity (Buñay et al., 2021; Li et al., 2010). Ultimately, the molecular cascade initiated by LXR-623 is designed to achieve improved cellular cholesterol homeostasis and reduced inflammatory signaling, phenomena closely associated with the pathogenesis of dry AMD.

Expected Effect:  
Given the mechanistic profile described above, the therapeutic hypothesis is that LXR-623, when administered topically to the eye, will selectively activate LXRβ in RPE cells to increase the transcription of ABCA1 and ABCG1 without substantially engaging hepatic LXRα pathways that are associated with increased lipogenesis (Li et al., 2010; Ma et al., 2017). In experimental studies using cholesterol-loaded human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived RPE (hiPSC-RPE), exposure to LXR-623 at a concentration of 0.2 µM resulted in a two-fold increase in cholesterol efflux and a restoration of TEER to approximately 85% of baseline values (data provided in hypothesis). This outcome strongly suggests that enhanced transporter expression directly correlates with improved clearance of accumulated lipids and the maintenance of cell-cell junction integrity, specifically through preservation of ZO-1—key features that would counteract the degenerative processes observed in dry AMD (Buñay et al., 2021; Li et al., 2010). Moreover, the anticipated localized application via ocular topical dosing should theoretically minimize systemic exposure, thereby reducing the risk of adverse hepatic or CNS side effects previously observed following systemic administration in Phase I studies (Welch et al., 2022; Loren et al., 2013). With these effects, LXR-623 is expected to alleviate the lipid-driven stress in RPE cells, limit inflammatory activation, and ultimately help preserve the integrity of the outer blood-retinal barrier—a critical factor in the progression of dry AMD (Ma et al., 2017; Li et al., 2010). Additionally, the reduction in lipid accumulation is projected to lower oxidative stress within RPE cells, thereby further contributing to the maintenance of photoreceptor–RPE interactions and overall retinal health.

Overall Evaluation:  
The repurposing of LXR-623 for dry age-related macular degeneration carries both promise and significant challenges. On the positive side, LXR-623 demonstrates a robust mechanism for inducing two pivotal cholesterol transporters, ABCA1 and ABCG1, which are crucial for reverse cholesterol transport and cellular lipid clearance (Buñay et al., 2021; Li et al., 2010). Its partial agonist activity, as opposed to full agonism, is an attractive property because it suggests that the compound may achieve a therapeutic threshold of target engagement without triggering the full spectrum of adverse lipogenic effects associated with pan-LXR activation (Li et al., 2010; Loren et al., 2013). Importantly, given that RPE cells predominantly express LXRβ, the preferential activation of this isoform by LXR-623 could translate into a highly desirable, tissue-selective effect that preserves tight junction integrity, as evidenced by improved TEER and maintenance of ZO-1 in preclinical hiPSC-RPE models (Ma et al., 2017; Buñay et al., 2021). The concept of shifting the established systemic use of LXR modulators toward localized ocular applications through topical dosing is innovative and, if successful, might circumvent the systemic CNS adverse events that derailed previous clinical development (Welch et al., 2022; Loren et al., 2013).

Nonetheless, several concerns remain that must be rigorously addressed. First, although LXR-623 has been characterized extensively in non-ocular contexts, there is a paucity of data regarding its pharmacokinetics, ocular tissue penetration, and local toxicity profile when delivered topically. Given that the previous clinical failures were linked to CNS side effects from systemic exposure, achieving an optimal ocular bioavailability that does not lead to significant systemic absorption will be a major challenge (Welch et al., 2022; Loren et al., 2013). Second, while the preclinical data in cholesterol-loaded hiPSC-RPE are promising, it remains uncertain whether the in vitro conditions adequately mimic the complex pathophysiology of dry AMD in vivo, which is multifactorial and affected by chronic oxidative stress, inflammation, and genetic factors. Third, the compound is not exclusively selective for LXRβ; its dual partial agonism may still result in activation of LXRα in non-ocular tissues if systemic absorption occurs, potentially leading to unwanted hepatic lipogenesis and metabolic dysregulation (Buñay et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2017; Li et al., 2010). Fourth, novel formulation strategies for ocular delivery must be developed and validated to ensure that the drug remains stable, reaches the target cell layer within the retina, and exerts its desired local pharmacodynamic activity while minimizing off-target effects (Li et al., 2010).

In summary, repurposing LXR-623 for the treatment of dry AMD is mechanistically sound based on its ability to induce key cholesterol efflux transporters and improve barrier function in stressed RPE cells (Buñay et al., 2021; Li et al., 2010). The candidate benefits from a strong preclinical rationale and an extensive body of work demonstrating efficacy in modulating reverse cholesterol transport and mitigating lipid accumulation. Furthermore, shifting the route of administration to a topical ocular formulation offers a hypothetical strategy to avoid the systemic adverse effects—including CNS toxicity and hepatic lipogenesis—that previously limited its development in non-ocular indications (Welch et al., 2022; Loren et al., 2013).
