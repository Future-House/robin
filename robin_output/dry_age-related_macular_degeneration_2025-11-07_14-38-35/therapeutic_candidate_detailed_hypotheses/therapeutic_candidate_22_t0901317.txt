Proposal for T0901317 (Cayman 10006515)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
T0901317 (TO901317; Cayman 10006515) is a synthetic, non-sterol small-molecule agonist of the liver X receptors (LXRs), discovered in early industry efforts (Tularik) to pharmacologically activate LXRα (NR1H3) and LXRβ (NR1H2) to drive reverse cholesterol transport and exert anti-inflammatory effects; it is widely used as a reference pan-LXR ligand in mechanistic and preclinical studies (Michael, Schkeryantz, & Burris, 2005; Bennett, Cooke, Edwards, Moir, & Ray, 2004; Berrodin et al., 2010). Crystallographic and biophysical data show that T0901317 binds within the LXR ligand-binding domain in a pocket of ~830 Å³, making a key hydrogen-bonding interaction with His435 while otherwise engaging primarily hydrophobic residues, and can adopt multiple conformations consistent with ligand- and promoter-selective cofactor recruitment (Bennett et al., 2004; Mukherjee & Mani, 2010; Berrodin et al., 2010). As a class, LXR agonists heterodimerize with RXR and activate DR-4 LXR response elements, upregulating genes that mediate cholesterol efflux and reverse cholesterol transport, notably ABCA1 and ABCG1, as well as APOE and ABCG5/ABCG8; they also induce lipogenic regulators such as SREBP-1c and downstream fatty acid synthesis genes (FASN, ACC), producing a characteristic lipogenic liability on systemic dosing (elevated triglycerides, hepatomegaly, hepatic steatosis) (Michael et al., 2005; Lund, Menke, & Sparrow, 2003; Bennett et al., 2004). Although T0901317 is prototypical for the LXR agonist class, it is pharmacologically promiscuous, with documented activation of FXR and PXR in addition to LXR, an important consideration for safety and on-target/off-target attribution (Choudhary et al., 2020; Velma et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2013). Within ocular and retinal biology, LXRs are expressed in the posterior eye (retina, RPE, choroid), are activated by endogenous oxysterols, and regulate cholesterol transport and inflammatory gene programs relevant to AMD pathogenesis, positioning T0901317 as a tool compound to probe LXR-dependent mechanisms in the RPE and outer retina (Choudhary et al., 2020; Sene & Apte, 2014; Rao & Fliesler, 2021). The LXR class has been explored preclinically in aging eye disease, with alternative ligands such as GW3965 and DMHCA also tested to dissociate beneficial ABCA1/ABCG1 induction from lipogenic effects and to evaluate local ocular delivery to avoid systemic liabilities (Choudhary et al., 2020; Sene & Apte, 2014; Vieira et al., 2020; El-Darzi et al., 2018; Velma et al., 2024).

Therapeutic History  
Outside ophthalmology, T0901317 and other LXR agonists demonstrated robust induction of ABCA1 and ABCG family transporters across cell and tissue systems, increased macrophage cholesterol efflux, raised HDL, and reduced atherosclerotic lesion burden in mouse models; however, they consistently triggered SREBP-1c–driven lipogenesis, hypertriglyceridemia, and hepatic steatosis in rodents, limiting their systemic therapeutic development (Plösch et al., 2002; Bennett et al., 2004; Lund et al., 2003). For example, T0901317 increased hepatic and intestinal Abca1 expression with ~60% HDL elevation, induced Abcg5/Abcg8, and markedly increased biliary and fecal neutral sterol excretion in mice, but also increased plasma triglycerides and VLDL-TG secretion via LXR-mediated SREBP-1c induction (Plösch et al., 2002; Lund et al., 2003). Adverse lipid effects have proven difficult to avoid even with LXRβ-selective scaffolds in humans (BMS-852927 increased plasma/hepatic triglycerides and LDL-C while lowering neutrophils), underscoring translational risk for the class and motivating local delivery strategies and nonlipogenic ABCA1 inducers (NLAIs) to separate efflux from lipogenesis (Kirchgessner et al., 2016; Velma et al., 2024; Lin & Gustafsson, 2015).

In ocular models, LXR biology has strong genetic and pharmacologic support. Both LXR isoforms are expressed in the RPE/choroid; loss of LXRα (Nr1h3−/−) produces AMD-like sub-RPE extracellular lipid deposits, disrupted RPE basal infoldings, and a pro-inflammatory outer-retina microenvironment with ADGRE1+ macrophage accumulation and cytokine upregulation, while LXRβ loss yields intracellular RPE lipid droplets (lipoidal degeneration), highlighting isoform- and process-specific control of lipid handling (Choudhary et al., 2020). Systemic GW3965 treatment in apoB100 transgenic mice reduced outer retinal inflammatory markers and APOE deposition in Bruch’s membrane, reduced ADGRE1+ cells, and improved RPE ultrastructure without overt systemic lipid perturbations in that setting, demonstrating in vivo ocular benefit from LXR activation (Choudhary et al., 2020).

RPE-directed studies of T0901317 provide mechanistic and functional evidence relevant to dry AMD. In primary human RPE and ARPE-19 challenged with oligomeric Aβ₁₋₄₀, T0901317 increased LXRα and ABCA1 expression and prevented induction of IL-6, IL-8, MCP-1 and p16, with suppression of NF-κB signaling (reduced p-IκB-α), consistent with an LXRα–ABCA1-dependent anti-inflammatory and anti-senescence effect in RPE (Dai et al., 2017). In the same disease framework of oxidative stress and mitochondrial vulnerability, TO901317 protected human RPE mitochondrial membrane potential from NaIO₃, H₂O₂, and cigarette smoke extract injury, while a different agonist (GW3965) was more effective against lipid-induced mitochondrial injury and lipid accumulation, indicating ligand-specific pharmacology and suggesting TO901317’s strengths in oxidant/Aβ stress versus lipid overload per se (Choudhary et al., 2020). Additional RPE-relevant evidence links T0901317 to improved lysosomal/autophagic processing under bisretinoid load: transcriptional upregulation of ABCA1 and ABCG1 by T0901317 in RPE increased cholesterol efflux and relieved A2E-induced deficits in phagosome maturation, situating LXR-driven efflux upstream of improved outer-segment processing, an AMD-relevant phenotype (Rao & Fliesler, 2021). In a distinct photoreceptor/RPE stress model (Abca4−/−), promoting cholesterol efflux with TO901316 reduced early endosome enlargement in RPE, a change linked to complement C3 intake/activation; this provides complementary evidence that LXR agonism can normalize cholesterol-dependent endosomal trafficking in diseased RPE (Landowski & Bowes Rickman, 2022).

With respect to ocular delivery and in vivo efficacy, beyond the apoB100 systemic study with GW3965, class evidence shows that local ocular administration of LXR agonists can restore tissue-specific cholesterol efflux and reduce choroidal neovascularization in aged mice, supporting the translational feasibility of local dosing strategies to avoid systemic triglyceride liabilities; note that these data are for the class and not compound-specific to T0901317 (Sene & Apte, 2014). In a CNV-relevant model, intravitreal TO901317 reduced CNV area and lipid accumulation in the lesion region and suppressed NF-κB signaling, with the authors implicating ABCG1-dependent cholesterol handling in RPE as part of the mechanism; these data reside in a wet AMD context but nevertheless demonstrate ocular bioactivity of intravitreally delivered T0901317 (Wu et al., 2023). At the same time, multiple sources stress that T0901317 has been shown to cause severe lipogenesis in diabetic mice and to activate FXR and PXR, raising safety concerns for systemic use and underscoring the need for local administration and/or next-generation LXR modulators to separate ABCA1/ABCG1 induction from lipogenesis (Vieira et al., 2020; Choudhary et al., 2020; Velma et al., 2024).

Mechanism of Action  
LXRs are oxysterol-sensing nuclear receptors that heterodimerize with RXR and bind DR-4 LXR response elements to regulate transcription of genes central to cholesterol efflux and lipid homeostasis (ABCA1, ABCG1, APOE, ABCG5/G8), while also inducing lipogenic programs via SREBP-1c and FASN (Michael et al., 2005; Lund et al., 2003; Bennett et al., 2004). In the LXR ligand-binding domain, T0901317 engages a conserved hydrogen-bond network (His435 and nearby residues) and induces receptor conformations that determine coactivator/corepressor exchange and gene-selective transactivation; such ligand-induced conformational ensembles help explain differences between T0901317 and GW3965 in gene and cell selectivity (Bennett et al., 2004; Mukherjee & Mani, 2010; Berrodin et al., 2010). At the target-gene level, T0901317 robustly upregulates ABCA1 and ABCG1 mRNAs and proteins in multiple cell types, increases cholesterol efflux to ApoA1/HDL, and elevates APOE expression, consistent with its intended enhancement of reverse cholesterol transport; induction of PLTP and LDLR-modulating IDOL has also been observed with LXR agonism (Michael et al., 2005; Bennett et al., 2004; Plösch et al., 2002; Berrodin et al., 2010; Sene & Apte, 2014). In neurons and astrocytes, T0901317 raised ABCA1 and reduced Aβ secretion, supporting a mechanistic link between ABCA1-mediated membrane cholesterol redistribution and protease access, while in macrophages LXR activation repressed multiple NF-κB–target inflammatory genes (iNOS, COX-2, IL-6, MMP-9), indicating classical LXR anti-inflammatory transrepression (Michael et al., 2005; Bennett et al., 2004; Mukherjee & Mani, 2010).

Mechanistically in the outer retina, RPE and neural retina express LXRα/β, ABCA1, ABCG1, apolipoprotein acceptors (ApoA1/E), and sterol transporters (NPC1, TSPO), assembling the machinery for ABCA1/ABCG1-mediated cholesterol efflux; RPE can form lipoprotein-like particles, and polarized ABCA1-dependent sterol transfer to ApoA1 occurs at both apical and basolateral membranes in differentiated RPE (Rao & Fliesler, 2021). In primary human fetal RPE monolayers, ABCA1-mediated efflux is generally weak under low-lipid media but becomes strongly upregulated on the apical side upon OxLDL loading, consistent with stimulus-responsive ABCA1 function in RPE that may be relevant in AMD-like lipid conditions (Lyssenko et al., 2018). Genetic loss-of-function underscores ABCA1/ABCG1 importance: double knockout in RPE yields cholesteryl ester droplet accumulation and retinal degeneration; notably, Abca1 single knockout alone is sufficient to drive early RPE lipid accumulation and morphological changes, whereas Abcg1 loss alone is milder, implicating ABCA1 as the primary driver at early stages (Rao & Fliesler, 2021; Storti et al., 2019). Oxysterols produced in the outer retina (e.g., 27-hydroxycholesterol) activate LXR to stimulate ABCA1-dependent efflux, linking local sterol metabolism to LXR activation (Rao & Fliesler, 2021).
