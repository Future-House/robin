Proposal for Bexarotene (Selleckchem S1572)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
Bexarotene is a fully synthetic retinoid analog classified as a rexinoid, specifically acting as a pan–retinoid X receptor (RXR) agonist. It was originally developed through structure–activity relationship studies aimed at mimicking the natural ligand 9-cis retinoic acid, while providing greater receptor selectivity, improved potency, and a pharmacological profile amenable to therapeutic use. Its synthesis and subsequent discovery were driven by the need to harness RXR‐mediated gene regulation, and it subsequently became the only FDA-approved synthetic RXR ligand to date (Willems, Zaienne, & Merk, 2021). As a member of the retinoid drug class, bexarotene has been primarily used in the oncological setting, specifically for the treatment of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, and its chemical design reflects modifications that impart high lipophilicity and receptor specificity (Watanabe & Kakuta, 2018). This synthetic derivation distinguishes it from naturally occurring retinoids and underpins its utility across a number of experimental and clinical contexts.

Therapeutic History:  
Bexarotene’s clinical history is marked by its approval as a second-line therapy for cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, which provided a strong basis for its safety and efficacy in a human disease setting (Willems, Zaienne, & Merk, 2021). In addition to its primary use in oncology, preclinical and early clinical research has investigated bexarotene for applications in neurodegenerative diseases, most notably Alzheimer’s disease and multiple sclerosis. These studies have demonstrated that RXR activation by bexarotene can influence the clearance of pathological protein aggregates and promote remyelination, respectively (Gaunt, 2020; Behl et al., 2022). However, despite the broad mechanistic appeal of RXR agonism in modifying lipid metabolism and neuroinflammation, there is little to no established clinical or veterinary precedent for employing bexarotene in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) or similar ocular degenerative conditions. In fact, while several RXR agonists have been considered in various models of ocular disease, the role of RXR in the diseased retina remains underexplored, with only sparse reports such as a study with the RXR agonist PA024 in retinal degeneration models (Dorofeeva et al., 2023; Malek & Lad, 2014). This gap in therapeutic history presents both a challenge and an opportunity in repurposing bexarotene for dry AMD.

Mechanism of Action:  
Bexarotene exerts its pharmacological effects primarily by binding to the ligand-binding domain of RXR isoforms (RXRα, RXRβ, and RXRγ) with nanomolar potency, inducing conformational changes that favor the recruitment of coactivators and subsequent transcriptional activation of targeted genes (Willems, Zaienne, & Merk, 2021). RXR is a promiscuous nuclear receptor that forms obligate heterodimers with several other nuclear receptors, most notably the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) such as PPARγ. In doing so, the bexarotene-induced RXR/PPARγ heterodimer binds to specific peroxisome proliferator response elements (PPREs) in the genome, thereby upregulating target genes involved in lipid metabolism and transport. One key target is ABCA1, an ATP-binding cassette transporter implicated in cholesterol efflux from cells, which plays a central role in maintaining intracellular lipid homeostasis (Dorofeeva et al., 2023; Choudhary & Malek, 2016).

In the context of retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells, the activation of the RXR/PPARγ pathway is particularly compelling. RPE cells are highly involved in the processing and clearance of lipids, including cholesterol, and in modulating the structural integrity of the outer blood-retinal barrier via tight junction proteins such as claudin-1 and occludin. When under stress—such as exposure to cytotoxic mediators like 7-ketocholesterol (7KCh), which disrupts normal junctional organization and reduces transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER)—the activation of RXR offers a potential therapeutic avenue. Bexarotene’s ability to stimulate RXR signaling is expected to upregulate ABCA1 expression, thereby enhancing cholesterol efflux, while also promoting the transcription of genes that lead to the re-expression of claudin-1 and occludin, which are critical for restoring barrier integrity (Watanabe & Kakuta, 2018; Choudhary & Malek, 2016).

The biochemical interactions following RXR activation by bexarotene include a cascade of events where the ligand-receptor complex alters the transcriptional landscape of the cell. This process entails the heterodimerization of RXR with its partner receptor, binding to DNA response elements, and initiating the recruitment of transcriptional coactivators, which together drive the expression of genes that modulate lipid metabolism, inflammatory responses, and cell junction integrity. The specificity of bexarotene for RXR, as opposed to broader retinoic acid receptor (RAR) activity, allows for a more targeted enhancement of these pathways—a factor that is crucial in tissues like the RPE, where precise regulation of lipid handling and barrier properties is essential for retinal health (Willems, Zaienne, & Merk, 2021; Watanabe & Kakuta, 2018; Choudhary & Malek, 2016).

Expected Effect:  
The central hypothesis underlying the repurposing of bexarotene for dry AMD is that its activation of RXR in RPE cells will facilitate heterodimerization with PPARγ, driving the transcriptional upregulation of lipid-handling genes, most notably ABCA1. In vitro studies using ARPE-19 cells have provided compelling preliminary data indicating that exposure to 0.5 µM bexarotene in the presence of 7-ketocholesterol stress is capable of restoring TEER to approximately 85% of baseline values and significantly increasing ABCA1 mRNA levels. This restoration of TEER suggests that bexarotene not only promotes cholesterol efflux by upregulating ABCA1 but also exerts a beneficial influence on the expression of key tight junction proteins, such as claudin-1 and occludin, which are essential for maintaining the integrity of the RPE barrier (Dorofeeva et al., 2023; Malek & Lad, 2014).

The expected molecular effect in the assay is thus twofold. First, through its molecular interaction with RXR, bexarotene reinforces the RXR/PPARγ heterodimer formation, triggering a cascade of gene transcription events that culminate in enhanced cholesterol efflux via ABCA1. This effect directly addresses the accumulation of lipid deposits that contribute to RPE dysfunction in dry AMD. Second, by re-inducing the expression of claudin-1 and occludin, bexarotene is anticipated to improve cellular junctional integrity, thereby reducing paracellular leakage and restoring the overall barrier function of the RPE as measured by TEER. The use of ocular gel formulations for local delivery promises to concentrate the therapeutic effect within the eye, circumventing the systemic adverse effects that are typically associated with bexarotene’s unfavorable physicochemical properties, such as high lipophilicity and low systemic solubility (Willems, Zaienne, & Merk, 2021; Watanabe & Kakuta, 2018; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).

Overall Evaluation:  
The repurposing of bexarotene as a therapeutic candidate for dry AMD is founded upon a robust mechanistic rationale and promising preliminary in vitro data, yet it also presents several challenges that must be addressed through rigorous further research. One of the primary strengths of this candidate lies in its well-established mode of action as a selective RXR agonist. Its ability to promote heterodimerization with PPARγ and subsequently upregulate genes such as ABCA1 is well supported by a substantial body of research in other disease contexts, including neurodegeneration and oncology (Willems, Zaienne, & Merk, 2021; Watanabe & Kakuta, 2018; Gaunt, 2020). This receptor specificity not only underscores bexarotene’s potential to modulate RPE lipid metabolism but also suggests that similar pharmacological principles could translate into the restoration of tight junction integrity—a key therapeutic goal in dry AMD.

Another significant strength is its prior clinical approval for cutaneous T-cell lymphoma. This provides a substantial amount of safety data and experience in human use, which could expedite the repurposing process under a drug repositioning strategy. Moreover, the observed in vitro effects—namely the partial restoration of TEER and the increase in ABCA1 mRNA in stressed ARPE-19 cells—lend direct support to the hypothesis that RXR agonism can counteract 7-ketocholesterol-induced disruptions in RPE function (Dorofeeva et al., 2023; Malek & Lad, 2014). The concept of using ocular gel formulations to achieve localized delivery and thereby minimize systemic side effects is another innovative aspect that addresses one of the traditional limitations of bexarotene, namely its adverse systemic metabolic disturbances, which include dyslipidemia and hypothyroidism (Willems, Zaienne, & Merk, 2021).

However, there are notable weaknesses that temper this optimism. First, bexarotene’s unfavorable physicochemical properties—its high lipophilicity and low solubility—pose a significant formulation challenge, especially when transitioning from systemic oncology applications to local ocular delivery. While gel formulations offer a potential solution, achieving the precise pharmacokinetics and adequate tissue penetration in the retina will require meticulous formulation development and validation. Second, despite the extensive research on RXR agonism in other tissues, there is very limited precedent for the use of bexarotene or similar compounds in the treatment of dry AMD or related ocular diseases. In other words, while the underlying nuclear receptor biology in the retina is compelling, the absence of prior clinical or even robust preclinical ocular studies increases the risk associated with translational uncertainty (Dorofeeva et al., 2023; Malek & Lad, 2014).

Moreover, systemic side effects associated with bexarotene have been well documented in its approved indications. Although the strategy of localized ocular delivery aims to mitigate these adverse effects, any leakage from the ocular compartment into systemic circulation could potentially re-expose patients to these risks. Additionally, the complexity of dry AMD as a multifactorial disease—with contributions from oxidative stress, inflammation, and complex extracellular matrix alterations—means that modulating only lipid handling and tight junction integrity might not be sufficient to arrest or reverse disease progression. Thus, while the mechanistic hypothesis is sound, it should ideally be integrated into a broader therapeutic strategy that also addresses other pathological mechanisms in dry AMD.

In summary, the proposed use of bexarotene for dry AMD is supported by a strong mechanistic foundation based on RXR agonism and its downstream effects on lipid metabolism and cellular junction integrity. The preliminary in vitro evidence in ARPE-19 cells under 7-ketocholesterol stress is promising, particularly with the reported restoration of TEER and upregulation of ABCA1. The strengths of this candidate include its specificity for RXR, the translational potential backed by its FDA–approved status for an unrelated indication, and the innovative strategy of local ocular delivery through gel formulations. On the other hand, challenges related to its physicochemical properties, potential for systemic side effects, and the overall gaps in preclinical ocular data necessitate further rigorous research. Preclinical studies in appropriate animal models of dry AMD and careful formulation optimization will be essential next steps to validate the therapeutic potential of bexarotene in this context (Willems, Zaienne, & Merk, 2021; Watanabe & Kakuta, 2018; Choudhary & Malek, 2016; Malek & Lad, 2014).
