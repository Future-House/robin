Proposal for BIRB 796 (Doramapimod; Selleck S1076)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
BIRB 796 (doramapimod) is a small-molecule p38 MAP kinase inhibitor discovered at Boehringer Ingelheim as part of a diaryl urea/pyrazolyl-urea medicinal chemistry effort to exploit an inactive, DFG-out conformation of p38 and thereby achieve high potency and kinase-family selectivity via an allosteric binding site adjacent to the ATP pocket (type II binding) (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Bagley et al., 2010; Xing, 2016; Coulthard et al., 2009). In contrast to classical ATP-site inhibitors (e.g., pyridinyl-imidazoles such as SB203580) that anchor in the hinge region and remain in the DFG-in active conformation, BIRB 796 engages a selectivity pocket opened by flipping the Asp-Phe-Gly (DFG) motif to a DFG-out state; this binding mode is incompatible with ATP binding and additionally disrupts the interaction between p38α and its upstream activators MKK3/MKK6 (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Bagley et al., 2010). Structural features include deep insertion of a t-butyl group into the hydrophobic “Phe-pocket” created by the DFG-out rearrangement, naphthyl contacts, favorable packing of a tolyl group in a Glu71-containing hydrophobic pocket, a morpholino that H-bonds with Met109, and a diaryl urea NH that forms an H-bond with Glu71 analogous to the adenine N1 interaction in ATP binding (Bagley et al., 2010). Kinetically, BIRB 796 is a prototypical slow-binding, slow-dissociating p38 inhibitor: association is ~500-fold slower and dissociation ~10^6-fold slower than the ATP-site pyridinyl-imidazole SK&F 86002, consistent with a major conformational rearrangement upon binding and prolonged target residence time (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012). Biophysically, reported potency is sub-nanomolar in binding assays (Kd ~0.1–<0.8 nM) with functional enzymatic or cellular IC50 values on the order of tens of nanomolar (e.g., ~63 nM), and the compound inhibits all p38 isoforms to varying degrees (pan-p38), with context-dependent reports of minimal or dual activity on JNK2 (Xing, 2016; Bagley et al., 2010; Yong et al., 2009; Coulthard et al., 2009). A microwave-assisted route to BIRB 796 was reported (aminopyrazole formation from pivaloylacetonitrile and p-tolylhydrazine; carbamate and aminonaphthol intermediates; DMSO coupling at 100°C), enabling improved yields for preclinical supply and mechanistic studies (Bagley et al., 2010).

As a chemotype, diaryl/urea-type type II kinase inhibitors that exploit DFG-out conformations were developed to expand selectivity beyond hinge-binding ATP mimetics, often achieving slow off-rates and high apparent affinity; within the p38 field, BIRB 796 became the best-characterized example of this allosteric/selectivity-pocket strategy and served as a clinical tool compound for inflammatory indications (Xing, 2016; Coulthard et al., 2009).

Therapeutic History  
BIRB 796 advanced through Phase I and Phase II programs and was reported as having reached Phase II/III development for autoimmune and inflammatory diseases including rheumatoid arthritis (RA), Crohn’s disease (CD), and psoriasis; a human endotoxemia model was used to demonstrate pharmacodynamic target engagement (suppression of TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-8, and effects on coagulation/fibrinolysis/endothelial activation) (Yong et al., 2009; Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Coulthard et al., 2009; Xing, 2016). Oral dosing regimens included 5–30 mg twice daily (RA) and 10–60 mg twice daily (CD); in endotoxemia, single doses up to 600 mg were tested and showed robust biomarker modulation (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Yong et al., 2009). Despite clear pharmacodynamic effects, randomized trials failed to demonstrate durable clinical efficacy in Crohn’s disease and psoriasis, and RA efficacy was not substantiated; development was discontinued after Phase II, with repeated administration associated with elevated liver transaminases (class-typical hepatotoxicity) (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012). Mechanistic work implicated a reactive naphthalene-epoxide metabolite (glutathione-conjugated) in the ALT elevations observed in CD patients, supporting on-drug hepatotoxicity as a liability for the chemotype (Machado et al., 2021). More broadly, many clinical p38 inhibitor programs (including BIRB 796) showed a stereotyped pattern of early biomarker suppression (e.g., CRP, soluble TNF receptor) that returned to near baseline by week 12, coincident with a lack of sustained clinical benefit, pointing to network compensation under chronic p38 blockade (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Xing, 2016). These outcomes, together with dose-limiting toxicities seen across the class, drove a field-wide shift toward alternative strategies (substrate-selective or docking-site inhibitors) rather than pan-p38 catalytic inhibition (Burton et al., 2021).

In preclinical and translational settings, BIRB 796 has been widely used as a pharmacological probe: it reduces LPS-induced TNF-α and other cytokines in vitro and in vivo; in a human endotoxemia model a 600 mg dose suppressed coagulation/fibrinolysis and endothelial activation; in tumor-associated contexts it inhibited IL-6 secretion and reduced tumor cell proliferation (Yong et al., 2009). In macrophage-centric COPD studies, BIRB-class inhibitors sometimes failed to suppress IL-8, underscoring cell-type-specific differences in p38 control of cytokine outputs; however, at very low concentrations (1 nM) BIRB-0796 exhibited synergistic cytokine suppression when combined with corticosteroids, reflecting complementary mechanisms (steroids do not block p38-HSP27 phosphorylation) (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012). In epithelial barrier biology, doramapimod protected the Sertoli cell tight-junction barrier against cadmium-induced injury, preserving localization of ZO-1, claudin-11, occludin, and adherens junction components and rescuing transepithelial electrical resistance (TER), while selectively blocking p38 phosphorylation (without suppressing JNK, CREB, or Akt activation) (Wang et al., 2023).

No registered clinical trials of p38 MAPK inhibitors (including doramapimod) for age-related macular degeneration (AMD) are listed on ClinicalTrials.gov under the queried terms, indicating no formal AMD program has been conducted in this class to date (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.). Mechanistically, p38 signaling has been implicated in retinal vascular and AMD-relevant processes (e.g., VEGF induction, matrix remodeling via MMP-2/MMP-14 and basigin, inflammasome activation, endothelial dysfunction and barrier leakage), which supports biological plausibility but not direct clinical translation for AMD (Burton et al., 2021). There is no RPE- or AMD-specific preclinical study of doramapimod in the provided materials, and none of the cited clinical programs tested dry AMD (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Burton et al., 2021).

Mechanism of Action  
p38 MAPKs (α/β/γ/δ) are stress-activated serine/threonine kinases that integrate inflammatory cues and drive transcriptional and post-transcriptional control of cytokines including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-8; downstream effectors include MK2/MK3 (MAPKAPK2/3), MSK1/2, and HSP27, which regulate mRNA stability, translation, and cytoskeletal dynamics (Machado et al., 2021; Coulthard et al., 2009; Burton et al., 2021). Canonical activation proceeds via MKK3 and MKK6 phosphorylation of the Thr-Gly-Tyr activation loop, but atypical, TAB1-dependent activation on endosomes also occurs in response to GPCR/cytokine stimuli, fueling vascular inflammation and barrier dysfunction; p38 pathway activity is further modulated by MAPK phosphatases (DUSPs) feeding back on p38 dephosphorylation (Burton et al., 2021).

BIRB 796 binds in a selectivity pocket adjacent to the ATP site and induces a DFG-out conformation that is catalytically incompetent, sterically and conformationally precluding ATP binding and preventing interaction with upstream kinases (MKK3/MKK6) (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Bagley et al., 2010). Structural contacts include insertion of a tert-butyl group into the DFG-derived Phe-pocket, hydrophobic contacts by a naphthyl group, a tolyl group accommodated in a Glu71-containing hydrophobic cavity, and a morpholino ring that H-bonds to Met109; importantly, a diaryl urea NH forms a hydrogen bond with Glu71 that mimics the adenine N1 interaction in ATP, highlighting how selectivity-pocket binders can spare classical hinge contacts yet achieve high affinity (Bagley et al., 2010). The kinetic signature of BIRB 796—extremely slow dissociation and slow association—reflects the energetic barrier to reorganize the DFG motif and surrounding activation loop, and is thought to confer prolonged target occupancy in cells (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012). As a result, BIRB 796 has sub-nanomolar binding affinity to non-activated p38α (Kd ~0.1–<0.8 nM) with functional inhibitory potency in the low- to mid-nanomolar range in biochemical/cellular assays (Xing, 2016; Bagley et al., 2010).

Isoform and pathway coverage is broad: multiple sources report inhibition of all p38 isoforms (α/β/γ/δ), a property linked to the allosteric binding mode, which avoids the Met109-dependent hinge interaction exploited by ATP-site inhibitors that often lack activity on γ/δ (Coulthard et al., 2009; Xing, 2016). Some profiling described BIRB 796 as selective against JNK2, whereas other reports noted dual p38/JNK activity—differences likely arise from assay format, activation state, and concentration ranges, but the possibility of JNK2 inhibition exists and complicates mechanistic attribution in certain systems (Yong et al., 2009; Bagley et al., 2010). Functionally, BIRB 796 suppresses LPS-induced TNF-α and lowers IL-6 and IL-8 in human endotoxemia, reduces p38 readouts such as phosphorylated HSP27 (a sensitive pharmacodynamic biomarker), and decreases phosphorylation of MK2, consistent with dampened pro-inflammatory transcription and mRNA stabilization (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Yong et al., 2009; Burton et al., 2021). In immortalized Werner syndrome fibroblasts, BIRB 796 prevents p38 activation, reduces pp38 and pHSP27, and thereby modifies senescence-associated phenotypes, reinforcing its activity on the p38→MK2/HSP27 axis in stressed/aged cells (Bagley et al., 2010). In primary Sertoli cells exposed to cadmium, doramapimod specifically blocked p38 phosphorylation, preserved junctional protein localization (e.g., ZO-1, occludin, claudin-11, JAM-A), and maintained barrier function (TER), while not suppressing parallel stress signals (pJNK, p-c-Jun, p-CREB, p-Akt), indicating target-pathway selectivity in vitro (Wang et al., 2023).

Clinical pharmacology confirms human anti-inflammatory effects: a 600 mg dose of BIRB-796BS in endotoxemia suppressed LPS-induced coagulation, fibrinolysis, and endothelial activation (Yong et al., 2009). However, in chronic inflammatory diseases, repeated dosing produced only transient biomarker suppression and limited clinical benefits; repeated administration caused liver-enzyme elevations, and mechanistic toxicology implicated a reactive naphthalene-epoxide metabolite (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Machado et al., 2021). These observations align with broader p38 inhibitor experience: pan-p38 blockade can interrupt beneficial anti-inflammatory signaling (e.g., MSK1/2-mediated) and distort feedback circuits, while disinhibiting upstream kinases (e.g., TAK1, MLK2/3) and activating JNK or NF-κB crosstalk, conferring dose-limiting toxicities and short-lived efficacy (Coulthard et al., 2009).

Expected Effect  
Assay hypothesis: In senescent retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), pharmacologic p38α/β inhibition with BIRB 796 will suppress SASP-like cytokines (particularly IL-6 and IL-8), reducing paracrine/autocrine downregulation of junctional proteins and preserving tight junction belts (e.g., ZO-1) and transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER).

Mechanistic rationale: p38 controls transcriptional and post-transcriptional programs that elevate IL-6 and IL-8 across multiple inflamed tissues and cell types; BIRB 796 lowers IL-6/IL-8 in human endotoxemia and tumor-cell paracrine models, and at low nanomolar concentrations can synergize with corticosteroids to suppress cytokines in macrophage systems, consistent with broad suppression of p38-driven inflammatory outputs (Yong et al., 2009; Albrecht & Laufer, 2012). Downstream, p38→MK2 phosphorylates HSP27 and RNA-binding proteins (e.g., tristetraprolin/TTP), which increases mRNA stability and translation of cytokine transcripts; BIRB 796 reduces pp38 and pHSP27 in senescence models, matching the predicted SASP attenuation mechanism (Burton et al., 2021; Bagley et al., 2010). In epithelial barrier contexts, doramapimod rescued tight-junction integrity and TER in primary Sertoli monolayers under toxic stress while maintaining junctional protein localization, providing a functional precedent that p38 inhibition can preserve epithelial-like barrier structure and function via junctional/cytoskeletal stabilization (Wang et al., 2023).

RPE disease-context rationale: Although no RPE-specific BIRB 796 data are provided here, p38 signaling is implicated in AMD-relevant processes (e.g., VEGF induction and angiogenesis, MMP-driven matrix remodeling, inflammasome activation, vascular endothelial dysfunction and barrier leakage), making p38 a plausible node in retinal inflammation and barrier pathology (Burton et al., 2021). Given that p38 activity drives IL-6/IL-8 production broadly, and that these cytokines can downregulate junctional proteins and disrupt epithelial barriers in many systems, it is reasonable to expect that BIRB 796 will lower MK2/HSP27 pathway activity, reduce IL-6/IL-8 secretion from senescent RPE, and preserve ZO-1 belts and TEER if p38-dependent SASP is operative in this cell type (Coulthard et al., 2009; Xing, 2016; Burton et al., 2021). Atypical (TAB1-mediated) p38 activation has been linked to endothelial barrier disruption; while not demonstrated in RPE here, such noncanonical activation could also contribute to epithelial stress responses and might be sensitive to allosteric type II inhibition (Burton et al., 2021).

Assay execution considerations: To test the hypothesis rigorously in senescent RPE, readouts should include (1) p38 pathway activity markers (pp38, pMK2, pHSP27), (2) secreted SASP cytokines (IL-6, IL-8), and (3) barrier metrics (continuous ZO-1 belts by immunofluorescence; TER dynamics over time), with BIRB 796 concentration–response spanning low nanomolar to low micromolar given reported cellular potencies and the 10 µM concentration that preserved Sertoli barrier function (Wang et al., 2023; Xing, 2016; Bagley et al., 2010). Given steroid independence of p38 signaling (p-HSP27 is not reduced by steroids), testing BIRB 796 alone and in combination with a glucocorticoid could probe pharmacologic additivity and compensatory pathways in RPE SASP (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012). Because IL-8 may be variably p38-dependent across cell types, the assay should be powered to detect partial suppression and explore time-dependent compensation (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012). Finally, inclusion of controls that assess atypical activation (e.g., TAB1–p38 engagement) and post-transcriptional endpoints (TTP phosphorylation status) could sharpen mechanistic attribution (Burton et al., 2021).

Translational considerations: Systemic doramapimod exposure is constrained by hepatotoxicity and lack of durable efficacy in chronic inflammation, plausibly due to network adaptation; any clinical path in dry AMD would require local ocular delivery to minimize systemic exposure and to bypass compensatory systemic responses, a principle consistent with proposed niches for p38 inhibitors in localized/short-term settings (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Coulthard et al., 2009). At present, there are no registered AMD trials of p38 inhibitors, so translational validation must proceed from RPE/retinal preclinical models (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Mechanistic fit: p38 MAPK is a master regulator of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6 and IL-8, with well-defined downstream effectors (MK2/MK3, HSP27) that also interface with cytoskeletal and junctional integrity; BIRB 796 potently and durably engages this pathway, reduces p38 phosphorylation readouts, and suppresses IL-6/IL-8 in human endotoxemia and other systems (Machado et al., 2021; Coulthard et al., 2009; Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Yong et al., 2009). The proposed RPE SASP-barrier mechanism is therefore biochemically coherent (Burton et al., 2021).  
- High potency and residence time: sub-nanomolar affinity, nanomolar functional inhibition, and extremely slow off-rate support strong target engagement at low free concentrations, which may be advantageous for intraocular dosing strategies designed to minimize systemic exposure (Xing, 2016; Albrecht & Laufer, 2012).  
- Allosteric type II mode and pan-p38 coverage: by stabilizing DFG-out, BIRB 796 inhibits α/β/γ/δ isoforms, potentially covering non-α p38 activity that could contribute to epithelial senescence or retinal inflammation; the allosteric site is exploited with favorable structural interactions and a defined pharmacophore (Coulthard et al., 2009; Bagley et al., 2010; Xing, 2016).  
- Barrier-protection precedent in epithelia: doramapimod preserved ZO-1/occludin/claudin-11 localization and TER in primary Sertoli monolayers under toxic injury while selectively blocking p38 phosphorylation, an effect directionally aligned with the goal of preserving RPE tight junctions and barrier function (Wang et al., 2023).  
- PD biomarkers to guide development: pHSP27 and pMK2 provide sensitive pathway engagement readouts, facilitating translational bridging from in vitro RPE to in vivo ocular models and potentially to human aqueous/vitreous sampling (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Burton et al., 2021).  
- Combination potential: BIRB-class inhibitors can synergize with corticosteroids at very low concentrations in cytokine suppression assays, reflecting complementary mechanisms of action and offering a combination path to mitigate compensation (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012).

Weaknesses and risks  
- Lack of RPE-specific data: No direct evidence in the provided materials demonstrates doramapimod effects on senescent RPE SASP, IL-6/IL-8, ZO-1 belts, or TEER; AMD- or RPE-specific proof of concept remains to be generated (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Burton et al., 2021).  
- Clinical class history of transient efficacy: Across chronic inflammatory indications, p38 inhibitors showed early biomarker suppression followed by reversion toward baseline and lack of durable benefit by ~12 weeks, likely reflecting pathway compensation and complex feedback/crosstalk that may also occur in chronic retinal disease (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Xing, 2016).  
- Hepatotoxicity and metabolic liability: Repeated systemic dosing raised liver enzymes, and mechanistic toxicology implicated a reactive naphthalene-epoxide metabolite for BIRB 796 specifically; while intraocular delivery could mitigate systemic exposure, any repurposing must address ocular-specific safety and formulation (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Machado et al., 2021).  
- Potential dual JNK2 activity: Reports vary on BIRB 796 selectivity; dual p38/JNK2 inhibition has been described, complicating mechanistic interpretation and potentially adding pathway liabilities in some cellular contexts (Bagley et al., 2010; Yong et al., 2009).  
- Isoform- and context-specific cytokine control: In alveolar macrophages and COPD models, IL-8 can be relatively resistant to p38 inhibition, cautioning against assuming uniform suppression of IL-8 in senescent RPE; parallel drivers (e.g., NF-κB) may require combination strategies (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012).  
- Strategic alternatives may be preferable: Substrate-selective p38 pathway interventions (e.g., ED-site inhibitor UM101) that spare beneficial MSK1 signaling, or inhibitors that block atypical TAB1-mediated activation, have shown preclinical advantages and may reduce compensation/toxicity risks compared with pan-p38 catalytic inhibition (Burton et al., 2021).

Decision-oriented assessment for dry AMD  
- Biological plausibility: Strong. p38 controls IL-6/IL-8 and MK2/HSP27 signaling that plausibly drive SASP and barrier dysfunction; doramapimod has repeatedly demonstrated suppression of these axes and preserved epithelial-like barrier function in vitro (Machado et al., 2021; Coulthard et al., 2009; Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Wang et al., 2023).  
- Translational tractability: Conditional. Systemic use is precluded by hepatotoxicity and lack of durable efficacy; however, local ocular delivery could deliver high intraocular drug levels while limiting systemic exposure, and the slow off-rate may support infrequent dosing, pending ocular PK/toxicity profiling (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Coulthard et al., 2009).  
- Evidence gaps: Critical. No RPE-focused or AMD-model data exist here; there are no registered AMD clinical trials for p38 inhibitors; demonstration in senescent RPE models is essential before advancing (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).

Recommended next steps  
- In vitro RPE senescence studies: Evaluate BIRB 796 concentration–response (1 nM–10 µM) on pp38/pMK2/pHSP27, IL-6/IL-8 secretion, ZO-1/occludin belt continuity, and TEER in primary human RPE and iPSC-RPE rendered senescent (e.g., replicative or stress-induced senescence) (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Burton et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023).  
- Mechanistic dissection: Assess TTP phosphorylation and mRNA decay of IL-6/IL-8; interrogate atypical TAB1-dependent activation signatures; compare to a substrate-selective p38 modulator where possible to benchmark compensation (Burton et al., 2021).  
- Combination testing: Explore additivity with a glucocorticoid and/or NF-κB modulation to overcome potential IL-8 resistance in RPE; measure durability after washout to probe rebound signaling (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012).  
- Ocular safety and delivery: Conduct ex vivo/in vivo ocular tolerability (ERGs, histology) and intraocular PK in relevant species; leverage the slow target off-rate to test low-dose intravitreal delivery designed to minimize systemic exposure (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Coulthard et al., 2009).  
- Prioritize de-risking strategies: If doramapimod exhibits ocular or systemic liabilities, consider pathway-refined alternatives (e.g., MK2 inhibitors; ED-site p38α inhibitors sparing MSK1; TAB1-site blockers) that may retain anti-SASP efficacy with reduced toxicity and compensation (Burton et al., 2021; Coulthard et al., 2009).

Conclusion  
On mechanistic grounds, BIRB 796 is well-suited to test the SASP-barrier hypothesis in senescent RPE: it is a potent, slow-off, allosteric type II inhibitor that suppresses IL-6/IL-8 outputs and p38→MK2/HSP27 signaling, and it preserves epithelial-like junctional integrity and TER in a primary Sertoli barrier model (Xing, 2016; Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; Yong et al., 2009; Wang et al., 2023). The absence of RPE- or AMD-specific evidence and the class’s clinical history of transient efficacy and hepatotoxicity are significant liabilities that limit immediate translational prospects; nonetheless, a local-delivery, RPE-focused preclinical program is justified to generate the missing data and to determine whether p38 suppression can stabilize RPE barrier function and SASP in dry AMD models (Albrecht & Laufer, 2012; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Coulthard et al., 2009). If positive, doramapimod could serve as a powerful chemical probe for isoform- and pathway-specific dissection in RPE senescence, while parallel exploration of pathway-refined or substrate-selective p38 modulators may offer a more sustainable clinical trajectory for chronic ocular disease (Burton et al., 2021; Coulthard et al., 2009).
