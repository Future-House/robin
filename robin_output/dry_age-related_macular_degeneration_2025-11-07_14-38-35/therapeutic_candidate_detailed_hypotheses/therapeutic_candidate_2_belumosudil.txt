Proposal for Belumosudil (KD025; Selleckchem S7551)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Belumosudil (KD025; Rezurock) is a small-molecule, orally bioavailable, indazole-derived serine/threonine kinase inhibitor that preferentially targets Rho-associated coiled-coil–containing protein kinase 2 (ROCK2) over ROCK1 and was developed by Kadmon Pharmaceuticals following licensing from Nano Terra (now approved in the United States for chronic graft-versus-host disease, cGVHD) (Blair, 2021; Mendoza & Jimenez, 2022; Przepiorka et al., 2022). Biochemical selectivity has been consistently reported across sources: IC₅₀ values are approximately 60–110 nM for ROCK2 with markedly weaker activity on ROCK1 (typically >10 µM), and orthogonal profiling notes ROCK2 ~0.105 µM and ROCK1 ~24 µM, confirming substantial isoform preference (Mendoza & Jimenez, 2022; Blair, 2021; Lake et al., 2020). As an ATP-competitive kinase inhibitor, belumosudil binds the catalytic domain of ROCK2, analogous to the mechanism of other clinically used ROCK inhibitors, but with enhanced isoform selectivity designed to improve tolerability relative to pan-ROCK agents (e.g., fasudil, ripasudil, netarsudil) (Cheng et al., 2025; Mendoza & Jimenez, 2022).

The broader class—ROCK inhibitors—modulates actin–myosin cytoskeletal dynamics by decreasing phosphorylation of myosin regulatory elements and altering actin turnover via downstream nodes (LIMK–cofilin, ERM proteins), with downstream effects on cell contractility, focal adhesions, cell junctions, migration, and myofibroblast differentiation (Cheng et al., 2025). In ophthalmology, ROCK inhibitors have established or emerging therapeutic roles: netarsudil and ripasudil lower intraocular pressure by relaxing trabecular meshwork and Schlemm’s canal, and corneal endothelial repair paradigms (topical ROCK inhibitors and cell therapy adjuncts) exploit cytoskeletal relaxation, enhanced adhesion, and proliferation to accelerate re-endothelialization (Cheng et al., 2025; Leong et al., 2025). Collectively, these features position belumosudil within a drug class with proven ocular pharmacology and strong plausibility for modulating epithelial/endothelial barrier biology through cytoskeletal re-tuning (Leong et al., 2025).

Therapeutic History  
Systemic immunomodulation and anti-fibrotic indications. Belumosudil is FDA-approved for treatment of cGVHD after at least two prior systemic therapies at an oral dose of 200 mg once daily with food; population pharmacokinetics indicate a mean elimination half-life of ~19 hours, primary CYP3A4 metabolism, and predominant fecal excretion, with two metabolites that also inhibit ROCK2 (Przepiorka et al., 2022; Blair, 2021). Across phase 2 programs in cGVHD, objective response rates (65–77%) were observed with 200 mg once or twice daily dosing, with corticosteroid sparing and acceptable tolerability, consolidating a favorable clinical safety/efficacy profile for chronic use in a heavily pretreated, immunologically dysregulated population (Jagasia et al., 2021; Zanin-Zhorov & Blazar, 2021). Beyond cGVHD, belumosudil has been explored in fibrotic diseases (interim idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis signal for attenuated FVC decline) and systemic sclerosis, consistent with class-wide anti-fibrotic properties driven by ROCK2 blockade (Mendoza & Jimenez, 2022).

Ocular use of ROCK inhibitors (class precedent). While belumosudil itself has not been reported in clinical ocular indications, the ROCK inhibitor class has multiple precedents in eye disease. Topical netarsudil and ripasudil are approved or widely used for glaucoma; intravitreal and topical fasudil have been explored for retinal diseases (e.g., diabetic macular edema) with reported safety in small series, and sustained-release ocular delivery of ROCK inhibitors (e.g., AR-13503) has been tested in preclinical models (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021; Cheng et al., 2025). These translational precedents establish that ROCK pathway modulation can be achieved in ocular tissues via topical, intravitreal, or sustained-release routes and that cytoskeletal/vascular barrier targets respond to this mechanism in the eye (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021).

Belumosudil in ocular preclinical models. In a laser-induced choroidal neovascularization/subretinal fibrosis model designed to isolate fibrotic progression, both fasudil (pan-ROCK) and belumosudil (ROCK2-selective) reduced fibrosis volume, preserved retinal morphology, increased retinal thickness, reduced pyknotic nuclei, and decreased type I collagen and α-SMA staining; corrected total cell fluorescence for ROCK1/ROCK2 was reduced, with particularly strong ROCK2 target engagement by belumosudil (occupancy down from 6.7% to 0.1%) (Zandi et al., 2025). Imaging mass cytometry (IMC) and western blot evidence showed reductions in p-MYPT1 in the choroid–RPE complex—consistent with ROCK pathway inhibition—alongside decreases in TGF-β1, fibronectin, vimentin, and α-SMA; belumosudil also more effectively reduced “unspecific” fibrogenic cell populations near the choroid–RPE boundary compared with fasudil and decreased macrophages/perivascular cells, suggesting anti-inflammatory as well as anti-fibrotic activity (Zandi et al., 2025). In vitro, 24-hour ROCK inhibitor exposure in fibroblastic cells altered morphology (loss of long processes, blunted periphery) and decreased cell numbers, consistent with cytoskeletal/phenotype modulation and possible anti-proliferative effects in fibrogenic cells (Zandi et al., 2025).

Dry AMD relevance and gaps. The above in vivo data position ROCK2 inhibition as an anti-fibrotic modifier of the choroid–RPE interface after laser injury, with partial restoration of retinal architecture; however, they do not test dry AMD pathobiology (e.g., sodium iodate RPE toxicity or geographic atrophy models) (Zandi et al., 2025). Direct ocular RPE barrier endpoints—TEER, FITC-dextran flux, and tight-junction protein integrity (ZO-1, claudin-19)—have not been reported for belumosudil in RPE, and the available studies do not include oxidative or complement injury paradigms (H₂O₂, NaIO₃) in ARPE-19, primary human RPE, or in vivo dry AMD models (Zandi et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025; Blair, 2021). Class-level in vitro RPE evidence exists for ROCK inhibition improving wound closure, adhesion, pigmentation, RPE65 expression, and photoreceptor outer-segment phagocytosis in iPSC-RPE with Y-27632, but without barrier readouts, and with limited recovery duration and small sample sizes (Bailey-Steinitz, 2021). Additional pluripotent RPE manufacturing work reports ROCK inhibition suppresses EMT programs (TGF-β/Wnt ligands, SNAI2, collagens) and promotes rapid tight-junction reformation in culture, supporting barrier-relevant cytoskeletal effects at the transcriptomic/phenotypic level, but without TEER or paracellular flux data under oxidative/complement stress (Croze, 2015). In sum, while class-level and belumosudil-specific ocular data support anti-fibrotic and cytoskeletal modulation at the choroid–RPE interface, there is no direct published evidence in the provided literature set that belumosudil rescues TEER, preserves claudin-19/ZO-1, or reduces FITC-dextran flux under H₂O₂ or complement challenge in RPE or that it improves outcomes in NaIO₃ models (Zandi et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025; Blair, 2021).

Mechanism of Action  
ROCK2 biology and junctional/cytoskeletal control. ROCK1/2 are AGC-family serine/threonine kinases activated by RhoA–GTP; they increase actomyosin contractility primarily by phosphorylating myosin light chain (MLC) directly and inhibiting myosin light-chain phosphatase via phosphorylation of the MYPT1 regulatory subunit, elevating p-MLC levels and stabilizing stress fibers and focal adhesions (Cheng et al., 2025). ROCK also phosphorylates LIM kinases to inactivate cofilin, which slows F-actin turnover and further stiffens the cytoskeleton; ERM proteins, adducin, intermediate filament proteins (vimentin, GFAP), and multiple cytoskeletal/membrane linkers are additional substrates that integrate cytoskeletal and junctional dynamics (Cheng et al., 2025). These pathways are sensitive to mechanical inputs (matrix stiffness) and link cytoskeletal remodeling to barrier integrity and cell adhesion, consistent with the observed roles of ROCK in endothelial hyperpermeability and junction stabilization in ocular tissues (Cheng et al., 2025; Leong et al., 2025).

Belumosudil target engagement in choroid–RPE constructs. In vivo in the choroid–RPE complex after laser injury, both fasudil and belumosudil lowered p-MYPT1, indicating on-pathway ROCK inhibition, and reduced fibrotic markers (α-SMA, vimentin, fibronectin, collagen I, TGF-β1), consistent with suppression of myofibroblast activation, ECM deposition, and TGF-β/Smad signaling output (Zandi et al., 2025). Imaging mass cytometry showed spatially localized fibrotic marker expression to the choroid–RPE boundary and a reduction in “unspecific” fibrogenic cell populations with belumosudil relative to fasudil, and a decrease in macrophage and perivascular cell signatures—integrating anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory mechanisms (Zandi et al., 2025). Quantitatively, ROCK2 staining occupancy fell from ~6.7% to 0.1% after belumosudil treatment, stronger than the reduction with fasudil (to 0.7%), supporting efficient ROCK2 target engagement by belumosudil (Zandi et al., 2025).

ROCK and epithelial barrier relevance. Although the RPE-specific tight-junction literature for belumosudil is not available in the provided sources, corneal endothelial studies link ROCK activity to stabilization of ZO-1 and the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump complex; pharmacologic ROCK inhibition promotes cytoskeletal relaxation, spreading, and cyclin-D–dependent proliferation, and has been leveraged clinically to accelerate re-endothelialization and junctional re-establishment (Leong et al., 2025). In vascular endothelia, RhoA/ROCK signaling drives VEGF-induced permeability and tube formation; ROCK inhibition modulates junctional architecture (e.g., “zipper-like” lymphatic endothelial junctions) and reduces permeability, supporting a mechanistic link to barrier tightening (Leong et al., 2025). In diabetic retinal pathology, ROCK1 activation contributes to RPE blebbing and tight-junction opening with outer blood-retinal barrier breakdown; pharmacologic ROCK inhibition (fasudil) improved barrier function and suppressed leukocyte migration and ICAM-1, reducing vascular leakage and neurodegeneration (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021). Together, these data reinforce that ROCK signaling is a proximal regulator of epithelial/endothelial junctions and barrier integrity and that inhibition—especially with an isoform-selective agent that engages ROCK2 in the choroid–RPE complex—has plausible junction-preserving effects in RPE monolayers (Leong et al., 2025; Zandi et al., 2025).

Anti-EMT and anti-fibrotic transcriptional programs. Transcriptomic studies in pluripotent stem cell–derived RPE and other epithelial contexts show ROCK inhibition suppresses EMT-associated transcriptional programs (TGF-β ligands, Wnt components, SNAI2, collagens) and promotes tight-junction reformation, consistent with decreased cytoskeletal tension and reduced MRTF/SRF-dependent transcription that drives myofibroblast markers (e.g., α-SMA) (Croze, 2015; Mendoza & Jimenez, 2022). ROCK2 inhibition also intersects with inflammatory transcriptional axes (STAT3/STAT5) and NF-κB, shifting Th17/Tfh toward Treg and reducing proinflammatory cytokines in PBMCs and cGVHD models, which could help resolve inflammatory components of AMD pathophysiology at the RPE–choroid interface (Blair, 2021; Jagasia et al., 2021; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021).

Cytoskeletal/ciliary modulation in RPE-lineage cells. Independent siRNA/drug screens in ciliogenesis models identified ROCK2 as a strong negative regulator of cilia incidence and length; short and prolonged belumosudil exposure increased cilia metrics in hTERT RPE-1 and mIMCD3 cells while reducing phosphorylated MLC and acto-myosin fiber-like structures, consistent with decreased ROCK2-driven contractility (Lake et al., 2020). Although ciliation per se is not a dry AMD therapeutic endpoint, the data are a sensitive readout of cytoskeletal reprogramming via ROCK2 and reinforce the target’s role in apical organization in epithelia with RPE lineage origin (Lake et al., 2020).

Expected Effect  
Assay context: H₂O₂ and complement injury in RPE monolayers. Under oxidative stress (H₂O₂) and complement attack, the RPE cytoskeleton becomes hypercontractile, junctional belts destabilize, and paracellular permeability increases—manifest as reduced transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) and increased FITC-dextran flux, with mislocalization or fragmentation of tight-junction proteins (ZO-1, claudin-19). ROCK activation (via RhoA–GTP, inflammatory mediators, or mechanical cues) exacerbates this by increasing p-MYPT1 and p-MLC to drive actomyosin tension and stress fiber formation (Cheng et al., 2025; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021). The class literature in ocular epithelia/endothelium indicates that ROCK inhibition relaxes cytoskeletal tension, promotes cell spreading and junctional complex re-assembly (ZO-1 stabilization reported in corneal endothelium), and reduces VEGF- or inflammation-driven hyperpermeability (Leong et al., 2025). In diabetic retinal models, ROCK pathway activation produces RPE blebbing and tight-junction disruption, with pharmacologic ROCK inhibition reversing barrier dysfunction and dampening leukostasis and leakage (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021).

Given these mechanisms, selective ROCK2 inhibition with belumosudil is expected to:
- Reduce p-MYPT1 and downstream p-MLC in RPE, decreasing acto-myosin contractility, stabilizing the circumferential actin belt and adherens/tight junctions (ZO-1/claudin-19), thereby rescuing TEER and lowering FITC-dextran flux under H₂O₂ or complement challenge (Zandi et al., 2025; Leong et al., 2025).
- Suppress EMT-like shifts and myofibroblast activation induced by oxidative/complement stress by lowering α-SMA/vimentin and ECM program activation (fibronectin, collagen), as seen in vivo after laser injury and in ROCK-inhibited RPE culture transcriptomics (Zandi et al., 2025; Croze, 2015).
- Provide anti-inflammatory benefits by reducing macrophage/perivascular cell accumulation seen in vivo and by directly modulating STAT3/STAT5-linked cytokine programs, potentially blunting complement-amplified inflammatory cascades at the RPE/choroid interface (Zandi et al., 2025; Blair, 2021; Jagasia et al., 2021).

Target expression and context. ROCK signaling is active in ocular tissues; p-MYPT1 is reduced in the choroid–RPE complex of laser-injured eyes after ROCK inhibition; ROCK1 translocates and drives cytoskeletal/junctional changes under hyperglycemia in RPE and endothelium; and ROCK2 expression is quantifiably reduced by belumosudil in the choroid–RPE complex—collectively validating target presence and drug engagement at the proposed site of action (Zandi et al., 2025; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021).

Important caveats. The provided literature set does not contain direct demonstrations that belumosudil rescues TEER or reduces FITC-dextran flux in RPE under H₂O₂ or complement injury, nor does it report preservation of claudin-19/ZO-1 localization in RPE with ROCK2-selective inhibition; these remain testable predictions grounded in mechanism and class precedent but not yet substantiated for belumosudil in RPE (Zandi et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025; Blair, 2021). Similarly, no data are provided here for NaIO₃-induced RPE degeneration models with belumosudil; the NaIO₃ discussion pertains to single-cell remodeling and EMT tendencies in dry AMD models, which conceptually align with anti-EMT and anti-fibrotic effects of ROCK inhibition, but require direct in vivo validation for this candidate (Zandi et al., 2025).

Pharmacokinetic/delivery considerations for the assay. Clinically, belumosudil has a ~19-hour half-life, ~64% bioavailability with food (in healthy subjects), and is metabolized by CYP3A4; ocular pharmacokinetics and tissue penetration are not reported in the provided sources (Przepiorka et al., 2022). In the laser-injury mouse model, intraperitoneal administration from day 35 to 49 post-laser achieved retinal/choroid pharmacodynamics (↓p-MYPT1, ↓markers), indicating systemic dosing can engage ocular targets in inflamed/fibrotic tissue in mice, though translational relevance to human ocular exposure remains to be determined and local delivery could be preferable for barrier-rescue assays (Zandi et al., 2025).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- On-pathway target engagement in choroid–RPE with functional tissue outcomes. Belumosudil decreases p-MYPT1 in the choroid–RPE complex, reduces fibrosis volume, attenuates α-SMA, vimentin, fibronectin, collagen I, and TGF-β1, and preserves retinal morphology and thickness in a subretinal fibrosis model; ROCK2 occupancy falls to near-baseline with treatment, indicating strong ocular target engagement (Zandi et al., 2025). These integrated molecular and histologic readouts provide robust evidence that isoform-selective ROCK2 inhibition can reprogram the pathological microenvironment at the RPE–choroid interface after injury—precisely where barrier restoration is therapeutically needed in dry AMD (Zandi et al., 2025).  
- Mechanistic alignment with barrier preservation. ROCK signaling is a proximal regulator of cytoskeletal tension and tight-junction stability; inhibition reduces MLC phosphorylation via MYPT1 de-phosphorylation, stabilizes junctions (corneal ZO-1; endothelial junction “zippering”), and mitigates hyperpermeability (Cheng et al., 2025; Leong et al., 2025). The candidate’s demonstrated ability to lower p-MYPT1 in the choroid–RPE complex and reduce myofibroblast activation fits the hypothesis that it can preserve epithelial barrier integrity under stress (Zandi et al., 2025).  
- Anti-EMT/anti-fibrotic transcriptional effects. ROCK inhibition suppresses TGF-β/Wnt-mediated EMT programs and promotes tight-junction reformation in pluripotent-derived RPE; in vivo reductions in α-SMA and ECM deposition further support anti-fibrotic activity coherent with preventing RPE-to-mesenchymal transitions that compromise the barrier (Croze, 2015; Zandi et al., 2025).  
- Anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory actions. Belumosudil reduces macrophage and perivascular cell signatures in vivo and systemically shifts Th17/Tfh toward Treg via STAT3/STAT5 modulation; these effects could dampen complement- and cytokine-driven RPE injury cascades implicated in dry AMD (Zandi et al., 2025; Blair, 2021; Jagasia et al., 2021; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021).  
- Favorable human safety experience and oral PK. A clinically approved safety profile with chronic administration, a manageable drug–drug interaction profile (CYP3A4), and a ~19-hour half-life provide a de-risked starting point for repurposing; dose selection can be guided by systemic exposures achieved in cGVHD (Przepiorka et al., 2022; Zanin-Zhorov & Blazar, 2021).

Weaknesses and knowledge gaps  
- No direct RPE barrier functional data for belumosudil. The literature set lacks TEER, FITC-dextran flux, or ZO-1/claudin-19 preservation data for belumosudil in RPE under oxidative/complement stress; consequently, the core hypothesis—TEER rescue with ROCK2 selectivity—remains inferential (Zandi et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025; Blair, 2021).  
- No NaIO₃ or dry AMD-specific in vivo efficacy with belumosudil. While NaIO₃ single-cell data support EMT and fibroblast-like RPE signatures relevant to ROCK pathway targeting, belumosudil has not been tested in NaIO₃ or geographic atrophy models here; the current in vivo evidence is laser-injury fibrosis focused (Zandi et al., 2025).  
- Isoform biology nuance in the retina. Diabetic retinal studies emphasize ROCK1 translocation and ROCK1-mediated blebbing/tight-junction disruption, with some reports noting ROCK2 localization as “undisturbed” in those models; this raises a strategic question whether ROCK2-selective inhibition alone is sufficient for barrier rescue across stress etiologies or whether dual inhibition is sometimes preferable (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021).  
- Ocular pharmacokinetics and delivery. There are no data on ocular tissue penetration, retinal/choroidal exposure, or intravitreal/topical formulations for belumosudil; although systemic dosing engaged ocular targets in mice after laser injury, human ocular delivery, durability, and safety require specific assessment (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021; Cheng et al., 2025). Class precedents (intravitreal/topical fasudil, topical netarsudil/ripasudil) are encouraging but not directly transferable to this scaffold without formulation work (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021; Cheng et al., 2025).  
- Off-target and pathway-network considerations. Although belumosudil is selective for ROCK2, it has minor ROCK1 and pathway-adjacent activities (e.g., mTOR), and widespread cytoskeletal re-tuning can have context-dependent effects (e.g., on ciliation) whose long-term retinal implications are unknown (Przepiorka et al., 2022; Lake et al., 2020).

Decision-oriented interpretation for dry AMD  
- Mechanistic plausibility: High. ROCK2 inhibition is expected to directly reduce cytoskeletal tension and stabilize epithelial junctions; on-pathway pharmacodynamic effects (↓p-MYPT1) and anti-fibrotic marker reductions in the choroid–RPE complex support action at the relevant tissue interface (Zandi et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025).  
- Translational anchor: Moderate. The candidate has a de-risked systemic safety profile and class-level ocular pharmacology, plus in vivo anti-fibrotic retinal benefits; however, barrier-specific efficacy in RPE under oxidative/complement stress has not yet been shown for belumosudil (Przepiorka et al., 2022; Zandi et al., 2025).  
- Differentiation vs pan-ROCK: Potentially favorable. In vivo, belumosudil achieved stronger ROCK2 engagement and larger decreases in “unspecific” fibrogenic cell populations than fasudil, aligning with the idea that ROCK2 drives key profibrotic cascades at the choroid–RPE interface, while avoiding ROCK1-linked hypotension liabilities systemically; whether ROCK1 contributions to RPE barrier breakdown under certain stressors would limit efficacy remains to be determined experimentally (Zandi et al., 2025; Mendoza & Jimenez, 2022).

Concrete implications for the proposed hypothesis  
- The specific claims that belumosudil has demonstrated +50% TER recovery in ARPE-19 under H₂O₂ stress, 40% RPE cell preservation and ERG improvement in a mouse NaIO₃ model, and an oral bioavailability ~45% with t₁/₂ ~4.8 h are not supported by the literature set provided; instead, the available PK indicates oral bioavailability is ~64% with food and a ~19-h half-life, and no TER/NaIO₃ efficacy data for belumosudil are referenced here (Przepiorka et al., 2022; Zandi et al., 2025; Blair, 2021). The hypothesized barrier rescue under oxidative/complement injury remains plausible mechanistically but requires direct validation in RPE models (Leong et al., 2025; Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021).

Program-level next steps (to enable a go/no-go)  
- In vitro RPE barrier assays: Primary human RPE and iPSC-RPE monolayers subjected to H₂O₂ and normal human serum (complement) injury; quantify TEER, FITC-dextran flux, and ZO-1/claudin-19 localization/intensity; include p-MYPT1 and p-MLC biochemical readouts as pharmacodynamics; benchmark belumosudil across a concentration range anchored to ROCK2 IC₅₀ and cell viability (Lake et al., 2020; Cheng et al., 2025).  
- Phenotypic rescue breadth: Replicate class-level findings with ROCK2 selectivity—adhesion, wound closure, POS phagocytosis, RPE65 expression—to establish that belumosudil recapitulates the iPSC-RPE Y-27632 phenotype profile while tightening the barrier (Bailey-Steinitz, 2021).  
- In vivo dry AMD model: NaIO₃ or other GA-like models with local ocular delivery (intravitreal or periocular sustained-release) to assess RPE survival, outer blood-retinal barrier integrity, visual function (ERG), and transcriptomic/IMC profiling for EMT/fibrosis and inflammatory modulation; include dose–exposure–response characterization (Zandi et al., 2025).  
- Ocular PK/formulation: Ocular tissue distribution after local delivery and/or systemic administration; stability and duration; comparative exposures in RPE/choroid vs retina with safety assessment (Mateos-Olivares et al., 2021; Cheng et al., 2025).

Overall, belumosudil is a compelling, mechanistically aligned candidate with de-risked systemic safety, clear ocular on-pathway antifibrotic activity at the choroid–RPE interface, and strong biological rationale for junctional stabilization in stressed RPE. The decisive gap is the absence of direct barrier-function rescue data in RPE under oxidative or complement injury and the lack of dry AMD model efficacy. Addressing these with focused preclinical studies will determine whether its ROCK2 selectivity translates into clinically meaningful protection of the outer blood–retinal barrier in dry AMD, and whether it offers advantages over pan-ROCK inhibitors in this context (Zandi et al., 2025; Leong et al., 2025; Mendoza & Jimenez, 2022).
