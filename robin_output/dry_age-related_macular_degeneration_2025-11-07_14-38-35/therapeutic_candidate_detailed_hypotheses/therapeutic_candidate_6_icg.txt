Proposal for ICG-001 (Sigma I2441)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
ICG-001 (Sigma I2441) is a synthetic small-molecule inhibitor originally identified for its ability to selectively disrupt the interaction between β-catenin and the transcriptional coactivator CBP (CREB-binding protein), thereby inhibiting TCF/LEF-dependent Wnt/β-catenin–driven transcription without reducing total β-catenin protein abundance (Wickline, 2013, pp. 26–30, 112–117). Mechanistically, ICG-001 is positioned within a class of β-catenin pathway modulators that block nuclear β-catenin activity rather than decreasing β-catenin protein levels; this distinguishes it from antisense/siRNA approaches and from inhibitors that act upstream in Wnt signaling (e.g., tankyrase inhibitors like XAV-939) (Wickline, 2013, pp. 26–30, 112–117). The CBP/β-catenin inhibitor class has been proposed as a more selective means to modulate β-catenin transcriptional output while sparing adherens junction functions that depend on β-catenin protein pools, a distinction supported by data showing ICG-001 does not trigger compensatory γ-catenin stabilization that follows β-catenin protein depletion (Wickline, 2013, pp. 94–99, 112–117).

Within the broader Wnt-targeting landscape, ICG-001 exemplifies downstream, transcriptional-coactivator–selective targeting, contrasted with agents that block β-catenin/TCF binding, Dishevelled PDZ interactions, Porcupine-mediated ligand maturation, or Frizzled receptor activity (e.g., OMP-18R5) (Assaf, 2018, pp. 28–31). Because Wnt pathway effects are highly context dependent and temporally dynamic across tissues, coactivator-selective modulation has been argued to mitigate systemic liabilities relative to global pathway inhibition (Assaf, 2018, pp. 28–31).

Therapeutic History  
ICG-001 itself is widely used as a research tool, and its clinical derivative/prodrug analogs (e.g., PRI-724/OP-724) have reached Phase I/IIa testing in liver fibrosis/cirrhosis, establishing a preliminary human safety/tolerability and feasibility record for CBP/β-catenin pathway inhibition in chronic disease. In HCV-induced cirrhosis, an open-label Phase 1 dose-escalation trial of PRI-724 administered continuous i.v. infusions (10–160 mg/m2/day; 1 week on/1 week off for 12 weeks) with primary outcomes focused on adverse events and exploratory anti-fibrotic endpoints (histology, serum fibrosis biomarkers); the program reported safety/tolerability and preliminary efficacy consistent with anti-fibrotic activity (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2014). In HBV/HCV-related cirrhosis, a Phase I/IIa open-label study (twice-weekly 4 h infusions; 140, 280, 380 mg/m2 per 4 h) evaluated safety, PK, and change in liver fibrosis area by biopsy at 12 weeks; the registry cites a derived EBioMedicine report on safety, tolerability, and anti-fibrotic effects (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2018). In advanced primary biliary cholangitis (PBC; Scheuer stage ≥III), OP-724 (formerly PRI-724) underwent a Phase I dose-finding trial (4 h i.v. infusions, twice weekly for 12 weeks; 140/280/380 mg/m2 cohorts) assessing serious adverse events, PK of OP-724 and metabolite C-82, and fibrosis-related endpoints (biopsy fibrotic area, FibroScan, Child-Pugh/MELD, ELF score) with feasibility/safety results published (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2019).

No clinical or preclinical ocular applications of ICG-001/PRI-724/OP-724 were identified in the provided context, and there are no reports here of use in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD), or retinal barrier disorders (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2014, 2018, 2019). Preclinical oncology literature summarized in the provided sources documents ICG-001’s activity in cancer cells (reduced proliferation and induced selective apoptosis via TCF-dependent signaling reduction) but does not address ocular tissues (Wickline, 2013, pp. 26–30). Thus, while the drug class has human safety experience in fibrotic liver disease, there is no direct evidence in the provided context for ophthalmic use or for dry AMD (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2014, 2018, 2019).

Mechanism of Action  
ICG-001 binds CBP and selectively disrupts the β-catenin/CBP complex, attenuating β-catenin–driven transcription of TCF/LEF target genes (e.g., Cyclin D1) without decreasing total β-catenin protein levels; in Hep3B cells, ICG-001 reduced TOPflash reporter activity and Cyclin D1 expression while leaving β-catenin abundance unchanged and without inducing compensatory γ-catenin stabilization (if anything, γ-catenin modestly decreased) (Wickline, 2013, pp. 94–99). Across multiple excerpts, ICG-001 is consistently described as selective for β-catenin/CBP over β-catenin/p300, reducing TCF-dependent Wnt output while sparing other β-catenin protein interactions; global β-catenin levels are unaffected, reinforcing a functional separation between nuclear transcriptional roles and membrane adherens junction pools (Wickline, 2013, pp. 26–30). Authors highlight that agents which lower β-catenin protein (ASO/siRNA) trigger γ-catenin compensation at adherens junctions—distinct from ICG-001, which blocks transcriptional activity without provoking such protein-level sensing/reciprocity (Wickline, 2013, pp. 112–117, 140–145). It remains explicitly unknown from these data whether ICG-001 perturbs adherens junction function in vivo; follow-up studies were recommended to test junctional tolerance to transcriptional blockade (Wickline, 2013, pp. 140–145).

Contextual biology of β-catenin coactivator usage indicates that CBP vs p300 partnering can impart divergent phenotypic outcomes; in fibrotic contexts, CBP-associated β-catenin activity has been linked with pro-fibrotic programs whereas p300-associated activity may be protective, though this literature is complex and model-dependent (Assaf, 2018, pp. 66–69). Canonical β-catenin signaling is also subject to Wnt-independent inputs that promote nuclear translocation/transactivation (e.g., PKA/Akt phosphorylation), and β-catenin exists in distinct pools with membrane-associated adherens junction functions that are regulated by tyrosine phosphorylation events, further implying that selective transcriptional blockade (CBP-directed) may spare adhesive roles (Wickline, 2013, pp. 21–26). Together, these mechanistic data support a rationale for testing CBP/β-catenin–selective inhibitors for diseases where β-catenin transcription drives pathology and where preserving epithelial junctional architecture is desirable (Wickline, 2013, pp. 26–30, 140–145).

Disease-Relevant Biology in RPE and Retinal Barriers  
RPE epithelial phenotype and outer blood–retina barrier (oBRB) integrity depend on tight junction (TJ) and adherens junction (AJ) complexes (ZO-1/occludin/claudins; E-cadherin/catenins). Loss of ZO-1 releases ZONAB to drive CDK4 and proliferation; downregulation of E-cadherin frees β-catenin to translocate to the nucleus and activate EMT/proliferation drivers (e.g., Snail, Cyclin D1), establishing a mechanistic link between junctional disruption and β-catenin–dependent transcriptional programs in RPE (Zhou et al., 2020, pp. 6–9). Reviews of RPE EMT highlight Wnt–β-catenin as a key mediator in the eye; experimental injury (laser photocoagulation) activates β-catenin signaling and facilitates RPE proliferation, reinforcing the pathway’s involvement in EMT and regenerative responses (Zhou et al., 2020, pp. 7–9).

Critically, primary hRPE culture studies demonstrate that Matrigel plus Activin A (MA) fosters epithelial morphology, increases TJ gene/protein expression (Claudin-1, Claudin-3, Occludin, ZO-1), improves apical ZO-1 polarity, and elevates TEER; this phenotype is accompanied by increased β-catenin levels and β-catenin/TCF target expression (TCF-4, Survivin, Cyclin D1, c-Myc), and is suppressed by the Wnt/β-catenin inhibitor XAV-939, implicating canonical β-catenin activity in barrier maturation under these culture conditions (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–11). The MA paradigm also shows anti-apoptotic effects (lower ROS, preserved mitochondrial potential, lower Annexin V positivity) linked to elevated β-catenin/Survivin, again consistent with β-catenin supporting epithelial survival when cell–cell contacts are intact (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–11).

Conversely, disease models and inflammatory contexts show scenarios where β-catenin activation correlates with barrier breakdown. In diabetic macular edema (DME) models, SMURF1 promotes WIF1 ubiquitination and degradation, activating Wnt/β-catenin–VEGF signaling, inducing autophagy, and disrupting RPE tight junctions; knockdown of SMURF1 restores WIF1, suppresses pathway activation, and rescues claudin-1/occludin/ZO-1 expression in ARPE-19 and in STZ mice (Liang et al., 2025, pp. 8–10). Inflammatory TNFα challenge in polarized ARPE-19 reduces TER, increases paracellular permeability, and disrupts ZO-1/claudins/occludin and AJ components; the HDAC inhibitor ITF2357 restores TER and junctional proteins and suppresses β-catenin overexpression and NF-κB signaling, supporting the concept that limiting β-catenin–linked transcription can be barrier-protective under inflammatory stress (Lim et al., 2024, pp. 10–19). Under oxidative stress relevant to dry AMD, ex vivo primary porcine RPE (ppRPE) exposed to NaIO₃ shows a significant reduction in ZO-1 immunopositive area (with unchanged TJP1 mRNA), indicating tight-junction localization loss; while the Wnt/β-catenin pathway is not measured directly in this model, the outcome establishes a testable context for barrier rescue (Wagner et al., 2024, pp. 14–15).

These data collectively underscore a context-dependent role of β-catenin in RPE: when epithelial contacts are robust, β-catenin activity can support maturation, TJ expression, and survival; under EMT/injury/inflammatory stress (e.g., DME/TNFα), β-catenin pathway activation correlates with junctional loss, VEGF induction, and autophagy, and pathway suppression can be protective (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–11; Liang et al., 2025, pp. 8–10; Lim et al., 2024, pp. 10–19).

Expected Effect  
Proposed assay context: RPE monolayers subjected to oxidative stress (e.g., NaIO₃, H₂O₂, or serum withdrawal models) with readouts including TEER, paracellular flux, and localization/expression of ZO-1 and claudin-1.

- Gene/protein expression in RPE: ZO-1, claudins, occludin, and cadherins/β-catenin are established components of RPE junctions and are readily measured; TEER increases in mature hRPE and decreases with injury provide a functional correlate of barrier status (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7; Zhou, 2022, pp. 51–55). β-catenin and downstream targets (Cyclin D1, Survivin, c-Myc, TCF-4) were upregulated in hRPE MA cultures and are suppressible by Wnt pathway inhibition, confirming pathway engagement in RPE (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–11).

- Hypothesized pharmacology: ICG-001 is expected to reduce β-catenin/CBP-dependent transcription (Cyclin D1, Survivin, TOPflash) without decreasing total β-catenin protein or stabilizing γ-catenin; this has been demonstrated in hepatocyte-derived models (Wickline, 2013, pp. 94–99, 112–117). In RPE, the immediate expected effect would be downregulation of β-catenin/TCF-LEF targets that are CBP-dependent. The direction of effect on junctional genes is uncertain based on available data: in MA-conditioned hRPE, canonical β-catenin activity correlates with increased claudin-1 and ZO-1 and higher TEER, and global pathway inhibition reduces these effects; thus CBP-targeted inhibition might risk dampening TJ upregulation under certain epithelial conditions (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–11). In inflammatory/DME-like contexts, β-catenin activation contributes to VEGF/autophagy-mediated TJ disruption, and Wnt pathway suppression is protective; in these contexts, CBP blockade could plausibly reduce pathway-driven barrier loss (Liang et al., 2025, pp. 8–10).

- Proliferation concerns: ICG-001 reduces Cyclin D1 in non-ocular cells, consistent with attenuating proliferation signaling (Wickline, 2013, pp. 94–99). In RPE EMT, β-catenin activation promotes proliferation via Cyclin D1; thus ICG-001 would be expected to mitigate proliferation-associated EMT programs rather than activate them (Zhou et al., 2020, pp. 6–7, 7–9). However, MA-driven β-catenin activity increases Survivin/Bcl2 and enhances anti-apoptotic resilience; therefore, under oxidative stress, attenuating β-catenin/CBP with ICG-001 could trade off survival signals against potential EMT suppression, and net effects on TEER may vary by stressor and co-signaling milieu (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–11).

- Net expectation in oxidative stress assays: Under acute oxidative stress where junctional proteins disassemble (e.g., NaIO₃ model reduces ZO-1 area), there is no direct evidence provided that CBP-selective β-catenin inhibition upregulates claudin-1/ZO-1 or restores TEER in RPE. In analogous inflammatory contexts, limiting β-catenin–linked transcription correlates with barrier recovery, but this was achieved with an HDAC inhibitor and via suppression of β-catenin overexpression/NF-κB, not CBP-selective Wnt blockade (Lim et al., 2024, pp. 10–19). Therefore, the claim that ICG-001 will “bias gene transcription toward junctional protein upregulation (claudin-1, ZO-1) in RPE under oxidative stress without activating proliferation pathways, leading to recovery of TEER and decreased paracellular flux” is mechanistically plausible in EMT/inflammatory settings but remains unsubstantiated for oxidative stress in RPE; the directionality of effect on junctional genes could invert depending on baseline epithelial status and co-activation of survival pathways (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7; Liang et al., 2025, pp. 8–10).

Therapeutic Risk–Benefit Considerations  
- Safety/translatability: Clinical-stage CBP/β-catenin inhibitors (PRI-724/OP-724) have demonstrated safety/tolerability in Phase I/IIa liver fibrosis trials, supporting repurposing potential; however, the dosing was i.v., and ocular delivery, PK/PD at the RPE, and retinal safety are uncharacterized in the provided data (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2014, 2019; Wickline, 2013, pp. 140–145).

- Mechanistic specificity: ICG-001 spares total β-catenin protein and does not trigger γ-catenin compensation, mitigating concerns about adherens junction destabilization that accompany β-catenin depletion; nevertheless, whether ICG-001 affects adherens junction functionality in epithelial tissues in vivo remains unknown, and this must be directly assessed in RPE (Wickline, 2013, pp. 140–145).

- Pathway context dependence: RPE barrier outcomes with Wnt/β-catenin modulation are bidirectional and context dependent—MA-induced β-catenin activity enhances TJs/TEER, while in DME/inflammation β-catenin activation correlates with TJ disruption; CBP-specific blockade could be beneficial in the latter but deleterious in the former (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7; Liang et al., 2025, pp. 8–10). This necessitates careful stressor stratification (oxidative versus inflammatory/VEGF-driven) in preclinical testing.

- Disease relevance to dry AMD: Oxidative stress disrupts RPE TJs and reduces ZO-1 localization in ex vivo ppRPE; EMT and barrier breakdown are recognized contributors to RPE dysfunction; however, there is no direct evidence here linking CBP/β-catenin inhibition to barrier rescue in oxidative injury models or to slowed geographic atrophy progression (Wagner et al., 2024, pp. 14–15; Zhou et al., 2020, pp. 6–7).

Experimental De-Risking Plan (RPE-Centric)  
Given the absence of direct ocular data for ICG-001, a staged validation plan is warranted:

- In vitro RPE barrier assays:  
  • Use primary hRPE on Transwells to maturity (baseline TER per established values) (Zhou, 2022, pp. 51–55). Challenge with oxidative stress (NaIO₃/H₂O₂) and inflammatory stress (TNFα) in parallel. Quantify TER, fluorescein paracellular flux, and immunolocalization of ZO-1/claudin-1/occludin and AJ proteins. Test ICG-001 across a potency range informed by cell studies (e.g., up to 10 μM) and compare with XAV-939 (Wnt inhibition control) and an HDAC inhibitor (ITF2357, positive control for barrier rescue under TNFα) (Lim et al., 2024, pp. 15–19; Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7; Wickline, 2013, pp. 112–117). Assess β-catenin target gene modulation (Cyclin D1, Survivin, TCF-4) and VEGF (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 9–11; Liang et al., 2025, pp. 8–10).

  • Hypothesis testing criteria: Evidence of TEER recovery and reduced paracellular flux with maintained or improved ZO-1/claudin-1 localization in oxidative and inflammatory paradigms without induction of proliferation markers (Cyclin D1) would support the proposed mechanism; failure or opposite effects would argue against it (Lim et al., 2024, pp. 15–19; Wickline, 2013, pp. 94–99).

- Ex vivo ppRPE coculture:  
  • Replicate NaIO₃-induced ZO-1 reduction and test whether ICG-001 preserves/restores ZO-1 area without elevating proliferation/EMT markers; track apoptosis/survival readouts given MA data tying β-catenin to anti-apoptotic signaling (Wagner et al., 2024, pp. 14–15; Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7).

- In vivo relevance:  
  • If in vitro/ex vivo are positive, progress to mouse models with RPE oxidative injury (e.g., NaIO₃ systemic exposure) and quantify RPE barrier integrity (ZO-1 localization, RPE flat-mounts), photoreceptor/RPE survival, and functional outcomes; consider delivery routes (intravitreal/subconjunctival) and PK (not available here) (Wagner et al., 2024, pp. 14–15).

- Mechanistic dissection:  
  • Evaluate whether CBP-specific blockade shifts β-catenin coactivator usage toward p300-dependent programs (no direct assays in provided texts) and whether junctional gene regulation exhibits CBP selectivity; this would mechanistically address the “biased transcription” claim (Assaf, 2018, pp. 66–69).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Defined, selective mechanism: ICG-001 disrupts β-catenin/CBP complexes and suppresses TCF-dependent transcription without lowering total β-catenin; it does not trigger γ-catenin compensation observed with β-catenin protein knockdown, offering a mechanistically precise intervention that may spare adherens junction architecture (Wickline, 2013, pp. 94–99, 112–117, 140–145, 26–30).

- Clinical-stage drug class: The CBP/β-catenin inhibitor analogs PRI-724/OP-724 have human safety and PK data in liver fibrosis/cirrhosis/PBC, supporting translational feasibility and providing a clinical development template, albeit outside ophthalmology (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2014, 2018, 2019).

- Biological plausibility in RPE EMT and barrier dysfunction: RPE EMT and barrier loss are linked to β-catenin nuclear signaling; under inflammatory/DME-like conditions, Wnt/β-catenin activation disrupts TJs via VEGF/autophagy and Wnt-pathway suppression restores claudin-1/ZO-1, suggesting that pathway attenuation can be barrier-protective in disease contexts where β-catenin drives pathogenic transcription (Liang et al., 2025, pp. 8–10; Zhou et al., 2020, pp. 6–7).

- Assay tractability: RPE barrier endpoints (TEER, paracellular flux, ZO-1/claudin-1 localization) are robust; β-catenin target genes provide PD markers to confirm on-target activity, enabling a decisive preclinical go/no-go within RPE-relevant stress paradigms (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7; Lim et al., 2024, pp. 15–19; Zhou, 2022, pp. 51–55).

Key Limitations and Risks  
- No direct ocular/RPE data for ICG-001: The provided context contains no studies of ICG-001 (or PRI-724/OP-724) in RPE or ocular tissues, no TEER/paracellular flux rescue data, and no AMD model outcomes; claims of junction-selective “bias” in RPE are speculative and unproven here (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2014; ClinicalTrials.gov, 2019; Wickline, 2013, pp. 140–145).

- Context-dependent and bidirectional β-catenin effects in RPE: In hRPE cultured with MA, β-catenin activity correlates with increased claudin-1/ZO-1 and higher TEER, and Wnt pathway inhibition (XAV-939) diminishes these; thus, in certain epithelial contexts, β-catenin/CBP inhibition could impair, not enhance, junctional gene expression and barrier function (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–11).

- Survival/proliferation trade-offs: β-catenin activation increased anti-apoptotic factors (Survivin/Bcl2) and protected hRPE from serum withdrawal stress; ICG-001’s suppression of β-catenin target genes could compromise survival signaling under oxidative stress, and the net effect on barrier integrity/survival requires direct testing (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7).

- Mechanistic uncertainty regarding “biased transcription” toward junctional genes: No evidence is provided that CBP-selective inhibition favors claudin-1/ZO-1 expression; indeed, Sox/TCF targets include Cyclin D1 and c-Myc, and the directionality of CBP versus p300 coactivator usage on RPE junctional gene programs is not defined by the present sources (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 9–11; Assaf, 2018, pp. 66–69).

- Delivery and ocular PK/PD unknowns: Clinical liver trials used i.v. infusions; ocular tolerability, retinal exposure, and local pharmacology for ICG-001/OP-724/PRI-724 are not addressed here, requiring formulation and delivery development (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2014; ClinicalTrials.gov, 2019).

Decision Implications  
On the present evidence, ICG-001 has a coherent, selective mechanism with clinical-stage validation in a non-ocular indication, and there is strong biological plausibility that β-catenin transcriptional blockade can counter EMT/proliferation programs in RPE. However, the specific hypothesis that ICG-001 will “bias” transcription to upregulate claudin-1/ZO-1 and restore TEER under oxidative stress is not supported by the provided literature; in fact, RPE studies show that β-catenin activation is associated with higher TJ expression/TEER in some contexts, and pathway inhibition reduces these features, whereas in inflammatory/DME contexts β-catenin activation is deleterious and suppression is protective (Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7; Liang et al., 2025, pp. 8–10). Therefore, a tightly scoped preclinical program should be undertaken to empirically resolve these context-dependent effects in RPE before committing to development for dry AMD.

Recommendation  
- Proceed with an in vitro/ex vivo feasibility package to test ICG-001 in RPE barrier models under defined stressors (oxidative vs inflammatory), with rigorous functional and molecular endpoints and on-target PD confirmation; include orthogonal comparators (XAV-939, ITF2357) to position CBP-selective modulation against broader Wnt suppression and epigenetic anti-inflammatory rescue (Lim et al., 2024, pp. 15–19; Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7). Advance only if ICG-001 reproducibly restores TEER and junctional localization without pro-EMT/proliferation signatures across oxidative stress conditions relevant to dry AMD (Wagner et al., 2024, pp. 14–15; Zhou et al., 2020, pp. 6–7).

- If positive, initiate ocular PK/tolerability and RPE-targeted delivery studies; if negative or mixed, deprioritize in favor of alternative approaches with direct evidence of barrier rescue in RPE (e.g., HDAC inhibitors in inflammatory settings), recognizing that pathway- and context-specificity critically determine outcomes in RPE biology (Lim et al., 2024, pp. 15–19; Lim et al., 2024, pp. 10–12).

In sum, ICG-001 is a mechanistically attractive, clinically precedented β-catenin/CBP inhibitor, but the claim of junction-focused, proliferation-sparing barrier rescue in oxidatively stressed RPE is currently hypothetical and not substantiated by the provided literature; decisive RPE-focused experiments are required to validate or refute its potential for dry AMD (Wickline, 2013, pp. 26–30; Guo et al., 2016, pp. 5–7; Liang et al., 2025, pp. 8–10).
