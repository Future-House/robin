Proposal for H-151 (MedChemExpress HY-12581)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
H-151 is a small-molecule antagonist of the innate immune adaptor STING (TMEM173) that was developed as part of a chemical class designed to prevent the palmitoylation-dependent activation of STING at the Golgi following cyclic dinucleotide binding and ER-to-Golgi trafficking (i.e., cGAMP-bound STING oligomerization and signalosome assembly) (Decout et al., 2021; Li et al., 2021). In its mechanistic class, H-151 is grouped with covalent STING inhibitors that target Cys91 within the transmembrane region–proximal cytosolic interface, thereby preventing activation-induced palmitoylation at Cys88/Cys91 and downstream signaling; nitrofuran analogs such as C-176 and C-178 are the canonical examples where Cys91-directed covalent modification has been mapped by probe chemistry and mass spectrometry (with consequent suppression of type I interferon and IL-6 in vivo) (Decout et al., 2021). STING activation is known to require ligand-induced conformational change, ER exit, and palmitoylation on Cys88/Cys91 to drive oligomerization/polymerization and recruitment of TBK1 and other signal transducers; antagonists that block this palmitoylation step are therefore positioned to broadly prevent both IRF3-dependent type I interferon and NF-κB–dependent inflammatory outputs (Li et al., 2021; Pan et al., 2021).

H-151 has been characterized in inflammatory disease models as a low molecular weight, irreversible inhibitor of human and mouse STING that blocks activation-induced palmitoylation and attenuates downstream signaling (with primary mechanistic references tracing to the discovery work summarized by Pan et al. and by Decout et al.) (Pan et al., 2021; Decout et al., 2021). In keratinocyte and immune-cell systems relevant to psoriatic inflammation, H-151 has reproducibly suppressed STING-dependent NF-κB and IRF3 phosphorylation, inhibited proinflammatory cytokine mRNA induction (e.g., IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α), and reduced STING protein levels, consistent with an antagonist mechanism upstream of canonical downstream effectors (Pan et al., 2021). Conceptually, H-151 belongs to the broader class of cGAS–STING pathway modulators that have been deployed to suppress sterile inflammation across multiple tissues; for the eye and retina, related pathway inhibitors (e.g., C-176, SN-011, cryptotanshinone) have shown efficacy in ischemic and neovascular models via suppression of microglial STING signaling and cytokine/chemokine/protease outputs, supporting the translatability of STING antagonism to ocular tissues (Ni et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2024).

Therapeutic History  
There is no reported clinical use of H-151 in humans, and no published veterinary or clinical trials in ophthalmology were identified in the provided literature set; H-151 has been used as a research tool compound in preclinical inflammatory models, most prominently in psoriasis, where it decreased lesion severity and cutaneous inflammatory readouts in the imiquimod model (Pan et al., 2021). In psoriasis-relevant cell systems, H-151 suppressed cGAMP-induced IRF3 and NF-κB p65 phosphorylation and reduced IFN-β, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α expression, consistent with on-pathway inhibition of STING and its downstream transcriptional programs (Pan et al., 2021). In vivo, topical H-151 improved psoriasis-like dermatitis and decreased markers linked to STING/NF-κB signaling, again without human clinical translation to date (Pan et al., 2021).

Ocular/retinal precedent for the STING target is strong even if H-151–specific data are sparse. In multiple retinal contexts, activation of cGAS–STING has been implicated in pathogenesis through DNA-damage and mitochondrial DNA leakage, leading to IRF3/NF-κB activation, SASP-like cytokine induction, angiogenesis, and barrier dysfunction (Hu et al., 2023). Genetic and pharmacologic pathway perturbations show benefit: genetic loss of STING limits endothelial senescence and vascular degeneration in diabetic models (Liu et al., 2023), genetic deletion along the cGAS–STING axis rescues Alu DNA–driven RPE toxicity (implicated in geographic atrophy) (Hu et al., 2023), and pharmacologic STING inhibitors (C-176, SN-011) suppress neovascularization in laser CNV and OIR models (Ni et al., 2024). Importantly for RPE biology, senescent ARPE-19 cells were reported to overproduce VEGF and this overproduction was prevented by H-151, providing a direct albeit single-study line of evidence that H-151 can modulate pathogenic angiogenic/SASP output in an RPE-derived cell line (Hu et al., 2023). Beyond H-151, epigenetic suppression of STING (JQ1), PPARα activation, and visual cycle modulation (reducing toxic retinoid stress) each attenuate cGAS–STING activation and downstream inflammation or degeneration, underscoring the therapeutic appeal of targeting this axis in retinal disease (Hu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022).

Mechanism of Action  
STING biology and the palmitoylation node. STING is a homodimeric ER-resident adaptor with an N-terminal multi-pass transmembrane domain and a cytosolic C-terminal ligand-binding domain; binding of 2’3’-cGAMP (produced by cGAS upon sensing cytosolic dsDNA) induces conformational rearrangements that enable ER exit and trafficking to the Golgi, where palmitoylation of Cys88 and Cys91 is critical for higher-order assembly and recruitment of TBK1 and other signaling proteins (Li et al., 2021). Palmitoylation-driven tetramerization/higher-order oligomerization is a central activation step for STING, and mutational or pharmacologic interference with Cys88/Cys91 palmitoylation abrogates downstream signal propagation (Pan et al., 2021; Li et al., 2021). Activated STING scaffolds TBK1 to phosphorylate IRF3 (e.g., at Ser366), triggering type I interferon gene expression, and engages NF-κB to induce a broad proinflammatory transcriptional program, including SASP components (Liu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2021). In retinal disease contexts, cGAS–STING activation (driven by nuclear/mtDNA release or Alu-derived nucleic acids) is linked to up-regulation of VEGF via NF-κB/HIF-1α in oxidative-stress–induced RPE senescence, endothelial and microglial inflammatory activation, and barrier compromise (Hu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Qiu et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025).

H-151 and palmitoylation blockade. H-151 is positioned mechanistically among irreversible covalent inhibitors of STING that target Cys91 to prevent activation-associated palmitoylation and signalosome formation (Decout et al., 2021). While detailed covalent adduct chemistry for H-151 itself is not contained in the supplied excerpts, authoritative mechanistic reviews depict H-151 in the “Targeting Cys91” class, alongside nitrofuran inhibitors (C-176, C-178) that have been shown to bind Cys91 selectively and irreversibly (but not Cys88) and that suppress in vivo IFN and IL-6 responses to STING agonism (Decout et al., 2021). Primary disease-model data support a functional blockade of STING signal transmission by H-151: it reduced STING protein abundance in keratinocytes and THP-1 monocytes, inhibited cGAMP-driven IRF3 and NF-κB p65 phosphorylation, and suppressed downstream cytokine expression (IFN-β, IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α) (Pan et al., 2021). In the psoriasis model, H-151 strongly downregulated NF-κB activity and IL-6 and reduced p-p65 and total p65, with a more variable impact on IRF3 phosphorylation depending on system and readout, suggesting it can dominantly blunt the NF-κB arm of STING signaling while at least partially reducing IRF3 activation (Pan et al., 2021). The lack of additivity with an NF-κB inhibitor (BAY 11-7082) on IL-6 suppression implies pathway overlap and supports that H-151’s anti-inflammatory effects in that model are mediated primarily by interference with STING → NF-κB–driven cytokine production (Pan et al., 2021).

Rationale in retinal cells and tissue. cGAS and STING are expressed across retinal cell types (RPE, microglia, endothelial cells, Müller glia, photoreceptors) and are upregulated in disease states (AMD/DR), with promoter accessibility and mRNA levels increased in diseased tissue, positioning the pathway as targetable across multiple ocular compartments (Hu et al., 2023). Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction cause cytosolic mtDNA accumulation that activates cGAS–STING; Alu-derived nucleic acids similarly drive cytosolic DNA sensing and RPE cytotoxicity in GA, with multiple studies showing that genetic deletion along the cGAS–STING axis rescues Alu DNA toxicity (Hu et al., 2023). In RPE biology specifically, STING is implicated in senescence-associated VEGF upregulation, and H-151 prevented VEGF overproduction in senescent ARPE-19 cells, indicating that palmitoylation-targeted STING antagonism can suppress a central angiogenic/SASP mediator in stressed RPE-like cells (Hu et al., 2023). In retinal vascular and microglial compartments, pathway inhibition reduces inflammatory and proteolytic mediators (e.g., lowering Il6 and Mmp9) and blunts vascular leakage and neovascular pathology, as shown with cryptotanshinone, C-176, and SN-011 (Qiu et al., 2025; Ni et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2024). Collectively, these data situate H-151’s palmitoylation blockade at a nodal point capable of broadly dampening IRF3 and NF-κB outputs in ocular cells that contribute to SASP, MMP expression, VEGF induction, and barrier compromise relevant to dry AMD (Hu et al., 2023).

Important mechanistic caveats. RPE models are heterogeneous in cGAS expression and DNA-sensor usage; some RPE cell lines lack cGAS and instead rely on other nucleic acid sensors (e.g., RIG-I, ZBP1), and the relative contribution of cGAS–STING versus alternative pathways varies by context (Hu et al., 2023). Moreover, the balance of STING outputs (IRF3/type I IFN versus NF-κB) can be cell/state dependent, as reflected by psoriasis-model findings where systemic IFN-β was unchanged despite local suppression of p-IRF3 and p65 in skin (Pan et al., 2021). These observations underscore that while H-151 inhibits upstream STING activation, the phenotype in any given ocular assay will reflect cell-type–specific wiring, stimulus context (Alu vs mtDNA vs oxidant stress), and cross-talk with other innate immune axes (Hu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023).

Expected Effect  
Assay context and target presence. The proposed assays—senescence models in RPE with readouts including TEER, ZO-1 continuity, SASP cytokines (IL-6/IL-8), and MMPs—interrogate processes (barrier integrity, SASP-driven inflammation, matrix remodeling) that are mechanistically tied to cGAS–STING–NF-κB signaling and are central to dry AMD progression (Blasiak, 2020; Ochoa Hernández et al., 2025; Terao et al., 2022). RPE expresses STING and engages cGAS–STING signaling under oxidative and senescent stress; senescent ARPE-19 cells overproduce VEGF via a STING/NF-κB/HIF-1α pathway, and H-151 prevented this VEGF overproduction, providing direct cell-type–relevant evidence of efficacy on a SASP/angiogenic factor in RPE (Hu et al., 2023). In addition, cGAS inhibition alleviates Alu RNA–induced immune responses and cytotoxicity in RPE, consistent with the pathway’s role in dsDNA/dsRNA-triggered sterile inflammation and suggesting that upstream DNA-sensor blockade mitigates RPE injury (Li et al., 2022).

Expected pharmacodynamic effects. Based on H-151’s mechanism at the palmitoylation node and its demonstrated suppression of STING-dependent IRF3 and NF-κB activation in immune and epithelial cells, we expect the following in RPE senescence assays:

- Proximal signaling: reductions in STING abundance (as seen in keratinocytes/THP-1), p-TBK1, p-IRF3, and NF-κB p65 phosphorylation following H-151 treatment under stimuli that engage cGAS–STING (e.g., H₂O₂-induced senescence, Alu cDNA transfection, mtDNA transfection) (Pan et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2022).

- Cytokines/SASP: decreased secretion and/or expression of IL-6 and potentially IL-8 and other SASP components; in non-ocular systems H-151 lowered IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α, IFN-β, and IFN-stimulated gene signatures, and in ARPE-19 senescence models H-151 prevented VEGF overproduction (Pan et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2023). Because NF-κB is a principal SASP driver in oxidant-stressed RPE, H-151’s suppression of NF-κB should proportionally lower SASP cytokines, including IL-6/IL-8, which are repeatedly implicated in AMD pathobiology (Terao et al., 2022; Blasiak, 2020; Ochoa Hernández et al., 2025).

- MMPs and matrix remodeling: in retinal microglia, pharmacologic STING inhibition reduced Mmp9 expression alongside Il6 and diminished vascular leakage in OIR, suggesting that blocking STING can lower proteases that degrade extracellular matrix and compromise barriers (Qiu et al., 2025; Terao et al., 2022). While H-151-specific MMP data in RPE have not been reported here, we would predict a reduction in MMP-9 in senescent RPE given the NF-κB dependence of SASP proteases and the precedent in ocular STING inhibition (Qiu et al., 2025; Terao et al., 2022).

- Barrier integrity (TEER and ZO-1): direct H-151 data on RPE TEER/ZO-1 are unavailable in the provided literature. However, STING activity has been linked to barrier dysfunction in retinal endothelium (inner barrier), with evidence that STING activation through TBK1/IRF3/IFN-β axes amplifies senescence and compromises barrier integrity; genetic STING deficiency mitigates such changes (Wu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023). In the outer retina, RPE degeneration driven by inflammatory stimuli is attenuated in STING knockout mice, supporting that STING inhibition can preserve epithelial integrity under inflammatory stress (Hu et al., 2023). Thus, we expect H-151 to preserve TEER and maintain ZO-1 continuity in RPE senescence models where cGAS–STING–NF-κB contributes to junctional remodeling and MMP-driven degradation, but this prediction requires experimental confirmation given the absence of direct H-151 barrier data (Hu et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2025).

- Anti-senescence signatures: in non-ocular systems, H-151 treatment decreased cGAS–STING pathway activation, reduced ISGs, lowered P16/P21, and decreased SA-β-gal activity, indicating that STING antagonism can blunt senescence programs and SASP (Ye et al., 2024). Given the overlap between senescence and innate immune signaling in RPE and AMD, we expect H-151 to reduce senescence markers in RPE senescence assays, again pending direct validation in ocular cells (Blasiak, 2020; Terao et al., 2022).

Expression in the assayed RPE lineage is supported by disease-relevant data showing STING expression and activation in retinal tissues and RPE under stress, including oxidative stress and Alu/mtDNA triggers; cGAS–STING pathway genes are broadly expressed in the retina and upregulated in disease, and RPE senescence engages STING-dependent NF-κB/HIF-1α signaling to drive VEGF (Hu et al., 2023).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Strong disease-mechanism fit. Dry AMD pathogenesis features chronic oxidative stress, DNA damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and senescence in RPE, with resultant SASP cytokines, VEGF, and MMPs that degrade extracellular matrix and compromise barriers; cGAS–STING is positioned as a key sensor/effector linking cytosolic DNA to NF-κB/IRF3 activation and SASP output in RPE and retinal cell types (Blasiak, 2020; Ochoa Hernández et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2019; Hu et al., 2023). Genetic and pharmacologic evidence across models (DR endothelium, Alu-driven RPE toxicity, ischemic/neovascular retinopathy) show that suppressing STING signaling reduces inflammation, senescence markers, angiogenic/proteolytic factors, leakage, and degeneration, indicating pathway validity and translational plausibility in the eye (Liu et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023; Ni et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2024).

- Direct RPE cell evidence for H-151. H-151 prevented VEGF overproduction in senescent ARPE-19 cells, directly supporting the candidate’s ability to suppress a central SASP/angiogenic mediator in an RPE-derived cell model (Hu et al., 2023). This aligns with the broader observation that STING upregulates VEGF in oxidative stress–induced RPE senescence via NF-κB/HIF-1α (Hu et al., 2023).

- Potent inhibition of NF-κB and cytokine outputs. In preclinical psoriasis systems, H-151 suppressed cGAMP-induced IRF3/NF-κB phosphorylation and reduced IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α, and IFN-β readouts, consistent with broad blockade of the principal proinflammatory axes downstream of STING (Pan et al., 2021). Given that NF-κB is a dominant inducer of SASP components in oxidant-stressed RPE, and IL-6/IL-8 and MMPs are canonical RPE SASP factors in AMD, H-151’s activity is well matched to reduce these pathological outputs (Terao et al., 2022; Blasiak, 2020; Ochoa Hernández et al., 2025).

- Target validation in retinal models via related inhibitors/genetics. STING inhibition by C-176/SN-011 reduces neovascularization in CNV/OIR; cryptotanshinone reduces Il6 and Mmp9 and vascular leakage through STING suppression in microglia; STING knockout provides protection against inflammatory retinal degeneration and endothelial senescence and barrier compromise, together providing multi-compartment evidence that STING blockade can ameliorate retinal inflammatory pathology (Ni et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023).

- Modality fit for intracellular target. Because cGAS and STING are intracellular, small molecules are preferred for retinal applications; the literature anticipates that blood–retina–penetrant small-molecule cGAS–STING inhibitors will be needed to translate this strategy to the clinic, supporting H-151’s modality selection (Hu et al., 2023).

Limitations and risks  
- Sparse ocular data for H-151. Beyond the ARPE-19 VEGF observation, there are no H-151 data for TEER, ZO-1 continuity, MMP modulation, or in vivo ocular efficacy in dry AMD models in the provided literature, so the central claims around barrier preservation remain predictions that require direct testing (Hu et al., 2023). By contrast, barrier-relevant evidence is stronger for alternative STING inhibitors (cryptotanshinone effects on Mmp9/leakage; C-176/SN-011 in angiogenesis models), indicating pathway relevance but not yet validating H-151 itself in retina (Qiu et al., 2025; Ni et al., 2024).

- Cell-type heterogeneity in DNA sensing. Some RPE models lack cGAS and may rely on other sensors (RIG-I, ZBP1), and the cGAS–STING axis can be variably engaged depending on the nature of the stressor (Alu vs mtDNA vs photo-oxidized A2E), potentially limiting the breadth of H-151’s efficacy across RPE senescence paradigms (Hu et al., 2023). Thorough profiling in human primary RPE and AMD-relevant triggers will be required.

- Pathway-output variability. H-151’s strongest effects in psoriasis were on NF-κB/IL-6, with partial or context-dependent impact on IRF3/IFN-β; while NF-κB is the prime SASP driver in RPE, specific disease contexts (e.g., dsDNA-driven type I IFN) may require robust IRF3 suppression to fully ameliorate pathology (Pan et al., 2021). The differential balance of NF-κB versus IRF3 outputs in RPE under various triggers should be experimentally mapped.

- Covalent inhibitor liabilities. Cysteine-targeting covalent inhibitors can carry risks of off-target reactivity and species-specific metabolic liabilities; development of covalent agents demands careful derisking of reactivity and ocular PK/PD, as underscored in general discussions of covalent inhibitor strategies and metabolism in drug discovery (Wang et al., 2022). Although H-151’s covalent target is genetically unique (Cys91 on STING), comprehensive safety and selectivity profiling—especially for intraocular delivery—is not available here.

- Immunological trade-offs. STING deficiency can increase susceptibility to tumorigenesis and reduce vaccine responses, and human STING haplotypes (e.g., HAQ) show variable responsiveness to cyclic dinucleotides, implying potential population variability in pathway dependence and therapeutic response (Hu et al., 2023). For ocular delivery, local exposure may mitigate systemic risks, but intraocular immunosuppression could theoretically predispose to infections; risk–benefit will need empirical assessment.

Evidence-based development plan (prioritized)  
- In vitro RPE assays. Use human primary RPE and ARPE-19 under AMD-relevant triggers (H₂O₂, Alu cDNA, mtDNA transfection) to quantify: STING pathway activation (p-TBK1, p-IRF3, p65), SASP cytokines (IL-6, IL-8), MMPs (MMP-9), and angiogenic factors (VEGF); barrier metrics by TEER and ZO-1 continuity; and senescence markers (P16, P21, SA-β-gal). Include STING agonist/antagonist controls (e.g., diABZI, C-176), and cGAS/STING knockdown to confirm on-target effects; the goal is to reproduce the VEGF suppression result and extend to barrier preservation and MMP control with H-151 (Hu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2022; Terao et al., 2022).

- Retinal explant and in vivo models. Move to sodium iodate or oxidant-driven RPE injury models and to Alu DNA–induced RPE degeneration to test whether H-151 reduces cGAS–STING activation, NF-κB signaling, cytokines/MMPs, and preserves RPE morphology; positive controls include JQ1 and C-176, which have shown efficacy in related contexts (Hu et al., 2023; Ni et al., 2024). The LIRD model shows cGAS–STING activation can be modulated upstream by visual-cycle inhibitors; testing H-151 in LIRD would further link pathway suppression to photoreceptor/RPE protection (Wang et al., 2022).

- Delivery and PK/PD. Evaluate intravitreal versus periocular dosing for ocular exposure, using formulation strategies with precedent (e.g., nanoparticle-loaded C-176) to optimize intraocular retention and cell uptake; measure intraocular H-151 levels and target engagement markers (p-STING, p-TBK1, p-IRF3) (Shao et al., 2024).

- Safety and immunology. Profile local tolerability and infection risk in the eye while monitoring systemic spillover; genotype stratification for STING haplotypes can be deferred to later phases but should be considered due to pathway variability (Hu et al., 2023).

Decision context  
On balance, H-151 is a mechanistically compelling, tractable small-molecule inhibitor that targets a validated inflammatory/senescence axis in retinal disease, with one direct RPE line of evidence (suppression of VEGF in senescent ARPE-19) and robust non-ocular and ocular-pathway support via related tools and genetic models (Hu et al., 2023; Ni et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023). The absence of H-151–specific data on RPE barrier metrics (TEER/ZO-1) and MMP modulation is the principal evidence gap relative to the stated hypothesis; however, the consistent linkage of cGAS–STING to IL-6/VEGF/MMP outputs and barrier failure across retinal compartments supports the plausibility that a palmitoylation-targeted STING antagonist like H-151 will deliver the predicted phenotype in senescent RPE (Hu et al., 2023; Terao et al., 2022; Qiu et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025).

Recommendation  
Advance H-151 into AMD-relevant in vitro RPE barrier and senescence assays with rigorous on-target controls and head-to-head comparison against alternative STING inhibitors (C-176/SN-011, cryptotanshinone) to establish class translatability and select the best ocular candidate by efficacy and safety; proceed to in vivo validation contingent on clear demonstration of TEER/ZO-1 preservation, SASP/MMP suppression, and favorable ocular PK/PD (Ni et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2024).

Strengths summary: strong mechanistic rationale in AMD, direct RPE evidence for VEGF suppression by H-151, broad anti–NF-κB/SASP activity of H-151 in inflammatory models, and multiple retinal models validating STING inhibition (Hu et al., 2023; Pan et al., 2021; Ni et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025). Weaknesses summary: no H-151 data yet on RPE barrier readouts or in vivo retinal protection; covalent-inhibitor liabilities and immunologic trade-offs require careful derisking; cell-type heterogeneity in DNA sensing mandates model selection rigor (Hu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022).

Taken together, H-151 is a high-priority mechanistic probe and a plausible therapeutic lead for dry AMD that merits focused preclinical validation in RPE barrier/senescence systems and in Alu/oxidative injury models of outer retinal degeneration, with parallel medicinal-chemistry and formulation work to address ocular delivery and covalent-inhibitor safety considerations (Hu et al., 2023; Shao et al., 2024).
