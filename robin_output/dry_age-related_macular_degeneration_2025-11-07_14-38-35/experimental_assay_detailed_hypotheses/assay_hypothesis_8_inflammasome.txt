Assay Overview  
This cell-based assay models one of the key innate-immune mechanisms thought to drive retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) dysfunction in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD): NLRP3 inflammasome activation by drusen constituents. Human RPE cells (e.g., ARPE-19 or iPSC-derived RPE) are exposed to physiologically relevant concentrations of cholesterol crystals or other drusen-associated danger-signals (oxidized lipids, complement factors). Activation of caspase-1 is monitored in real time by a fluorescent‐labeled inhibitor of caspase‐1 (FLICA), while secretion of mature IL-1β (and optionally IL-18) into culture supernatants is quantified by sandwich ELISA. This provides a rapid, quantitative readout of NLRP3 assembly/function and allows screening of small‐molecule or biologic inhibitors of inflammasome signaling.

Biomedical Evidence  
Genetic and histopathologic studies have repeatedly implicated chronic, low-grade inflammation and innate immunity in dry AMD. Drusen—extracellular deposits under the RPE—contain cholesterol crystals, oxidized lipoproteins, complement activation products, and Alu RNAs, all of which can serve as danger signals triggering the NLRP3 inflammasome. Animal and ex vivo human studies show that NLRP3 activation in RPE leads to caspase-1–dependent release of IL-1β/IL-18, RPE pyroptosis, and secondary photoreceptor death. Clinically, elevated IL-1β and other inflammasome‐related cytokines have been detected in the vitreous and choroid of dry AMD patients. Inhibition of NLRP3 has been protective in murine AMD models, supporting a central pathogenic role.

Previous Use  
Variants of this inflammasome assay have been used to identify NLRP3 inhibitors (e.g., MCC950, glyburide), to validate anti-inflammatory natural products (curcumin derivatives), and to elucidate the mechanistic link between Alu RNA accumulation and RPE pyroptosis. Key discoveries include demonstration that drusen components directly activate NLRP3 in human RPE and that inflammasome blockade rescues RPE survival in vitro. These findings provided proof of principle for targeting innate immunity in early‐stage dry AMD.

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths:  
• Directly measures a disease‐relevant, functional endpoint in RPE.  
• Amenable to medium‐throughput screening and dose–response analyses.  
• Uses well‐characterized readouts (IL-1β ELISA, caspase-1 FLICA) with high sensitivity and specificity.  

Weaknesses:  
• Two-dimensional RPE monocultures lack Bruch’s membrane and choroidal interactions.  
• Drusen in vivo are complex; single components may not fully recapitulate multifactorial activation.  
• Chronic, low-grade inflammasome activity in patients may be difficult to mimic in acute in vitro exposures.  

In sum, an NLRP3 inflammasome activation assay in RPE is a robust, biologically justified platform for discovering and profiling therapeutics aimed at halting or reversing early inflammatory events in dry AMD.
