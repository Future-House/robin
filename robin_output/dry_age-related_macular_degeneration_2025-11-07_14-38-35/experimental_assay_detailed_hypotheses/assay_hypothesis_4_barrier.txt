Assay Overview  
• Aspect of disease modeled: In dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD), retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) barrier dysfunction—driven by chronic oxidative stress—leads to subretinal debris accumulation, inflammation, and vision loss. Measuring transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) in RPE monolayers under oxidative challenge models the tight‐junction integrity loss seen in early dry AMD.  
• Measurements: TEER values (Ω·cm²) are recorded at baseline and at intervals up to 48 h after oxidative insult (e.g., H₂O₂ or paraquat). A standard voltohmmeter (EVOM2 or equivalent) with chopstick electrodes is used. Parallel wells are fixed and immunostained for tight‐junction proteins (ZO-1, occludin) and quantified by confocal microscopy to validate TEER changes.  
• Biological material: Human iPSC-derived RPE cells differentiated on Transwell filters to form polarized monolayers with established high baseline TEER (>200 Ω·cm²). Oxidative stress is induced in the apical chamber; test compounds are added 1 h before or concurrent with stressor.

Biomedical Evidence  
• Central role of RPE barrier breakdown: In dry AMD, cumulative oxidative damage to RPE tight junctions compromises the outer blood–retina barrier, permitting subretinal fluid accumulation, drusen formation, and local complement activation. Clinical imaging studies (OCT, fundus autofluorescence) correlate early RPE dysfunction—even before frank geographic atrophy—with changes in outer retinal barrier properties.  
• Oxidative stress as a driver: Genetic risk alleles (e.g., in CFH, ARMS2) and environmental factors (smoking, light exposure) both converge on heightened reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation in RPE. Postmortem AMD eyes exhibit reduced expression of key tight‐junction proteins and markers of lipid peroxidation in RPE, linking oxidative injury to barrier compromise.  
• Functional readout relevance: TEER directly measures paracellular permeability, a functional endpoint that ties molecular events (e.g., ZO-1 phosphorylation, actin cytoskeleton remodeling) to barrier competence. Drugs that preserve TEER under oxidative stress are likely to maintain RPE barrier in vivo and slow progression of geographic atrophy.

Previous Use  
• Nozaki et al., JCI 2006: Demonstrated that subretinal injection of complement‐activating drusen components induced TEER loss in primary RPE, rescued by complement inhibitors.  
• Subsequent screening: Several groups have used TEER in ARPE-19 or primary RPE to identify antioxidants (e.g., N-acetylcysteine) and small‐molecule kinase inhibitors that prevent H₂O₂‐induced drop in resistance.  
• Mechanistic insights: TEER decline has been mapped to disruption of ZO-1/occludin complexes, Rho/ROCK‐mediated actomyosin contraction, and mitochondrial dysfunction. This has guided testing of modulators of the RhoA pathway and mitochondrial protectants.  
• Translation toward 3D models: TEER assays informed the engineering of RPE‐Bruch’s membrane constructs, where barrier integrity correlated with subretinal deposit formation in vitro.

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
1. Pathophysiological relevance: Directly models a key early event in dry AMD—RPE tight-junction disruption under oxidative stress.  
2. Quantitative, reproducible: TEER is a sensitive, non‐destructive readout, enabling kinetic studies and dose–response curves in 96-well Transwells.  
3. Human iPSC-RPE: Patient‐derived lines carrying high‐risk genotypes (CFH, ARMS2) permit evaluation of gene–environment interactions.  
4. High throughput potential: Automated TEER systems and parallel immunostaining allow moderate screening throughput.

Weaknesses  
1. Limited cellular complexity: Does not capture contributions of choroidal endothelial cells, microglia, or complement‐producing immune cells in dry AMD.  
2. Simplified stress model: Acute H₂O₂ insult may not fully recapitulate chronic, low‐grade oxidative damage and lipid peroxidation in aging RPE.  
3. Variability in iPSC lines: Differentiation efficiency and baseline TEER can vary between clones, requiring rigorous QC and multiple lines for reproducibility.  
4. Endpoint specificity: TEER reduction reflects generic barrier loss and cannot distinguish between apoptosis, necrosis, or junctional remodeling without orthogonal assays (e.g., caspase activity, live-dead staining).

Conclusion  
TEER measurement in oxidative‐stressed iPSC-RPE monolayers is a well‐validated, disease-relevant assay for early‐stage screening of barrier‐protective therapeutics in dry AMD. Its strengths in functional readout and human relevance make it a valuable component of an in vitro phenotypic assay cascade, but it should be complemented by co-culture models, chronic stress paradigms, and downstream markers of inflammation and phagocytosis to fully capture the multifactorial pathogenesis of dry AMD.
