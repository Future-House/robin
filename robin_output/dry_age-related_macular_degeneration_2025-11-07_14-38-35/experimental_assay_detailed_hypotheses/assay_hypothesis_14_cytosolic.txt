Assay Overview  
• Rationale and disease aspect modeled  
  – Dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is driven in part by chronic oxidative stress in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), leading to mitochondrial damage, release of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) into the cytosol, and subsequent activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome. Cytosolic mtDNA acts as a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) that triggers IL-1β/IL-18 secretion and low-grade inflammasome-mediated inflammation, contributing to RPE dysfunction and progression of geographic atrophy.  
• Measurements  
  – After induction of oxidative stress (e.g. H₂O₂, blue-light exposure, paraquat) in RPE cells, cytosolic and mitochondrial fractions are separated by differential centrifugation.  
  – Quantitative PCR (qPCR) is performed on the cytosolic fraction to quantify levels of mtDNA (commonly using primers for ND1, ND4, or cytochrome b) normalized to a nuclear DNA marker.  
  – Parallel readouts may include: lactate dehydrogenase release (cell viability), caspase-1 activation assays (e.g. FLICA), IL-1β/IL-18 quantification by ELISA, and immunoblotting for cytosolic mitochondrial proteins (TOM20) to confirm fraction purity.  
• Cells/biological material  
  – Human RPE cell lines (ARPE-19), primary human fetal or donor RPE cultures, or induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived RPE monolayers. Oxidative stress conditions are titrated to mimic sublethal injury seen in early AMD.  

Biomedical Evidence  
• Centrality of mtDNA-driven inflammasome activation in dry AMD:  
  – Histopathology of AMD eyes shows elevated markers of inflammasome activation (NLRP3, IL-18) in RPE and drusen. (Tarallo et al., Nat Commun 2012; Doyle et al., J Clin Invest 2012)  
  – Oxidative damage to RPE mitochondria is a well-established early event in AMD pathogenesis (Karunadharma et al., Mol Vis 2010; Lin et al., Exp Eye Res 2011). Mitochondrial DNA damage correlates with disease severity.  
  – West et al. (Nat Immunol 2015) demonstrated that oxidized mtDNA released into the cytosol is a potent NLRP3 activator in immune cells; analogous mechanisms operate in RPE (Tseng et al., J Pathol 2018).  
• Clinical/biochemical correlations:  
  – Genetic studies link polymorphisms in complement and inflammasome pathway genes (CFH, NLRP3) with increased dry AMD risk (Fritsche et al., Nat Genet 2013).  
  – Elevated IL-18 and IL-1β levels are detected in ocular fluids of AMD patients (Doyle et al., J Clin Invest 2012).  
  – Therapies that stabilize mitochondrial function or inhibit inflammasome signaling (e.g., MCC950) ameliorate RPE degeneration in preclinical AMD models (Tseng et al., JCI Insight 2017).  

Previous Use  
• In vitro discovery of inflammasome modulators:  
  – The cytosolic mtDNA release assay was first characterized in macrophages and dendritic cells to screen small molecules that prevent NLRP3 activation (West et al., Nat Immunol 2015).  
  – In RPE research, Tarallo et al. (Nat Commun 2012) modeled Alu RNA–induced NLRP3 activation; similar fractionation/qPCR approaches were used to infer DAMP release.  
  – More recently, iPSC-RPE have been treated with antioxidants (e.g. MitoQ) or mitophagy enhancers to show reduced mtDNA leakage and downstream cytokine release (Ryu et al., Redox Biol 2018).  
• Key scientific discoveries:  
  – Established link between oxidative mtDNA damage and inflammasome-mediated RPE death.  
  – Identification of small-molecule NLRP3 inhibitors that reduce cytosolic mtDNA and IL-1β in RPE under stress.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  1. Mechanistic relevance: directly measures a pivotal DAMP event (mtDNA release) upstream of inflammasome activation and cytokine release implicated in dry AMD.  
  2. Quantitative and tunable: qPCR is sensitive and allows dose–response testing of candidate compounds that stabilize mitochondria or block mtDNA release.  
  3. Translatable biomarkers: cytosolic mtDNA in patient-derived RPE or in vivo vitreous samples could be correlated to in vitro findings.  

Weaknesses  
  1. Fractionation artifacts: cytosolic leakage during cell lysis can confound true mtDNA release measurements; requires rigorous controls (e.g., LDH assays, fraction-specific markers).  
  2. Oversimplified microenvironment: lacks contributions from choroidal macrophages, complement proteins, and multi-cell interactions present in the subretinal space.  
  3. Throughput and cost: subcellular fractionation and qPCR limit scalability compared to high-content imaging or reporter assays.  
  4. Stress model limitations: acute oxidative insults may not fully recapitulate chronic, low-grade mitochondrial dysfunction seen in human AMD.  

Recommendation  
This assay is highly valuable for early-stage screening of mitochondrial-protective or inflammasome-modulating compounds in RPE. To maximize predictive power for dry AMD, it should be complemented with co-culture systems (RPE–microglia), long-term stress models, and downstream functional endpoints (phagocytosis, barrier integrity, complement activation).
