Assay Overview  
The proposed ferroptosis rescue assay models iron‐ and lipid peroxidation–driven retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) death, an increasingly recognized contributor to dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD). Key assay elements:  
• Cell model: Human RPE cell line (e.g. ARPE-19) or primary human fetal RPE cultured to confluence and polarization on Transwell inserts.  
• Induction of ferroptosis: Treatment with an inhibitor of system Xc– (e.g. erastin) or a GPX4 inhibitor (e.g. RSL3), combined with low-to-moderate iron loading (ferric ammonium citrate or holotransferrin) to amplify lipid peroxidation.  
• Rescue conditions: Co-treatment with known ferroptosis inhibitors (ferrostatin-1, liproxstatin-1) or candidate antioxidants.  
• Readouts:  
  – Cell viability (e.g. MTT/WST-1, ATP assays) to quantify overall protection.  
  – Lipid ROS (C11-BODIPY［581/591］ fluorescence) measured by flow cytometry or high-content imaging.  
  – Labile iron pool (calcein-AM quenching or FerroOrange imaging).  
  – Biomarkers of lipid peroxidation (4-hydroxynonenal adduct ELISA, malondialdehyde assay) and GPX4 protein levels by Western blot or immunofluorescence.  
This assay thus recapitulates RPE vulnerability to iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation and provides quantitative endpoints for both injury and rescue.

Biomedical Evidence  
A growing body of histopathology, biochemical and clinical literature supports iron-driven oxidative damage and ferroptosis‐like mechanisms in dry AMD:  
• Post-mortem AMD eyes show elevated iron deposition in RPE and Bruch’s membrane (Hahn et al., Exp Eye Res 2003; Hahn et al., Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 2003).  
• Lipid peroxidation products (4-HNE, MDA) are enriched in drusen and RPE cells of AMD patients (Beatty et al., Prog Retin Eye Res 2000; Hollyfield et al., PNAS 2008).  
• Genetic and animal studies link impaired antioxidant defenses (e.g. GPX4, ferritin dysregulation) to RPE degeneration (Ueta et al., Free Radic Biol Med 2012; Hahn et al., PNAS 2015).  
• In vitro, RPE cells exposed to iron and pro‐oxidants undergo cell death with morphological and biochemical hallmarks of ferroptosis (Doonan et al., Cell Death Dis 2014; Liu et al., Redox Biol 2019).  
Clinically, low plasma levels of GPX4 substrates and high systemic iron levels correlate with faster geographic atrophy progression (Lange et al., JAMA Ophthalmol 2018), implicating ferroptotic processes in AMD pathogenesis.

Previous Use  
• Doonan et al. (2014) first applied an iron + erastin RPE assay to demonstrate that ferrostatin-1 prevents RPE death, highlighting ferroptosis as a targetable process in AMD models.  
• Stockwell’s laboratory adapted similar rescue assays in neuronal and hepatic cell types to screen for novel ferroptosis inhibitors, yielding liproxstatin-1 and other tool compounds later shown effective in RPE contexts (Dixon et al., Nat Chem Biol 2014; Conrad et al., Nat Chem Biol 2016).  
• High-content adaptations have been used to rank potency of small molecules and natural extracts for lipid peroxidation suppression in ARPE-19 (Zhou et al., Redox Biol 2020).  
Key discoveries include the central role of GPX4 and system Xc– in RPE survival under oxidative stress and identification of structurally diverse ferroptosis inhibitors that rescue RPE in vitro and in mouse geographic atrophy models.

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths:  
• Directly models a mechanistically validated node (iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation) in dry AMD pathogenesis.  
• Multiplexed, quantitative readouts enable high-throughput screening and structure–activity relationship studies.  
• Assay rescue by known ferroptosis inhibitors offers positive controls and validates on-target effects.  

Weaknesses:  
• Ferroptosis may represent only one pathway of RPE degeneration; compounds effective here may not address complement‐mediated inflammation or drusen clearance.  
• ARPE-19 cells can differ from in vivo RPE in iron handling and antioxidant capacity; primary or iPSC‐derived RPE may be required for translational fidelity.  
• High iron/erastin concentrations risk off-target oxidative damage not strictly representative of the slow chronic stress in AMD.  

Conclusion: With optimization (physiologic iron loads, polarized primary RPE), the ferroptosis rescue assay is a robust, target-validated platform for early screening of dry AMD therapeutics aimed at preserving RPE integrity.
