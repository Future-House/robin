Assay Overview  
The proposed assay uses Seahorse XF technology to perform real‐time bioenergetic profiling of retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells in a 96-well format. Primary human RPE or iPSC-derived RPE are seeded and, after stabilization, sequential injections of the following modulators are applied:  
 · Oligomycin to inhibit ATP synthase and reveal proton leak  
 · FCCP (carbonyl cyanide-4-(trifluoromethoxy)phenylhydrazone) to uncover maximal respiratory capacity  
 · Rotenone/antimycin A to block mitochondrial respiration entirely  
Basal and stimulated oxygen consumption rate (OCR) parameters (basal respiration, ATP‐linked respiration, maximal respiration, spare respiratory capacity) and extracellular acidification rate (ECAR; glycolytic flux) are recorded in real time. This directly models the balance between mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis in RPE—two pathways thought to be perturbed in dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD).  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Mitochondrial Dysfunction in AMD Pathogenesis  
   • Multiple histopathological studies of human donor eyes with dry AMD report structural mitochondrial abnormalities in RPE (swollen cristae, reduced number) and decreased expression of electron transport chain components (IOVS 2006; Kaarniranta et al., 2019).  
   • RPE become progressively less able to meet their high energy demands, leading to impaired phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments, lipofuscin accumulation, and oxidative stress—all hallmarks of early dry AMD.  
2. Clinical Correlations  
   • Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) damage and specific mtDNA haplogroups have been statistically linked with AMD risk (Lin et al., 2011).  
   • Systemic metabolic shifts (reduced NAD+, altered glycolytic intermediates) have been detected in plasma of AMD patients versus age-matched controls (Chiu et al., 2017).  
3. Therapeutic Rationale  
   • Agents that bolster mitochondrial function or shift energy metabolism (e.g., NAD+ precursors, mitochondrial biogenesis activators) are under preclinical investigation for dry AMD. Measuring OCR/ECAR in RPE directly informs on-target engagement of these modalities.  

Previous Use  
• Feher et al. (IOVS 2006) originally applied Seahorse respirometry to primary human RPE to catalog basal OCR/ECAR and responses to oxidative insults (e.g., A2E).  
• Subsequent studies have used XF assays to demonstrate:  
   – Protection by nicotinamide and nicotinamide mononucleotide against A2E-induced OCR decline (Brown et al., Exp Eye Res 2019).  
   – Efficacy of mitochondria‐targeted antioxidants (MitoQ, SS-31) in preserving spare respiratory capacity in iPSC-RPE exposed to blue light (Grant et al., Free Radic Biol Med 2020).  
   – Metabolic switching induced by PGC-1α overexpression in RPE models of oxidative stress (Zhang et al., Cell Death Dis 2018).  
These studies support the assay’s utility in detecting modulatory effects of metabolic therapeutics.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
 • Disease relevance: Directly measures key metabolic deficits documented in AMD pathogenesis.  
 • Quantitative and high-throughput: 96-well format allows screening of multiple compounds/doses.  
 • Dynamic multiparametric readouts: OCR and ECAR give insight into shifts between OXPHOS and glycolysis, proton leak, and reserve capacity.  
 • Proven track record: Prior RPE studies have yielded reproducible metrics that correlate with functional endpoints (e.g., phagocytosis, ROS levels).  

Weaknesses  
 • Simplified model: Lacks photoreceptor–RPE crosstalk, immune/inflammatory components, drusen formation, and choriocapillaris influences.  
 • Variability: iPSC-RPE lines display line-to-line metabolic variability; primary RPE availability and donor age can confound results.  
 • Sensitivity to culture conditions: Confluent versus subconfluent cells, substrate composition, and assay buffer pH can all impact OCR/ECAR measurements.  
 • Off-target effects: Some test compounds may alter extracellular acidification through non-glycolytic pathways (e.g., CO2 hydration), requiring orthogonal validation.  

Conclusion  
Overall, Seahorse XF bioenergetic profiling of primary or iPSC-derived RPE provides a robust, mechanistically relevant in vitro assay for screening and mechanistic evaluation of metabolic therapeutics targeting dry AMD. Its high sensitivity to mitochondrial and glycolytic function makes it well suited for early‐stage drug discovery, although hits will require follow-up in more complex co-culture or organoid systems to fully capture the multifactorial biology of the disease.
