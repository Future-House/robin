Query: Review protocols for generating human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived RPE models of dry AMD, emphasizing scalable phagocytosis assays of photoreceptor outer segments, transepithelial resistance measurements, and high-throughput adaptation for screening autophagy modulators or complement inhibitors.

Below are 15 distinct, experimentally tractable hypotheses or therapeutic‐strategy concepts—each formulated for testing in human iPSC–derived RPE monolayers and readily adaptable to scalable phagocytosis assays, TEER measurements, and high-throughput screens of autophagy modulators or complement inhibitors. For each idea we note critical protocol elements (differentiation, POS assay, TEER readout, HT format, and candidate intervention).

1. CFH Risk Allele–Mediated Phagocytic Deficit  
   • Hypothesis: The AMD-associated CFH Y402H variant compromises RPE clearance of POS.  
   • Protocol notes: Differentiate iPSCs from Y402H homozygotes vs. isogenic controls via feeder-free, small-molecule induction (CHIR99021, SB431542). Mature RPE for 6–8 weeks until >90% pigmented.  
   • Assays: 96-well automated POS uptake using pHrodo-labeled bovine POS; TEER measured by multi-well impedance system; HT screening of compstatin analogs (e.g., APL-2) at 10–100 nM to rescue phagocytosis.

2. C3 Overactivation and Lysosomal Stress  
   • Hypothesis: Excess C3b on RPE surface induces lysosomal dysfunction and impaired digestion of POS.  
   • Protocol notes: Expose mature iPSC-RPE to human serum with elevated C3b.  
   • Assays: High-content imaging of DQ-BSA degradation as lysosomal readout; POS phagocytosis in 384-well plates; TEER + impedance kinetics; screen small-molecule C3 inhibitors (e.g. Cp40 derivatives).

3. Autophagy Decline via mTOR Hyperactivation  
   • Hypothesis: Chronic mTORC1 activation in RPE reduces autophagic flux, leading to lipofuscin accumulation and POS buildup.  
   • Protocol notes: Transduce RPE with lentiviral Rheb overexpression; differentiate in Transwell plates.  
   • Assays: LC3-GFP reporter flux in 96-well format; automated POS uptake; TEER; HT titration of rapamycin analogs (everolimus) and novel ULK1 activators.

4. Inflammasome–Mediated Barrier Breakdown  
   • Hypothesis: NLRP3 inflammasome activation disrupts RPE tight junctions and TEER.  
   • Protocol notes: Prime mature RPE with IL-1β + ATP; culture on 96-well Transwells.  
   • Assays: Caspase-1 activity (FLICA) + ZO-1 immunostaining; TEER kinetics; POS phagocytosis; screen NLRP3 inhibitors (MCC950 analogs).

5. Mitochondrial DNA Release Triggers Complement  
   • Hypothesis: Mitochondrial damage releases mtDNA, binding C1q and initiating classical complement on RPE apical surface.  
   • Protocol notes: Treat RPE with low-dose rotenone to induce mtDNA stress.  
   • Assays: qPCR of extracellular mtDNA; POS uptake; TEER; high-throughput addition of anti-C1q antibodies or small peptide inhibitors.

6. Cholesterol Accumulation Impairs Autophagic Flux  
   • Hypothesis: Excess intracellular cholesterol in RPE perturbs lysosomal acidification and autophagy.  
   • Protocol notes: Pulse RPE with LDL + ACAT inhibitor to load cholesterol; culture on 96-well plates.  
   • Assays: LysoTracker intensity; LC3 puncta quantification; POS phagocytosis; TEER; HT screening of LXR agonists or Niemann–Pick modulators.

7. Drusen Lipid Extracts Promote Complement Deposition  
   • Hypothesis: Human drusen lipid extracts activate alternative complement on RPE, impairing POS clearance.  
   • Protocol notes: Isolate drusen from donor eyes; treat RPE monolayers.  
   • Assays: C3a ELISA in supernatant; phagocytosis (pHrodo-POS); TEER; automated co-addition of factor D inhibitors.

8. Oxidative Stress–Induced Autophagy Block  
   • Hypothesis: Chronic low-level H2O2 exposure inhibits autophagosome–lysosome fusion in RPE.  
   • Protocol notes: Expose RPE to sublethal H2O2 pulses over 1 week.  
   • Assays: mCherry-GFP-LC3 tandem reporter; phagocytic index; TEER; 384-well screening of antioxidants plus autophagy enhancers (trehalose).

9. Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype (SASP) and TEER Loss  
   • Hypothesis: Senescent RPE secrete IL-6/IL-8, disrupting tight junctions and phagocytosis.  
   • Protocol notes: Induce senescence via repeated low-dose irradiation; verify p16INK4a upregulation.  
   • Assays: Cytokine multiplex in supernatant; ZO-1 staining + TEER; POS assay; screen JAK/STAT inhibitors.

10. ABCA4 Knockdown and POS Lipid Handling Defects  
   • Hypothesis: Reduced ABCA4 in RPE impedes retinoid export after POS ingestion, causing RPE stress.  
   • Protocol notes: siRNA knockdown of ABCA4 in mature iPSC-RPE; culture in high-throughput 96-well.  
   • Assays: All-trans-retinal accumulation by HPLC; phagocytosis; TEER; screen retinoid cycle modulators.

11. HTRA1 Overexpression Amplifies Complement Response  
   • Hypothesis: AMD-linked HTRA1 upregulation destabilizes Bruch’s membrane analog, increasing C5b-9 deposition on RPE.  
   • Protocol notes: Overexpress HTRA1 via lentivirus; co-culture with synthetic Bruch’s membrane ECM.  
   • Assays: Membrane permeability + TEER; complement MAC staining; phagocytosis; screen anti-C5 antibodies.

12. Impaired Glycolysis Alters POS Clearance  
   • Hypothesis: Metabolic shift away from glycolysis in aging RPE reduces ATP for phagosome processing.  
   • Protocol notes: Culture RPE in galactose instead of glucose to force oxidative metabolism.  
   • Assays: Seahorse glycolysis stress test; phagocytic throughput in 96-well; TEER; screen AMPK activators.

13. APOE Isoform–Dependent Lipid Handling  
   • Hypothesis: APOE4 expression in RPE leads to defective POS lipid efflux and complement activation.  
   • Protocol notes: Gene-edit iPSCs to express APOE3 vs. APOE4; differentiate to RPE.  
   • Assays: Lipid droplet quantification; C3 cleavage; phagocytosis; TEER; screening LXR agonists.

14. Impaired Autophagy–Lysosome Fusion via V-ATPase Dysfunction  
   • Hypothesis: AMD-associated V-ATPase subunit polymorphisms slow lysosomal acidification, blocking POS degradation.  
   • Protocol notes: CRISPR introduce V0a1 SNPs; mature RPE on Transwell.  
   • Assays: Lysosomal pH (LysoSensor ratiometric); POS assay; TEER; HT screen of V-ATPase activators or proton pump modulators.

15. Drusen-Like Hydroxyapatite Crystals Induce Complement  
   • Hypothesis: Synthetic hydroxyapatite nanoparticles mimicking drusen cores hyperactivate alternative complement on RPE.  
   • Protocol notes: Seed RPE on 384-well plates; add graded nano-crystals.  
   • Assays: C3b/iC3b deposition by high-content immunofluorescence; phagocytosis; TEER; screen factor B inhibitors.

Common Protocol Highlights:  
– iPSC-RPE Differentiation: feeder-free monolayer induction using dual SMAD inhibition, CHIR99021, Activin A; maturation 4–8 weeks; confirm RPE markers (RPE65, BEST1).  
– Scalable Phagocytosis: isolate bovine or porcine POS, label with pHrodo or fluorescent retinoid analogs, incubate 4 h, automated wash/imaging in 96–384-well format.  
– TEER & Impedance: use multi-well TEER plates (e.g., Ag/AgCl electrode arrays) or xCELLigence impedance platform for real-time barrier monitoring.  
– High-Throughput Adaptation: robotic liquid handling for multi-step assays, automated confocal or spinning-disk imaging, image-analysis pipelines for puncta/counts, dose–response plate layouts for library screening of autophagy enhancers or complement pathway inhibitors.
