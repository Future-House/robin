Query: Investigate lipoprotein accumulation and drusen formation mechanisms within RPE cells in dry AMD, examining roles of apolipoproteins, lipid peroxidation, and cholesterol efflux pathways, while identifying high-content imaging and lipidomics approaches suitable for in vitro screening of candidate therapeutics.

Below is a concise, actionable plan for interrogating lipoprotein accumulation and drusen‐like deposit formation in human RPE cells, together with five commercially-available, mechanistically defined small molecules or peptides (with catalog numbers) that you can screen in a high-content in vitro assay combined with targeted lipidomics.

1.  Disease Model and Key Readouts  
   a.  Cell system  
       •  Human iPSC-derived RPE monolayers, or primary fetal RPE, cultured on transwells or ECM-coated plates to form a tight barrier.  
       •  Challenge with human oxidized LDL (oxLDL, e.g. Kalen Biomedical cat. #2094) or lipid-enriched serum to induce intracellular neutral lipid and cholesterol ester accumulation and apolipoprotein secretion.  
   b.  High-content imaging (HCI) endpoints  
       •  Neutral lipid droplets: BODIPY™ 493/503 (Thermo FIsher D3922)  
       •  Free cholesterol: filipin III (Sigma F9765)  
       •  ApoE/ApoB immunofluorescence (anti-ApoE clone EP1374Y, Abcam ab183597; anti-ApoB clone 2D8, Novus NBP2-32192)  
       •  Lysosomal function/colocalization: LysoTracker™ Deep Red (Thermo L12492)  
       •  Image analysis: PerkinElmer Opera Phenix or Thermo CellInsight arrays; segmentation for RPE cell boundaries, droplet count/area, intensity per cell.  
   c.  Targeted lipidomics  
       •  Extraction: methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE) extraction (Folch variant) with internal standards from Avanti Polar Lipids (e.g. SPLASH LIPIDOMIX®).  
       •  Instrumentation: HILIC-LC–MS/MS or shotgun lipidomics on QTOF or triple-quad (SCIEX Lipidyzer).  
       •  Readouts: cholesteryl esters (CE), triglycerides (TG), oxidized phospholipids (OxPL), lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC), malondialdehyde (MDA) adducts.  

2.  Pathways/Targets of Interest  
   –  ABCA1/ABCG1-mediated cholesterol efflux (LXR activation)  
   –  ACAT (SOAT1)-catalyzed cholesterol esterification  
   –  Lipid peroxidation/ferroptosis  
   –  Apolipoprotein–lipid assembly (ApoA1, ApoE)  
   –  Carbonyl trapping on peroxidized lipids  

3.  Five Novel, Commercially Available Candidates for Dry AMD Screening  
   Each has: a) clear mechanism; b) preclinical or related-disease rationale; c) minimal off-target pleiotropy; d) catalog number for rapid ordering.  

   1)  GW3965 (LXRα/β agonist)  
       •  Mechanism: selective synthetic agonist of liver-X receptors; upregulates ABCA1, ABCG1, ApoE, promotes cholesterol efflux from RPE.  
       •  Preclinical rationale: atherosclerosis models show enhanced reverse cholesterol transport; LXR activation reduced lipid load in macrophages and improved barrier function in epithelial cells.  
       •  Specificity: nuclear receptor ligand with well-defined target.  
       •  Novelty for AMD: not yet tested in RPE/drusen context.  
       •  Catalog: Cayman Chemical #70420, purity ≥98%.  

   2)  Avasimibe (ACAT/SOAT1 inhibitor)  
       •  Mechanism: non-competitive inhibitor of acyl-CoA:cholesterol acyltransferase; blocks conversion of free cholesterol to cholesterol esters, reducing CE droplet formation.  
       •  Preclinical rationale: reduces foam cell formation in vascular models; in vitro studies in adipocytes show decreased CE accumulation and enhanced efflux.  
       •  Specificity: potent (IC50 ~2 µM for SOAT1), minimal activity on SOAT2 at screening doses.  
       •  Catalog: Cayman Chemical #10008549, purity ≥95%.  

   3)  Ferrostatin-1 (lipid peroxidation/ferroptosis inhibitor)  
       •  Mechanism: radical-trapping antioxidant; blocks phospholipid hydroperoxide accumulation and ferroptotic cell death.  
       •  Preclinical rationale: protects neuronal and epithelial cells from oxidative injury; in RPE-like ARPE-19 cells, prevents H₂O₂-induced lipid peroxidation.  
       •  Specificity: selective for ferroptosis; does not inhibit apoptosis or necroptosis.  
       •  Catalog: Sigma-Aldrich SML0583, ≥98% purity.  

   4)  2-Hydroxybenzylamine (2-HOBA)  
       •  Mechanism: carbonyl scavenger; traps reactive dicarbonyls (e.g. malondialdehyde, 4-hydroxynonenal), preventing crosslinking of apolipoproteins in drusen.  
       •  Preclinical rationale: reduces protein carbonylation and atherosclerotic lesion formation in mice; preserves barrier integrity in oxidant-stressed epithelial monolayers.  
       •  Specificity: reacts preferentially with lipid-derived dicarbonyls; minimal reactivity with other aldehydes.  
       •  Catalog: Cayman Chemical #13382, ≥95%.  

   5)  ApoA-I mimetic peptide 4F  
       •  Mechanism: 18-amino-acid amphipathic helix; mimics ApoA-I function to promote ABCA1-dependent cholesterol efflux, has anti-inflammatory properties.  
       •  Preclinical rationale: reduces lipid accumulation and inflammation in atherosclerotic plaques and in brain models of amyloid pathology.  
       •  Specificity: designed for ABCA1 interaction; no known nuclear receptor activation.  
       •  Catalog: AnaSpec AS-65507 (N-terminal acetylated, C-terminal amidated, ≥95% HPLC purity).  

4.  Proposed In Vitro Screening Workflow  
   1.  Pre-treat RPE monolayers 24 h before lipid challenge with dose range (0.1–10 µM small molecules; 1–20 µg/mL peptide).  
   2.  Co-incubate with oxLDL or lipid mix for 48–72 h.  
   3.  Fix cells and stain for BODIPY, filipin, ApoE (IF). Acquire ≥9 fields/well on Opera Phenix (20× objective).  
   4.  Automated segmentation: measure per-cell droplet count, total droplet area, per-cell cholesterol intensity, ApoE puncta.  
   5.  In parallel wells, extract lipids for LC–MS/MS: quantify CE/TG ratios, OxPL species, MDA-lysine adducts.  
   6.  Calculate percent rescue vs. vehicle: reduction in neutral lipid area, free cholesterol, oxidative adducts; increase in ApoE secretion into media (by ELISA).  

5.  Validation and Next Steps  
   –  Hit criteria: ≥50% reduction in neutral lipid droplet area + ≥30% normalization of CE/TG ratio + reduction of OxPL by LC–MS.  
   –  Secondary assays: transepithelial resistance (TEER), viability (ATP assay), cytokine secretion (IL-6, IL-8).  
   –  In vivo bridge: test top hits in complement-sufficient dry AMD mouse models (e.g. ApoE−/− or Cfh−/− aged mice) for drusen-like lesion reduction.  

These five agents—GW3965, avasimibe, ferrostatin-1, 2-HOBA and ApoA-I mimetic 4F—together cover complementary mechanisms (cholesterol efflux, CE suppression, lipid peroxidation blockade, carbonyl scavenging and apolipoprotein-mediated clearance). All are commercially available with catalog numbers for rapid deployment. The combined high-content imaging plus targeted lipidomics approach will allow quantitative, mechanism-specific triage of candidates for dry AMD drug development.
