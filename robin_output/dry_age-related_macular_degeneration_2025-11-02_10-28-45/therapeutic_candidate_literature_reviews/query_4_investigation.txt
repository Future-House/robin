Query: Investigation of autophagy pathway enhancers, including mTORC1 inhibitors, AMPK activators, or TFEB agonists, that increase phagolysosomal maturation and accelerate phagocytosis kinetics of photoreceptor outer segments in RPE cells, with mechanistic confirmation, in vivo dry AMD model validation, and known ocular PK/safety data

Evidence in RPE culture supports mTORC1 inhibition and TFEB activation as autophagy/lysosome enhancers that lower POS-derived autofluorescent granules (AFGs, lipofuscin-like) via lysosomal mechanisms. In hfRPE and ARPE‑19, rapamycin reduced POS-dependent AF by ~30% when dosed after AFGs appeared and shifted signaling to TFEB activation (↓pS6, ↓pTFEB/TFEB) with GADD34 induction and increased LAMP1; the effect required functional lysosomal hydrolases (abrogated by Lalistat‑2, leupeptin/pepstatin, or CTSD knockout), and other mTOR inhibitors (INK‑128, compound H) similarly reduced AF, indicating pathway specificity (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 10-13, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 29-36, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 36-39). Complementary genetic activation of TFEB (AAV TFEB-CA S142A/S211A) decreased AFG load, consistent with TFEB-driven CLEAR network induction; aged/AMD RPE show increased TFEB phosphorylation and reduced nuclear TFEB, and constitutively active TFEB increased CLEAR genes and reduced POS‑dependent granules, mechanistically linking TFEB to improved phagolysosomal clearance (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 10-13, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7). POS feeding elicits transient mTOR activation followed by inhibition; endogenous TFEB remains largely phosphorylated, suggesting a bottleneck relieved by rapamycin/TFEB activation (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 6-10).

Direct acceleration of phagolysosomal maturation kinetics (e.g., phagosome–lysosome fusion timing) is not demonstrated; readouts are reduced AFG burden and lysosome-dependent clearance (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 29-36). AMPK activators show RPE-protective autophagy effects but no POS‑specific kinetic enhancement here; AMPKα2 can even mediate inhibition of POS phagocytosis under oxidative stress (si2023theroleof pages 10-12, si2023theroleof pages 17-18).

In vivo dry AMD validation is not shown; NaIO3 injury data with rapamycin are limited and not POS‑clearance specific (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 13-18, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6). Ocular PK/safety are not characterized here; one review notes rapamycin’s favorable ocular PK yet limited clinical efficacy and adverse events, and proposes rapalogs/nanoparticles, while a registry query found no AMD trials explicitly linking these mechanisms to ocular PK/safety endpoints; constitutive TFEB activation raises safety concerns (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 7-9, Clinical Trial Search: 9187a8299d30, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 13-18).

In vitro RPE data mechanistically support mTORC1 inhibition and TFEB activation to boost lysosomal capacity and reduce POS-derived lipofuscin-like material. However, explicit acceleration of phagolysosomal maturation kinetics, in vivo dry AMD efficacy, and ocular PK/safety data are not established in the provided sources. Clinical translation will require kinetic assays in RPE, dry AMD model validation, and rigorous ocular PK/tolerability studies (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 10-13, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7, Clinical Trial Search: 9187a8299d30).
