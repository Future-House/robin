Query: Query for small molecules targeting bridging ligands like MFG-E8, Gas6, or Protein S that enhance recognition and engulfment of photoreceptor outer segments by RPE cells in kinetic assays, including studies confirming target engagement, functional enhancements in AMD RPE models, and ADME/safety profiling

The current literature described in the provided excerpts establishes that bridging ligands, including MFG-E8, Gas6, and Protein S, play a critical role in mediating MerTK‐ and integrin αvβ5–dependent phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments by RPE cells (albert2015triamcinoloneregulatedapoptophagocytic pages 12-12). Biological studies using agents such as Gas6 and triamcinolone have demonstrated that enhancing these pathways increases phagocytic clearance in cell‐based assays (albert2015triamcinoloneregulatedapoptophagocytic pages 10-11, albert2015triamcinoloneregulatedapoptophagocytic pages 1-2, albert2015triamcinoloneregulatedapoptophagocytic pages 8-9). However, none of the excerpts provide any evidence of small molecules specifically designed to target or mimic these bridging ligands. In contrast, several reports focus on small‐molecule MERTK inhibitors that impair rather than enhance phagocytosis, and these studies include target engagement and spatial ADME/safety profiling in preclinical models (hamm2022pharmacologicalinhibitionof pages 9-10). Furthermore, there are no kinetic/phagocytic assays or in vivo AMD RPE models reported for small‐molecule agonists that boost bridging‐ligand function. 

No small molecules have yet been reported with confirmed target engagement, functional efficacy in AMD models, or comprehensive ADME/safety profiling for enhancing RPE phagocytosis via MFG-E8, Gas6, or Protein S pathways. This represents an unmet need in the field.
