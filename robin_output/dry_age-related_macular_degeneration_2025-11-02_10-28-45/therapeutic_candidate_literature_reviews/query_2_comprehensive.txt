Query: Comprehensive review of repurposed clinically approved drugs with documented ocular tissue penetration that modulate MerTK receptor signaling or its downstream adaptor proteins in RPE cells, enhance outer segment clearance kinetics in vitro, demonstrate efficacy in dry AMD animal or ex vivo models, and have characterized ADME properties and human safety profiles

The available data indicate that several clinically approved oncology drugs such as gilteritinib, cabozantinib, sitravatinib, merestinib, and crizotinib exhibit documented ocular tissue penetration, with preclinical studies demonstrating preferential accumulation in melanin‐rich tissues (williamson2021bloodretinalbarrier pages 4-5, williamson2021bloodretinalbarrier pages 5-6). These compounds, which inhibit MerTK either as a primary target or off‐target effect, have been characterized in terms of physicochemical properties, ADME profiles, and human safety in oncology settings (williamson2021bloodretinalbarrier pages 5-6). However, the evidence in the context of retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cell biology reveals that MerTK inhibition—exemplified by experimental compounds such as AZ14145845—impairs photoreceptor outer segment uptake and leads to retinal degeneration rather than enhancing outer segment clearance kinetics (hamm2022pharmacologicalinhibitionof pages 9-10, hamm2022pharmacologicalinhibitionof pages 5-7, hamm2022pharmacologicalinhibitionof pages 1-2). No repurposed agent among these drugs has demonstrated efficacy in dry AMD animal or ex vivo models or the ability to improve outer segment clearance in vitro. Although ADME and human safety profiles are well characterized in non‐ocular indications, there is insufficient evidence that any of these approved drugs can beneficially modulate MerTK signaling in RPE cells for the treatment of dry AMD (williamson2021bloodretinalbarrier pages 1-2, williamson2021bloodretinalbarrier pages 2-4).

There is no current repurposed clinically approved drug that meets all the specified criteria. Further research is needed to identify or develop agents that safely modulate MerTK signaling in RPE cells to enhance outer segment clearance and demonstrate efficacy in dry AMD models.
