The provided excerpts do not include any studies reporting that GPCR modulators—specifically targeting GPR91, GPR40, or downstream second messenger pathways—accelerate phagocytic uptake kinetics in RPE cells. Several reports focus on agents such as L‐2‑oxothiazolidine‑4‑carboxylate (OTC) acting through GPR109A to reduce inflammatory and oxidative stress markers in RPE cells and in AMD‐relevant mouse models (promsote2015rpeasa pages 102-107, promsote2015rpeasa pages 111-116, promsote2015rpeasa pages 222-226), yet no direct assessments of phagocytic uptake kinetics are provided. Similarly, studies investigating GPR143 activation by L-DOPA demonstrate effects on exosome release and pigment regulation (figueroa2020agproteincoupled pages 5-8, figueroa2020agproteincoupled pages 1-3, figueroa2020agproteincoupled pages 3-5) but do not examine enhancements in RPE phagocytic function. In addition, systems pharmacology efforts using combination GPCR modulators assessed receptor specificity and ocular pharmacokinetics with safety endpoints such as ERG recordings (orban2018acombinationof pages 1-2, orban2018acombinationof pages 13-14, orban2018acombinationof pages 4-6, orban2018acombinationof pages 8-9); however, these studies did not measure phagocytic uptake nor target GPR91 or GPR40. 

There is no evidence in the provided context supporting GPCR modulators or second messenger activators that accelerate phagocytic uptake kinetics in RPE cells with validated specificity, efficacious outcomes in dry AMD or ex vivo human RPE models, and detailed ocular PK/tolerability profiles. Further research is required to identify and validate such agents.
