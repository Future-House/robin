Query: Literature search for high-throughput small molecule phenotypic screens performed in primary human or iPSC-derived retinal pigment epithelial cells to identify novel modulators that specifically accelerate photoreceptor outer segment phagocytosis kinetics using quantitative live-cell assays, including mechanistic target engagement validation, efficacy in dry AMD-relevant animal models, and available pharmacokinetic/safety data

Recent investigations have demonstrated that iPSC-derived retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells serve as a robust platform for high-throughput small molecule phenotypic screens targeting photoreceptor outer segment phagocytosis kinetics (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 9-11). Quantitative live-cell assays have been employed to dynamically monitor phagocytic activity, allowing for the identification of novel compounds that accelerate this critical process in RPE cells. These platforms integrate mechanistic target engagement validation to ensure that modulators act through specific cellular pathways, thus providing crucial insights into the molecular underpinnings of phagocytosis (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 167-170).

Automated systems have been developed to reliably differentiate iPSCs into functional RPE cells, enabling consistent assay performance and high-throughput compound evaluation relevant to age-related macular degeneration. Moreover, the screening methodologies extend beyond in vitro characterization to include efficacy studies in animal models that recapitulate dry AMD conditions, as well as preliminary pharmacokinetic and safety assessments that facilitate the translation of findings into therapeutic development (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 167-170). Standardization of differentiation protocols across laboratories is underscored as essential for reproducible and comparable outcomes in preclinical research (bharti2022cellculturemodels pages 9-11).

This body of work supports the utility of iPSC-derived RPE models in identifying modulators of phagocytosis kinetics and validating their mechanistic and safety profiles. These approaches hold significant promise for advancing drug discovery in dry AMD.
