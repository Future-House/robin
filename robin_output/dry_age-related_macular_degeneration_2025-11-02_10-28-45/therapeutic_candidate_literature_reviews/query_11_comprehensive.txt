Query: Comprehensive review of molecular mechanisms underlying impaired photoreceptor outer segment phagocytosis in dry age-related macular degeneration, focusing on dysregulation of MerTK, αvβ5 integrin, MFG-E8, and downstream signaling pathways in primary human AMD RPE and in vivo models

Failure of retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) clearance phagocytosis in dry AMD arises from defects at three levels: receptor/bridging modules that recognize phosphatidylserine (PS) on photoreceptor outer segments (POS), cross-talk that licenses internalization via MerTK, and degradative pathways that mature and clear phagosomes; primary human AMD RPE exhibit reduced phagocytosis that can be restored by extracellular factors, supporting the causal relevance of these nodes (inana2018rpephagocyticfunction pages 6-10). In vivo genetics show that loss of MerTK or αvβ5 is sufficient to block POS clearance and trigger retinal degeneration, establishing these receptors as nonredundant gatekeepers of RPE phagocytosis (inana2018rpephagocyticfunction pages 6-10).

POS present a diurnal PS “eat-me” signal that is captured by αvβ5 integrin on RPE apical microvilli through the bridging ligand MFG‑E8, and ANXA5 preserves αvβ5 surface levels; loss of αvβ5 or MFG‑E8 abolishes the post–light-onset phagocytic burst and produces arrhythmic, basal phagocytosis in mouse models (xu2024theformationand pages 7-9, xu2024theformationand pages 14-16). αvβ5 is necessary for POS binding whereas MerTK is required for cup formation and ingestion; FAK couples to αvβ5 and is activated upon binding, and dominant‑negative FAK prevents MERTK phosphorylation and engulfment without impairing attachment (lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 11-12). MerTK activation additionally requires PS-bridging ligands (Gas6, protein S) and provides a negative feedback that limits αvβ5‑dependent particle binding, indicating bidirectional integrin–TAM coordination (enderlin2024retinalatrophyinflammation pages 1-2, zihni2025phagocytosisbythe pages 10-11). Post‑translational regulation of MerTK by proteolytic cleavage generates soluble MerTK with circadian dynamics; a cleavage‑resistant MerTK mouse shows reduced phagocytosis, disrupted phagocytic rhythm, retinal atrophy, and inflammation, linking MerTK availability to disease progression in vivo (enderlin2024retinalatrophyinflammation pages 1-2).

Downstream, MerTK recruits SH2 adaptors, activates PI3K, and drives actin remodeling via the p130Cas/CrkII/Dock180 GEF→Rac→WAVE→Arp2/3 pathway to build the F‑actin cup, while αvβ5–MFG‑E8 independently triggers Rac1 to prime lamellipodial cup assembly (xu2024theformationand pages 7-9, lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 12-14). Integrin–FAK–Src signaling reinforces these cytoskeletal programs, and actomyosin control (MRCKβ–myosin II, myosin VIIa) plus Rab/AKT pathways coordinate cup closure and early phagosome trafficking; inhibitory Sema4D–plexinB1 signaling suppresses Rac1 and reduces POS internalization, and RhoA/ROCK blockade can partially rescue engulfment when MERTK is deficient (zihni2025phagocytosisbythe pages 10-11, lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 27-29, lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 12-14). Defects downstream of uptake exacerbate AMD pathology: compromised cathepsin D function, lipofuscin/A2E and iron‑driven oxidative stress, and impaired endolysosomal/autophagic trafficking hinder phagolysosomal degradation and promote toxic accumulation (inana2018rpephagocyticfunction pages 6-10, egbejimi2022decipheringunderlyingmechanisms pages 88-92).

Human AMD RPE are mechanistically rescuable: conditioned media from hUTC provide RTK ligands and bridge molecules (MFG‑E8, TSP‑1/2) that upregulate MERTK, PLD1 and PAK3, downregulate ARHGAP9, and restore PI3K/Rho‑GTPase effector balance and phagocytic capacity, demonstrating pathway reversibility ex vivo (inana2018rpephagocyticfunction pages 11-12, inana2018rpephagocyticfunction pages 6-10).

Dry AMD–relevant phagocytic failure reflects coordinated breakdown of PS recognition (MFG‑E8–αvβ5), MerTK licensing and availability, and actin/actomyosin remodeling and phagolysosomal processing. Primary human AMD RPE and in vivo models converge on these nodes, and targeted augmentation of TAM ligands/bridges and cytoskeletal effectors can reinstate POS clearance. These data nominate αvβ5–FAK–MerTK–Rac circuitry and lysosomal competence as therapeutic focal points (lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 11-12, enderlin2024retinalatrophyinflammation pages 1-2, lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 12-14, inana2018rpephagocyticfunction pages 6-10).
