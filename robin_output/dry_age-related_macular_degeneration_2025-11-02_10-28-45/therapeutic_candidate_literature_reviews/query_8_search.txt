Query: Search for small molecule regulators of cholesterol efflux transporters (ABCA1, ABCG1) or LXR target genes in RPE cells that reduce intracellular lipid accumulation and enhance phagocytosis kinetics of outer segments in vitro, with target validation, in vivo proof-of-concept in dry AMD models, and existing PK/safety information

Synthetic LXR agonists such as GW3965, TO901317, and T0901317 have been employed as small-molecule regulators of cholesterol efflux in RPE cells. In vitro studies in ARPE-19 and iPSC‐derived RPE models demonstrate that these compounds activate LXR signaling and upregulate target genes including ABCA1, leading to enhanced ApoAI‐mediated cholesterol efflux, reduced intracellular lipid accumulation, and improved phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments (florian2022regulationofabca1 pages 5-7, florian2022regulationofabca1 pages 7-9, choudhary2020lxrsregulatefeatures pages 4-5). In vivo proof‐of‐concept is provided by studies in apoB100‐expressing mouse models of dry AMD, where dietary administration of GW3965 resulted in significant decreases in Bruch’s membrane lipid deposition, reduced markers of retinal inflammation, and improved fundus morphology (choudhary2020lxrsregulatefeatures pages 2-4, choudhary2020lxrsregulatefeatures pages 9-11, xie2022activationoflxrs pages 1-2). Although these studies report minimal systemic toxicity and some evidence of bioavailability—such as the capacity of these agonists to cross the blood–brain barrier—detailed pharmacokinetic data and comprehensive safety profiles are not provided in the excerpts (choudhary2020lxrsregulatefeatures pages 13-14, choudhary2020lxrsregulatefeatures pages 17-18). 

These findings indicate that LXR agonists are promising regulators of cholesterol efflux and phagocytic function in RPE cells. Further evaluation of their PK and safety parameters is required to support their translational potential in the treatment of dry AMD.
