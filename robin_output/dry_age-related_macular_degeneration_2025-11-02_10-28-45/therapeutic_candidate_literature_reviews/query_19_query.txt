Query: Query genetic risk factors (ARMS2/HTRA1, CFH, C3) for dry AMD and their mechanistic links to impaired RPE phagocytosis in cell and animal models, including rescue experiments and pathway validation

Genetic risk factors at the ARMS2/HTRA1, CFH, and C3 loci significantly contribute to dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) by perturbing complement regulation and impairing retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) phagocytosis. ARMS2, with risk polymorphisms such as rs10490924, enhances complement opsonization of apoptotic cells through properdin binding, and its deficiency leads to the accumulation of cellular debris that the RPE must clear. In combination with HTRA1 variants, which are implicated in extracellular matrix remodeling, these alterations exacerbate complement dysregulation and impair RPE phagocytosis in cell-based models (lin2018theroleof pages 67-70, bhumika2024geneticinsightsinto pages 7-9, lin2018theroleof pages 24-26).

CFH risk alleles, notably the Y402H polymorphism, diminish CFH’s capacity to bind critical ligands, resulting in unrestrained activation of the alternative complement pathway. This leads to increased deposition of C3 breakdown products and the membrane attack complex (MAC), thereby fostering inflammatory stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and phagocytic failure in RPE cells. Rescue experiments in iPSC-derived RPE models using candidate drugs have validated the potential to restore complement regulation and RPE function (ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 15-16, ebeling2021impairedmitochondrialfunction pages 16-18, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 1-2, landowski2018unravelingthein pages 43-49).

Genetic variants in C3 further modulate complement activation; animal models demonstrate that aberrant C3 activity contributes to RPE homeostatic imbalance and defective phagocytosis (bhumika2024geneticinsightsinto pages 6-7, malek2020modelsofpathologies pages 18-21).

Genetic risk variants in ARMS2/HTRA1, CFH, and C3 converge to impair RPE phagocytosis by disrupting complement regulation. Targeted interventions that restore complement homeostasis have been validated in cell and animal models, offering promising therapeutic avenues for dry AMD.
