Query: Search for autophagy–lysosome pathway alterations in AMD RPE, including TFEB nuclear translocation, mTOR signaling imbalance, and resultant defects in phagosome maturation and clearance kinetics

Autophagy–lysosome clearance is compromised in AMD RPE. POS phagocytosis imposes a high lysosomal burden; incomplete degradation yields lipofuscin-like autofluorescent granules (AFG), with reduced autophagy proteins/flux, swollen autolysosomes, and accumulation of lipid droplets, glycogen, and damaged mitochondria indicative of impaired autophagic flux and lysosomal function (lysosome–phagosome fusion is necessary for AFG formation, but loss of catalytic capacity—especially Cathepsin D—exacerbates AFG) (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 3-5). Defects in phagosome maturation include failed fusion leading to enlarged lysosomes and slowed POS proteolysis when CTSD is deficient; upstream uptake defects (e.g., MerTK) can further depress ingestion (si2023theroleof pages 4-5).

The mTOR–TFEB axis is imbalanced during POS processing: synchronous POS uptake causes early mTORC1 activation followed by later inhibition, yet TFEB protein that rises during maturation remains largely phosphorylated/inactive; AMD donor RPE show increased TFEB phosphorylation and reduced nuclear TFEB, consistent with a bottleneck in TFEB activation (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 10-13, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 13-18). This is accompanied by decreased GADD34 and elevated p-eIF2α, blunting TFEB-dependent lysosomal programs; age/disease-associated mTORC1 overactivation further suppresses autophagy and lysosomal function (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 6-10, si2023theroleof pages 7-9).

Restoring TFEB activity improves clearance. Rapamycin lowers pS6 and the pTFEB/TFEB ratio, induces GADD34 and lysosomal biogenesis (e.g., LAMP1), and reduces established AFG, but its efficacy requires intact lysosomal proteases/lipases and CTSD (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 10-13, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 13-18). Constitutively active TFEB increases CLEAR targets (e.g., CTSB, LAMP2, ATP6VOA1) and markedly reduces POS-dependent AFG and A2E (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7).

Multiple modifiers converge to slow clearance kinetics in AMD RPE: impaired lysosomal acidification via V-ATPase/CRYBA1–AKT/mTOR signaling, iron overload with NRF2–TFEB crosstalk defects reducing TFEB nuclear translocation and ferritinophagy, mitochondrial dysfunction limiting ATP-dependent steps, and reduced mitophagy/autophagosome maturation, all aggravate lipofuscin accumulation and autophagy inhibition (kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3, si2023theroleof pages 7-9, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 11-13, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 3-5, si2023theroleof pages 9-10).

AMD RPE exhibit impaired autophagic flux, lysosomal enzyme deficits, and defective phagosome maturation/clearance that are mechanistically linked to an mTORC1–TFEB activation bottleneck with reduced TFEB nuclear translocation. Pharmacologic mTORC1 inhibition or genetic TFEB activation restores lysosomal biogenesis and reduces lipofuscin-like burden, contingent on preserved lysosomal hydrolase activity. Mitochondrial, acidification, iron, and NRF2–TFEB coupling defects further decelerate clearance kinetics in AMD RPE (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 10-13, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 3-5).
