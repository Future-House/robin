Query: Review of complement pathway inhibitors or modulators (C3a, C5a, or MAC regulators) that indirectly restore or enhance RPE phagocytosis kinetics in vitro, including studies demonstrating reversal of phagocytic defects in dry AMD animal models or patient-derived RPE, with ADME and ocular safety data

Across preclinical RPE and retinal models, complement modulation has repeatedly reversed cellular defects that are mechanistically upstream of RPE phagocytic throughput, even though direct phagocytosis kinetics were not reported in these excerpts. C3 inhibition (compstatins) restored lysosomal function and reversed disease phenotypes in RPE systems, including an AMD-relevant CFH Y402H patient-variant RPE model, and suppressed drusen formation and retinal degeneration in vivo, supporting improved intracellular degradative capacity required for photoreceptor outer segment processing (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 24-25, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 7-9). C3a/C3aR signaling appears pathogenic in RPE: C3a induces sub‑RPE deposits and alters proteasome function, while C3aR blockade prevents these deposits, increases proteasome activity, alleviates neuroinflammation, and restores visual function—findings consistent with enhanced clearance pathways relevant to phagocytosis (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 7-9, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 24-25, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 25-27). For the C5 axis, C5a primes RPE inflammasome activation and aggravates inflammation, whereas C5 depletion/C5aR inhibition suppresses priming; however, beneficial and detrimental effects have both been observed, underscoring context-specific roles and no direct phagocytosis readouts here (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 9-10, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13).

MAC regulation has been beneficial in ocular models: AAV-mediated delivery of CD59 reduced complement-driven NLRP3 activation, and soluble/membrane-targeted CD59 attenuated CNV, indicating downstream complement blockade can mitigate inflammatory injury relevant to RPE function (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 24-25, kassa2019complementinhibitionas pages 22-26).

ADME and ocular safety: compstatin-based C3 inhibitors show prolonged intraocular residence, retinal tissue distribution, and suppression of drusen in nonhuman primates; ANX007 (anti-C1q) demonstrated intravitreal PK/target engagement and tolerability; the oral factor D inhibitor danicopan shows high, sustained ocular exposure with melanin binding; GT005 (Factor I gene therapy) reduced CNV without systemic adverse effects; anti-C5 showed no definite toxicity in mice in select studies (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 25-27, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 10-11, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 9-10, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13). None of the excerpts provide quantitative RPE phagocytosis kinetics or explicit reversal of phagocytic defects in dry AMD animal models or patient-derived RPE beyond lysosomal/proteasome rescue (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 24-25, kassa2019complementinhibitionas pages 22-26, kassa2019complementinhibitionas pages 1-7).

Complement C3/C3a pathway inhibition and MAC regulation indirectly support RPE phagocytic function by restoring lysosomal/proteasome activity and reducing deposit formation in RPE models, but direct phagocytosis-kinetics data are not reported here (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 24-25, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 7-9). Available ADME/safety data come mainly from primate ocular PK for C3/C1 agents and favorable exposure profiles for factor D inhibitors, with limited toxicity signals noted preclinically (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 25-27, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 9-10). No excerpted study demonstrates quantitative rescue of RPE phagocytosis in dry AMD models or patient-derived RPE (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 24-25, kassa2019complementinhibitionas pages 1-7).
