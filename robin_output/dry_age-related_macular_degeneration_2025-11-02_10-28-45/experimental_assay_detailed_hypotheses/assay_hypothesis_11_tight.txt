Assay Overview: This assay models outer blood–retinal barrier (oBRB) failure by culturing human RPE as polarized monolayers on transwells and quantifying tight-junction integrity via transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) and ZO‑1 continuity. TEER is recorded with epithelial volt/ohm meters as a functional barrier readout, while post-assay ZO‑1 immunostaining assesses junctional continuity and RPE tiling; optional paracellular flux (e.g., 70 kDa FITC‑dextran) can be added to quantify permeability. Cells can be ARPE‑19 for throughput or primary/iPSC‑derived RPE for higher physiological fidelity and polarity (including VEGF/PEDF secretion profiling) (obert2015targetingthetight pages 40-45, obert2015targetingthetight pages 53-57, lynn2018aconvenientprotocol pages 17-18, kim2024high‐throughputmicrofluidic3d pages 12-15).

Biomedical Evidence: RPE tight-junction disruption and oBRB breach are central to both dry and wet AMD, enabling inflammatory and angiogenic factor flux and contributing to retinal degeneration. ZO‑1 is a core scaffold for tight, adherens, and gap junctions; VEGF, oxidative, and inflammatory signals can drive ZO‑1 disorganization, TJ disassembly, and increased permeability. Complement-driven drusen-like pathology in a human 3D oBRB model caused RPE atrophy and a ~20× TER drop, directly linking AMD features to barrier failure. Cytokine and hypoxic/oxidative stressors (e.g., TGF‑β+TNF‑α, ML228, NaIO3) induce EMT-like morphology, elevate IL‑1β/IL‑6/IL‑8, shift VEGF/PEDF balance, and reduce TEER, recapitulating molecular hallmarks relevant to dry AMD (obert2015targetingthetighta pages 9-13, song2023bioprinted3douter pages 19-23, kim2024high‐throughputmicrofluidic3d pages 7-10, kim2024high‐throughputmicrofluidic3d pages 1-4).

Previous Use: ARPE‑19 transwell TEER/ZO‑1 assays have been used as a discovery tool to stabilize junctions and preserve barrier against VEGF with a connexin‑mimetic peptide (αCT‑1), with concordant in vivo improvements in CNV and light-damage models, supporting translational relevance. An oBRB microphysiological system leveraging TEER/ZO‑1/permeability was used for preclinical-style testing: oxidative/inflammatory injury reduced TEER and increased permeability; an antioxidant (NACA) restored TEER to ~80% of control and anti‑VEGF partially rescued barrier and inhibited neovascularization; the platform is HTS-compatible (obert2015targetingthetight pages 53-57, obert2015targetingthetighta pages 53-57, obert2015targetingthetight pages 1-9, kim2024high‐throughputmicrofluidic3d pages 7-10, kim2024high‐throughputmicrofluidic3d pages 10-12, kim2024high‐throughputmicrofluidic3d pages 1-4).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths include mechanistic proximity to oBRB pathology, quantitative functional and structural readouts, scalability, and integration with polarity/secretome and permeability assays; the approach has demonstrated drug-responsiveness and HTS feasibility. Weaknesses include ARPE‑19 immaturity and modest TEER, TEER ceiling effects in severe damage, and limited capture of chronic dry AMD phenotypes (drusen, Bruch’s membrane biomechanics) without advanced 3D/complement contexts; microfluidic geometries can also deviate from native BM thickness. Using primary or iPSC-RPE, longer maturation, complement or chronic oxidative/inflammatory challenges, and coupling TEER with ZO‑1 continuity and permeability improves disease fidelity (kim2024high‐throughputmicrofluidic3d pages 4-7, kim2024high‐throughputmicrofluidic3d pages 10-12, lynn2018aconvenientprotocol pages 17-18, song2023bioprinted3douter pages 19-23).

Tight junction integrity assays on RPE transwells using TEER and ZO‑1 are appropriate, mechanistically grounded tools to screen therapeutics that preserve oBRB function in dry AMD. They are quantitative, drug-responsive, and link to disease-relevant pathways, especially when paired with polarity/secretome and permeability measures. Model refinements (primary/iPSC‑RPE, chronic stressors, complement) increase their relevance to dry AMD pathophysiology (obert2015targetingthetight pages 40-45, song2023bioprinted3douter pages 19-23, kim2024high‐throughputmicrofluidic3d pages 7-10).
