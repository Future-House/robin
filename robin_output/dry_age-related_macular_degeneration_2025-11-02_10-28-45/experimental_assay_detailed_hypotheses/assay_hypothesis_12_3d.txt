Assay Overview:
This assay uses human RPE 3D spheroids/organoids that spontaneously form sub‑RPE, drusen‑like deposits over time and under stress; deposits are then quantified by high‑content confocal imaging of drusen components (ApoE and complement proteins such as CFH/C3/C5/C9), with orthogonal lipid (Nile Red) and mineral (hydroxyapatite) stains and ultrastructure (TEM) as supporting endpoints (and qPCR for complement/apolipoprotein transcripts) (usui2019invitrodrusen pages 19-23, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 4-7, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 7-10, mesch2021…vitromodel pages 77-79, mesch2021…anin pages 74-77, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 4-5, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 1-2). iPSC-derived RPE organoids are preferred for long-term culture and maturity, forming an ECM/Bruch’s membrane–like basal compartment and showing age-associated increases in TIMP3, ApoE, lipids, HAP, and complement transcripts (C3, CFH) (mesch2021…vitromodel pages 77-79, mesch2021…anin pages 77-79). Imaging workflows should emphasize en face acquisition of the outer RPE layer/basal surface, with sectioning/optical clearing to mitigate pigment/autofluorescence, and include whole‑well tiling and automated segmentation for quantitative, unbiased analysis (mesch2021…foragerelated pages 74-77, mesch2021…anin pages 74-77, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 4-5).

Biomedical Evidence:
Drusen are an early, defining lesion of dry AMD, consisting of extracellular lipid-protein-mineral deposits between RPE and Bruch’s membrane; they contain apolipoproteins (ApoE/ApoJ), complement components, ECM proteins, and hydroxyapatite, and are clinically linked to disease progression (shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 1-2, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 4-5). RPE are a principal source of drusen constituents and can autonomously produce drusen components, making RPE-only systems biologically grounded (mesch2021…anin pages 24-27). Complement dysregulation is central in AMD, and complement components (e.g., C9) and regulators (CFH/CFI) localize to sub‑RPE deposits in vitro and in human tissue (shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 4-5). Aged hiPSC-RPE organoids recapitulate multiple drusen hallmarks—TIMP3, ApoE, neutral lipids, HAP—and show increased C3/CFH expression, supporting relevance of ApoE/complement readouts (mesch2021…vitromodel pages 77-79, mesch2021…anin pages 77-79).

Previous Use:
RPE spheroids have been shown to form drusen-like “buds” between the RPE and an elastic layer; glycoxidized nanospheres and oxidative stressors increase deposit incidence and modulate ApoE/CFH/C3/C5 expression/localization, establishing manipulable phenotypes suitable for quantification (usui2019invitrodrusen pages 19-23, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 4-7, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 7-10). A 3D bioprinted outer BRB with a BrM-like ECM generated APOE‑positive sub‑RPE deposits upon complement‑competent human serum challenge and paired structural imaging with functional readouts (TER, RPE/choroid integrity), illustrating that complement‑engaged systems yield robust, imageable ApoE deposits (song2023bioprinted3douter pages 19-23). iPSC‑RPE monolayer models identified modulators of AMD‑associated proteins and complement/inflammatory programs (e.g., differential effects of C3 suppression vs nicotinamide), supporting the use of molecular endpoints alongside imaging (saini2017retinaldiseasemodeling pages 127-133). Ex vivo porcine RPE monolayers reproducibly accumulate ApoE‑ and complement‑positive deposits and provide established imaging/quantification workflows, though APOE dynamics can be variable, underscoring the need for multi‑marker panels (shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 4-5, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 12-13, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 3-6).

Overall Evaluation:
Strengths: biologically anchored to a core AMD lesion; human iPSC‑RPE organoids mature in long‑term culture and accumulate relevant drusen markers; complement and ApoE are imageable and responsive to stress/complement challenge; multiple orthogonal endpoints (lipid, HAP, TEM, transcriptomics) increase confidence; reduced handling vs 2D and compatibility with high‑content imaging on the spheroid surface (mesch2021…vitromodel pages 77-79, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 19-23, mesch2021…anin pages 77-79, song2023bioprinted3douter pages 19-23, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 4-5). Weaknesses: absent choroid/choriocapillaris and immature Bruch’s membrane limit physiological clearance and may alter deposit morphology; strong pigment/autofluorescence and spheroid heterogeneity complicate deep imaging; TEER is not feasible; serum exposure in spheroids is apical vs basal in vivo; readouts (Nile Red, complement induction) can be variable; culture timelines are long and throughput moderate; compositional differences vs human macular drusen require careful marker selection and orthogonal validation (e.g., ELISA, mass spectrometry) (mesch2021…anin pages 77-79, mesch2021…vitromodel pages 74-77, usui2019invitrodrusen pages 4-7, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 74-77, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 4-5, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 3-6, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 13-14).

A 3D RPE spheroid/organoid drusen assay is biologically justified and supports quantitative imaging of ApoE/complement accumulation relevant to early dry AMD. It is best positioned for mechanism-focused studies and medium‑throughput screening with orthogonal validation, not as a sole decision‑making screen. Addressing imaging constraints, adding complement challenge and ECM cues, and incorporating multi‑endpoint panels will improve robustness and translational value (usui2019invitrodrusen pages 19-23, mesch2021…vitromodel pages 77-79, song2023bioprinted3douter pages 19-23).
