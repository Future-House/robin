It models the mitochondrial bioenergetic decline central to dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) pathogenesis by quantifying basal respiration, ATP production, and spare respiratory capacity using real-time metabolic flux analysis (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 14-15, ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 12-14).

Biomedical Evidence: Mitochondrial dysfunction is a recognized hallmark of dry AMD, with compromised mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and altered glycolytic activity contributing to RPE degeneration (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 36-40, louie2022assessmentofposterior pages 11-15). Clinical studies correlate these bioenergetic deficits with disease progression, justifying the focus on mitochondrial health when evaluating novel therapeutics aimed at restoring energy homeostasis in the RPE (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 49-52, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 32-36).

Previous Use: Seahorse assays have been widely applied to test mitochondrial-targeted drugs in iPSC-derived and primary RPE models, uncovering drug-induced improvements in metabolic parameters and providing key insights into the mitochondrial underpinnings of AMD (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 9-11, gong2020stemcell‐derivedretinal pages 10-10). Such studies have guided early-stage drug discovery efforts and mechanistic evaluations in AMD (ebeling2022testingmitochondrialtargeteddrugs pages 4-6).

Overall Evaluation: This assay’s strengths include real-time, quantitative measurements of mitochondrial function and its direct relevance to AMD pathogenesis, making it a robust platform for drug discovery. However, potential weaknesses encompass variability in patient-derived cells, challenges in replicating in vivo RPE polarity on assay-specific plates, and the need for careful extrapolation of in vitro results to clinical settings (zhang2024longtermmonitoringof pages 14-15, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 144-148).

Seahorse bioenergetics profiling is a valuable and mechanistically relevant platform for evaluating therapeutic candidates in dry AMD.
