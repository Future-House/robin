Assay Overview: This assay embeds primary human RPE (preferably hfRPE or AMD-donor RPE) in fluorescently labeled collagen I/IV hydrogels and quantifies extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation as loss of fluorescence over time. Mechanistic secondary readouts include gelatin zymography of conditioned media for MMP-2/9 activity, ELISA/qPCR for VEGF and PEDF, ELISA for complement factors (C3a, CFH), transepithelial resistance (TER), and apical/basal fractionation to resolve vectorial protease release. Collagen I/IV are the principal RPE ligands in Bruch’s membrane, and RPE-derived gelatinases are standardly assayed by zymography; complement readouts from RPE cultures are also established (eamegdool2020extracellularmatrixand pages 10-11, eamegdool2020extracellularmatrixand pages 8-10, bandyopadhyay2012matrixmetalloproteinaseactivity pages 1-2, fernandezgodino2018changesinextracellular pages 21-24, treumer2012vectorialreleaseof pages 1-2, an2006secretedproteomeprofiling pages 1-2).

Biomedical Evidence: Bruch’s membrane (BrM) thickening, collagen-rich basal deposits, and impaired ECM turnover are central to early dry AMD; RPE-derived MMP-2/9 govern ECM remodeling and their dysregulation is implicated in deposit/drusen biogenesis (nita2014agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 1-2, nita2014agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 2-3). Loss of active MMP-2 promotes collagen IV accumulation and deposits after oxidant injury, whereas sustaining MMP-2 activation via MMP-14/TIMP-2 preserves ECM turnover (marincastan~o2005nonlethaloxidantinjury pages 8-9, alcazar2007mmp14andtimp2 pages 1-1). Complement attack on RPE acutely shifts MMP-2/9 activity and alters VEGF/PEDF in the extracellular space; pharmacologic gelatinase inhibition rebalances VEGF/PEDF without changing expression, indicating that extracellular proteolysis is causal (bandyopadhyay2012matrixmetalloproteinaseactivity pages 1-2). C3a–C3aR signaling inhibits the proteasome, increases MMP-2 activity, impairs ECM turnover, and drives basal deposit formation; C3aR antagonism prevents deposits, establishing a tractable RPE model of complement–MMP–ECM coupling (fernandezgodino2018c3atriggersformation pages 1-2). Abnormal ECM (EFEMP1 R345W or AMD BrM) increases basal MMP-2 activation and triggers complement tick-over, reproducing thick collagenous basal deposits (fernandezgodino2018changesinextracellular pages 8-11, fernandezgodino2018changesinextracellular pages 35-38).

Previous Use: Although RPE-in-gel fluorescence-loss assays have not been directly reported in AMD, closely related readouts (gelatin zymography, VEGF/PEDF assays, C3a/CFH ELISA) have been used to quantify RPE gelatinase activity and complement activation, and to test pathway interventions such as C3aR antagonism that block deposit formation (fernandezgodino2018changesinextracellular pages 21-24, fernandezgodino2018c3atriggersformation pages 1-2). Laser perturbation and explant models show that increasing activated MMP-2 basally can enhance BrM transport and is associated with drusen regression, supporting the causal role of protease-driven ECM remodeling (zhang2012lasermediatedactivationof pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—Directly measures an AMD-relevant mechanism (RPE MMP-2/9–mediated ECM proteolysis), is quantitative and multiplexable with zymography, VEGF/PEDF, TER, and complement readouts, and can capture vectorial basal protease release and complement/oxidative modulation (bandyopadhyay2012matrixmetalloproteinaseactivity pages 1-2, treumer2012vectorialreleaseof pages 1-2, fernandezgodino2018changesinextracellular pages 21-24). Weaknesses—BrM is multilaminar (collagens I/III/IV/VI, elastin, proteoglycans) with age-dependent crosslinking and TIMP regulation; simple collagen gels may misrepresent substrate composition and susceptibility. MMP signals are context dependent: oxidant stress decreases MMP-2 activation while abnormal ECM/complement can increase it, complicating a unidirectional normalization endpoint. Predictive validity for drusen regression requires anchoring to EFEMP1-mutant ECM or AMD BrM explants with concurrent MMP/TIMP and complement measurements (nita2014agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 1-2, nita2014agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 2-3, marincastan~o2005nonlethaloxidantinjury pages 8-9, fernandezgodino2018changesinextracellular pages 8-11, fernandezgodino2018changesinextracellular pages 18-21).

A collagen-fluorescence degradation assay is mechanistically well-justified for early dry AMD, provided it is paired with orthogonal MMP-2/9 activation, VEGF/PEDF, and complement readouts and validated against disease-relevant ECM contexts. It should be configured with collagen IV-rich, crosslinked substrates and basal/apical compartmentalization to maximize physiological relevance. On its own, it is necessary but insufficient; linkage to ex vivo AMD BrM or EFEMP1-mutant ECM models will strengthen translational validity (bandyopadhyay2012matrixmetalloproteinaseactivity pages 1-2, fernandezgodino2018changesinextracellular pages 8-11, treumer2012vectorialreleaseof pages 1-2).
