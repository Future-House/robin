Assay Overview:  
This assay employs ARPE-19 cells exposed to sublytic complement to induce membrane attack complex (MAC) formation. Following treatment, cells are loaded with Fluo‑4 AM to monitor intracellular Ca²⁺ transients in real time. The measured endpoints include the kinetics of Ca²⁺ influx—specifically, an initial rapid transient followed by a sustained plateau—which serve as proxies for sublytic MAC triggers of downstream inflammasome activation and VEGF secretion (rohrer2014prolongedsrckinase pages 4-6, rohrer2014prolongedsrckinase pages 1-4).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Sublytic MAC formation is a central event in the pathogenesis of dry age‑related macular degeneration, as it activates inflammatory pathways, including NF‑κB, and promotes pro‑inflammatory cytokine release in retinal pigment epithelial cells (cao2016theroleof pages 1-6). The biphasic Ca²⁺ response observed is mechanistically linked to Src kinase activation and altered gene expression, which further underlie chronic inflammation and endothelial growth factor deregulation (busch2023increasedplasmalevel pages 2-3, rohrer2014prolongedsrckinase pages 4-6). This in vitro model thus recapitulates critical aspects of the disease’s pathological cascade documented in both biomedical and clinical literature.

Previous Use:  
ARPE‑19-based assays measuring MAC‑induced Ca²⁺ influx have been previously employed to elucidate the roles of complement activation in RPE injury and VEGF overproduction, providing a mechanistic basis for therapeutic intervention in AMD (yang2014retinalpigmentepithelial pages 1-2, rohrer2014prolongedsrckinase pages 4-6). Such approaches have yielded key insights into the modulation of lethal versus sublytic complement effects in RPE cells (yang2014retinalpigmentepithelial pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths of this assay include its direct modeling of a key pathogenic process in dry AMD and its quantitative, real‑time readout of intracellular Ca²⁺ signaling. It is amenable to high‑throughput screening for compounds that inhibit MAC formation and downstream inflammasome activation. However, limitations arise from the use of an immortalized cell line that may not fully replicate primary RPE responses, as well as challenges in correlating acute signaling events with long‑term therapeutic outcomes in complex in vivo systems (brandstetter2016nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 95-99, merle2023thenoncanonicalimpact pages 17-21).

This assay is a valuable tool for early‑stage drug discovery targeting complement‑mediated RPE injury in dry AMD. It offers mechanistic insights into acute Ca²⁺ signaling events that are pivotal in disease progression.
