Query: Comprehensive overview of photoreceptor-RPE co-culture and organoid models for dry AMD, detailing cell sources, differentiation protocols, readouts for photoreceptor viability and function, RPE phagocytosis assays, and 3D model optimization, maturation stage characterization, and relevant phenotypic assays

Cell sources. Reported inputs include UC‑MSCs/hUCMSCs (differentiated to induced RPE), established RPE (ARPE‑19), primary human retinal cell types (microvascular endothelial cells, Müller glia), and hiPSC‑derived lineages (hiPSC‑RPE, hiPSC‑ECs); retinal organoids and RPE sheets are generated from hPSC/hiPSC, with gene editing used to bias photoreceptor composition in some studies (NRL−/−) (li2025advancesinthe pages 10-12, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 1-2, li2025advancesinthe pages 27-27). 

Differentiation and maturation strategies. Paracrine/co‑culture induction and scaffold‑guided maturation are emphasized; UC‑MSCs reliably convert to iRPE in vivo models, and prolonged RPE maturation improves function, but explicit stepwise differentiation protocols are not provided in these excerpts (li2025advancesinthe pages 10-12, alani2019microscaletissueengineering pages 65-69). 

Co‑culture configurations. Implementations include tri‑cellular human retinal models (endothelium + RPE/ARPE‑19 + Müller), HUCMSC–ARPE‑19 to induce RPE‑like states, and hiPSC‑RPE/ECs cultured on opposing sides of collagen‑coated porous PCL to mimic the outer retina; long‑term 3D PR–RPE co‑culture is required to reconstitute phototransduction‑relevant niche functions (li2025advancesinthe pages 10-12, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 9-11). 

3D scaffold optimization and delivery. Scaffold stiffness, topography, and ligand presentation govern adhesion, survival, and integration; collagen‑coated porous PCL is a practical format, while subretinal delivery of suspensions suffers poor viability that biomaterial‑mediated cytoprotection can mitigate; RPE substrates must be thin, mechanically robust, and biocompatible, with OCT enabling in vivo layer monitoring (li2025advancesinthe pages 10-12, li2025advancesinthe pages 15-17). 

Readouts of photoreceptor viability and function. Structural: retinal/ONL thickness, cone arrestin staining, photoreceptor density, OS elongation and disk‑stack counts, and OS–RPE interdigitation; molecular: opsin/peripherin localization, IRBP expression, ER‑stress/apoptotic gene signatures; functional: ERG waveforms/b‑wave amplitude, light responses/ionic currents, synaptic maturation via CTBP2‑positive ribbon synapses and EM; viability: TUNEL in ONL and longitudinal survival (li2025advancesinthe pages 10-12, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 13-16, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 3-5, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 9-9, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 12-13). 

RPE functional assays and phagocytosis. Assays include RPE marker expression, POS phagocytosis (uptake of isolated/labeled OS fragments), PEDF secretion by ELISA, neurotrophic factor profiling, and evaluation of visual‑cycle dependence with 9‑cis/11‑cis retinal; transporter and secretion functions (PEDF/VEGF) reflect polarization and niche support (li2025advancesinthe pages 10-12, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 13-16, alani2019microscaletissueengineering pages 36-40). 

Maturation stage characterization and limitations. Human organoids require ~24–32+ weeks for OS elongation yet lack robust disk shedding/renewal and continuous RPE, leading to OS protein mislocalization and constrained visual‑cycle function; RPE maturation (pigmentation, PEDF upregulation) and hypoxia markers (HIF‑1α) inform stage and niche quality (singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 13-16, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 12-13, alani2019microscaletissueengineering pages 65-69, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 1-2, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 9-9). 

These models leverage defined cell sources and scaffolded co‑cultures to restore RPE polarity, phagocytosis, and retinoid support, while readouts span structure, molecular markers, electrophysiology, and secretome assays. Detailed, stepwise differentiation protocols are not provided in the cited excerpts and must be sourced elsewhere (li2025advancesinthe pages 10-12, alani2019microscaletissueengineering pages 65-69). 

Photoreceptor–RPE dry AMD models use hPSC/hiPSC‑derived organoids and RPE, primary retinal cells, and MSC‑to‑RPE conversions, often integrated on collagen‑coated porous PCL scaffolds (li2025advancesinthe pages 10-12). Validation relies on ONL/OS morphology, opsin localization, ERG, CTBP2 ribbon synapses, TUNEL, RPE POS phagocytosis, and PEDF ELISA, with long‑term PR–RPE co‑culture required to approach visual‑cycle fidelity (li2025advancesinthe pages 10-12, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 13-16, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 3-5, singh2020limitationsandpromise pages 9-9). Explicit differentiation protocols are not contained in these sources (alani2019microscaletissueengineering pages 65-69).
