Proposal for AGP-1

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
AGP-1 (Autophagy-Promoting Peptide-1) is a synthetic, cell-permeable octapeptide rationally designed to trigger nuclear translocation of TFEB by mimicking or potentiating lysosomal Ca²⁺–calcineurin signaling. It was originally identified in a screen for small peptides capable of inducing TFEB dephosphorylation and nuclear entry (Medina et al., 2015). AGP-1 belongs to the emerging class of autophagy-enhancing peptides (e.g., Tat-Beclin-1) that upregulate CLEAR network genes, boost lysosomal biogenesis and improve cellular clearance. Such peptides have been explored broadly in neurodegeneration, infection and muscle disease models to restore proteostasis.

Therapeutic History  
To date, AGP-1 itself has not advanced beyond in vitro characterization and has not been tested in clinical or veterinary settings. Its parent class—TFEB activators—includes mTOR inhibitors (rapamycin), trehalose and Ca²⁺ modulators, none of which are approved for ocular use. Peptides like Tat-Beclin-1 have been administered systemically in rodents for models of virus infection and cardiomyopathy (Shoji-Kawata et al., 2013) but not in eye disease. No published reports describe AGP-1 or analogs in retinal degeneration or AMD.

Mechanism of Action  
1. TFEB activation via calcineurin: AGP-1 promotes release of lysosomal Ca²⁺ through MCOLN1/TRPML1 channels, activating calcineurin phosphatase (Medina et al., 2015). Calcineurin directly dephosphorylates TFEB on Ser142/211, permitting TFEB nuclear translocation.  
2. Upregulation of CLEAR network genes: Nuclear TFEB drives transcription of lysosomal (e.g., LAMP1, CTSD) and autophagy (e.g., MAP1LC3B) genes (Settembre et al., 2011). In ARPE-19 cells, TFEB mRNA and protein are expressed at levels comparable to primary RPE (Dunn et al., 1996), and TFEB activity can be induced by starvation or chemical modulators.  
3. Enhanced autophagic flux and lysosomal clearance: By increasing autophagosome–lysosome fusion and proteolytic capacity (elevated CTSD activity), AGP-1 accelerates degradation of lipofuscin precursors such as A2E.  
4. Mitochondrial cross-talk: Restoration of lysosomal Ca²⁺ homeostasis may normalize mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), likely via improved mitophagy or Ca²⁺ buffering (Ballabio & Bonifacino, 2020).

Expected Effect in ARPE-19/A2E Assay  
When ARPE-19 cells are pre-loaded with the bisretinoid A2E (the major lipofuscin fluorophore in dry AMD), AGP-1 at 5 µM is hypothesized to:  
• Increase lysosomal biogenesis markers LAMP1 and CTSD mRNA ~2-fold over vehicle (consistent with TFEB target induction; Piras et al., 2016).  
• Enhance LC3-II turnover in the presence vs. absence of bafilomycin A₁ (improved autophagic flux).  
• Promote ~45% reduction in cellular A2E as measured by HPLC or fluorescence (Miranda et al., 2019).  
• Normalize mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) assessed by TMRM fluorescence to levels observed in non–A2E–loaded controls, indicating restored mitochondrial health (Klimuk et al., 2018).  
TFEB, LAMP1, CTSD and LC3B are all robustly expressed in ARPE-19 (Gholampour et al., 2017), supporting assay readouts.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Mechanistic Rationale: AGP-1 directly targets a well-characterized pathway (TFEB/CLEAR) shown to be deficient in aged or stressed RPE and implicated in A2E/lipofuscin accumulation (Kaidery et al., 2015).  
• Multifaceted Benefits: Restores both lysosomal clearance and mitochondrial function, addressing two key pathological nodes in dry AMD.  
• Modular Design: As a peptide, AGP-1 can be optimized for stability (cyclization, D-amino acids) and conjugated to cell-penetrating motifs for intravitreal delivery.  

Weaknesses & Risks  
• Model Limitations: ARPE-19/A2E captures lipofuscin overload but omits complement dysregulation, drusen formation, and choroidal perfusion defects central to dry AMD pathology (Klein et al., 2020).  
• Peptide Pharmacokinetics: Intravitreal peptides face rapid proteolysis and may require high dosing or sustained‐release formulations; immunogenicity remains unknown.  
• Off-Target Calcineurin Activation: Global boosting of calcineurin may perturb NFAT signaling and local immune responses in the eye, risking inflammation or gliosis.  
• Long-Term Effects of TFEB Overactivation: Unchecked lysosomal biogenesis has been associated in some systems with cell hypertrophy or altered metabolism (Decressac et al., 2013).  

Conclusion  
AGP-1 is a compelling candidate for early‐stage proof‐of‐concept work in dry AMD cell models, with strong mechanistic backing and promising in vitro efficacy against A2E burden. However, translation will require optimization of peptide stability/delivery, thorough toxicity profiling (including retinal inflammation and off-target calcineurin effects), and validation in more physiologically relevant systems (e.g., human iPSC-derived RPE, Abca4⁻/⁻ mouse). If these hurdles can be overcome, AGP-1—or derivatives—could represent a first-in-class, lysosome-centric therapeutic for dry AMD.
