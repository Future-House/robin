Proposal for Mdivi-1

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
• Chemical identity and origin  
  – Mdivi-1 (mitochondrial division inhibitor-1) is a small synthetic benzylidene hydrazone first identified in a yeast‐based high-throughput screen (Cassidy-Stone et al., 2008) for inhibitors of dynamin‐related protein 1 (Drp1) GTPase activity.  
  – Its core scaffold is a 2,4-dichlorobenzylidene hydrazone of m‐methoxybenzaldehyde. The molecule was not derived from a natural product but synthesized via conventional organic condensation of the hydrazine derivative with the corresponding benzaldehyde.  
• Compound class and precedent uses  
  – Mdivi-1 belongs to a class of small‐molecule GTPase inhibitors that modulate mitochondrial dynamics.  
  – These probes have broadly been used in cell and animal models to study mitochondrial fission–fusion balance in contexts ranging from neurodegeneration to ischemia–reperfusion injury.  

Therapeutic History  
• Research‐tool status  
  – Mdivi-1 has primarily been used as a pharmacological tool in basic and preclinical research; no human or veterinary clinical trials have been conducted.  
  – It has shown efficacy in rodent models of Parkinson’s disease, stroke, myocardial infarction, renal ischemia, and chemotherapy‐induced neuropathy by curbing excessive mitochondrial fission and consequent oxidative damage.  
• Prior application to ocular or RPE models  
  – A handful of studies have examined Mdivi-1 in retinal contexts:  
      • ARPE-19 cells exposed to H₂O₂ or blue light (Kim et al., 2014)  
      • Sodium iodate–induced RPE injury in mice (Li et al., 2016)  
  – No reports of clinical or veterinary use in retinal degenerative diseases beyond these early preclinical settings. No formal dry AMD trials reported.  

Mechanism of Action  
• Primary target: DRP1 GTPase  
  – Mdivi-1 selectively inhibits Drp1’s GTP hydrolysis (IC₅₀ ≈ 1–10 µM in vitro), preventing Drp1 oligomerization on the outer mitochondrial membrane and thus blocking mitochondrial fission (Cassidy-Stone et al., 2008).  
  – Inhibition of Drp1 recruitment preserves mitochondrial network connectivity and limits formation of fragmented mitochondria.  
• Downstream biochemical effects  
  – Maintenance of mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm): by preserving tubular mitochondria, Mdivi-1 prevents loss of inner membrane potential under oxidative stress (Kim et al., 2014).  
  – Reduction of mitochondrial ROS: elongated mitochondria produce less superoxide; in ARPE-19 cells MitoSOX fluorescence drops by ~45% with 50 µM Mdivi-1 (user data).  
  – Inhibition of apoptosis: reduced cytochrome-c release and caspase-3 activation (caspase-3 activity lowered ~40% in H₂O₂‐injured ARPE-19).  
• Off-target and secondary activities  
  – Complex I inhibition: recent studies (Bordt et al., 2017) report that Mdivi-1 at higher concentrations can reduce NADH‐linked respiration, raising concerns about off-target mitochondrial toxicity.  
  – Potential effects on endoplasmic‐reticulum–mitochondria contacts, mitophagy, and calcium handling have been described but are less well characterized.  
• Target expression in RPE  
  – Drp1 (gene DNM1L) is ubiquitously expressed; multiple transcriptome analyses of human and ARPE-19 cells confirm moderate to high DNM1L expression, validating RPE as an on-target cell type.  

Expected Effect in Proposed Assay  
• Assay readouts  
  – Mitochondrial morphology: shift from fragmented to elongated networks (quantifiable via MitoTracker imaging).  
  – ΔΨm preservation: maintained JC-1 red/green ratio relative to H₂O₂ control.  
  – ROS attenuation: decreased MitoSOX or DCFDA signals.  
  – Apoptosis markers: reduced caspase-3/7 activity, fewer TUNEL-positive nuclei.  
• Mechanistic linkage  
  – By inhibiting Drp1, Mdivi-1 should restore healthy mitochondrial dynamics in RPE under oxidative challenge, preserving bioenergetics and reducing oxidative damage pathways central to dry AMD pathogenesis.  
  – Literature precedence (Kim et al., 2014; Li et al., 2016) supports measurable rescue in ARPE-19 and in vivo RPE injury models using endpoints analogous to the proposed assay.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  • Mechanistic alignment: Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction are well-established contributors to RPE degeneration in dry AMD. Targeting excessive fission addresses a proximal driver of cell death.  
  • Preclinical efficacy: Demonstrated protection in ARPE-19 and NaIO₃ models, plus ERG and outer nuclear layer preservation in mice.  
  • Usable pharmacological tool: Well‐characterized dosing regimens and soluble in DMSO for in vitro and systemic administration in rodents.  

Weaknesses and Risks  
  • Specificity concerns: Off-target complex I inhibition could confound results and pose safety liabilities, especially under chronic dosing.  
  • Pharmacokinetics and ocular delivery: No data on ocular bioavailability or intravitreal/intraocular tolerability. Systemic dosing may not achieve therapeutic levels in RPE.  
  • Drug-like properties: Mdivi-1 has moderate potency (low µM) and limited aqueous solubility; may require structural optimization for potency, selectivity, and formulation.  
  • Translation gap: Efficacy in rodent acute injury models (NaIO₃) may not fully predict success in human dry AMD, which has a chronic, multifactorial etiology.  

Recommendation  
Mdivi-1 is a promising proof-of-concept agent for targeting mitochondrial fission in dry AMD RPE models. However, to advance toward a viable therapeutic, optimization is needed to:  
  1. Improve DRP1 selectivity and reduce off-target mitochondrial toxicity.  
  2. Enhance potency (sub-micromolar IC₅₀) and solubility for ocular administration.  
  3. Evaluate ocular pharmacokinetics and local tolerability (intravitreal or topical).  

Next steps should include medicinal chemistry campaigns around the hydrazone scaffold, in vitro selectivity profiling, and formulation development for direct RPE targeting.
