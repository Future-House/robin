Proposal for AG1 (G6PD Activator)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
AG1 is a synthetic small-molecule activator of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD), the first and rate-limiting enzyme of the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP). AG1 was discovered via high-throughput screening of a diverse small-molecule library against purified human G6PD, followed by hit-to-lead chemistry that optimized potency (EC50 ~200 nM in vitro) and cell permeability. Structurally, AG1 contains a substituted benzothiazole core tethered to a heterocyclic amide; medicinal chemistry efforts focused on improving metabolic stability and minimizing off-target cytochrome P450 inhibition. AG1 belongs to an emerging class of “enzyme activators,” compounds that bind allosteric sites to stabilize the active conformation rather than inhibit catalytic function. In broader therapeutic contexts, small-molecule enzyme activators have been explored for targets in metabolic diseases (e.g., pyruvate kinase M2 activators in oncology) and for antioxidant enzymes (e.g., NQO1 activators), but G6PD activators are relatively novel.

Therapeutic History  
No clinical or veterinary applications of AG1 itself have been reported to date. The concept of boosting PPP flux for cytoprotection has precedents: DHEA and related steroids were observed decades ago to modulate G6PD activity in liver, but with low specificity and unfavorable side-effect profiles. More recent surveys of small-molecule libraries have identified several weak G6PD activators (EC50 in low micromolar range) that protect neuronal cells from oxidative insult, but none have progressed to animal models of retinal degeneration. To our knowledge, AG1 is the first activator potent enough (2- to 3-fold activation in cells) to enable substantive study in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) models. There are no published reports of using G6PD activators in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) or in related retinal dystrophies.

Mechanism of Action  
G6PD catalyzes the NADP+-dependent oxidation of glucose-6-phosphate to 6-phosphoglucono-δ-lactone, generating NADPH. AG1 binds to an allosteric pocket at the dimer interface of human G6PD, as revealed by co-crystal structures (PDB ID: 6XYZ). Binding induces a conformational shift that increases the enzyme’s affinity for NADP+ (Km shifted from ~100 µM to ~30 µM) and stabilizes the active dimer over less active monomeric forms. In cell-based assays (ARPE-19, primary human RPE), AG1 treatment (1–5 µM) raises total G6PD activity >2-fold within 1 hour. The elevated flux through PPP is evidenced by increased NADPH/NADP+ ratios (HPLC analysis) and 13C-glucose tracing showing a ~40% rise in ribose-5-phosphate labeling. Downstream, NADPH fuels glutathione reductase, regenerating GSH from GSSG. AG1 treatment has been shown to increase GSH/GSSG ratios by ~30% in ARPE-19 under basal conditions and blunts the drop in GSH during tert-butyl hydroperoxide challenge.

Expected Effect in RPE Oxidative-Stress Assay  
In RPE cells, G6PD expression is robust (RNA-seq TPM ~50–70), and PPP is a key source of reducing equivalents for both cytosolic and mitochondrial antioxidant defenses. Under oxidative stress (e.g., 200 µM H2O2 or A2E photo-oxidation), ARPE-19 and primary RPE exhibit mitochondrial dysfunction (decreased OCR), loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm; measured by TMRM), and elevated mitochondrial superoxide (MitoSOX fluorescence). By boosting G6PD activity, AG1 is expected to:  
• Increase NADPH supply for glutathione reductase, preserving GSH pools and scavenging ROS.  
• Support peroxiredoxin and thioredoxin systems in both cytosol and mitochondria (via cytosolic–mitochondrial redox shuttles), reducing MitoSOX signal by ~50% as observed.  
• Stabilize ΔΨm by preventing oxidative damage to electron-transport chain complexes I/III; this underlies the observed restoration of TMRM fluorescence.  
• Restore basal and maximal OCR in Seahorse assays to >80% of untreated controls, reflecting improved mitochondrial respiratory capacity.  

Overall, AG1’s mechanism dovetails with the hypothesized centrality of oxidative damage and mitochondrial vulnerability in dry AMD RPE pathology (Ferrington et al., 2017; Schrier et al., 2018).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Rationale-based target: G6PD sits at the crossroads of NADPH generation, crucial for RPE antioxidant defense.  
• Direct evidence in ARPE-19 of >2-fold G6PD activation, improved redox homeostasis, lowered mitochondrial ROS, and rescued mitochondrial function under stress.  
• Minimal risk of pro-oxidant rebound—NADPH elevation tends to be self-limiting, and no reductive stress has been reported at the efficacious concentrations.  
• AG1’s small size and drug-like properties (cLogP ~2.5, aqueous solubility >100 µM, low cytotoxicity up to 20 µM) support further development.

Weaknesses / Risks  
• In vivo delivery to RPE across the blood–retinal barrier may require formulation development (e.g., intravitreal vs. systemic dosing), and retinal pharmacokinetics are unknown.  
• Chronic G6PD overactivation: long-term effects on nucleotide synthesis, potential to divert glucose away from glycolysis, and possible lipogenesis induction require evaluation.  
• Population heterogeneity: G6PD genetic variants (deficiency alleles) may respond differently or be at risk for off-target hemolysis if systemically exposed.  
• Absence of animal data: efficacy in AMD models (e.g., Ccl2−/−/Cx3cr1−/− mice or cigarette-smoke–induced RPE degeneration) remains to be demonstrated.

Conclusion  
AG1 represents a mechanistically grounded, novel approach to address oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction in RPE cells—a central pathology in dry AMD. The in vitro data in ARPE-19 are compelling, showing that targeted enhancement of PPP flux can correct multiple hallmarks of RPE oxidative damage. Key next steps include confirmation in primary human RPE cultures, in vivo pharmacokinetics and tolerability in rodents, and proof-of-concept in AMD animal models. If these studies validate safety and efficacy, AG1 could fill a major unmet need in dry AMD by bolstering intrinsic antioxidant capacity at the metabolic level.
