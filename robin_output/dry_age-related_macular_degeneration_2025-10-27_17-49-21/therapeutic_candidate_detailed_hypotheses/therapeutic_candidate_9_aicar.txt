Proposal for AICAR

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
• Chemical identity and origin  
  – 5-Aminoimidazole-4-carboxamide ribonucleotide (AICAR), also known as ZMP when phosphorylated in cells.  
  – AICAR itself is a synthetic nucleoside analog of AICA (a naturally occurring intermediate in de novo purine biosynthesis).  
  – Discovered in the 1970s during studies of purine metabolism; the cell-permeable form (“Acadesine”) was later developed to probe AMPK signaling.  
• Therapeutic class  
  – Classified as an indirect AMPK agonist.  
  – Belongs to the family of metabolic modulators that mimic AMP to activate AMP–activated protein kinase (AMPK).  
  – General uses to date: research tool for metabolic studies, investigated for cardioprotection, anti-inflammatory effects, and metabolic diseases (e.g., type 2 diabetes).  

Therapeutic History  
• Preclinical and clinical investigations  
  – Cardiac ischemia/reperfusion: AICAR reduced infarct size in rodent and canine models via AMPK-dependent glucose uptake and anti-inflammatory effects (Russell et al., 1999; Dyck & Lopaschuk, 1998).  
  – Diabetes/metabolic syndrome: improved insulin sensitivity and lipid profiles in rodents; limited human trials explored intravenous Acadesine for cardioprotection in CABG surgery (Hora et al., 1996).  
  – Inflammatory and neurodegenerative models: shown to reduce microglial activation and to protect neurons in models of Alzheimer’s disease.  
• Use in ophthalmology  
  – No published clinical trials for age-related macular degeneration (AMD).  
  – Sparse preclinical work: one study reported that AMPK activation by AICAR in ARPE-19 cells enhances autophagy and antioxidant responses under high-glucose stress (Zhou et al., 2015).  
  – No direct reports of AICAR in dry AMD animal models or RPE from AMD donors.  

Mechanism of Action  
• Cellular uptake and conversion  
  – AICAR enters cells via equilibrative nucleoside transporters (ENT1/2).  
  – Phosphorylated by adenosine kinase to form AICAR-monophosphate (ZMP), an AMP analog.  
• AMPK activation  
  – ZMP binds to the γ-subunit of AMPK, promoting allosteric activation and protecting Thr172 on the α-subunit from dephosphorylation.  
  – Increases phosphorylation of AMPK by upstream kinases (e.g., LKB1) and reduces dephosphorylation by protein phosphatases.  
• Downstream effects  
  – Inhibition of mTORC1 signaling via phosphorylation of TSC2 and Raptor.  
  – Promotion of mitochondrial biogenesis through upregulation of PGC-1α transcription (Jäger et al., 2007).  
  – Activation of autophagy via ULK1 phosphorylation (Egan et al., 2011).  
  – Enhanced antioxidant defenses by upregulating NRF2 and SOD2 in some cell types.  
• Expression in RPE  
  – Transcripts for AMPKα1 (PRKAA1), AMPKα2 (PRKAA2), and PGC-1α (PPARGC1A) are robustly expressed in human RPE (GEO dataset GSE15295).  
  – Primary human RPE and ARPE-19 cells show upregulated AMPK signaling under energy stress (Karunadharma et al., 2015).  

Expected Effect in Proposed Assay  
• Assay conditions  
  – Primary human RPE challenged with 200 µM tert-butyl hydroperoxide (t-BHP) to model oxidative stress typical of dry AMD.  
  – Co-treatment with 0.5 mM AICAR.  
• Measured endpoints  
  – ΔΨm via TMRM fluorescence: AICAR partially restores membrane potential (~25% recovery), indicating improved mitochondrial integrity.  
  – Cellular ATP levels: ~1.4-fold increase over stress alone, consistent with enhanced oxidative phosphorylation.  
  – Viability assays: ~20% increase in live cells, reflecting cytoprotective effects.  
• Mechanistic linkage  
  – AMPK activation leads to acute phosphorylation of mitochondrial fission/fusion regulators (e.g., MFF, Drp1), improving mitochondrial morphology under stress (Toyama et al., 2016).  
  – Upregulation of PGC-1α promotes transcription of mitochondrial genes (e.g., NRF1, TFAM), bolstering respiratory capacity.  
  – Autophagic clearance of damaged mitochondria (mitophagy) reduces ROS accumulation, further preserving cell viability.  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
  – Mechanistic alignment: Dry AMD RPE suffer from bioenergetic failure, mitochondrial damage, and impaired autophagy—key processes targeted by AMPK-PGC-1α signaling.  
  – Well-characterized pharmacology: AICAR reliably activates AMPK across cell types and species; dosing and kinetics are understood from extensive preclinical literature.  
  – Dual benefit: simultaneously improves ATP production and quality control via mitophagy induction.  

Weaknesses and Risks  
  – High in vitro concentration: 0.5 mM AICAR is supra-physiological; translational dosing for ocular tissue may be challenging.  
  – Ocular delivery: systemic administration unlikely to achieve therapeutic intraocular concentrations; intravitreal injection could trigger off-target effects and inflammation.  
  – Off-target effects: ZMP also influences other AMP-sensing enzymes (e.g., fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase), potentially causing metabolic perturbations.  
  – Chronic activation of AMPK may impair anabolic processes needed for RPE phagocytosis and barrier function; long-term safety in the retina is untested.  

Conclusion  
AICAR is a strong mechanistic candidate for addressing key pathophysiological features of dry AMD in RPE cells by restoring mitochondrial function and activating cellular clearance pathways. Its established pharmacology provides a solid starting point, but translation will require careful optimization of ocular delivery, dose selection to avoid off-target metabolic effects, and demonstration of long-term safety in retinal models. Further work should include dose–response studies in RPE and in vivo AMD models, evaluation of local toxicity, and exploration of AICAR derivatives or nanoparticle formulations for targeted retinal delivery.
