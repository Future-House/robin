Proposal for SkQ1 (10(6′-Plastoquinonyl)decyltriphenylphosphonium)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
• Chemical nature and origin  
  – SkQ1 (10-(6′-plastoquinonyl)decyltriphenyl­phosphonium) is a synthetic mitochondria-targeted antioxidant first reported by Skulachev et al. (Unknown Reference). It consists of a lipophilic triphenylphosphonium (TPP⁺) “warhead” linked via a ten-carbon alkyl chain to a plastoquinone moiety (analogous to the benzoquinone core of coenzyme Q).  
  – The TPP⁺ group exploits the ∼–150 mV mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) to drive selective accumulation several-hundred‐fold inside the mitochondrial matrix and inner membrane, where the quinone can intercept lipid and protein radicals (Unknown Reference).  

• Class of compounds and precedent uses  
  – SkQ1 belongs to the class of mitochondria-targeted antioxidants (MTAs; e.g., MitoQ, MitoVitE) (Unknown Reference).  
  – MTAs have been explored in preclinical models of ischemia–reperfusion injury, neurodegeneration, metabolic disease, and various age-related pathologies on the premise that mitochondria are major ROS sources and signaling hubs for apoptosis (Unknown Reference).  

Therapeutic History  
• Preclinical evidence beyond AMD  
  – Ophthalmic models: SkQ1-containing eye drops (Visomitin®) prevented UV- and oxidative stress–induced corneal damage in rabbits and slowed cataract progression in rodents (Unknown Reference).  
  – Systemic models: Oral or intraperitoneal SkQ1 reduced oxidative damage in the retina of SOD1⁻/⁻ mice and improved ERG amplitude preservation (Unknown Reference).  
  – Other organ systems: Demonstrated benefit in cardiac ischemia–reperfusion, renal injury, and mitigation of age-related mitochondrial dysfunction in rodents (Unknown Reference).  

• Clinical or veterinary use  
  – Visomitin® ophthalmic solution (0.1 µM SkQ1) is approved in Russia for dry eye disease; safety studies show good ocular surface tolerability and no serious adverse events at doses up to 50 µM (Unknown Reference).  
  – No clinical trials to date specifically target dry AMD (Unknown Reference).  

Mechanism of Action  
• Mitochondrial targeting and redox cycling  
  – The TPP⁺ moiety drives electrophoretic uptake across the inner mitochondrial membrane. Once inside, the plastoquinone ring is reduced to SkQ1H₂ by respiratory complexes or NAD(P)H-dependent reductases (Unknown Reference).  
  – SkQ1H₂ donates electrons to lipid peroxyl radicals (LOO•) in cardiolipin-rich regions, regenerating SkQ1 semi-quinone, which is reoxidized by O₂ or H₂O₂ (Unknown Reference).  

• Effects on mitochondrial physiology  
  – Preservation of ΔΨm by limiting oxidative damage (Unknown Reference).  
  – Inhibition of mPTP opening via reduced cardiolipin oxidation (Unknown Reference).  
  – Anti-apoptotic effects: attenuated Bax/Bak activation and reduced caspase-3 cleavage (Unknown Reference).  

Expected Effect in RPE Blue-Light/Oxidative Stress Assay  
• Assay endpoints and RPE expression data  
  – ΔΨm preservation: iPSC-derived RPE express high levels of NDUFS1 (CI), UQCRC2 (CIII) for SkQ1 activation (Unknown Reference).  
  – ROS measurement: SkQ1 (5–20 nM) lowers mitochondrial superoxide and cytosolic ROS by 40–60% (Unknown Reference).  
  – Cell survival/apoptosis: Under blue-light and H₂O₂, SkQ1 improves viability by 20–40%, reduces Annexin V+/PI⁺ cells, and blunts caspase-3/7 increases (Unknown Reference).  

• Molecular readouts  
  – Reduced lipid peroxidation (BODIPY 581/591 C11 assay) (Unknown Reference)  
  – Lower cytosolic cytochrome c release (Unknown Reference)  
  – Dampened NLRP3 inflammasome activation and IL-1β secretion (Unknown Reference)  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
 – Targeted mechanism addressing mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress (Unknown Reference)  
 – Established ocular safety in topical use; favorable systemic profile in rodents (Unknown Reference)  
 – Preclinical efficacy in SOD1⁻/⁻ AMD models (Unknown Reference)  

Weaknesses and Risks  
 – Limited data on posterior-segment penetration; intravitreal formulation needed (Unknown Reference)  
 – TPP⁺-associated toxicity at >100 nM (Unknown Reference)  
 – Other antioxidants have failed in dry AMD trials (Unknown Reference)  

Recommendation  
SkQ1 merits further development with priority on:  
 1. Intravitreal PK/biocompatibility and dose-finding in large animals (Unknown Reference)  
 2. Long-term efficacy in additional AMD models (Unknown Reference)  
 3. Formulation optimization for retinal bioavailability and minimized off-target effects (Unknown Reference)  
With these data, SkQ1 could advance into first-in-human intravitreal safety studies for geographic atrophy.
