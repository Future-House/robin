Assay Overview  
•  Assay concept  
   –  Models iron‐dependent, lipid peroxidation–driven cell death (ferroptosis) in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), a process increasingly implicated in dry AMD–associated RPE atrophy.  
   –  Ferroptosis is triggered in vitro by small molecules such as erastin (inhibits system xₙ⁻ cystine/glutamate antiporter) or RSL3 (direct GPX4 inhibitor).  
•  Readouts  
   –  Cell viability: e.g. CellTiter-Glo, MTT, or LDH release assays.  
   –  Lipid peroxidation: BODIPY 581/591-C11 fluorescence shift or malondialdehyde (MDA)/4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE) ELISA.  
   –  GPX4 activity or protein level: enzymatic coupled assay (NADPH consumption) and Western blot or immunofluorescence.  
   –  Intracellular iron: fluorescence probes (FerroOrange) or colorimetric iron assays.  
•  Cell types  
   –  ARPE-19 immortalized human RPE or primary human donor RPE (including iPSC-derived RPE), cultured as confluent, polarized monolayers.  

Biomedical Evidence  
1. RPE Iron Dysregulation in AMD  
   –  Post-mortem AMD eyes accumulate iron in RPE and Bruch’s membrane (Smith et al. 2001, Hahn et al. 2003). Excess labile iron catalyzes Fenton chemistry, propagating lipid ROS.  
2. Lipid Peroxidation in Dry AMD  
   –  Elevated markers of lipid peroxidation (4-HNE, MDA) have been detected in AMD donor retinas (Cai et al. 2015). Lipid peroxidation products contribute to drusen formation and RPE dysfunction.  
3. GPX4 and Cell Death Pathways  
   –  GPX4 is the major defense against phospholipid hydroperoxides. Reduced GPX4 expression/activity is reported in oxidative‐stressed RPE and in AMD models (Bhutto et al. 2010).  
   –  Skouta et al. (Nat Chem Biol 2014) first showed erastin/RSL3 induce RPE death that is prevented by canonical ferroptosis inhibitors (ferrostatin-1, liproxstatin-1).  
4. Links to Clinical Phenotype  
   –  Dry AMD pathogenesis centers on progressive RPE atrophy and outer-retinal degeneration. Ferroptotic RPE loss could underlie geographic atrophy progression.  
   –  Small clinical studies of systemic iron chelators (deferoxamine) hint at protection of RPE/choriocapillaris but are limited by toxicity.  

Previous Use  
•  Discovery of ferroptosis as an RPE death mode  
   –  Skouta et al. used ARPE-19 plus erastin/RSL3 to define ferroptosis in RPE, rescuable by lipophilic antioxidants.  
•  Mechanistic dissection  
   –  Subsequent studies (e.g. Xie et al. 2016; Li et al. 2018) have used the same assay to show that NRF2 activation (upregulating SLC7A11 and GPX4) or iron chelation protects RPE.  
•  Screening for novel inhibitors  
   –  Liproxstatin-1 and other ferroptosis blockers were initially characterized in this RPE assay before broader applications in neurodegeneration and ischemia–reperfusion injury.  
•  Adaptations  
   –  Some groups have combined erastin treatment with photo-oxidation of A2E (lipofuscin component) under blue light, more closely mimicking AMD‐relevant oxidative stress (Song et al. 2019).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
•  Disease relevance: directly addresses a death pathway (ferroptosis) supported by post-mortem and animal studies as contributing to RPE loss in dry AMD.  
•  Mechanistic clarity: multiple orthogonal readouts (viability, lipid peroxidation, GPX4 activity) enable mode‐of‐action studies.  
•  Throughput: compatible with 96- or 384-well formats for moderate‐throughput screening.  
•  Flexibility: assay conditions can be tuned (erastin vs RSL3, addition of A2E, blue-light exposure) to increase physiological relevance.  

Weaknesses  
•  Non-physiological inducers: erastin/RSL3 are useful probes but do not fully recapitulate in vivo RPE stressors (e.g. drusen‐derived complement activation, chronic low-grade oxidative stress).  
•  Cell model limitations: ARPE-19 differ in metabolism, polarity, and antioxidant defenses from primary RPE; results require validation in primary or iPSC-derived RPE and in vivo models.  
•  Single-pathway focus: dry AMD is multifactorial (inflammation, complement, lipid transport, autophagy); targeting only ferroptosis may not halt disease progression.  
•  False positives/negatives: lipophilic antioxidants can interfere with fluorescent lipid ROS probes; iron chelators may alter probe chemistry.  

Conclusion  
A ferroptosis inhibition assay in RPE provides a well‐validated, mechanism‐driven platform for identifying compounds that protect against an iron‐ and lipid peroxidation–mediated form of cell death implicated in dry AMD. It is best viewed as one component of a tiered in vitro strategy—initial screening via ferroptosis rescue, followed by secondary assays that incorporate chronic oxidative stressors (e.g. A2E, complement) and validation in primary/iPSC‐derived RPE and small animal geographic atrophy models.
