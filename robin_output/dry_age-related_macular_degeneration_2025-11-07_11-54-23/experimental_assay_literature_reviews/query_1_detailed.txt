Query: Detailed characterization of complement activation pathways in dry age-related macular degeneration RPE cultures: focus on C3a/C5a generation, complement regulatory protein expression, and ELISA and flow cytometry assays for complement deposition quantification

Complement effector generation and signaling. In iPSC‑derived RPE (iRPE), complement‑competent human serum (CC‑HS) robustly increases anaphylatoxins on both sides of the monolayer by ELISA (e.g., apical C3a ~7× in CFH(Y/Y); apical C5a ~3×), and induces strong downstream signaling (pAKT ~7×; pERK 2–3×), consistent with overt C3aR1/C5aR1 activation and a cell‑autonomous alternative‑pathway source of C3a/C5a (apical > basal) (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 4-5, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 6-7). C3aR1 and C5aR1 are expressed and enriched apically (RNA‑seq, WB, immunostaining), and apical‑only CC‑HS exposure suffices to produce a 4–5× TER drop; CC‑HS also yields MAC (C5b‑9) within APOE+ sub‑RPE deposits (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 6-7, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 3-4).

Complement regulation. iRPE express multiple regulators (CD46, CD55, CFD, CFH, CFI); risk/aging conditions reduce these inhibitors and decrease apical CFH secretion, weakening local control (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 12-13). CC‑HS drives a >200× increase in basal CFH secretion with no apical change, explaining polarized sensitivity to complement (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 6-7, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 4-5). Depletion/blockade at C3/C5 or C3aR1/C5aR1 reduces sub‑RPE APOE accumulation, functionally linking anaphylatoxins to pathology (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 6-7).

Assays and quantification. ELISA was used to quantify apical/basal CFH, C3a and C5a, with biological replicates and multiple‑comparison t‑tests, supporting a cell‑autonomous AP activation model (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 4-5, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 12-13). Complement deposition in RPE cultures was quantified primarily by imaging: APOE deposits co‑stain for C5b‑9; lipid/protein deposits were quantified (BODIPY, Nile red, APOE) and validated by TEM/SEM (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 3-4). High‑risk CFH(Y402H) RPE show increased C3b and C5b‑9 (volume/cell) and lysosomal internalization; C3 inhibition (Cp40) reduces both, confirming C3‑dependence (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 7-13, cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2). Flow‑cytometric quantification of complement deposition on RPE is not reported in these passages; flow cytometry was used to quantify C3/C4/C5 activation on photoreceptor outer segments with pathway attribution by selective serum depletion (not RPE) (katschke2018classicalandalternative pages 4-4). Several excerpts explicitly lack ELISA/flow‑cytometry protocols for deposited fragments in RPE cultures (behnke2022ifnβimmunomodulationand pages 31-33, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 6-7).

Dry AMD iRPE activate the alternative pathway and generate C3a/C5a, with apically enriched C3aR1/C5aR1 signaling that drives TER loss and sub‑RPE MAC‑positive deposits; these effects are attenuated by C3/C5 depletion or C3aR1/C5aR1 blockade (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 4-5, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 6-7, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 3-4). Regulators (CD46, CD55, CFH, CFI) are diminished under disease conditions; CC‑HS elicits polarized CFH secretion (basal >> apical) (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 12-13, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 4-5). ELISA provides apical/basal CFH/C3a/C5a quantification, while complement deposition in RPE is quantified by imaging; flow cytometry for deposition is not reported for RPE in these excerpts (sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 4-5, sharma2021epithelialphenotyperestoring pages 3-4, behnke2022ifnβimmunomodulationand pages 31-33).
