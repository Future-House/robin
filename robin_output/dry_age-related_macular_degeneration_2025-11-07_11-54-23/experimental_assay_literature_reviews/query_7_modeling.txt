Query: Modeling mitochondrial dysfunction in dry AMD RPE cells: approaches for assessing mitochondrial DNA damage, mitophagy rates, and biogenesis using qPCR for mtDNA copy number, Parkin translocation assays, and mitotracker-based flow cytometry

To model mitochondrial dysfunction in RPE relevant to dry AMD, perturb mitochondria with CCCP, antimycin A, or NaIO3 and pair structural readouts (TEM) and autophagy markers with functional flux controls to avoid misinterpreting steady-state accumulations (bafilomycin A1 or leupeptin) (datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 2-4, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 11-12, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 9-11). mtDNA damage is a primary lesion in AMD RPE; quantify mtDNA copy number as a biogenesis proxy and assess integrity using qPCR-based assays sensitive to ROS-induced lesions, which are established in ocular studies (zhang2023oxidativestressinvolvedmitophagy pages 6-7, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 12-13, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 2-3). Because biogenesis is regulated by PGC-1α/AMPK, couple mtDNA qPCR with bioenergetic endpoints (e.g., OCR/ATP) to interpret compensatory versus failing biogenesis in diseased RPE models (bhattacharya2022modelingofmitochondrial pages 1-2).

For Parkin translocation, deploy complementary imaging and biochemistry: show PRKN enrichment in the mitochondrial fraction with concurrent loss from cytosol and detect PINK1-dependent p-PRKN-Ser65 by immunoblot after depolarization; validate pathway dependence with PINK1 knockdown (datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 14-15, datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 2-4). In situ, quantify PRKN/PINK1 colocalization with mitochondrial markers and verify recruitment ultrastructurally by immunogold TEM in RPE tissue; increased pS65-PRKN and PRKN–mitochondria colocalization indicate engagement of the PINK1–PRKN axis, but flux must be demonstrated separately (gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 3-7, gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 10-12, gurubaran2020mitophagyinthe pages 12-14).

For Mitotracker-based flow cytometry, quantify mitochondrial content/potential as a surrogate for mass and health, include depolarization controls, and interpret shifts alongside autophagy flux modulation; orthogonally validate mitophagy with the pH-sensitive mCherry–eGFP–COX8A reporter (datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 2-4, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 4-5). In RPE, mitigate lipofuscin/autofluorescence that can distort Mitotracker signals (e.g., Sudan black B or spectral strategies), and corroborate with membrane potential dyes (e.g., TMRM) and lysosomal markers/flux to avoid false inferences about mitochondrial quantity (skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 11-12, datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 14-15, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 4-5).

In dry AMD RPE models, use mtDNA qPCR to track biogenesis and damage, Parkin translocation assays to read out early mitophagy activation, and Mitotracker-based cytometry to quantify mitochondrial mass/health under rigorously controlled flux conditions. Combine these with flux inhibitors and orthogonal reporters to distinguish activation from blockade of late mitophagy in lipofuscin-rich RPE.
