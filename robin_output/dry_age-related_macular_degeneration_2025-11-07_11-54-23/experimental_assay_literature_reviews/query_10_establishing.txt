Query: Establishing co-culture models of RPE and choroidal endothelial cells to study sub-RPE deposit formation: development of 3D culture systems, drusen-like material accumulation assays, and imaging mass spectrometry to analyze extracellular matrix remodeling

Rationale and architecture. The RPE–Bruch’s membrane–choriocapillaris form a single functional unit in which early, prognostic pathology manifests as sub‑RPE lipid/protein deposition; thus, co‑cultures that recreate RPE–choroidal endothelial interplay are necessary to study drusen biogenesis and ECM remodeling, a need explicitly noted for RPE+choriocapillary endothelial co‑culture in Transwell‑based deposit models (ghareeb2020coculturetechniquesfor pages 2-5, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 7-9).

3D co‑culture system design. Practical platforms include: (i) 3D collagen I gels where choroidal endothelial cells form choriocapillaris‑like tubes with RPE layered on top; (ii) trilayer HUVEC–amnion–RPE constructs; and (iii) electrospun BrM mimetics (e.g., PCL–gelatin) seeded with hRPE and RF‑6A on opposite faces (∼10 μm pores; ∼100 μm thickness, thicker than native BrM), all complemented by Transwell noncontact co‑cultures and organ‑on‑chip devices seeding ARPE‑19 and HUVECs across thin porous PDMS membranes under perfusion and controlled mechanics; standard readouts include TEER, dextran permeability, and junctional/vascular marker immunostaining (ghareeb2020coculturetechniquesfor pages 9-10, wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 6-7, wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 7-9, nicholls2024developmentofan pages 34-38).

Drusen‑like accumulation assays. Insert material/thickness/permeability govern retention: thin (10 μm) polyester Transwells promote diffuse ApoE‑positive surface deposits, whereas thicker (100 μm) membranes trap punctate ApoE in cavities; long‑term culture (12–26+ weeks) yields BLinD, basal mounds, and early hard‑drusen morphologies (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 7-9). Longitudinal Nile red vital imaging with semi‑automated blob analysis quantifies deposit number/area and size distributions, while acknowledging lipid selectivity and re‑staining constraints; ECM remodeling is tracked by basal collagen V “rafts,” and barrier status by TEER (shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 2-3, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 6-7, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 3-6, nicholls2024developmentofan pages 34-38). Compositionally, immunostaining shows APOE‑rich deposits that selectively accrue vitronectin/clusterin/APCS from serum and engage complement; C5b‑9 is deposited via an antibody‑independent classical pathway (C1q‑dependent), with EM revealing membrane‑bounded multivesicular and non‑membrane‑bounded particle conglomerates (forest2015developmentofcell pages 36-41, forest2015developmentofcell pages 41-46).

Imaging mass spectrometry and metalomics. Secondary ion mass spectrometry (SIMS) provides spatial ion maps of Ca+, Ca2PO4+ (HAP), phosphatidylcholine headgroups, and amino‑acid fragments, co‑localizing mineral, lipid, and protein within deposits; synchrotron μXRF maps Ca/Fe/Zn and μXRD/radial diffraction confirms hydroxyapatite, enabling multi‑scale mineral speciation (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 7-9, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 1-2). These systems allow controlled perturbation of pH, Ca2+/phosphate, and trace metals to interrogate ECM remodeling; metal‑dependent mechanisms, including zinc’s roles in deposit metal correlations and complement interactions, can be probed within this framework (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 7-9, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 10-11).

Complementary 3D RPE organoids and spheroids, maintained long‑term, develop drusen‑like APOE/TIMP3 clusters, calcification, and BrM‑like ultrastructure, providing human 3D contexts to validate ECM remodeling endpoints alongside endothelial co‑cultures (mesch2021agedhumanipscrpe pages 1-4, mesch2021…foragerelateda pages 74-77).

A robust strategy is to build RPE–choroidal endothelial co‑cultures on thin, BrM‑like porous supports or microfluidic chips, quantify drusen‑like material longitudinally with lipid dyes and deposit‑specific immunostaining plus complement assays, and resolve composition/phase by SIMS with μXRF/μXRD. Insert properties, ionic milieu, and trace metals should be systematically tuned to test barrier‑driven retention and mineralization mechanisms of sub‑RPE deposition (wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 6-7, wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 7-9, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 2-3, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 7-9, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 1-2, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 10-11).
