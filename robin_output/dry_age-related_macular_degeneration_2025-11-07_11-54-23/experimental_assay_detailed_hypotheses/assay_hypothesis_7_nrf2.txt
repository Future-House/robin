Biomedical Evidence: NRF2 is the master redox transcription factor in RPE; its activation restores antioxidant capacity, while age-related decline or genetic loss causes RPE degeneration and AMD-like phenotypes, establishing causal relevance. In vivo, Nrf2−/− mice (and Nrf2/PGC-1α dKO) develop sub-RPE deposits, mitochondrial damage, autophagy/proteostasis defects, and vision loss; conversely, NRF2 overexpression or peptide delivery protects RPE and preserves function. Clinically and ex vivo, AMD is characterized by elevated oxidative damage, reduced antioxidant status, and benefits from antioxidant supplementation (AREDS), supporting oxidative stress as a central driver and NRF2 as a therapeutic axis. NRF2 also transcriptionally regulates many autophagy genes, interacts with mitochondrial quality control, and integrates energy sensing (AMPK), linking the reporter to proteostasis and mitochondrial nodes central to dry AMD pathogenesis.
