Assay Overview:  
This assay utilizes a ratiometric LysoSensor dye to measure lysosomal pH and BODIPY‑pepstatin A to quantify active cathepsin D in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells. It models a key aspect of dry AMD pathogenesis—the impairment of lysosomal acidification and subsequent failure of protease activation that leads to lipofuscin accumulation and compromised photoreceptor outer segment degradation. Measurements are taken by live cell fluorescence imaging and quantitative microscopy in biologically relevant systems such as iPSC‑derived RPE or ARPE‑19 cells (li2025sustainedlysosomaldelivery pages 17-20, li2025sustainedlysosomaldelivery pages 25-28).  

Biomedical Evidence:  
Lysosomal dysregulation is central to dry AMD, where disturbed acidification impairs lysosomal enzyme function and autophagic clearance, leading to RPE dysfunction and drusen formation (lynn2021oligomericaβ142induces pages 1-2, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 2-3). Cathepsin D activity, dependent on an acidic lysosomal environment, is crucial for maintaining RPE homeostasis, and its malfunction has been directly linked to disease pathology (li2025sustainedlysosomaldelivery pages 1-6, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7).  

Previous Use:  
Similar approaches have been demonstrated in studies where nanoparticle-mediated reacidification restored lysosomal function and cathepsin D activity in RPE models (li2025sustainedlysosomaldelivery pages 1-6, li2025sustainedlysosomaldelivery pages 13-17). The combination of pH and protease activity probes has provided mechanistic insights into lysosomal competence and its perturbation by oxidative stress or Aβ accumulation (li2025sustainedlysosomaldelivery pages 17-20).  

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay is strong in its mechanistic relevance and offers quantifiable, physiologically pertinent readouts in disease-relevant RPE systems. Potential weaknesses include probe sensitivity to experimental conditions and cell-to-cell heterogeneity in dye uptake, which must be rigorously controlled (li2025sustainedlysosomaldelivery pages 13-17, li2025sustainedlysosomaldelivery pages 17-20).  

Lysosomal pH and cathepsin D activity assays effectively model a central pathogenic mechanism in dry AMD. They provide a robust platform for early-stage therapeutic testing when used in validated RPE culture systems.
