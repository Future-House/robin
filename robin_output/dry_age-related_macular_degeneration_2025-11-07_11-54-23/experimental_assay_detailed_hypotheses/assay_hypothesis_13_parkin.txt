Assay Overview:  
The Parkin translocation mitophagy assay is designed to monitor the PINK1-dependent recruitment of Parkin to depolarized mitochondria in RPE cells, typically using immunofluorescence imaging and subcellular fractionation. In this assay, cells such as ARPE‑19 or human iPSC-derived RPE treated with mitochondrial stressors (e.g., CCCP or FCCP) are evaluated for Parkin relocation from the cytosol to the mitochondria, along with related readouts such as p-Parkin (S65) levels, MAP1LC3B lipidation, and SQSTM1 accumulation. These measurements help assess early mitophagy initiation events relevant to RPE mitochondrial quality control. (datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 2-4, skeie2021mitophagyanemerging pages 11-12)

Biomedical Evidence:  
Mitochondrial dysfunction and resultant oxidative stress are central to the pathogenesis of dry AMD, where impaired mitophagy is linked to RPE degeneration and an epithelial–mesenchymal transition phenotype. Studies have demonstrated reduced basal levels of PINK1 and disordered mitophagic flux in AMD RPE, supporting the hypothesis that enhancing mitochondrial clearance could improve RPE health. These findings have substantial backing from clinical observations and experimental models indicating that PINK1/Parkin pathway deficits contribute to RPE deterioration in early AMD. (datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 9-12, datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 1-2)

Previous Use:  
This assay has been applied in vitro using ARPE‑19 and primary RPE models to link PINK1 deficiency with impaired mitophagy and downstream signaling changes leading to EMT. Notably, modulation of this pathway (e.g., via antioxidants or genetic manipulations) has been shown to reverse pathological phenotypes in experimental systems. (datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 2-4, datta2023mitophagyinitiatesretrograde pages 8-9)

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths of the assay include its mechanistic relevance, reproducibility in established RPE models, and its ability to identify compounds that enhance early mitophagic events. However, a key weakness is that Parkin translocation assays capture only the initiation phase of mitophagy and may miss later flux or lysosomal clearance defects, which are also important in dry AMD pathology. Complementary assays measuring autophagic flux and mitochondrial function are thus recommended to fully assess therapeutic impact.  

Enhancing mitochondrial clearance by restoring PINK1/Parkin-mediated mitophagy is promising for preserving RPE health. Testing compounds with this assay, alongside flux and functional endpoints, will provide a robust evaluation strategy for dry AMD therapies.
