Assay Overview: A long-term 3D co-culture of human RPE with choroidal endothelial cells on thin porous PET/polyester supports or microfluidic chips that function as a Bruch’s membrane analogue. The assay models sub-RPE drusen biogenesis driven by RPE secretion and basolateral entrapment under an outflow barrier, with endothelial participation in barrier/permeability and extrinsic factor exchange. Primary readouts: (1) sub-RPE deposit burden and composition by immunostaining (APOE, vitronectin, complement) and lipid stains; (2) mineralization by HAP-specific probes and high-resolution SIMS mapping of Ca+, Ca2PO4+, PC lipids, and amino acids; (3) mineral identity/trace metals by µXRF/µXRD; (4) ECM remodeling (COL4/TIMP3) and ultrastructure (EM); (5) barrier integrity (ZO-1 localization, TEER, endothelial permeability) and soluble factors (e.g., VEGF-A). Culture is maintained 12–26+ weeks; substrate choice and perfusion are variable to tune deposit morphology and physiology (e.g., microfluidic flow) (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 7-9, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 6-7, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 2-3, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 3-4, alrashed2016theroleof pages 127-131, galloway2017druseninpatientderived pages 4-5, wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 4-6).

Biomedical Evidence: Drusen—lipid/protein/ECM- and mineral-rich accumulations between RPE and BrM—are the defining substrate of dry AMD and central to progression risk, making their formation and composition a core therapeutic target (APOE, TIMP3, complement; HAP calcification emerges early) (mesch2021towardsanin pages 24-27, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 1-2). RPE is an active source of drusen material; an outflow barrier in BrM promotes entrapment, with local lipids/Ca/PO4 nucleating HAP that binds proteins, mirroring early AMD histopathology (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 7-9, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 10-11). Complement dysregulation and ECM remodeling are integral to drusen biology and are measurable in culture and human tissue (shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 13-14, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 4-5, galloway2017druseninpatientderived pages 4-5).

Previous Use: Primary RPE and porcine “drusen-in-a-dish” systems generated ApoE+ lipid- and HAP-containing sub-RPE deposits confirmed by SIMS and synchrotron XRF/XRD, establishing mechanistic links between lipid retention, mineral nucleation, and protein accretion (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 6-7, pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 1-2). Patient hiPSC-RPE autonomously formed APOE/TIMP3+ deposits with COL4 remodeling and RPE-intrinsic complement upregulation; serum altered composition (complement/VTN) without increasing deposit number, supporting intrinsic vs extrinsic contributions and assay manipulability (galloway2017druseninpatientderived pages 1-1, galloway2017druseninpatientderived pages 7-7, galloway2017druseninpatientderived pages 4-5). These platforms have been positioned for therapeutic screening with quantitative image pipelines and media manipulations to modulate deposition (shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 14-15, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 1-2, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 3-6).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: disease-proximal, multi-omic, orthogonal readouts (APOE, lipids, HAP by SIMS/XRD/XRF, complement, ECM, EM) tied to causal mechanisms; captures RPE-autonomous initiation plus endothelial/barrier effects; tunable extracellular milieu (Ca2+/phosphate, serum) and perfusion enable mechanism testing and target engagement (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 7-9, galloway2017druseninpatientderived pages 1-1, wu2022tissueengineeringin pages 4-6). Limitations: long culture times and heterogeneity; some lesion types absent (e.g., BLamD, subretinal deposits); APOE signal can be variable in certain porcine setups; mineralization not universal in all RPE lines; immune components are missing unless added; organoid autofluorescence can confound fluorescence readouts (mitigated by SIMS/EM) (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 7-9, shaw2024characterizationofdrusen pages 3-6, malek2018modelsofretinal pages 7-9, mesch2021towardsanin pages 74-77). Net, this assay is well justified and suitable for mechanism-focused and lead-optimization studies of anti-drusen/anti-calcification strategies, with clear, quantitative, and translatable endpoints (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 6-7, shaw2025porcinesubretinalpigment pages 1-2).

A 3D RPE–endothelial co-culture that measures APOE-positive, HAP-containing sub-RPE deposits via SIMS and complementary modalities directly targets core dry AMD pathobiology. It integrates RPE-intrinsic drusen biogenesis with barrier/endothelial influences and supports multi-parameter, mechanistically anchored drug evaluation. Time and complexity are the main trade-offs, but the biological fidelity and orthogonal readouts justify its use.
