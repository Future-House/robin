Overall Evaluation: Strengths and weaknesses of this assay for testing therapeutics for dry age-related macular degeneration.

Assay Overview: The assay quantifies intracellular neutral lipid droplets (Nile red) and unesterified cholesterol (filipin) in human iPSC-derived RPE (iRPE) engineered or selected for the CFH Y402H risk genotype to model cell-autonomous lipid handling defects linked to complement biology in dry AMD. Imaging is performed by fluorescence microscopy/high-content screening; Nile red–positive droplets are segmented and quantified per area/cell, and filipin signal reports free cholesterol burden; analysis can be standardized using published image-analysis thresholds for iRPE Nile red assays and complemented with cell counts and normalization to protein or nuclei (DAPI) (tsai2021impairedcholesterolefflux pages 2-3, saadane2016retinalhypercholesterolemiatriggers pages 33-34). CFH(Y402H) iRPE are chosen because CFH binds ApoB/E lipoproteins and the Y402H allele is a major AMD risk factor tied to lipoprotein interactions and lipid persistence at Bruch’s membrane; RPE lipid handling is cell-autonomous and amenable to in vitro modeling (landowski2022targetinglipidmetabolism pages 10-12).

Biomedical Evidence: Lipid-rich drusen are a hallmark of AMD; local retinal cholesterol metabolism and lipid trafficking genes (ABCA1/ABCG1, LIPC, CETP) are genetically and biochemically implicated in disease, and drusen/subretinal deposits are enriched in cholesterol species (free and esterified) (lin2022dyslipidemiainagerelated pages 1-2, lin2022dyslipidemiainagerelated pages 2-3). RPE synthesizes/secretes lipoproteins and accumulates lipid with age; CFH-Y402H mice on cholesterol-rich diets develop AMD-relevant dysfunction, supporting the disease relevance of cholesterol trafficking in complement-risk backgrounds (landowski2022targetinglipidmetabolism pages 10-12). Cholesterol burden drives senescence and dysfunction: ABCA1/g1 deficiency increases RPE lipid droplets and Bruch’s membrane thickness in vivo; senescent-cell clearance reduces RPE lipid and improves ERG, and exogenous cholesterol induces p16/p21, linking cholesterol to retinal degeneration biology (terao2024cholesterolaccumulationpromotes pages 6-9, terao2024cholesterolaccumulationpromotes pages 9-11, terao2024cholesterolaccumulationpromotes pages 3-4). Filipin imaging robustly detects unesterified cholesterol in retina, with ACAT1/LCAT localization demonstrating cholesterol esterification machinery in situ (saadane2016retinalhypercholesterolemiatriggers pages 23-25, saadane2016retinalhypercholesterolemiatriggers pages 33-34).

Previous Use: Nile red droplet quantification has been implemented in human iRPE to model drusen-related lipid accumulation with defined imaging parameters, establishing feasibility of neutral-lipid imaging in iRPE (though not in CFH Y402H) (tsai2021impairedcholesterolefflux pages 2-3). In RPE models, interventions that enhance efflux or remodel intracellular lipid trafficking (e.g., LXR activation, ASM inhibition) reduce lipid-associated and complement-linked phenotypes, illustrating that RPE lipid readouts are pharmacologically responsive (landowski2022targetinglipidmetabolism pages 14-15, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 13-15). In vivo, manipulating cholesterol efflux or senescent burden causally modulates RPE lipid accumulation and function, validating lipid burden as a mechanistic endpoint (terao2024cholesterolaccumulationpromotes pages 6-9, terao2024cholesterolaccumulationpromotes pages 9-11).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—Mechanistically anchored to core AMD biology (drusen lipids, local cholesterol handling, CFH-lipoprotein/complement linkage), human iRPE relevance, quantitative and scalable imaging, and orthogonal validation paths (lipidomics, ABCA1/ABCG1 expression, EM, senescence/SASP markers) (landowski2022targetinglipidmetabolism pages 10-12, terao2024cholesterolaccumulationpromotes pages 3-4, lin2022dyslipidemiainagerelated pages 1-2). Weaknesses—Direct validation of Nile red/filipin in CFH(Y402H) iRPE for drug screening is not yet shown; lipid stains alone do not capture complement activation, lysosomal/mitochondrial dysfunction, or functional RPE endpoints; translational gaps necessitate multi-model confirmation and correlation to downstream phenotypes (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 1-2, landowski2022targetinglipidmetabolism pages 10-12, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 13-15). Implementing positive/negative controls (ABCA1/ABCG1 perturbation), and pairing with targeted lipidomics and functional assays will be required for qualification (terao2024cholesterolaccumulationpromotes pages 3-4, landowski2022targetinglipidmetabolism pages 14-15).

This assay is biologically well-justified and technically feasible in human iRPE, with strong links to AMD lipid/complement genetics and pathology. It is suitable as a primary mechanistic screen if embedded in a pipeline with orthogonal and functional readouts to mitigate translational risk (landowski2022targetinglipidmetabolism pages 10-12, lin2022dyslipidemiainagerelated pages 1-2).
