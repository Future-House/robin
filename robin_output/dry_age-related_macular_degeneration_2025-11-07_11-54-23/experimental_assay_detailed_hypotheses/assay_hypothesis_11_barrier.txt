Assay Overview:  
This assay employs RPE monolayers grown on permeable supports where barrier integrity is quantified by measuring transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) and paracellular flux of fluorescent dextran. The cells—typically ARPE-19 or fhRPE—are challenged with disease-relevant insults such as VEGF or complement factors to simulate stress‐induced barrier breakdown. Candidate drugs are evaluated on their ability to preserve TEER and reduce dextran permeability, thereby restoring or maintaining junctional integrity (ablonczy2009pigmentepitheliumderivedfactor pages 1-2, campa2013effectofvegf pages 1-2).

Biomedical Evidence:  
RPE barrier disruption is central to AMD pathogenesis, contributing to subretinal fluid accumulation, edema, and inflammation that precede advanced disease stages. VEGF and complement activation are implicated in compromising tight junction protein expression and paracellular barrier function, correlating with clinical features of dry AMD (melhorn2012amodelto pages 19-22, kozlowski2015senescentretinalpigment pages 5-6). Quantitative barrier readouts thus serve as relevant surrogates for early pathological changes that might be modulated by therapeutic interventions.

Previous Use:  
TEER and fluorescent-dextran flux assays have been employed in various in vitro RPE models to study VEGF-induced permeability changes and to evaluate protective factors such as PEDF (ablonczy2009pigmentepitheliumderivedfactor pages 1-2, ablonczy2011humanretinalpigment pages 1-2). These approaches have provided mechanistic insights and aided early drug discovery by linking in vitro barrier function with molecular and cellular events relevant to AMD (kozlowski2015senescentretinalpigment pages 3-5, melhorn2012amodelto pages 86-88).

Overall Evaluation:  
This assay is a robust, quantitative platform that directly measures a key aspect of RPE dysfunction implicated in dry AMD. Its strengths lie in its sensitivity to VEGF and complement challenges and potential scalability for high-throughput screening. However, variable model maturity and differences in cell source may affect assay reproducibility and translatability, necessitating optimization and the inclusion of complementary readouts to fully capture the multifactorial nature of AMD (ablonczy2011humanretinalpigment pages 1-2, melhorn2012amodelto pages 70-73).

This assay offers a biologically relevant and methodologically sound approach to evaluate barrier-protective therapeutics for dry AMD. It combines quantitative barrier measurements with disease-relevant challenges, although optimizing RPE model parameters remains critical for maximal clinical translation.
