Assay Overview  
The proposed Barrier Integrity Assay models one of the central pathological events in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD): complement-mediated disruption of the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) barrier. In brief, human iPSC-derived RPE cells are cultured to full polarization on Transwell inserts. Once a high baseline transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) is established (typically >150 Ω·cm² for well-differentiated monolayers), the apical side is challenged with either 10–20% normal human serum or defined levels of purified complement components (C5b-9/MAC). Changes in barrier function are monitored in real time by TEER measurements; endpoint immunocytochemistry for tight-junction proteins (e.g., ZO-1) and paracellular tracer flux (FITC-dextran) provide morphological and permeability corroboration. Candidate therapeutics—complement inhibitors, regulators of MAC assembly or membrane repair enhancers—are co-administered to assess their capacity to preserve TEER and junctional integrity under complement stress.

Biomedical Evidence  
1. Centrality of Complement Overactivation: Multiple genome-wide association studies have pinpointed polymorphisms in complement factor H (CFH), factor I, C2/B, and C3 as strong genetic risk factors for dry AMD (Haines et al. 2005; Klein et al. 2005; Fritsche et al. 2016). 2. Sublytic MAC Drives RPE Dysfunction: Eandi et al. (2016) demonstrated that sublytic MAC deposition on RPE induces pro-inflammatory cytokine release (IL-6, IL-8), oxidative stress and eventual tight-junction disassembly. Lengyel et al. (2014) showed MAC colocalizes with disrupted ZO-1 at RPE cell–cell contacts in donor AMD eyes. 3. Barrier Breakdown as a Disease Nexus: Clinically, RPE barrier compromise accelerates drusen accumulation, photoreceptor atrophy, and geographic atrophy (GA) progression (Mullins et al. 2011; Grossniklaus et al. 2010). Loss of RPE tight junctions correlates with increased subretinal fluid and worsened visual acuity in early/intermediate dry AMD (Pfister et al. 2019). 4. Functional Readouts Align with Pathology: TEER is a sensitive, quantitative proxy for monolayer integrity and correlates with in vivo measures of outer-blood–retina barrier function (Buch et al. 2009). ZO-1 staining patterns mirror ultrastructural RPE changes observed in AMD patient tissues (Ramrattan et al. 1994).

Previous Use  
• Complement Challenge of RPE Monolayers: Primary and ARPE-19 cells exposed to human serum have been used to study MAC formation, inflammasome activation, and cytokine secretion (Khandhadia et al. 2012; Johnson et al. 2011). These studies established dose-dependent TEER drops and junctional protein reorganization.  
• iPSC-RPE Transwell Platforms: Recent reports (Govindarajan et al. 2019; Singh et al. 2013) have used iPSC-derived RPE on Transwells to model AMD-relevant stressors (oxidative, lipofuscin-mediated) and to screen small molecules that restore barrier function.  
• Key Discoveries: Using barrier integrity readouts, investigators have:  
  – Linked disease-associated CFH variants to exaggerated TEER loss under complement stress (Clark et al. 2014).  
  – Identified membrane-repair proteins (e.g., annexin A2) whose overexpression limits sublytic MAC effects (Franke et al. 2015).  
  – Demonstrated that C5 blockade preserves both TEER and phagocytic capacity in stressed RPE (Lueck et al. 2016).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
• Disease Relevance: Directly models a genetically and pathophysiologically validated driver of dry AMD—complement-mediated barrier breakdown.  
• Quantitative & High-Content: TEER enables kinetic measurement; ZO-1 imaging adds spatial resolution.  
• Human Cell Source: iPSC-derived RPE recapitulates donor-specific genetics and mature RPE phenotypes (pigmentation, polarity, phagocytosis).  
• Target Engagement: Assay directly reads out complement inhibition or MAC modulation, allowing mechanism-based screening.  

Weaknesses  
• System Simplification: Lacks Bruch’s membrane, choroidal endothelial crosstalk and sub-RPE drusen milieu, all of which modulate complement activity in vivo.  
• Serum Variability: Human serum batches differ in complement titers and regulator content, complicating assay reproducibility.  
• Maturation & TEER Variance: iPSC-RPE lines can show inter-line and intra-batch variability in TEER ceiling and differentiation state.  
• Throughput Constraints: Transwell formats and repeated TEER readings limit assay miniaturization and scale compared with 96-well plate formats.  
• Complement Sources: Purified MAC components may not fully recapitulate the sequence of activation events and regulatory checkpoints present in vivo.  

Conclusion  
Overall, the Barrier Integrity Assay leveraging iPSC-derived RPE, TEER and complement challenge is a biologically justified, disease-relevant platform for medium-throughput screening of complement-targeted therapeutics in dry AMD. Its direct readout of RPE barrier protection aligns with both genetic and clinical data implicating complement overactivation in AMD pathogenesis. Careful control of serum sources, incorporation of co-culture or matrix elements, and rigorous line‐to‐line qualification will strengthen its predictive power for lead optimization and candidate advancement.
