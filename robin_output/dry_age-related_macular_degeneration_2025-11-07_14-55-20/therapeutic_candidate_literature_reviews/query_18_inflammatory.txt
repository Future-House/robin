Query: Inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-18, and complement fragments affecting RPE phagocytic receptor signaling in dry AMD, with data from in vitro RPE cultures treated with disease-relevant cytokine profiles and in vivo models demonstrating functional phagocytosis impairment

Inflammatory mediators such as TNF‐α, IL‐1β, IL‐18, and complement fragments are well documented to activate NF‐κB and NLRP3 inflammasome pathways in RPE cells during dry AMD, establishing a pro‐inflammatory milieu that could indirectly affect multiple cellular functions (cao2016theroleof pages 140-143, cao2016theroleof pages 91-99, celkova2015nlrp3inflammasomeand pages 8-10). However, none of the excerpts provide direct experimental data showing that these mediators alter phagocytic receptor signaling—i.e., receptor expression or phosphorylation of proteins such as MerTK, αvβ5, or CD36—in RPE cells or induce a measurable impairment in phagocytic function using in vitro assays with disease‐relevant cytokine profiles (dacosta2015investigationsofthe pages 67-72, dacosta2015investigationsofthe pages 131-136, gao2017nlrp3inflammasomeactivity pages 30-35).

Similarly, while in vivo studies demonstrate increased inflammasome activity and complement deposition, they lack quantitative functional phagocytosis assessments to conclusively link inflammatory signaling to altered photoreceptor outer segment clearance (celkova2015nlrp3inflammasomeand pages 3-5, khan2023beyondthecomplement pages 2-3). One report noted that stimulation of autophagy did not change RPE phagocytic uptake despite impairing lysosomal degradation capacity, suggesting possible indirect effects on phagocytosis yet not directly implicating phagocytic receptor signaling (celkova2015nlrp3inflammasomeand pages 8-10, shafi2024cytokinedrivennfîºbactivation pages 18-21).

In summary, the current evidence supports the concept that inflammatory mediators contribute to RPE dysfunction in dry AMD, but there is insufficient direct in vitro and in vivo data linking these mediators to altered phagocytic receptor signaling or impaired functional phagocytosis. Further targeted studies are needed to conclusively establish this relationship.
