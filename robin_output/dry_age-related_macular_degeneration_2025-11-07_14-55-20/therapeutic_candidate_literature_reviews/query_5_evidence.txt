Query: Evidence for screening natural product libraries for RPE phagocytic activity enhancement: identification of lead compounds with defined chemical structures, mechanism-of-action validation, dose-response in dry AMD–relevant RPE models, and in vivo tolerability assessments

Multiple high-throughput screening studies using hESC‐derived RPE have identified compounds that enhance photoreceptor outer segment phagocytosis. Lead compounds such as ramoplanin and pyrithione zinc have been isolated, and mechanistic studies implicate pathways involving ERMN‐mediated actin stabilization and S1PR5 signaling, with dose‐response analyses performed in both wild‐type and MERTK mutant RPE models (schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 7-9, schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 3-5, schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 5-7). However, these efforts primarily involved pharmacological and synthetic compound libraries rather than dedicated natural product libraries. For natural product screening, several studies have evaluated extracts or mixtures for related endpoints such as mitochondrial function or cyto‐protection in ARPE-19 cells (lee2025phenotypicdrugdiscovery pages 1-5, wong2022discoveringthepotential pages 38-39, lee2025phenotypicdrugdiscovery pages 5-6), but they did not directly assess enhancements in RPE phagocytic activity. Moreover, while some synthetic leads have been chemically characterized (lee2025phenotypicdrugdiscovery pages 6-7), the identified phagocytosis enhancers have only been defined at a broad class level without detailed chemical structures. Finally, none of the studies provide in vivo tolerability assessments for these compounds, leaving a gap in preclinical safety data (schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 13-14, schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 2-3).

The existing evidence demonstrates in vitro phagocytosis enhancement with defined mechanistic insights and dose-responsive effects in disease-modeled RPE. However, no direct screening of natural product libraries for phagocytic enhancement—with fully characterized lead structures and in vivo tolerability—has been reported.
