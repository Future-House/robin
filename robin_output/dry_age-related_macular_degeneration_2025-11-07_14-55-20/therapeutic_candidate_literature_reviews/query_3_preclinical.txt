Query: Preclinical studies evaluating novel small molecules or biologics targeting lysosomal or autophagy pathways to restore defective photoreceptor outer segment clearance in AMD-like animal models, including mechanism confirmation and pharmacokinetics measurements

Multiple preclinical studies have explored modulating lysosomal and autophagy pathways in RPE cell models to improve photoreceptor outer segment (POS) clearance. In one approach, constitutively active TFEB (TFEB‐S210A) expressed in Cryba1 KO RPE cells increased CLEAR network gene expression and markedly reduced the accumulation of lipofuscin‐like granules, suggesting that enhanced TFEB activity may restore defective POS clearance (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7). Likewise, rapamycin administration in ARPE-19 cells induced autophagy via mTOR inhibition, leading to reduced A2E accumulation and demonstrating a favorable ocular pharmacokinetic profile that has prompted proposals for nanoparticle delivery systems (falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 5-7, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 18-20). Trehalose, acting as a TFEB-mediated autophagy inducer, provided cytoprotection against hydroquinone-induced oxidative stress in in vitro AMD-like models and is being advanced for testing in BALB/c mice; however, explicit in vivo assays quantifying POS phagocytosis and detailed PK measurements remain to be performed (abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 160-165, abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 32-37). In addition, candidates such as metformin and synthetic triterpenoids have shown promising modulation of autophagy and RPE protection, yet rigorous mechanism-confirmation experiments and comprehensive pharmacokinetic assessments in AMD-like animal models are still lacking (abokyi2021antioxidativeroleof pages 23-27, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 18-20, marchesi2024differenttherapeuticapproaches pages 17-19).

These data indicate a promising direction for lysosomal/autophagy-targeted therapies in AMD, but further integrated preclinical studies incorporating quantitative POS clearance assays and detailed pharmacokinetics are required. Future work should focus on mechanism confirmation and optimization of dosing regimens to ensure translational success.
