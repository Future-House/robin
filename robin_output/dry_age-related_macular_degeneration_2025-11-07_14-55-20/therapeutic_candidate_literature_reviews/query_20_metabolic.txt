Query: Metabolic interplay between glycolysis, fatty acid oxidation, and phagocytosis in RPE cells under dry AMD stressors, with evidence on how substrate flexibility modulates phagocytic capacity, including stable isotope tracing studies and potential metabolic targets for therapeutic enhancement

RPE cells display a finely tuned balance between glycolysis and fatty acid oxidation (FAO) to sustain their phagocytic capacity under stress conditions such as those seen in dry AMD. Under physiological conditions, RPE preferentially oxidizes fatty acids derived from native photoreceptor outer segments (OS) to drive ketogenesis, evidenced by a robust, time‐dependent release of β-hydroxybutyrate that is tightly coupled to OS ingestion and phagosome maturation (reyesreveles2017phagocytosisdependentketogenesisin pages 1-2). This FAO-driven ketogenesis not only disposes of lipid cargo but may also fuel RPE mitochondrial function while sparing glucose for photoreceptors (hurley2021retinametabolismand pages 12-13).

Stable isotope tracing studies using 13C-labeled substrates (e.g., glucose, palmitate) have provided insights into these fluxes, revealing that RPE cells can flexibly switch between oxidative metabolism and glycolysis. Although direct tracer evidence during phagocytosis under AMD-like stress is limited (tsantilas2021metabolicfunctionina pages 45-48, hass2024acetylcoacarboxylaseinhibition pages 1-2), the reported data support the notion that substrate quality and metabolic state directly modulate phagocytic function.

Potential therapeutic targets to enhance phagocytosis include key nodes in FAO and ketogenesis (CPT1A, mitochondrial trifunctional protein, HMGCS2) and regulators of glycolytic flux such as PPARγ, as well as interventions that maintain substrate integrity (reyesreveles2017phagocytosisdependentketogenesisin pages 1-2, hurley2021retinametabolismand pages 12-13). 

RPE cells rely on switching between glycolysis and FAO to meet energy demands during phagocytosis under AMD stress, and manipulating these pathways offers promising therapeutic avenues. Enhancing FAO while restraining maladaptive glycolysis may ultimately bolster phagocytic capacity and support retinal health.
