Query: Genetic and functional studies implicating complement factor H dysregulation in RPE dysfunction and reduced phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments in dry AMD pathology, with insights into how complement activation interferes with RPE clearance machinery

Genetic and biochemical data identify CFH/FHL‑1 as the dominant local brakes on alternative‑pathway amplification at Bruch’s membrane (BrM), with the common CFH Y402H polymorphism reducing binding to heparan sulfate and other ligands, thereby weakening complement control and permitting early, focal C3b/MAC deposition in BrM/choriocapillaris and later at the RPE (drusen), consistent with a chronic complement stress that precedes overt disease (wilke2024complementregulationin pages 5-6, armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 17-18, romerovazquez2021interlinkbetweeninflammation pages 7-9, armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 8-10, wilke2024complementregulationin pages 3-5). FHR proteins antagonize FH on C3b and accumulate locally, further biasing toward activation; FHR4/3 competition and age‑related loss of BrM heparan sulfate compound the deficit in immobilized FH/FHL‑1 (romerovazquez2021interlinkbetweeninflammation pages 7-9, romerovazquez2021interlinkbetweeninflammation pages 10-12, schafer2021complementfactorhrelated pages 1-3). FHL‑1 also signals via RPE α5β1 integrin to confer oxidative resilience, implying that reduced FHL‑1 retention impairs RPE stress tolerance at the BM/RPE interface (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 17-18).

Functional studies in RPE show that CFH loss triggers an NF‑κB–dependent proinflammatory program with marked C3/CFB upregulation and cytokine release, a phenotype that complements evidence of elevated complement in AMD tissues (armento2021cfhlossin pages 1-2, armento2021cfhlossin pages 2-3, armento2021cfhlossin pages 9-10). In a Y402H‑RPE model, complement modulation restored lysosomal function, directly linking CFH status to the phagolysosomal machinery required for POS processing (armento2021cfhlossin pages 14-16). Exogenous FH limits oxidative injury, mitochondrial damage, and complement activation in RPE, whereas endogenous FH loss increases lipid peroxidation and reduces viability; AMD‑risk 402H associates with mitochondrial abnormalities, consistent with energy‑dependent phagocytosis deficits (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 11-12). In vivo, reduced CFH increases lipoprotein retention in BrM, drives sub‑RPE deposits with complement activation, and is associated with RPE changes and visual dysfunction, positioning CFH upstream of deposit‑driven pathology (toomey2015regulationofagerelated pages 5-6, toomey2015regulationofagerelated pages 1-2). FHR‑3 can be taken up by RPE to activate the “complosome,” inflammasome, and proinflammatory signaling, reinforcing local complement dysregulation (schafer2021complementfactorhrelated pages 1-3).

Mechanistically, uncontrolled AP turnover opsonizes and injures the RPE milieu (C3b/MAC), while anaphylatoxins (C3a/C5a) recruit/activate immune and mast cells, heighten NF‑κB/NLRP3 signaling, and remodel ECM, collectively impairing RPE bioenergetics, integrin–BM interactions, and phagolysosomal capacity needed for POS clearance (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 8-10, armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 10-11, romerovazquez2021interlinkbetweeninflammation pages 5-7). The Y402H change weakens FH binding to MDA/oxLDL and mCRP and reduces iC3b generation on oxidized surfaces, fostering accumulation of oxidized POS adducts and macrophage/microglial activation that further suppresses RPE phagocytosis (kauppinen2016inflammationandits pages 7-8). Age‑dependent BrM HSPG loss reduces FH/FHL‑1 anchoring, exacerbating complement amplification at the RPE/BM interface under oxidative and hypoxic stress linked to impaired POS handling (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 5-7).

Taken together, CFH dysregulation—via Y402H impaired localization/ligand binding and FHR antagonism—permits excessive C3b/MAC and anaphylatoxin signaling at BrM/RPE, driving NF‑κB/inflammasome activation, mitochondrial/lysosomal stress, and integrin dysfunction that reduce RPE phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 8-10, armento2021cfhlossin pages 14-16, armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 17-18, kauppinen2016inflammationandits pages 7-8). Functional rescue of lysosomes by complement modulation and protection by recombinant FH underscore the causal link between complement control and the RPE clearance machinery (armento2021cfhlossin pages 14-16, armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 11-12).

CFH/FHL‑1 are the principal local regulators of alternative‑pathway activity at Bruch’s membrane; risk variants and FHR competition reduce their anchoring and ligand interactions, enabling early C3b/MAC deposition and anaphylatoxin signaling at the RPE interface (wilke2024complementregulationin pages 5-6, romerovazquez2021interlinkbetweeninflammation pages 7-9, romerovazquez2021interlinkbetweeninflammation pages 10-12). RPE‑intrinsic CFH loss drives NF‑κB inflammation and complement upregulation, while complement modulation restores lysosomal function, linking CFH status to POS phagocytosis (armento2021cfhlossin pages 2-3, armento2021cfhlossin pages 14-16). Complement activation impairs RPE clearance through opsonization/MAC injury, anaphylatoxin‑driven inflammasome/cytokine signaling, oxidative/ECM remodeling, and loss of FH‑mediated protection against oxidized POS cargo (armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 8-10, kauppinen2016inflammationandits pages 7-8, armento2021thecomplementsystem pages 10-11).
