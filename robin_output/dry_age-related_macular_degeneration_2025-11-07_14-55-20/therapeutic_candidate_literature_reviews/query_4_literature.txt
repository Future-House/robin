None of the provided sources report a modulator of CD36 or TIM-4 that demonstrates enhancement of RPE phagocytosis together with quantitative in vitro potency (e.g., EC50/IC50 for phagocytosis), genetic target validation in RPE by knockdown/overexpression, and ocular toxicity or safety after intraocular administration (yang2022thepastand pages 7-8, yang2022thepastand pages 5-7, mellal2019immunometabolicmodulationof pages 2-4). 

CD36 ligands have been studied preclinically: the azapeptide MPE-001 shows low micromolar CD36 binding, is CD36-dependent in vivo, and preserves retinal structure/function in blue light injury, but it lacks direct RPE phagocytosis enhancement data and formal ocular safety analyses in the cited work (mellal2019immunometabolicmodulationof pages 2-4, mellal2019immunometabolicmodulationof pages 4-5, mellal2019immunometabolicmodulationof pages 9-11). EP80317 reduced subretinal oxLDL accumulation and Bruch’s membrane thickening and partially preserved photoreceptor function in ApoE−/− mice, yet potency metrics, RPE-specific phagocytosis assays, and ocular safety reporting are not provided (yang2022thepastand pages 10-11). 

Mechanistic and correlative data support CD36 in RPE uptake of oxidized lipids and POS handling, and CD36 deficiency causes retinal/choroidal pathology, but the requested potency and RPE-targeted genetic perturbation datasets are not reported in these excerpts (yang2022thepastand pages 8-10, yang2022thepastand pages 22-24, yang2022thepastand pages 10-11). Other putative modulators (e.g., KDdiA‑PC agonist; sulfosuccinimidyl oleate inhibitor) are listed without the necessary potency, RPE genetic validation, or ocular safety data (yang2022thepastand pages 7-8, yang2022thepastand pages 5-7). TIM-4 is not studied in RPE here; its mention is limited to macrophage literature without RPE relevance or modulators (vorselen2022cellsurfacereceptors pages 7-9). For safety, CD36-dependent oxLDL uptake activates NLRP3 in human RPE, highlighting a potential inflammatory liability, but no formal ocular toxicity studies of CD36 ligands are described (gnanaguru2016oxidizedlipoproteinuptake pages 9-9, mellal2019immunometabolicmodulationof pages 2-4).

Evidence here supports CD36 biology in RPE and reports CD36 ligands with in vivo efficacy, but not the combination of in vitro potency for RPE phagocytosis, RPE-specific genetic validation, and ocular safety data. No TIM-4 modulators in RPE are provided. I cannot answer.
