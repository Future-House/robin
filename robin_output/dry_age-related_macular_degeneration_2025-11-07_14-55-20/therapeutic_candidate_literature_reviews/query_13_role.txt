Query: Role of oxidative stress-induced lipid peroxidation products such as A2E and 7-ketocholesterol in compromising RPE phagocytic function, including evidence from biochemical assays, genetic models, antioxidant rescue in patient-derived RPE and in vivo AMD models with functional phagocytosis readouts

Oxidative stress–induced lipid peroxidation products, notably A2E and 7‑ketocholesterol, have been implicated in compromising RPE phagocytic function by targeting lysosomal and autophagic pathways. Biochemical assays in cultured RPE cells demonstrate that A2E, a major lipofuscin component, inhibits phagolysosomal degradation of photoreceptor outer segments through mechanisms that include a reduction in lysosomal protease activity and membrane destabilization (klettner2013cellularandmolecular pages 36-42, klettner2013cellularandmolecular pages 29-36). Concurrently, 7‑ketocholesterol disrupts phagocytosis by interfering with the uptake and processing of outer segments via aberrant ERK signaling, thereby inducing local inflammation (zhang2020autophagyinagerelated pages 10-11).

Genetic models further substantiate these findings. For example, mutations in phagocytic mediators such as MerTK in the retinal dystrophic RCS rat model link defective phagocytosis directly to retinal degeneration, supporting a role for oxidized lipid products in the pathogenic cascade (klettner2013cellularandmolecular pages 29-36, zhang2020autophagyinagerelated pages 10-11). Although several studies report that antioxidant interventions (e.g., zinc, rapamycin, EGCG) can mitigate oxidative stress and partially restore lysosomal and mitochondrial function in RPE models (zhang2020autophagyinagerelated pages 10-11, zhang2020autophagyinagerelated pages 2-4), the context does not provide explicit details on patient-derived RPE rescue experiments or in vivo AMD models with direct functional phagocytosis readouts. 

The available evidence thus supports a model in which oxidative lipid peroxidation products impair RPE photoreceptor outer segment degradation through lysosomal/autophagic dysfunction and proinflammatory signaling. This impairment promotes the accumulation of toxic debris and contributes to AMD pathogenesis.
