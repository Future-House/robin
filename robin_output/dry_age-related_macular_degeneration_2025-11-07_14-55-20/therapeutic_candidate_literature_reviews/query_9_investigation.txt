Query: Investigation of repurposed FDA-approved drugs that modulate mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation or ER stress pathways to enhance RPE lysosomal clearance of photoreceptor debris, including human ocular PK data and tissue distribution

Repurposed FDA‐approved agents such as metformin and dimethyl fumarate (DMF) have been identified as modulators of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and cellular stress pathways that could, in principle, enhance RPE autophagy and lysosomal clearance of photoreceptor debris. Metformin, a first‐line antidiabetic drug, activates AMPK and has been shown to stimulate autophagic flux in RPE cells, supporting improved mitochondrial function and potentially promoting debris clearance (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 18-20, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 14-16). DMF, through its activation of Nrf2 and subsequent upregulation of antioxidant defenses, represents another FDA-approved intervention that may confer cytoprotection and improve lysosomal function in the RPE (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 18-20).

Similarly, rapamycin (sirolimus), an mTOR inhibitor with established FDA approval for other indications, has been evaluated via intravitreal and subconjunctival routes in ocular clinical trials. This drug has demonstrated properties consistent with the enhancement of autophagy; however, the available excerpts do not include explicit human ocular pharmacokinetic parameters or tissue distribution data, despite references to clinical trials and dosing regimens (ji2021thepresentand pages 17-18, wang2022mtorsignallingpathway pages 16-17, wang2022mtorsignallingpathway pages 24-25, ji2021thepresentand pages 9-10). In addition, sodium 4-phenylbutyrate (PBA) is noted as a chemical chaperone that mitigates ER stress in retinal models, although direct evidence linking it to enhanced RPE lysosomal clearance remains preclinical (das2022drugdiscoverystrategies pages 6-8).

These findings suggest that while metformin, DMF, and rapamycin offer mechanistic plausibility for modulating mitochondrial function and autophagy in RPE cells, the literature currently lacks robust human ocular PK and tissue distribution data. Further clinical pharmacokinetic studies are needed to definitively characterize intraocular exposure profiles and validate these agents’ efficacy in enhancing photoreceptor debris clearance.

Metformin and rapamycin show promise for repurposing to improve RPE clearance functions, but detailed human ocular pharmacokinetic and tissue distribution data remain unavailable. Further studies are required to bridge these preclinical findings with clinical pharmacologic characterization.
