Query: Impact of age-related changes in lipoprotein deposition and drusen formation on RPE receptor expression and phagocytic capacity, including ultrastructural evidence, quantification of scavenger receptor levels, and correlations with clinical imaging biomarkers in human donor eyes

Ultrastructural evidence shows age-dependent accumulation of lipoprotein-like particles within Bruch’s membrane (BrM) and drusen: TEM and freeze-etch EM identify irregular 33–267 nm particles, electron-lucent ~75 nm particles, and solid 80–100 nm particles that can occupy >30% of the inner collagenous layer, with a thin, densely packed lipoprotein-like layer external to the RPE basal lamina in older eyes; membranous debris associates histopathologically with large drusen and RPE pigmentary change on clinical exam (ebrahimi2011lipidslipoproteinsand pages 3-4, handa2007newmolecularhistopathologic pages 5-8). Quantitatively, human drusen contain abundant neutral lipid (EC and PC) and protein (~37–44% of druse volume comprised of ~77 nm lipid particles), and proteomics detects RPE-associated proteins within drusen (wang2010abundantlipidand pages 1-2). Age-related BrM thickening and reduced hydraulic conductivity further constrain RPE–choroid exchange, plausibly compounding lipid retention and RPE stress (bhutto2012understandingagerelatedmacular pages 7-8).

Functionally, oxidative stress drives RPE extracellular vesicle/microparticle (RMP) release that correlates with drusen burden in donor RPE/choroid (r=0.85), with greater RMPs in older tissue; these lipid-rich RMPs are taken up via CD36, and blocking CD36 reduces uptake (shani2018theimplicationof pages 59-64, shani2018theimplicationof pages 48-54). RMP exposure reduces viability and proliferation, induces p15/p21-mediated G0/G1 arrest and senescence, and directly impairs POS phagocytosis, indicating diminished RPE clearance capacity under oxidative, lipid-rich conditions (shani2018theimplicationof pages 59-64, shani2018theimplicationof pages 48-54). Consistent with this, RPE phagocytic ability declines with age and is markedly reduced in AMD donor eyes (wong2022exploringthepathogenesis pages 6-7). In mice, loss of CD36 impairs oxLDL uptake, accelerates sub-RPE deposit formation and BrM thickening with age, and compromises outer retinal function, supporting a causal role for scavenger-mediated lipid clearance (picard2010cd36playsan pages 3-5).

Quantification of scavenger receptor levels in human donor RPE is limited: drusen proteomics detected SCARB2 and the RPE expresses class B scavenger receptors with AMD-associated SCARB1 variants, but age/drusen-stratified protein-level measurements for CD36/SR-BI in human donors are not provided in these excerpts (wang2010abundantlipidand pages 7-9, handa2007newmolecularhistopathologic pages 5-8). Clinicopathologic correlations are sparse: membranous debris corresponds to large drusen/pigmentary change and FA shows prolonged choroidal filling with diffuse BrM thickening, but paired donor-eye clinical imaging linked to receptor levels or phagocytic metrics is not available here; the RMP–drusen correlation lacks premortem imaging/phenotypes (ebrahimi2011lipidslipoproteinsand pages 3-4, bhutto2012understandingagerelatedmacular pages 7-8, shani2018theimplicationof pages 59-64, lyssenko2018directionalabca1mediatedcholesterol pages 21-23).

In sum, ultrastructure and composition implicate age-related, lipoprotein-rich deposits in BrM/drusen that stress the RPE and impair phagocytic function, with CD36-mediated uptake of lipid-rich particles as a key pathway modulating RPE fate (ebrahimi2011lipidslipoproteinsand pages 3-4, wang2010abundantlipidand pages 1-2, shani2018theimplicationof pages 59-64, picard2010cd36playsan pages 3-5). However, these excerpts do not provide quantitative, age-stratified scavenger receptor levels in human RPE or robust correlations to clinical imaging biomarkers in donor eyes (wang2010abundantlipidand pages 7-9, lyssenko2018directionalabca1mediatedcholesterol pages 21-23).
