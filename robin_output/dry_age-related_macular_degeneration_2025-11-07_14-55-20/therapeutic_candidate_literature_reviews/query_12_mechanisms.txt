Query: Mechanisms of lysosomal and autophagy impairment contributing to accumulation of photoreceptor outer segment debris in RPE cells during dry age-related macular degeneration, including studies in patient-derived RPE and in vivo AMD-like models

Impaired degradation of photoreceptor outer segment (POS) debris in dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) is driven by defective lysosomal and autophagy pathways in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells. Under normal conditions, RPE employs both canonical autophagy and LC3‐associated phagocytosis (LAP) to process the daily high burden of ingested POS. However, increased AKT2 activity interferes with TFEB/TFE3 nuclear translocation, thereby reducing lysosomal biogenesis and hydrolase expression needed for efficient cargo degradation (ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 1-2, ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 6-6). As a consequence, RPE cells exhibit impaired autophagic flux marked by p62 accumulation, enlarged LAMP-1–positive vesicles, and stalled autophagosome–lysosome fusion, as observed in patient-derived AMD RPE cultures (golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 9-10, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 1-2, golestaneh2018dysfunctionalautophagyin pages 5-9).

Oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction further compromise these degradative pathways, leading to lipofuscin deposition that exerts toxic feedback by further inhibiting lysosomal enzyme activity (intartaglia2022autophagyinthe pages 15-17, kaarniranta2023autophagyinagerelated pages 3-5, sinha2016lysosomesregulatorsof pages 1-2). In vivo AMD-like models—including Akt2 knock-in and CTSD-deficient mice—recapitulate these defects, showing disrupted RPE polarity, lipid accumulation, drusen formation, and photoreceptor loss (ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 1-2, si2023theroleof pages 4-5). In addition, genetic predispositions such as the CFH Y402H variant exacerbate autophagy failure via heightened AKT2 signaling (ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 1-2, ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 6-6).

Defective lysosomal and autophagy functions in RPE lead to inadequate clearance of POS debris, promoting drusen deposition and retinal degeneration. These findings in patient-derived cells and animal models highlight critical nodes—including the AKT2/TFEB axis and oxidative stress—that may serve as therapeutic targets in dry AMD.
