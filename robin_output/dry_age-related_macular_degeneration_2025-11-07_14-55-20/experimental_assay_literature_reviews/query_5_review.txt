Query: Review studies on cholesterol and neutral lipid handling in RPE cells and drusen formation in dry AMD, including roles of apolipoproteins, ABCA1 transporter, and lipid peroxidation products, and outline cell-based assays to quantify intracellular lipid accumulation, foam cell formation, and lipofuscin deposition

Cholesterol and neutral lipid homeostasis in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) is maintained through coordinated uptake, intracellular processing, and efflux. RPE cells acquire cholesterol via LDL receptor–mediated uptake and phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments, and excess cholesterol is exported by the ABCA1 transporter through nascent HDL formation, a process regulated by LXR activation in response to oxysterols (lyssenko2018directionalabca1mediatedcholesterol pages 1-3; federica2019impairedabca1abcg1mediatedlipid pages 2-3). Locally produced apolipoproteins such as APOE and APOB act as lipid acceptors and contribute to the formation of RPE‐derived lipoprotein particles, which accumulate extracellularly as drusen, with apolipoprotein isoforms modulating deposit characteristics (lyssenko2018directionalabca1mediatedcholesterol pages 21-23; lyssenko2018directionalabca1mediatedcholesterol pages 23-25). Lipid peroxidation products, including oxidized cholesterol species and bisretinoids such as A2E, further impair RPE function, trigger inflammatory signaling, and exacerbate drusen biogenesis (federica2019impairedabca1abcg1mediatedlipid pages 28-29; alrashed2016theroleof pages 127-131).

Cell-based assays to quantify these defects include radiolabeled cholesterol efflux assays with defined extracellular acceptors (e.g., apoA‑I or HDL) to assess ABCA1 functionality (lyssenko2018directionalabca1mediatedcholesterol pages 15-17, 19-21); histochemical staining with OilRedO or LipidTOX followed by image analysis to evaluate intracellular neutral lipid accumulation and foam cell formation (federica2019impairedabca1abcg1mediatedlipid pages 2-3, 15-16); and autofluorescence imaging to quantify lipofuscin deposition and A2E accumulation in long-term cultured RPE monolayers (federica2019impairedabca1abcg1mediatedlipid pages 28-29; alrashed2016theroleof pages 127-131).

RPE lipid dysregulation plays a central role in drusen formation and dry AMD pathogenesis. Quantitative cell-based assays that measure cholesterol efflux, intracellular lipid storage, and lipofuscin accumulation provide robust tools for dissecting these complex pathogenic mechanisms.
