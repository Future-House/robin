Query: Assess the current state of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived RPE models for dry age-related macular degeneration research, detailing standard differentiation protocols, disease-relevant genetic backgrounds, functional characterization assays, and challenges in reproducibility and scalability for high-throughput drug screening

Induced pluripotent stem cell (hiPSC)‐derived retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) models for dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) are advancing through both spontaneous and directed differentiation approaches. Standard protocols use either spontaneous differentiation via bFGF withdrawal or directed, small molecule‐driven methods—employing agents such as Nicotinamide, Activin A, and CHIR99021—to efficiently generate RPE in planar (2D) cultures and three‐dimensional organoid formats, thereby better recapitulating native architecture and function (truong2021automatinghumaninduced pages 1-3, regent2019automationofhuman pages 1-2, gagliardi2018generationandselection pages 68-70). 

Patient-specific hiPSC lines carrying disease-associated variants, for instance the CFH Y402H polymorphism, have been leveraged to model complement dysregulation and autophagy defects central to dry AMD pathology, thereby providing a genetic basis for disease-specific in vitro studies (lin2021humanpluripotentstemcellderived pages 9-10, cheng2023humanretinalorganoids pages 1-3). Functional characterization of these models relies on a battery of assays, including immunocytochemistry for key RPE markers (RPE65, BEST1, MITF), quantitative RT-PCR, transmission electron microscopy for ultrastructural assessment, transepithelial resistance measurements for barrier function, and phagocytosis assays using fluorescently labeled photoreceptor outer segments (truong2021automatinghumaninduced pages 1-3, mesch2021…anin pages 74-77, regent2019automationofhuman pages 8-10). 

Challenges persist regarding reproducibility and scalability for high-throughput drug screening. Manual liquid handling, lengthy differentiation timelines, inter-line variability, and the predominantly adherent culture formats contribute to operator-dependent variability—issues that are being addressed through automation, scalable robotics, and standardized, xeno-free protocols (regent2019automationofhuman pages 1-2, li2025advancesinthe pages 8-10, gagliardi2018generationandselection pages 73-76).

hiPSC-derived RPE models show promise for mimicking dry AMD pathology. Refinement in automation and standardization is key to achieving reproducible, high-throughput drug screening platforms.
