Query: Explore high-content imaging and phenotypic screening platforms tailored for RPE cell models, summarizing fluorescent reporters, automated image analysis pipelines, multiplexed readouts for cell morphology, organelle health, cell death, and mitochondrial dynamics to enable mechanistic drug evaluations in dry AMD assays

High-content screening (HCS) provides a multiplexed, automated microscopy framework to phenotype mitochondrial dysfunction and redox biology at scale, and is directly transferable to RPE models for dry AMD mechanistic drug evaluation, including 384-well plate formats and iPSC-derived cells (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 5-7, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 7-8). Robust core readouts are mitochondrial morphofunction (morphology + Δψ) and ROS/redox, which have been validated in disease-relevant screening contexts (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 5-7).

Reporter selection: Δψ via TMRM/TMRE (TMRM favored for low toxicity/fast equilibration) enables kinetic, pharmacologically validated measurements with oligomycin/rotenone/FCCP; combine with mitochondrial structural labels (mito-GFP or MitoTracker) to compute morphofunction (fragmentation, length, branching) (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 1-2, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 2-3, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 8-8, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 7-8). ROS/redox panels include CM-H2DCFDA and DHE for total oxidative stress (qualitative), mitochondria-targeted MitoSOX with stringent image-level QC to exclude non-mitochondrial signal, HyPer and roGFP/rxYFP for compartmental ratiometric readouts, and MitoPerOx/C11-BODIPY for lipid peroxidation (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 3-4, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 8-8). ATP can be monitored using BTeam/ATeam or newer chemical probes (ARP-1, RSL+), with oxygen consumption assessed orthogonally by Seahorse/high-resolution respirometry when spatially resolved O2 imaging is impractical (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 3-4, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 8-8, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 7-8). Ratiometric probes improve quantitation but increase spectral load and data volume; plan spectral allocation accordingly (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 2-3, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 3-4).

Assay implementation: establish uniform RPE monolayers with PDL/laminin coatings; validated mitochondrial labeling parameters (e.g., MitoTracker Red FM 100 nM, 20 min, 37°C) scale to plate formats (dolatiardejani2023developmentofa pages 50-54). Include viability dyes (eFluor780), stringent gating, and imaging flow cytometry to remove debris/doublets; prior work shows ~20% of apparent double-positives are artefacts (dolatiardejani2023developmentofa pages 93-96, dolatiardejani2023developmentofa pages 89-93).

Automated analysis: deploy pipelines that segment nuclei/cytoplasm/mitochondria, extract morphometrics, intensities, textures, and colocalization, and classify phenotypes using supervised ML with morphological binning; such approaches have quantified Δψ+morphology and enabled ML classification of mitochondrial phenotypes in retinal cells (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 8-8, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 5-7). For dynamics and time-lapse, combine CNN backbones with LSTM for temporal classification; use Mitometer for 2D/3D mitochondrial timelapse (with downstream aggregation to handle large outputs) and ABANICCO for robust color unmixing in multiplex assays (blanco2022bookofabstracts pages 78-81). Validate Δψ channels by acute inhibitors during live imaging to ensure on-target sensitivity (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 1-2).

Scaling and downstream mechanism: 384-well HCS has detected Δψ/morphology defects in iPSC-derived neurons and supports hepatocyte drug-tox assays using mitochondrial parameters, indicating direct portability to iPSC-RPE screening (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 5-7). Visual Cell Sorting can convert image-derived phenotypes into FACS-sortable tags to recover live RPE subpopulations for transcriptomics, enabling image-to-molecule mechanistic linkage at high throughput (US12298297B2 pages 53-53). Bioenergetic follow-up via Seahorse/Oroboros or high-resolution respirometry provides orthogonal mechanism-of-action confirmation (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 3-4, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 8-8).

In RPE models, a practical HCS stack combines mito-GFP/MitoTracker, TMRM, ROS/redox reporters, and ATP sensors with ML-driven morphofunction quantification, kinetic pharmacologic challenges, and stringent artefact controls to nominate and de-risk dry AMD candidates (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 8-8, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 1-2, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 3-4). This modular platform is immediately adaptable to RPE and supports unbiased, mechanistic drug evaluation at screening scale (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 5-7).

A fit-for-purpose RPE HCS platform should multiplex mitochondrial morphofunction (TMRM + morphology), ROS/redox, and ATP with ML-based phenotype classification in 96–384-well formats, validated by acute mitochondrial perturbations and orthogonal respirometry (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 1-2, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 5-7, iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 3-4). Rigorous artefact exclusion and live-cell sorting (Visual Cell Sorting) enable downstream molecular profiling of image-defined RPE subpopulations, linking phenotypes to mechanism for dry AMD drug discovery (dolatiardejani2023developmentofa pages 93-96, US12298297B2 pages 53-53). This configuration is supported by published HCS precedents and probe guidance directly applicable to RPE adaptation (iannetti2019liveimagingreadoutsand pages 7-8).
