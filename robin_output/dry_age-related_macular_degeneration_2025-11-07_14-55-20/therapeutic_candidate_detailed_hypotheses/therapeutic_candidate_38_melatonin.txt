Proposal for Melatonin

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Melatonin (N-acetyl-5-methoxytryptamine) is an endogenously produced indoleamine synthesized in the pineal gland from serotonin via arylalkylamine N-acetyltransferase (AANAT) and hydroxyindole O-methyltransferase (HIOMT) (Hardeland et al., 2006). First isolated in 1958 (Lerner et al., 1958), it belongs to the class of small-molecule pleiotropic neurohormones. Exogenous melatonin is manufactured by chemical synthesis, typically via O-methylation of 5-hydroxytryptamine followed by N-acetylation. As a therapeutic class, indoleamines have been used primarily for sleep regulation, jet-lag, circadian rhythm disorders, and as systemic antioxidants.

Therapeutic History  
•  General human use: approved over-the-counter for insomnia and circadian rhythm sleep disorders at doses between 0.5 and 10 mg (Zisapel, 2018).  
•  Antioxidant/anti-inflammatory: evaluated in neurodegenerative disorders (Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s) for its ROS-scavenging and mitochondrial protective properties (Reiter et al., 2000; Cardinali et al., 2010).  
•  Retinal protection: shown to prevent light-induced photoreceptor loss in rodent models (Ma et al., 2012) and NaIO₃-induced RPE degeneration (Li et al., 2019).  
•  Clinical studies in AMD: no registered clinical trials using melatonin for dry AMD; a single small open-label study (n = 20) suggested stabilized visual acuity with oral melatonin but lacked controls (“Unpublished conference abstract,” 2015).

Mechanism of Action  
1.  Receptor-mediated signaling  
    •  Melatonin binds GPCRs MT1 (MTNR1A) and MT2 (MTNR1B) with nanomolar affinity (Chong & Sugden, 1994). MT1/MT2 are expressed in human RPE and photoreceptors (Stauch et al., 2016; Idriss et al., 2020).  
    •  Activation of MT1/MT2 inhibits adenylate cyclase via Gi proteins, reducing cAMP, modulating downstream PKA/CREB pathways (Dubocovich, 2007). In RPE, this signaling upregulates antioxidant defenses (Gueron et al., 2016).  
2.  Mitochondrial actions  
    •  A mitochondrial localization of MT1 has been described on the inner mitochondrial membrane, where melatonin counteracts Ca²⁺-induced permeability transition pore opening and prevents cytochrome c release (Franco et al., 2011).  
    •  Receptor-independent: directly scavenges hydroxyl, peroxyl, and singlet oxygen species; stimulates expression/activity of SOD, GPx, and catalase (Tan et al., 1999).  
3.  Mitophagy and mitochondrial turnover  
    •  Melatonin enhances PINK1/Parkin-mediated mitophagy in neuronal and hepatic cells, via SIRT3 activation and FoxO3a deacetylation (Kwon et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2016).  
    •  In ARPE-19 cells, melatonin treatment increased LC3II accumulation, decreased p62, and improved mitochondrial membrane potential (Gueron et al., 2016).  
4.  Phagocytosis support  
    •  ATP generated from healthy mitochondria fuels αvβ5- and MERTK-mediated engulfment of photoreceptor outer segments (POS) (Mazzoni et al., 2014). Melatonin-driven mitochondrial preservation may thus enhance POS clearance.

Expected Effect in RPE Assay  
In an in vitro RPE culture assay (e.g., ARPE-19 or primary human RPE):  
•  Antioxidant protection: melatonin (0.1–10 µM) is expected to reduce H₂O₂-induced ROS (DCF fluorescence) by ≥ 40 % after 24 h (Gueron et al., 2016).  
•  Mitophagy activation: increased Parkin recruitment to mitochondria (immunofluorescence) and elevated LC3II/p62 flux (western blot) within 12 h treatment at 5 µM (Zhang et al., 2016).  
•  Mitochondrial function: preservation of mitochondrial membrane potential (JC-1 assay) and ATP levels (luminescent assay) following oxidative challenge (Franco et al., 2011).  
•  POS phagocytosis: enhanced uptake of FITC-labeled POS by ≥ 25 % over vehicle, mediated by ATP support (as in Mazzoni et al., 2014). Expression of MTNR1A/B in RPE confirmed by qPCR (Idriss et al., 2020) supports receptor-dependent effects.

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
•  Strong mechanistic rationale targeting two critical AMD pathways: oxidative mitochondrial injury and defective POS clearance.  
•  Extensive safety record in humans; well-characterized PK with favorable blood-retina penetration (Back et al., 2010).  
•  Dual receptor-mediated and direct antioxidant effects reduce risk of target resistance.

Weaknesses  
•  Endogenous melatonin levels decline with age and in AMD patients, potentially limiting receptor availability (Yang et al., 2021).  
•  Rapid first-pass metabolism (t½ ≈ 30 min) may require sustained-release formulation to maintain therapeutic retinal concentrations.  
•  Possible receptor desensitization with chronic high-dose use.

Conclusion  
Melatonin is a promising repurposing candidate for dry AMD, with multimodal actions on RPE mitochondrial health and phagocytic capacity. Key next steps include dose-response optimization in RPE assays, development of retinal-targeted delivery systems, and validation of functional endpoints (mitophagy flux, POS clearance) before in vivo efficacy studies.
