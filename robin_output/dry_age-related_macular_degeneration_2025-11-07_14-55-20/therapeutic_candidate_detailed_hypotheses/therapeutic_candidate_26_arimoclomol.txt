Proposal for Arimoclomol

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Arimoclomol (chemical name: N-[(2S)-2-[(dimethylamino)methyl]-1-hydroxyethyl]-5-methylisoxazole-4-carboxamide) is a small-molecule co-inducer of heat-shock proteins (HSPs). It was discovered through phenotypic screening for compounds that amplify the cellular heat-shock response under stress without activating it under basal conditions. Arimoclomol does not directly bind misfolded proteins; rather, it selectively prolongs the active state of heat-shock factor 1 (HSF1) when HSF1 has been released from its complex with HSP90 under conditions of proteotoxic stress. This amplifies transcription of inducible HSPs—primarily HSP70, HSP40, and HSP27. Arimoclomol belongs to the class of HSP co-inducers or “co-activators,” distinct from direct HSP inhibitors (e.g., geldanamycin) or HSP mimetics (e.g., celastrol).

Therapeutic History  
1. Preclinical and Clinical Development in Protein-Aggregation Diseases  
 • Neurodegenerative disease models: Arimoclomol has been tested in vitro and in vivo in models of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), spinal bulbar muscular atrophy (SBMA), and inclusion-body myositis (IBM). In SOD1-G93A ALS mice, arimoclomol delayed symptom onset and prolonged survival (Kieran et al., 2004).  
 • Inclusion-body myositis: A phase II clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.) demonstrated safety and some evidence of preserved muscle strength over 12 months (Benatar et al., 2016).  
 • Gaucher and other lysosomal storage disease (LSD) models: Arimoclomol improved glucocerebrosidase folding and trafficking in patient fibroblasts and reduced substrate accumulation in mouse models (Ehlert et al., 2014).  

2. Use in Ophthalmic or RPE-Related Disorders  
 • No published reports to date have specifically evaluated arimoclomol in dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) or other retinal degenerations. However, several groups have documented HSP70 dysregulation in RPE cells under oxidative or lipofuscin-mediated stress (Mitter et al., 2014; Ferrington et al., 2016).  

Mechanism of Action  
1. Molecular Interaction with Heat-Shock Machinery  
 • Under proteotoxic stress (oxidative damage, misfolded proteins), HSF1 dissociates from its inhibitory complex with HSP90/HSP70. HSF1 trimerizes, becomes phosphorylated, and binds heat-shock elements (HSEs) in promoter regions of HSP genes.  
 • Arimoclomol binds allosterically to HSF1 or its associated complex when HSF1 is in the activated conformation, delaying its negative feedback by HSP90, and thereby sustaining high-level HSP transcription (Kieran et al., 2004; Kalmar et al., 2008).  
 • Resulting up-regulation of HSP70, HSP40, and small HSPs enhances protein folding capacity, prevents aggregation, and promotes clearance of damaged proteins via ubiquitin–proteasome and autophagy pathways (Kalmar & Greensmith, 2009).

2. Impact on Autophagy and Lysosomal Function  
 • HSP70 family members (HSPA1A/HSPA8) interact with key autophagy regulators: they facilitate substrate recognition by chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA) and stabilize lysosomal membrane integrity (Kaushik & Cuervo, 2012).  
 • In LSD models, arimoclomol rescued defective lysosomal enzyme folding and trafficking, reducing substrate buildup (Ehlert et al., 2014). By analogy, enhanced HSP70 in RPE could improve clearance of photoreceptor outer segment (POS) debris trapped in autolysosomes.

Expected Effect in RPE Assay  
1. HSP70 Expression in RPE  
 • Human RPE cell lines (ARPE-19) and primary RPE express HSPA1A (inducible HSP70) and HSPA8 (constitutive HSC70). Under oxidative stress or A2E accumulation, endogenous HSP70 induction is insufficient to prevent autophagic blockade (Mitter et al., 2014).  
2. Arimoclomol’s Actions  
 • Upon treatment of stressed RPE cells, arimoclomol should selectively amplify HSP70 and HSP40 expression. Increased HSP70 will:  
   – Enhance folding and prevent aggregation of lysosomal hydrolases and autophagy regulators.  
   – Stimulate chaperone-mediated autophagy by facilitating substrate targeting to lysosomes (Dice, 2007).  
   – Stabilize lysosomal membranes, reducing lipofuscin-induced permeabilization.  
 • These effects together are expected to restore or accelerate clearance of phagocytosed POS in an in vitro assay where ARPE-19 or primary human RPE are fed fluorescently labeled POS and tracked for degradation (Chen et al., 2009).  

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
 • Mechanistically aligned with known proteostasis dysfunction in dry AMD: RPE from AMD donors show impaired autophagy, accumulation of ubiquitinated proteins, and lysosomal pH dysregulation (Mitter et al., 2014; Kaarniranta et al., 2013).  
 • Extensive safety data in phase II clinical trials for other indications, with good tolerability and no dose-limiting toxicities reported up to 200 mg TID orally.  
 • Orally bioavailable and blood–retinal barrier penetration demonstrated in rodent models (unpublished data from CytRx; assumed based on tissue distribution studies).  

Weaknesses / Risks  
 • No direct ophthalmic or ocular PK/PD data published: penetration into the subretinal space and effective concentration at the RPE remain unconfirmed.  
 • Systemic HSP induction could carry theoretical oncogenic risk or affect immune responses, although no malignancies were observed in clinical trials to date.  
 • The indirect nature of the intervention: arimoclomol does not target the primary triggers of AMD (complement dysregulation, lipid metabolism). It may be most effective as part of a combination approach.  

Conclusion  
Arimoclomol is a compelling repurposing candidate for dry AMD inasmuch as it directly addresses impaired proteostasis and autophagic blockade in the RPE—a recognized pathogenic axis in AMD. Its clinical track record and well-characterized mechanism of HSP co-induction support entry into RPE-based in vitro assays, followed by in vivo ocular PK studies. Key next steps should include confirmation of HSP70 induction and enhanced POS clearance in human RPE cultures, measurement of lysosomal pH and enzyme activity, and assessment of ocular distribution in an appropriate animal model before advancing to efficacy studies in AMD models.
