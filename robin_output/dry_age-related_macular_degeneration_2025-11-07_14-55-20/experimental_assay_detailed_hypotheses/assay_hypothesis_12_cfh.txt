Overall Evaluation: Strengths and weaknesses of this assay for testing therapeutics for dry age-related macular degeneration.

Assay Overview: This assay measures RPE-secreted complement regulatory activity by quantifying the conversion of exogenously added C3b to iC3b in conditioned media using a neoepitope-specific ELISA. It models the fluid-phase cofactor activity of CFH/CFI, a mechanism that is central to dampening alternative pathway amplification in the outer retina. The assay employs RPE cell cultures (e.g., iPSC-RPE or ARPE-19) as the source of secreted regulators and delivers a quantitative readout reflective of complement inhibitory function (cao2016theroleof pages 35-41, cao2016theroleof pages 31-35).

Biomedical Evidence: Complement dysregulation, particularly involving CFH and CFI, is strongly implicated in dry AMD pathogenesis. Genetic variants such as CFH Y402H correlate with altered complement control, disrupted C3b processing, and deposition of complement activation products in drusen (cao2016theroleof pages 35-41, cox2023functionalassessmentof pages 46-50). Clinically, elevated levels of complement activation fragments, including iC3b and MAC, have been reported in AMD, supporting the biological relevance of monitoring the C3b–iC3b step (cox2023functionalassessmentof pages 56-60, lashkari2018amonoclonalantibody pages 2-4).

Previous Use: ELISA-based C3b conversion assays have been successfully deployed in plasma to assess CFI functionality, and studies have demonstrated modulation by agents such as amyloid β and anti-Aβ antibodies (lashkari2018amonoclonalantibody pages 13-16, lashkari2018amonoclonalantibody pages 1-2). However, their adaptation to RPE-conditioned media remains less explored and may require optimization to address matrix effects (lashkari2018amonoclonalantibody pages 4-6, cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 2-4).

Overall Evaluation: The assay’s major strength lies in its direct, quantitative measurement of a key complement regulatory mechanism implicated in AMD. It offers a precise readout for drug-mediated rescue of CFH/CFI activity, which is central to mitigating complement overactivation in dry AMD. Weaknesses include potential limitations in capturing membrane-bound regulation and intracellular complement events, and the need for rigorous validation in RPE-conditioned environments to ensure physiological relevance (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2, lashkari2018amonoclonalantibody pages 2-4).

This CFH/CFI complement regulation assay is promising for screening therapeutics targeting complement dysregulation in dry AMD. It offers a direct biochemical readout, though complementary endpoints may be necessary to fully capture the disease’s complexity.
