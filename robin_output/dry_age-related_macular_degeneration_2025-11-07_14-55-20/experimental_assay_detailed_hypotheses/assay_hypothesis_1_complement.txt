Overall Evaluation: Strengths and weaknesses of this assay for testing therapeutics for dry age-related macular degeneration.

Assay Overview: This assay quantifies sub-lytic C5b-9/MAC deposited on retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) monolayers after exposure to complement-active human serum, modeling terminal complement attack at the RPE–Bruch’s interface implicated in dry AMD pathogenesis. Confluent ARPE-19 or primary human/porcine RPE on Transwells are optionally primed (e.g., CEP adducts or oxidative stress), challenged apically and/or basolaterally with 5–10% normal human serum (NHS) versus matched controls (heat-inactivated serum, C7- or C9-depleted serum ± purified C9, anti-C5–treated serum), fixed (4% PFA), and immunolabeled for C5b-9 with confocal imaging. Quantitation uses MAC puncta per cell or % cells with high MAC burden, alongside surface CD59, and can be paired with functional readouts (TER, VEGF, cytokines, NF-κB, LDH cytotoxicity) to connect deposition with RPE response (mulfaul2021invitromeasurement pages 7-11, lueck2015complementstimulatesretinal pages 1-2, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 106-116, tan2018organelletraffickingina pages 106-116, annamalai2021naturalimmunoglobulinmbased pages 3-4).

Biomedical Evidence: Complement activation products, including C5b-9/MAC and C3a/C5a, are abundant in drusen and Bruch’s membrane/choriocapillaris and increase with age and AMD, linking terminal pathway activity to disease tissues. Sub-lytic MAC and anaphylatoxins drive RPE inflammation and VEGF/MMP induction, while loss/dysfunction of regulators (CFH, CD46, CD59) and risk variants support a causal role for dysregulated complement in AMD. RPE rapidly assemble MAC on the basal surface after serum exposure and clear it by endocytosis; blocking endocytosis retains MAC and induces mitochondrial defects, underscoring biological relevance of measuring surface MAC dynamics on RPE (kassa2019complementinhibitionas pages 7-10, kassa2019complementinhibitionas pages 1-7, behnke2022ifnβimmunomodulationanda pages 31-33, behnke2022ifnβimmunomodulationand pages 31-33, georgiannakis2015retinalpigmentepithelial pages 1-2).

Previous Use: Serum-challenge RPE assays robustly detect MAC and complement dependence using heat-inactivated and pathway-depleted sera; CEP priming plus NHS yields specific MAC staining absent in controls, establishing assay specificity. Dose-dependent C5b-9 deposition on ARPE-19 induces pro-inflammatory cytokines, adhesion molecules, and NF-κB nuclear translocation without overt lysis, providing pharmacodynamic endpoints. A2E treatment or Abca4−/− background lowers CD59 and elevates MAC, and C9-depleted serum ± C9 dissects terminal pathway requirements. Anti-C5 blockade prevents MAC-driven changes (e.g., ApoE accumulation), and alternative pathway–directed inhibitors reduce complement on stressed RPE with functional rescue (TER/VEGF). Complement-dependent soluble SC5b-9 can be quantified in RPE supernatants, extending readouts beyond surface imaging (mulfaul2021invitromeasurement pages 7-11, lueck2015complementstimulatesretinal pages 1-2, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 106-116, tan2018organelletraffickingina pages 106-116, yang2017complementmediatedregulationof pages 1-2, yang2017complementmediatedregulationof pages 3-4, annamalai2021naturalimmunoglobulinmbased pages 3-4, fernandezgodino2015alocalcomplement pages 10-11).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: mechanistically proximal readout of terminal complement on the target cell; robust internal controls (heat-inactivated, complement-depleted/reconstituted serum, anti-C5); sensitivity to disease-relevant modifiers (oxidative stress, A2E, CD59 levels); and integration with functional outputs (TER, cytokines, VEGF, viability). The assay reports target engagement for C5 and AP inhibitors and captures RPE handling of MAC (assembly/clearance). Weaknesses: RPE resist MAC via high CD59, sometimes requiring priming for dynamic range; inter-donor variability (e.g., ApoE levels) and differing cell sources complicate standardization; and predictive validity for clinical efficacy is unproven. Reports emphasize that while complement inhibition is biologically and clinically relevant, in vitro MAC imaging lacks multi-site analytical validation and should be paired with orthogonal endpoints (SC5b-9 ELISA, TER/cytokines) and rigorous controls (C7/C9 depletion, anti-C5) across multiple human donors. Note species constraints of some complement tools and that robust MAC is more prominent on choroidal ECs than RPE in some settings, guiding assay optimization (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 24-25, yang2017complementmediatedregulationof pages 3-4, fernandezgodino2015alocalcomplement pages 10-11, zeng2016molecularresponseof pages 7-8).

Imaging C5b-9 on RPE after serum challenge is a mechanistically relevant, controllable, and pharmacodynamically informative assay for early testing of complement-targeted therapeutics in dry AMD. It is best deployed with stringent complement controls, RPE priming paradigms, and paired functional readouts, recognizing that formal predictive validation versus in vivo/clinical outcomes is still lacking (mulfaul2021invitromeasurement pages 7-11, lueck2015complementstimulatesretinal pages 1-2, georgiannakis2015retinalpigmentepithelial pages 1-2, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 24-25).
