Overall Evaluation: Strengths and weaknesses of this assay for testing therapeutics for dry age-related macular degeneration.

Assay Overview: The assay models canonical inflammasome activation in RPE as a two-signal process linked to dry AMD: NF-κB–dependent priming (e.g., IL-1α/TNFα/CEP) followed by activation by AMD-relevant triggers (drusen components such as Aβ/C1q/oxidized lipids, lysosomal membrane permeabilization, extracellular ATP→P2X7→K+ efflux) (gao2015nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 3-4, gao2015nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 1-3, tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 9-10, brandstetter2015lightinducesnlrp3 pages 9-11, wang2019efficacyofnovel pages 1-2). Core readouts are active caspase-1 (FLICA or p20/p10 immunoblot), IL-1β/IL-18 secretion (ELISA), ASC specks, and downstream pyroptotic/apoptotic markers (GSDMD cleavage, LDH release; optionally caspase-3/GSDME) (tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 1-2, marneros2023roleofinflammasome pages 1-3, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84, gao2018evidenceforthe pages 1-2). Recommended cell systems include primary human RPE or iPSC-RPE (with IL-1α priming), with ARPE-19 used cautiously due to TLR4/priming idiosyncrasies; co-culture with microglia can be added to increase physiological relevance (tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 9-10, hytti2021differentialexpressionof pages 2-4, ildefonso2016thenlrp3inflammasome pages 1-4).

Biomedical Evidence: Inflammasome activation (NLRP3/ASC/caspase-1) and IL-1β/IL-18 maturation have been documented in RPE exposed to drusen constituents, oxidative stress, lipofuscin photooxidation, and ATP/P2X7, and inflammasome machinery and outputs are detectable in AMD tissue and models (gao2015nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 1-3, tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 1-2, brandstetter2015lightinducesnlrp3 pages 9-11, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84, wang2016nlrp3upregulationin pages 3-6). In vivo, chronic Aβ stimulation in rat eyes elevated caspase-1, IL-1β/IL-18 and triggered pyroptotic/apoptotic programs in RPE (gao2018evidenceforthe pages 1-2). CASP-1–dependent pathways drive photoreceptor degeneration across retinal insults, underscoring mechanistic relevance of caspase-1/pyroptosis readouts; however, sensor identity (NLRP3 vs others) and cellular source (microglia/macrophages vs RPE) vary by context (wooff2020caspase1dependentinflammasomesmediate pages 8-10, wooff2020caspase1dependentinflammasomesmediate pages 6-8, marneros2023roleofinflammasome pages 6-7).

Previous Use: RPE inflammasome assays have supported target validation and test compound ranking. Selective NLRP3 inhibitors suppressed IL-1β and LDH release after LMP/ROS/P2X7 stimulation in human RPE and reduced light-induced activation ex vivo (wang2019efficacyofnovel pages 1-2). Complement/MAC blockade reduced age-dependent caspase-1 cleavage in RPE/choroid in vivo, indicating translatability of caspase-1 readouts for target engagement (gao2015nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 3-4). NF-κB blockade abrogated Aβ-triggered inflammasome responses in rat eye, reinforcing the two-signal paradigm (gao2018evidenceforthe pages 1-2). Cathepsin inhibition prevented LMP-driven caspase-1 activation/IL-1β release in RPE (tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 9-10, tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: mechanistically grounded; AMD-relevant stimuli; robust, orthogonal endpoints (active caspase-1 p20, IL-1β/IL-18, ASC specks, GSDMD/LDH); demonstrated pharmacologic sensitivity and in vivo concordance for upstream blockade (gao2015nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 3-4, wang2019efficacyofnovel pages 1-2, gao2018evidenceforthe pages 1-2, marneros2023roleofinflammasome pages 1-3). Limitations: RPE require careful priming; ARPE-19 is an imperfect surrogate; NLRP3 expression/readouts are variable and antibody specificity is problematic; gasdermin usage is context-dependent; IL-18 biology is bidirectional; anti–IL-1 strategies do not inhibit caspase-1 or IL-18; inflammasome activity in vivo may be dominated by microglia/macrophages, reducing the predictive value of RPE-only NLRP3 assays (tseng2013nlrp3inflammasomeactivation pages 9-10, wooff2020caspase1dependentinflammasomesmediate pages 1-2, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84, marneros2023roleofinflammasome pages 1-3, wooff2020caspase1dependentinflammasomesmediate pages 6-8, marneros2023roleofinflammasome pages 3-4, marneros2023roleofinflammasome pages 6-7). Mitigations: use primary/iPSC-RPE with defined priming, quantify active caspase-1 (p20) and ASC specks, include microglia co-cultures or parallel microglia assays, and employ diverse AMD-relevant triggers to de-risk sensor bias (brandstetter2015lightinducesnlrp3 pages 9-11, korhonen2024inflammasomeactivationin pages 82-84, ildefonso2016thenlrp3inflammasome pages 1-4).

This inflammasome assay is useful for mechanism-of-action studies and for rank-ordering NLRP3/caspase-1–pathway modulators in dry AMD, provided rigorous priming, controls, and multiple endpoints are used. Its standalone predictive value for therapeutic efficacy is limited; complement with microglia-focused models and downstream CASP-1/pyroptosis readouts to increase translational relevance (wang2019efficacyofnovel pages 1-2, marneros2023roleofinflammasome pages 6-7).
