Assay Overview:  
This assay employs high-content, multiplexed live‐cell imaging in a 96‐well format to simultaneously monitor mitochondrial membrane potential (via TMRM), mitochondrial mass and morphology (via MitoTracker), and reactive oxygen species (via ROS probes) in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells. It is designed to capture dynamic bioenergetic and structural mitochondrial parameters that are altered in dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD). The assay is typically applied to primary human RPE (haRPE) or iPSC‑derived RPE from donors with and without AMD (ferrington2017alteredbioenergeticsand pages 7-7, fisher2022humanipscand pages 11-12).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Mitochondrial dysfunction is a central contributor to AMD pathogenesis. Studies using donor-derived RPE have consistently demonstrated reduced glycolysis, impaired mitochondrial respiration, and increased oxidative stress upon challenge (ferrington2017alteredbioenergeticsand pages 7-7, fisher2022investigatingmitochondrialfission pages 10-10, ferrington2017alteredbioenergeticsand pages 1-2). Moreover, alterations in mitochondrial dynamics, mitophagy, and compensatory metabolic shifts have been linked mechanistically to the progressive degeneration seen in AMD (ferrington2020mitochondrialdefectsdrive pages 8-10, fisher2022investigatingmitochondrialfission pages 9-10). This assay’s ability to provide real-time data on these parameters directly addresses core disease mechanisms relevant to clinical progression and therapeutic intervention.

Previous Use:  
High-content live-cell imaging assays incorporating TMRM, MitoTracker, and ROS probes have been used to profile mitochondrial function and drug responses in RPE models, revealing disease-specific vulnerabilities and differential responses to mitochondrial-targeted therapeutics (fisher2022humanipscand pages 2-4, fisher2022humanipscand pages 14-15, fisher2022investigatingmitochondrialfission pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay has significant strengths: it is scalable, provides multiparametric data on mitochondrial health, and directly targets pathways implicated in dry AMD pathobiology. Challenges include model variability and the need for complementary biochemical validations to overcome potential artifacts from in vitro culture conditions (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 7-8, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 2-3).  

This high-content mitochondrial morphofunction assay is well-suited for early drug discovery in dry AMD. It offers critical insights into mitochondrial dysfunction—a key contributor to the disease—and enables screening of therapeutics that restore cellular bioenergetics in RPE cells.
