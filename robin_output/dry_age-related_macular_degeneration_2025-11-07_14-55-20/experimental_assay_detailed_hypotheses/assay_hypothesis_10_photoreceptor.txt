Assay Overview: This assay employs fluorescently labeled rod outer segments (POS) to quantify RPE phagocytosis using flow cytometry and imaging. It measures key aspects of phagocytic efficiency including binding, internalization, and lysosomal colocalization, which serve as surrogates for uptake and degradation. The assay is applied to cultured RPE cells derived from primary human donors or iPSC-RPE, and its readouts can be modulated under oxidative or genetic stress conditions to mimic aspects of dry AMD pathology (inana2018rpephagocyticfunction pages 1-2, stahl2021evaluatingthephagocytosis pages 1-5).

Biomedical Evidence: RPE dysfunction in POS clearance is intimately linked to the pathogenesis of dry AMD, where impaired phagocytosis contributes to lipofuscin and drusen accumulation, increased oxidative stress, and disrupted autophagy. Clinical and experimental studies have identified altered RPE phagocytic function as a critical early event in AMD progression, underscoring the assay’s relevance (mazzoni2014understandingphotoreceptorouter pages 2-4, liang2023comprehensiveanalysisof pages 1-2).

Previous Use: Similar assays have been successfully used to compare phagocytic efficiencies between normal and AMD-derived RPE cells and to test candidate therapeutics—including cell-conditioned media and receptor-targeted ligands—that rescued phagocytic deficits (inana2018rpephagocyticfunction pages 1-2, zhang2023improvedlipofuscinmodels pages 1-3).

Overall Evaluation: The assay’s strengths lie in its direct measurement of a disease-relevant RPE function, quantitativeness, and adaptability for high-throughput screening. Its weaknesses include potential variability from donor differences and the incomplete recapitulation of the in vivo AMD microenvironment, necessitating follow‐up in complementary models (stahl2021evaluatingthephagocytosis pages 12-16, westenskow2012usingflowcytometry pages 2-2).

This assay is a valuable tool for early-stage drug discovery in dry AMD. It provides mechanistic insights into RPE dysfunction but should be integrated with additional models to fully capture disease complexity.
