Assay Overview: This assay uses live retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells (primary or iPSC‐derived) exposed to an acute H2O2 challenge to mimic oxidative stress, a key driver of dry AMD. TMRM is employed to monitor mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψm) by quantifying fluorescence intensity changes reflective of mitochondrial integrity, while MitoSOX specifically detects mitochondrial superoxide levels. Data acquisition is performed via live-cell high-content imaging and/or flow cytometry (cano2014oxidativestressinduces pages 9-10, he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 7-8).

Biomedical Evidence: Mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress are central to dry AMD pathogenesis. In RPE cells, increased mitochondrial ROS production and decreased Δψm have been linked to impaired ATP production, apoptosis, and altered bioenergetics that underlie disease progression (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 3-5, fisher2018perspectiveonamd pages 2-2). Clinically, oxidative injury is corroborated as a critical contributor to AMD by numerous studies, and antioxidant-based approaches have shown therapeutic benefit (he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 16-17, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 3-5).

Previous Use: Live-cell assays using Δψm dyes (e.g., JC-1, TMRM) and mitochondrial ROS probes (e.g., MitoSOX, DHE) have been successfully applied to RPE oxidative stress models. These methods have demonstrated age-dependent vulnerabilities and have been fundamental in testing mitochondrial-targeted pharmacotherapies (he2010mitochondriaimpairmentcorrelates pages 1-2, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 14-16).

Overall Evaluation: This assay offers robust, rapid functional readouts relevant to the core mechanisms of dry AMD. Strengths include its ability to monitor critical mitochondrial parameters in real time and its applicability to early-stage therapeutic screening. Limitations include the reliance on an acute H2O2 challenge—which may not fully recapitulate chronic disease—and potential issues with dye specificity that necessitate complementary validation (ferrington2017alteredbioenergeticsand pages 7-8, mitter2014dysregulatedautophagyin pages 15-16).

This assay provides a mechanistically relevant approach to evaluate mitochondrial dysfunction in RPE cells for dry AMD drug discovery. It is a valuable tool when used alongside additional endpoints to capture the multifactorial nature of the disease.
