Assay Overview:  
This proposed assay quantifies ABCA1‐mediated efflux of radiolabeled cholesterol from retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells to exogenous acceptors such as apoA‑I or HDL. It models a key pathogenic process in dry AMD—dysfunctional cholesterol export leading to intracellular lipid accumulation and subsequent drusen formation. Measurements include radiolabeled cholesterol release quantified by scintillation counting and complementary lipid accumulation assessments (e.g., OilRed O, Nile red staining). The approach can be performed in various cell sources including human ARPE‑19 lines, iPSC‑derived RPE, and primary human fetal RPE monolayers (lyssenko2018directionalabca1mediatedcholesterol pages 21-23, florian2022regulationofabca1 pages 7-9).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Defective cholesterol efflux in the RPE is central to AMD pathogenesis as supported by genetic and experimental evidence. Loss‐of‐function studies show that impaired ABCA1 activity leads to lipid accumulation, RPE degeneration, and photoreceptor dysfunction, features that mirror dry AMD pathology (federica2019impairedabca1abcg1mediatedlipid pages 1-2, florian2022regulationofabca1 pages 1-2). Human genetic studies further demonstrate that AMD-associated ABCA1 variants compromise efflux function in RPE models (florian2022regulationofabca1 pages 11-12, rao2021cholesterolhomeostasisin pages 31-33).

Previous Use:  
This assay has been previously applied to elucidate the impact of genetic risk alleles and environmental stressors (e.g., hypoxia) on ABCA1 function in RPE, and to test the efficacy of LXR agonists in restoring efflux capacity (florian2022regulationofabca1 pages 7-9, lyssenko2018directionalabca1mediatedcholesterol pages 5-7).

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths include a direct, quantitative readout of a mechanism that is both molecularly and clinically relevant to dry AMD, as well as its adaptability across different RPE models for pharmacologic modulation. Weaknesses involve potential variability between cell sources, challenges in high-throughput adaptation, and the need to correlate in vitro efflux changes with in vivo therapeutic outcomes (federica2019impairedabca1abcg1mediatedlipid pages 16-18, lyssenko2018directionalabca1mediatedcholesterol pages 11-13).

This assay is a promising tool for assessing therapeutics targeting cholesterol dysregulation in dry AMD. It provides a biologically relevant, mechanistic readout that can drive early-stage drug discovery and validation efforts.
