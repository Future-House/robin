Assay Overview:  
This assay employs BODIPY and Oil Red O staining to quantify the accumulation of neutral lipid droplets—and potentially bisretinoid‐induced droplets—in RPE cells, typically using ARPE‐19 or primary human RPE cultures. The assay models the intracellular lipid handling defect observed in dry AMD, where excess lipid accumulation compromises RPE function. Quantitative imaging (e.g., automated time‐lapse microscopy and fluorescence quantification) is used to measure lipid droplet number, size, and intensity.

Biomedical Evidence:  
Dysregulated lipid metabolism is central to dry AMD pathogenesis, as studies have linked intracellular lipid accumulation with impaired phagocytosis, oxidative stress, and downstream complement activation. Genetic models (e.g., RPE‐specific ABCA1/ABCG1 knockouts) recapitulate key AMD features by promoting lipid deposition and subsequent retinal degeneration. Moreover, both clinical correlations and in vitro data underscore the role of lipid dyshomeostasis in drusen biogenesis and inflammatory activation (federica2019impairedabca1abcg1mediatedlipid pages 2-3, landowski2022targetinglipidmetabolism pages 1-2).

Previous Use:  
Multiple studies using ARPE‐19 cells with fatty acid loading and oleic acid models have successfully employed lipid droplet staining to evaluate alterations in lipid homeostasis and to screen compounds such as GLP‐1 receptor agonists (tsou2025liraglutideattenuatesffainduced pages 1-2, tsou2025liraglutideattenuatesffainduced pages 10-11). These studies have revealed that pharmacologic inhibition of droplet formation rescues phagocytic and oxidative defects.

Overall Evaluation:  
The assay’s primary strength lies in its quantitative capture of a disease-relevant phenotype—altered lipid homeostasis—in a controlled in vitro system. Its simplicity, reproducibility, and adaptability for high-throughput screening make it attractive for early drug discovery. However, the assay does not encompass extracellular deposit formation, complement activation, or the full complexity of AMD pathology, necessitating complementary functional endpoints and in vivo validation (yako2022lipiddropletaccumulation pages 5-7, lin2022dyslipidemiainagerelated pages 5-6).

This lipid droplet accumulation assay offers a valuable platform to screen compounds targeting lipid dysregulation in dry AMD. It should be integrated with additional assays to address broader aspects of disease pathogenesis.
