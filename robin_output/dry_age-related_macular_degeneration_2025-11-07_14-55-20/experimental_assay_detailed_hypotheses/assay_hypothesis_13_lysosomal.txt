Assay Overview: This assay models an early, calcium-triggered membrane repair response of RPE to sub-lytic complement attack by quantifying lysosomal exocytosis as β-hexosaminidase (β-Hex) activity released into apical and basal compartments after 10% NHS or purified C9 challenge, with heat-inactivated serum and C9-depleted serum as controls to define specificity and sub-lytic conditions (minutes scale) (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 1-3, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 106-116). β-Hex is measured fluorimetrically using 4-methyl-umbelliferyl-N-acetyl-β-D-glucosaminide, ideally on polarized primary or hiPSC-RPE monolayers on Transwells to capture secretion polarity and paired with orthogonal readouts (surface LAMP2, Syt7-pHluorin TIRF exocytic events, LDH cytotoxicity, Ca2+ imaging) (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 6-6, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 97-102). Cells: polarized primary porcine RPE, Abca4−/− mouse RPE ex vivo, or human iPSC-RPE (including CFH Y402H risk) to span genetic and lipofuscin/cholesterol risk states (tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 97-102, cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2).

Biomedical Evidence: Complement dysregulation with increased MAC deposition is a central AMD feature; sub-lytic MAC perturbs barrier integrity, elevates Ca2+, and drives mitochondrial stress and proinflammatory signaling in RPE (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 1-1, tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 5-6). RPE mounts a rapid lysosome–plasma membrane fusion within seconds–minutes that limits sustained Ca2+ rises and prevents MAC-induced mitochondrial injury; failure of this response with cholesterol/ASMase activation (e.g., A2E, oxLDL, Abca4−/−) links directly to AMD-associated pathology (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 1-3, tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 4-5). Human iPSC-RPE with CFH Y402H exhibit increased C5b-9 handling at lysosomes, swollen/fragile lysosomes, and rescue by C3 inhibition, reinforcing a clinically relevant complement–lysosome axis (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2). The RPE’s extreme lysosomal burden and age-related lipofuscin/cholesterol stressors couple lysosomal function and extracellular deposit biology to AMD progression (cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 58-61, cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 34-37).

Previous Use: β-Hex release after NHS is established as a biochemical readout of complement-triggered lysosomal exocytosis, exhibiting apical/basal polarity and strict dependence on active complement (HI-NHS negative) (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 1-3). Live Syt7-pHluorin imaging confirms exocytosis within seconds; events and β-Hex release are strongly reduced by A2E and restored by cholesterol efflux or ASMase inhibition, which also normalizes CD59 recycling and prevents complement-induced mitochondrial injury in vitro and in Abca4−/− mice (tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 80-84, tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 4-5). The enzymatic assay is specified (4-MU substrate) and used alongside surface LAMP2, MAC quantification, Ca2+ imaging, and LDH to define sub-lytic windows (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 6-6, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 106-116).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—mechanistically anchored to a rapid, Syt7-dependent defense directly engaged by AMD-relevant sub-lytic MAC; quantitative, polarity-resolved, and pharmacologically responsive to cholesterol/ASMase modulation with in vivo corroboration; readily integrated with orthogonal imaging and viability controls (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 5-6, tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 4-5, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 97-102). Weaknesses—published work lacks assay performance metrics (dynamic range, Z’), and β-Hex is an indirect proxy requiring controls for lysis (LDH), validation against LAMP2/Syt7 imaging, and discrimination from passive leakage; pigment and dye confounds and the acute nature of the readout necessitate pairing with chronic endpoints and clinical correlation (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 1-1, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 106-116, cardoso2018stemcellderivedretinal pages 164-167, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 7-12).

This assay captures a validated, drug-modifiable RPE defense against sub-lytic complement central to AMD biology and is well-suited for mechanistic screening, especially for cholesterol/ASMase/complement-modulating strategies (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 1-1, tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 5-6). It should be implemented with stringent orthogonal validation and viability controls and complemented by chronic functional readouts to enhance translational relevance (tan2016protectiveresponsesto pages 6-6, tan2018organelletraffickingin pages 106-116).
