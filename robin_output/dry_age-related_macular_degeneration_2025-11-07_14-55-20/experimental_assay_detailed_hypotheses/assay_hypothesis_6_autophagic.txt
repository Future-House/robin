Assay Overview: The tandem LC3 reporter assay employs an mCherry‐GFP‐LC3 construct to distinguish autophagosomes (displaying both GFP and mCherry fluorescence) from autolysosomes (exhibiting only mCherry signal due to GFP quenching in acidic environments) in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells. This method quantifies autophagic flux under conditions of A2E photooxidation or drug treatment, following protocols similar to those described by Pinelli et al. (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8, pinelli2025retinalautophagyfor pages 25-26). Primary RPE or ARPE-19 cell lines, subject to oxidative stress, provide the biological context, with fluorescence microscopy and image quantification serving as key readouts.

Biomedical Evidence: Autophagy dysregulation is central to dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) pathogenesis, as impaired clearance of oxidized proteins, lipids, and damaged mitochondria in the RPE underlies drusen formation and functional decline (nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 9-11, pinelli2023autophagyactivationpromoted pages 7-9). The assay models this disease-relevant autophagic impairment by measuring LC3 dynamics under A2E-induced oxidative stress, a known contributor to lipofuscin accumulation and AMD progression (nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 11-13, pinelli2025retinalautophagyfor pages 3-4).

Previous Use: This assay has been successfully used to validate autophagic flux modulation in RPE under various conditions, with pharmacologic controls (e.g., chloroquine) demonstrating predictable alterations in fluorescence patterns (cox2025age‐associateddeclinein pages 7-8). Prior studies have also linked LC3 lipidation changes to therapeutic interventions, including light-induced autophagy and phytochemical treatments in AMD models (pinelli2025retinalautophagyfor pages 25-26, nita2023antioxidativeroleof pages 16-18).

Overall Evaluation: The main strength of this assay lies in its ability to dynamically measure autophagosome formation and degradation in a system highly relevant to AMD pathology. However, its interpretation may be challenged by RPE-specific lysosomal dysfunction and the need for complementary biochemical readouts (e.g., p62 turnover) to fully validate autophagic flux alterations (pinelli2023autophagyactivationpromoted pages 2-4, pinelli2023autophagyactivationpromoted pages 23-24).

This assay offers a robust and clinically relevant approach to evaluating therapeutics for dry AMD by directly linking autophagic dynamics to disease mechanisms. It is particularly useful when paired with complementary assays to overcome inherent limitations in RPE lysosomal function.
