Proposal for Astrocyte-derived exosomes enriched for miR-146a (Research product)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
Astrocyte‐derived exosomes enriched for miR-146a are an innovative, biomimetic class of therapeutic agents that combine the natural properties of extracellular vesicles with the regulatory functions of microRNAs. Exosomes are nano‐sized vesicles (typically 40–160 nm in diameter) generated in the endosomal pathway and released by almost all cell types, including astrocytes, which are a fundamental component of the retinal microenvironment (Heo & Ryu, 2024). In this candidate, exosomes are isolated from retinal astrocytes and selectively engineered to become enriched with miR-146a, a microRNA known for its anti‐inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties. Because astrocytes are intimately involved in neuronal support and retinal homeostasis, their exosomes offer an inherent targeting advantage for diseased retinal regions, particularly in age-related macular degeneration (AMD) where the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) is compromised (Romero et al., 2024). The enrichment with miR-146a transforms these naturally secreted vesicles into a targeted drug delivery vehicle that can modulate specific intracellular pathways in the RPE. This approach falls within the broader therapeutic class of exosome‐based drug delivery systems, a class that has increasingly attracted attention for its ability to transport nucleic acids, proteins, and lipids with high specificity and minimal immunogenicity (Du & Palczewski, 2022). Exosome therapy has been previously explored in preclinical models for various retinal pathologies using different source cells, such as mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) and RPE cells (Heo & Ryu, 2024; Liu et al., 2023). Engineering exosomes to carry therapeutic miRNAs like miR-146a exemplifies a precision medicine strategy in which the carrier is not only inert but provides its own biological effects derived from its cell of origin, contributing to the overall therapeutic profile.

Therapeutic History:  
While there is robust literature documenting the application of exosome-based therapies in ocular diseases, the specific use of astrocyte-derived exosomes enriched with miR-146a is a relatively novel proposition. In several studies, exosomes isolated from mesenchymal stem cells have been engineered to carry anti-inflammatory microRNAs, including miR-146a, and have shown neuroprotective effects in retinal degeneration models such as retinitis pigmentosa and diabetic retinopathy (Zhang et al., 2022). Moreover, astrocyte-derived exosomes have been explored for their intrinsic neuroprotective and anti‐angiogenic properties. For instance, retinal astrocyte-derived exosomes have been demonstrated to contain multiple anti-angiogenic molecules including endostatin, PEDF, and TIMP-1, which contribute to the suppression of choroidal neovascularization in laser-induced models (Mead & Chow, 2023; Romero et al., 2024). Although these studies primarily target neovascular AMD, their underlying mechanisms emphasize the utility of astrocyte-based exosome therapy in modulating critical retinal pathways. In the context of dry AMD, which is characterized by chronic RPE degeneration, oxidative stress, and complement system dysregulation, the strategy of utilizing exosomal delivery to target RPE dysfunction is supported by evidence that exosome‐mediated miRNA transfer can restore lysosomal function and reduce inflammatory cascades in retinal cells (ElShelmani et al., 2021; Guan et al., 2025). Additionally, complement dysregulation and increased complement factor expression are central features of dry AMD pathology, and prior studies have implicated exosomal cargo in modulating inflammatory and complement pathways (Guan et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023). Although direct clinical or extensive preclinical experience with astrocyte-derived exosomes enriched for miR-146a in dry AMD is limited, the analogous applications of MSC-derived exosomes carrying miR-146a establish an important precedent and provide a mechanistic rationale for further investigation (Zhang et al., 2022; Berber et al., 2017).

Mechanism of Action:  
The therapeutic mechanism of this candidate operates at multiple molecular levels, integrating the known functions of both miR-146a and exosomal delivery systems. miR-146a is recognized as a key negative regulator of inflammation through its ability to target components in the Toll-like receptor (TLR) and nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) signaling pathways. Specifically, miR-146a targets interleukin-1 receptor–associated kinase 1 (IRAK1) and TNF receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6), leading to a reduction in NF-κB activation and subsequent downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Berber et al., 2017; ElShelmani et al., 2021). In the context of dry AMD, complement overactivation has been identified as a pivotal event contributing to RPE degeneration. Complement factor H (CFH) and complement factor B (CFB) play significant roles in regulating the alternative complement pathway, and dysregulation of these factors leads to excessive complement activation, resulting in increased levels of complement fragments such as C3a that promote inflammation and lysosomal stress (ElShelmani et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021). The proposed mechanism asserts that miR-146a, delivered via astrocyte-derived exosomes, can penetrate the RPE cell layer and downregulate the transcription and/or translation of complement components such as CFH and CFB. By limiting the production of these factors, miR-146a effectively reduces the downstream formation of the inflammatory anaphylatoxin C3a, alleviating lysosomal overload and preserving the acidic lysosomal environment necessary for optimal activity of degradative enzymes like cathepsin D (Heo & Ryu, 2024; Zhang et al., 2022). This modulation of complement activity is essential because aberrant complement activation in the RPE has been linked to photoreceptor outer segment (POS) accumulation and impaired phagocytosis, which are hallmarks of dry AMD progression (Romero et al., 2024; Sanghani et al., 2022). Furthermore, exosomes themselves possess intrinsic targeting and cell–cell communication properties; their membrane proteins, such as tetraspanins (CD63, CD81) and other surface markers, facilitate uptake by recipient cells and can mediate tissue-specific homing to the RPE (Liu et al., 2023; Romero et al., 2024). The combination of miR-146a’s molecular action on inflammatory and complement pathways with the natural delivery proficiency of astrocyte-derived exosomes creates a synergistic effect that is expected to restore lysosomal integrity and enhance functional POS degradation in high-risk CFH backgrounds (ElShelmani et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2022).

Expected Effect:  
The expected therapeutic effect in the treatment of dry AMD is multilayered. In vitro studies utilizing RPE cell cultures derived from patient lines with high-risk CFH expression are anticipated to show that treatment with miR-146a-enriched astrocyte exosomes results in a significant downregulation of complement factors, notably CFH and CFB. This downregulation would be reflected in reduced levels of the complement activation fragment C3a, which is known to exacerbate inflammation and lysosomal dysfunction in RPE cells (Romero et al., 2024; Berber et al., 2017). As a direct consequence of complement normalization, lysosomal integrity is expected to be maintained through the preservation of an acidic pH environment and the restoration of cathepsin D activity. Cathepsin D plays a critical role in the degradation of POS material taken up by RPE cells during the daily process of photoreceptor renewal (Liu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2022). Enhanced clearance of POS is hypothesized to reduce the accumulation of toxic waste products and drusen formation, a defining characteristic of dry AMD. In addition, the anti-inflammatory action of miR-146a should attenuate the chronic low-grade inflammation observed in the retinal microenvironment, thereby mitigating further RPE and photoreceptor damage (Heo & Ryu, 2024; Liu et al., 2020).  
At the molecular level, the treatment is anticipated to modulate intracellular signaling cascades by reducing NF-κB–mediated transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines, which in turn helps to suppress the local inflammatory milieu and prevent further complement activation (Berber et al., 2017; ElShelmani et al., 2021). The exosomal vehicle ensures that miR-146a is delivered in a protected and concentrated form, which maximizes cellular uptake and preserves functional integrity during circulation. Given their natural ability to cross biological barriers, these astrocyte-derived exosomes are expected to efficiently reach the RPE layer, and this targeted delivery is crucial for achieving the desired therapeutic outcomes with minimal off-target effects (Guan et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023). Furthermore, the experimental assays based on RPE cell cultures and animal models of dry AMD are designed to evaluate both the biological activity of the delivered miR-146a and the restoration of lysosomal function. Expected readouts include a decrease in complement factor mRNA and protein levels, improved lysosomal enzyme activity (especially cathepsin D), reduced accumulation of POS debris, and ultimately, preservation of RPE and photoreceptor viability (Guan et al., 2025; ElShelmani et al., 2021). These effects, if validated, could translate into a slowing of disease progression or even a reversal of certain pathological features in dry AMD.

Overall Evaluation:  
The candidate therapeutic of astrocyte-derived exosomes enriched for miR-146a holds considerable promise for the treatment of dry AMD due to its innovative design and strong mechanistic rationale. One of the most significant strengths of this approach lies in its biomimetic nature. By harnessing exosomes derived from retinal astrocytes, the therapy benefits from inherent targeting properties that increase the likelihood of efficient delivery to the RPE, a key cell type implicated in dry AMD pathology (Romero et al., 2024; Mead & Chow, 2023). Furthermore, the use of miR-146a—an endogenous regulatory molecule with well-documented anti-inflammatory effects—adds an additional layer of specificity to the treatment by directly modulating the inflammatory milieu and complement dysregulation that are central to dry AMD (Berber et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2022). This dual action, combining restoration of lysosomal integrity through improved POS degradation and suppression of complement-mediated inflammation, addresses two critical pathogenic processes simultaneously.  

However, despite its notable strengths, several challenges and uncertainties remain. First, while the therapeutic concept is strongly supported by preclinical studies involving MSC-derived exosomes and by literature demonstrating the beneficial effects of exosomal miRNAs in retinal diseases (Zhang et al., 2022; Du & Palczewski, 2022), the specific application of astrocyte-derived exosomes enriched with miR-146a for dry AMD remains to be thoroughly evaluated. There is limited direct evidence in the literature showing that astrocyte-derived exosomes naturally enriched or engineered to carry miR-146a can downregulate complement factor expression in RPE cells in a clinically meaningful way (Liu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2023). This represents a gap in knowledge that would need to be addressed through rigorous preclinical investigation, including detailed dose–response studies and assessments of long-term efficacy and safety.  

Another potential weakness lies in the scalability and reproducibility of exosome isolation from astrocytes. Exosome production can vary depending on the source, the culture conditions, and the methods used for enrichment and purification. Manufacturing a consistent and high-quality exosome product that meets clinical grade standards is a well-recognized challenge in the field of exosome therapeutics (Liu et al., 2023; Guan et al., 2025). In addition, while exosomes are known for their low immunogenicity, the possibility of immune reactions, especially when they are over-engineered or when administered repeatedly, cannot be entirely discounted and will require careful evaluation (Romero et al., 2024).  

From a mechanistic standpoint, although the theoretical framework linking miR-146a with the downregulation of complement factors CFH and CFB is compelling, the precise molecular interactions within RPE cells must be validated by robust in vitro and in vivo experiments. Detailed studies are needed to confirm the extent to which miR-146a directly modulates these complement components, and whether additional targets or off-target effects might complicate the therapeutic outcome (Zhang et al., 2022; Berber et al., 2017). Furthermore, while the restoration of lysosomal function and POS degradation is a promising endpoint, the complexity of lysosomal biology in the context of chronic degenerative diseases such as dry AMD means that multifactorial interventions might be necessary. It will be essential to demonstrate that the observed improvements in lysosomal enzyme activity (e.g., cathepsin D) translate into meaningful clinical benefits, such as slowed disease progression or improved visual function (ElShelmani et al., 2021; Romero et al., 2024).  

In summary, the concept of using astrocyte-derived exosomes enriched for miR-146a as a therapeutic candidate for dry AMD is innovative, leveraging the natural targeting, biocompatibility, and multi-functional capabilities of exosomes along with the potent anti-inflammatory and complement‐modulating properties of miR-146a. The preclinical evidence derived from analogous studies using MSC-derived exosomes and the general literature on exosomal miRNA therapies in retinal diseases provides a solid foundation for this approach (Zhang et al., 2022; Heo & Ryu, 2024; Liu et al., 2023). Nevertheless, the strategy will require comprehensive validation, including optimization of exosome isolation and engineering protocols, detailed mechanistic studies in relevant cellular models, and robust in vivo efficacy assessments in dry AMD animal models before clinical translation can be considered. With targeted efforts to address these challenges, this therapeutic candidate could represent a significant advance in the treatment of dry AMD, offering a precision medicine approach that directly tackles both complement overactivation and lysosomal dysfunction at the cellular level (Du & Palczewski, 2022; Guan et al., 2025; ElShelmani et al., 2021).

Overall, the combination of bioengineered exosomes and miR-146a offers mechanistic specificity and a novel route of administration that may overcome many of the limitations inherent in current dry AMD therapies, particularly those related to anti-VEGF treatments which are primarily indicated for wet AMD. While further preclinical investigations are essential to fully elucidate the pharmacodynamics, safety profile, and therapeutic window of this candidate, the approach holds promise as a next-generation drug candidate for dry AMD that could potentially improve patient outcomes by preserving retinal structure and function through the reestablishment of homeostatic cellular processes (Mead & Chow, 2023; Sanghani et al., 2022; Song et al., 2024).
