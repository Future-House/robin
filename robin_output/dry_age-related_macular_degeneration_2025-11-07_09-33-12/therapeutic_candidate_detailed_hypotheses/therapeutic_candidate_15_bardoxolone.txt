Proposal for Bardoxolone Methyl (CDDO-Me; Reata Pharmaceuticals)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
Bardoxolone methyl (also known as CDDO‐Me) is a fully synthetic derivative of the naturally occurring pentacyclic triterpenoid oleanolic acid. The parent molecule, oleanolic acid, is widely found in many plants and has been used for centuries in traditional medicine owing to its modest anti‐inflammatory and hepatoprotective properties. However, to overcome its relatively low intrinsic potency and poor pharmacokinetics, medicinal chemists modified oleanolic acid by introducing key structural changes. In particular, the synthesis of CDDO (2-cyano-3,12-dioxoolean-1,9-dien-28-oic acid) involved the installation of a cyano group at the C2 position and oxidation at positions C3 and C12, generating an electrophilic cyanoenone moiety; subsequent esterification at the C28 carboxyl group yielded bardoxolone methyl as the C28 methyl ester. These chemical modifications resulted in a molecule with two electrophilic Michael acceptor sites that are essential for its biological action, notably its ability to react reversibly with cysteine thiols in target proteins. Being part of the synthetic oleanane triterpenoids family, bardoxolone methyl falls into a class of compounds that has been extensively explored for their anti‐inflammatory, antioxidant, anticancer, and cytoprotective activities in various disease models (Borella et al., 2019).

This class of synthetic triterpenoids has historically been pursued to potentiate the natural but modest bioactivities of plant‐derived triterpenes such as oleanolic and ursolic acids. Early research aimed at enhancing anti‐inflammatory activity led to the discovery of potent derivatives such as CDDO, CDDO‐Im (the imidazolide derivative), and ultimately bardoxolone methyl. Their design was motivated by the need to generate highly potent, orally bioavailable agents capable of activating key cytoprotective pathways. The robust preclinical efficacy observed with these compounds in various models of oxidative stress, inflammation, and even tumorigenesis provided proof‐of‐concept for their use as drug candidates (Borella et al., 2019; Sakashita et al., 2021). Consequently, bardoxolone methyl has emerged as a leading representative of this chemical class, notable for its finely tuned electrophilic reactivity and its ability to induce a broad spectrum of protective gene products.

Therapeutic History:  
Bardoxolone methyl has a well‐documented history of investigation in both preclinical and clinical settings across various therapeutic indications. Originally developed for its potent anti‐inflammatory and antioxidant properties, it was first investigated in oncology where its pro‐apoptotic and antiproliferative effects were characterized in multiple cancer cell types such as pancreatic, colorectal, ovarian, and breast cancer. Early preclinical studies demonstrated that at nanomolar and low micromolar concentrations, CDDO and its derivatives could exert robust cytoprotective effects via the activation of the nuclear factor erythroid 2‐related factor 2 (Nrf2) pathway, as well as induce cell cycle arrest and trigger apoptosis in cancer cells (Borella et al., 2019; Tanase et al., 2022).

Following its anticancer exploration, a serendipitous clinical observation during early oncology trials revealed that patients treated with bardoxolone methyl experienced improved renal function, as measured by an elevation in the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). This finding prompted a pivot in therapeutic focus toward kidney diseases, particularly chronic kidney disease (CKD) associated with type 2 diabetes. The promising renoprotective data initially generated in Phase I oncology studies led to Phase II trials (such as the BEAM trial) where bardoxolone methyl displayed remarkable efficacy in increasing eGFR in CKD patients. However, in a subsequent Phase III study (the BEACON trial), concerns were raised after an unexpected increase in cardiovascular events, particularly heart failure, halted the trial. These mixed clinical outcomes have since spurred ongoing efforts to better understand and optimize the safety profile of bardoxolone methyl while leveraging its strong cytoprotective properties (Mathis & Cui, 2016; Sakashita et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2014).

Notably, while bardoxolone methyl has been extensively investigated in oncology and renal diseases, there is currently no record or published clinical trial data indicating that this compound has been used for treating dry age‐related macular degeneration (dry AMD) or similar ocular conditions. Nonetheless, its underlying mechanism—primarily Nrf2 activation—is highly relevant to the pathobiology of dry AMD, given that oxidative stress and proteostasis imbalance are central to RPE cell dysfunction. Thus, while previous therapeutic applications have focused on systemic diseases, the repositioning of bardoxolone methyl to an ocular indication represents a scientifically plausible and innovative strategy that builds on its earlier demonstrated biochemical activities in other organ systems (Borella et al., 2019; Sakashita et al., 2021).

Mechanism of Action:  
Bardoxolone methyl’s primary mode of action is its potent and selective activation of the Keap1/Nrf2/ARE (antioxidant response element) pathway. Under basal conditions, Nrf2 is sequestered in the cytoplasm by its inhibitory binding partner Keap1, which targets Nrf2 for ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation. Bardoxolone methyl, owing to its electrophilic cyanoenone structure, covalently modifies reactive cysteine residues on Keap1 (including key sensors such as Cys151), thereby inhibiting its E3 ligase activity. This modification prevents the degradation of Nrf2, allowing it to accumulate and translocate into the nucleus (Hong et al., 2012; Mathis & Cui, 2016).

Once in the nucleus, Nrf2 dimerizes with small Maf proteins and binds to ARE sequences in the promoter regions of various cytoprotective genes. This leads to the transcriptional induction of a battery of antioxidant and phase II detoxification enzymes, including heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), NAD(P)H quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1), glutathione S-transferases, and several molecular chaperones involved in protein folding and proteostasis. Through these gene products, bardoxolone methyl exerts robust antioxidant, anti‐inflammatory, and cytoprotective effects that help mitigate oxidative stress and repair cellular damage (Mathis & Cui, 2020; Tanase et al., 2022).

In addition to Nrf2‐mediated gene induction, bardoxolone methyl has been shown to modulate other signaling pathways that contribute to its anti‐inflammatory profile. For instance, it can inhibit NF-κB signaling, which reduces the transcription of pro‐inflammatory cytokines, and also affect pathways involving JAK/STAT and PI3K/Akt/mTOR, all of which have been implicated in the control of cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival (Sporn et al., 2011). The dual capacity to both upregulate protective gene networks and downregulate inflammatory signaling is central to the pharmacological action of bardoxolone methyl and underscores its potential to modulate cellular processes that are dysregulated in conditions characterized by oxidative and proteostatic stress (Cores et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2014).

Furthermore, crystallographic and proteomic studies have provided molecular‐level evidence for the reversible Michael‐addition interaction between bardoxolone methyl and target cysteine residues on Keap1. This “dock and lock” mechanism ensures that the electrophilic reactivity of the compound is directed preferentially toward specific regulatory cysteines, thereby limiting non‐specific alkylation and supporting its relatively high potency as an Nrf2 activator (Borella et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2014).

Expected Effect in Dry AMD:  
The hypothesis for repurposing bardoxolone methyl in dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) is grounded firmly in its ability to activate Nrf2 and thereby bolster the endogenous antioxidant defenses of the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE). In the RPE, oxidative damage is a pivotal pathomechanism that contributes to the accumulation of lipofuscin, impairment of autophagic flux, and ultimately, decreased phagocytic clearance of photoreceptor outer segments (POS). As Nrf2 plays a critical role in maintaining redox homeostasis within the RPE, its activation is expected to alleviate oxidative and proteostatic stress, thereby protecting critical functions such as phagocytosis and lysosomal degradation.

At an effective concentration in the submicromolar range (with an EC₅₀ estimated around 0.1 µM), bardoxolone methyl is anticipated to induce the transcription of genes such as HO-1 and NQO1 as well as chaperone proteins that ensure proper protein folding and degradation. The upregulation of these cytoprotective proteins may protect phagocytic receptors from oxidative modifications and preserve lysosomal enzymatic activity, which is essential for the degradation of ingested POS. This action is particularly crucial because impaired phagocytic function contributes to the accumulation of toxic lipofuscin and other debris in RPE cells—a hallmark of dry AMD pathogenesis (Borella et al., 2019; Sakashita et al., 2021).

Moreover, the robust activation of Nrf2 by bardoxolone methyl can help counterbalance the deficit in endogenous antioxidant capacity observed in the RPE of patients with dry AMD, as Nrf2 deficiency has been shown experimentally to exacerbate lipofuscin accumulation and autophagy blockade. Preclinical studies in non‐ocular systems have consistently demonstrated that treatment with CDDO‐Me leads to a marked increase in the expression of antioxidant genes and proteins that are implicated in both oxidative stress management and the maintenance of proteostasis (Hong et al., 2012; Sporn et al., 2011). Extrapolating from these findings, it is reasonable to expect that treatment with bardoxolone methyl in dry AMD cultures would result in a more effective clearance of POS by correcting the underlying oxidative imbalance and restoring lysosomal function. It is also plausible that enhanced expression of chaperones and stress‐response proteins would contribute to improved recycling of degraded photoreceptor material, thereby reducing the burden of intracellular debris (Cores et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2014).

In addition, the effects of bardoxolone methyl on other signaling cascades—such as its inhibitory impact on NF-κB—could further reduce inflammatory responses within the RPE. Chronic inflammation is increasingly recognized as a contributing factor to AMD progression, and the suppression of pro‐inflammatory mediators would be expected to create a more favorable cellular environment for tissue repair and maintenance of RPE function (Sporn et al., 2011). Collectively, by activating Nrf2 and modulating inflammatory signaling pathways, bardoxolone methyl is expected to restore the cellular homeostasis of RPE cells, leading to enhanced phagocytosis of POS and potentially slowing or even reversing the degenerative processes characteristic of dry AMD.

Overall Evaluation:  
Bardoxolone methyl represents an innovative and promising candidate for repurposing in the treatment of dry age‐related macular degeneration, particularly given its well‐characterized ability to activate the Nrf2 pathway and trigger a potent antioxidant and proteostatic response. One clear strength of this compound is its robust preclinical and clinical profile in other indications; extensive studies in oncology and chronic kidney disease have established its capability to induce protective gene networks, coupled with a well‐defined mechanism of action that is directly applicable to combating oxidative stress—one of the central features of dry AMD pathogenesis (Borella et al., 2019).

From a mechanistic standpoint, bardoxolone methyl’s electrophilic structure enables it to interact in a highly regulated manner with the Keap1 protein. This interaction leads to the stabilization and nuclear translocation of Nrf2, culminating in the transcriptional activation of a spectrum of cytoprotective genes, including HO-1, NQO1, and various chaperones. These genes are known to be expressed in RPE cells and are essential for maintaining the integrity of phagocytic receptors and lysosomal enzymes that mediate POS clearance. Thus, the molecular rationale for its use in dry AMD is strong, as it addresses a fundamental aspect of RPE cell dysfunction (Mathis & Cui, 2016; Hong et al., 2012).

In terms of therapeutic history, while bardoxolone methyl has not previously been applied to ocular indications, its repositioning for dry AMD is scientifically warranted by the extensive evidence linking Nrf2 deficiency to the progression of RPE degeneration and lipofuscin accumulation. The previously observed benefits in non‐ocular systems, such as improvements in renal function via Nrf2 activation, support the idea that similar cytoprotective processes could be beneficial in the eye, particularly if local delivery or ocular formulation approaches can be developed to maximize efficacy while minimizing systemic exposure (Sakashita et al., 2021; Tanase et al., 2022).

Nevertheless, there are several challenges and weaknesses that must be carefully considered. First, the systemic use of bardoxolone methyl has been marred by significant cardiovascular safety concerns, as evidenced by the terminated BEACON trial in CKD patients. Although these adverse events were observed in a systemic context and might be mitigated by localized (for example, intravitreal) administration in the eye, they nonetheless raise caution regarding the compound’s off‐target effects and overall safety profile. The intrinsic electrophilicity that underlies its mechanism also bears the potential risk of non‐specific protein modification and cytotoxicity if not carefully controlled, particularly in a delicate tissue such as the retina (Mathis & Cui, 2016; Zhou et al., 2014).

Second, while preclinical ocular safety data are being cited as supportive evidence for repositioning, robust and comprehensive studies specific to retinal tissues and RPE cell models must be undertaken to confirm that the drug does not induce unintended damage or disrupt other vital functions of the retina. Pharmacokinetic challenges—such as achieving and maintaining an effective therapeutic concentration in the RPE without eliciting systemic exposure—will also need to be addressed. The EC₅₀ for Nrf2 activation in RPE is hypothesized to be approximately 0.1 µM, and this level must be reliably achieved in an ocular setting, ideally through controlled‐release formulations or targeted delivery systems (Cores et al., 2020).

An additional consideration is the complexity of dry AMD itself, which is influenced not only by oxidative stress and impaired autophagy but also by chronic low‐grade inflammation, complement dysregulation, and extracellular matrix alterations in Bruch’s membrane. Although bardoxolone methyl’s anti‐inflammatory effects via NF-κB inhibition are a potential asset, it remains to be determined how these multiple pathways will interact within the unique microenvironment of the retina. Tailored preclinical studies that specifically evaluate the compound’s impact on POS clearance, RPE phagocytosis, and lysosomal function in models of dry AMD are essential to validate the underlying hypothesis (Sporn et al., 2011; Zhou et al., 2014).

On the strength side, the repositioning of bardoxolone methyl is supported by its extensive clinical investigation in other indications. The existence of Phase I and II human data provides a valuable foundation regarding dosing, pharmacokinetics, and some aspects of safety, which can significantly de‐risk early translational studies in the ocular domain. The compound’s well‐documented capacity to activate Nrf2—a pathway known to be highly relevant in the aging RPE and in the pathogenesis of dry AMD—further underscores its potential utility. Moreover, the synthetic nature of bardoxolone methyl facilitates the possibility of further chemical optimization and the development of novel formulations (such as nanoparticle‐ or liposome‐based ocular delivery systems) that may enhance its safety and efficacy profile when administered locally (Borella et al., 2019; Borella et al., 2019; Sporn et al., 2011).

In summary, bardoxolone methyl appears to be a promising candidate for repurposing in dry age‐related macular degeneration based on its potent Nrf2‐activating properties and its capacity to upregulate antioxidant and proteostasis genes. These effects could theoretically restore and enhance the phagocytic function of RPE cells, thereby improving the clearance of photoreceptor outer segments and reducing lipofuscin accumulation—a central pathogenic feature of dry AMD. However, significant challenges remain, including addressing the compound’s systemic safety concerns, ensuring specific ocular delivery, and confirming efficacy and safety in relevant RPE and retinal models. Rigorous preclinical studies focusing on ocular pharmacodynamics, toxicity, and long‐term effects in models of dry AMD are warranted before advancing to clinical trials for this indication. While the repurposing hypothesis is scientifically sound and mechanistically compelling, the unique challenges of ocular drug development must be navigated cautiously (Mathis & Cui, 2016; Tanase et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2014; Thomas, 2012; Dao et al., 2015).

Overall Evaluation:  
Bardoxolone methyl’s robust mechanistic action as an Nrf2 activator—coupled with its documented preclinical and early clinical efficacy in other indications—positions it as a highly attractive repurposed drug candidate for dry AMD. Its molecular design, derived from oleanolic acid and enhanced through strategic electrophilic modifications, underpins a powerful ability to upregulate antioxidant defenses and restore proteostasis. These actions directly address the cellular deficits observed in dry AMD, namely the accumulation of oxidative damage, impaired autophagy, and subsequent dysfunction of the RPE.  

Strengths of the candidate include:  
• A well‐characterized synthetic origin with clearly defined structure–activity relationships, ensuring potent Nrf2 activation at low concentrations (Borella et al., 2019).  
• Substantial preclinical data indicating its ability to induce cytoprotective gene networks such as HO-1, NQO1, and molecular chaperones that are crucial for maintaining RPE function (Mathis & Cui, 2016; Hong et al., 2012).  
• An extensive history in clinical testing in systemic diseases (oncology and CKD), which provides valuable information regarding dosing and pharmacokinetics that could inform ocular application (Zhou et al., 2014; Sakashita et al., 2021).  
• The underlying hypothesis is supported by evidence that Nrf2 deficiency exacerbates RPE dysfunction and lipofuscin accumulation, suggesting that restoring Nrf2 activity could ameliorate key pathological features of dry AMD.

Weaknesses and challenges include:  
• The previous systemic administration of bardoxolone methyl was associated with significant cardiovascular adverse events, and while local delivery to the eye may mitigate this risk, careful ocular safety studies are essential (Tanase et al., 2022; Mathis & Cui, 2016).  
• There is a paucity of data on the compound’s effects in retinal tissues specifically; it has not been previously tested in dry AMD or similar ocular models, necessitating robust preclinical validation in RPE cells and animal models (Borella et al., 2019; Sakashita et al., 2021).  
• The inherent electrophilic reactivity of bardoxolone methyl, while central to its mechanism, poses a risk of off‐target protein modification and possible cytotoxicity, particularly in the sensitive environment of the retina (Zhou et al., 2014; Cores et al., 2020).  
• Achieving the optimal therapeutic window in the eye may require innovative formulation strategies to ensure sustained, localized exposure at the low nanomolar range that is effective for Nrf2 activation without provoking adverse effects.  
• Given the multifactorial nature of dry AMD pathogenesis—encompassing oxidative stress, inflammation, and extracellular matrix alterations—it is critical to consider that Nrf2 activation alone may not fully address all aspects of the disease, implying that combination therapies or adjunctive treatments might ultimately be necessary (Sporn et al., 2011).

In conclusion, the repurposing of bardoxolone methyl for dry AMD is a scientifically sound and potentially transformative strategy, especially if its potent Nrf2 activation can be harnessed safely within the ocular milieu. Its capacity to induce elegant cytoprotective responses aligns well with the need to mitigate oxidative damage and restore autophagy in stressed RPE cells. Nonetheless, before committing significant resources to clinical trials in this indication, it is imperative to undertake thorough preclinical studies focused on ocular pharmacokinetics, toxicity, and efficacy in relevant models of dry AMD. These investigations will not only validate the underlying hypothesis but also inform the development of appropriate delivery systems to maximize local efficacy and minimize systemic risk. With these considerations addressed, bardoxolone methyl holds considerable promise as a repurposed therapeutic candidate that could significantly improve outcomes for patients suffering from dry age‐related macular degeneration (Mathis & Cui, 2016; Tanase et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2014; Dao et al., 2015; Cuadrado et al., 2019).
