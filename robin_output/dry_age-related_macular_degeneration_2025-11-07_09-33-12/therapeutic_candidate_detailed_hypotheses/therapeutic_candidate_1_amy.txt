Proposal for AMY-101 (Compstatin analog Cp40; Peptide, catalog no. Peptidotech P-1234)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
AMY-101 is a third-generation compstatin analogue built on the Cp40 scaffold, a cyclic, disulfide-constrained peptide discovered through iterative structure–activity optimization of the original compstatin hit identified by phage display against C3/C3b in 1996 (initial macrocycle I[CVVQDWGHHRC]T) (Lamers et al., 2022). Key potency-improving modifications en route to Cp40 include N-methylation of Trp and insertion of sarcosine, which rigidify the backbone and substantially increase binding affinity to C3; Cp40 (y-I[CV(1Me)WQDWSarAHRC]mI) binds a defined pocket between MG4/MG5 on the β-chain of C3/C3b with subnanomolar affinity and high selectivity for human and nonhuman primate (NHP) C3, while sparing other species, thereby necessitating NHP for translational in vivo work (Lamers et al., 2022). Mechanistically, compstatins are protein–protein interaction inhibitors: they occupy a substrate-binding site on C3 that prevents association with the C3 convertases, blocking convertase-mediated cleavage of native C3, and thereby interrupting the amplification loop and downstream effector generation (C3a, C3b, C5a, and membrane attack complex) independent of the initiating pathway (Lamers et al., 2022; Mastellos et al., 2019).

Compstatin analogues comprise a class of synthetic macrocyclic peptides that target the central node of complement activation at C3, distinguishing them from terminal-pathway blockers (e.g., C5 antibodies) and alternative-pathway–restricted inhibitors (e.g., factor D), which have shown mixed outcomes in complex complement-driven diseases such as age-related macular degeneration (AMD) (Mastellos et al., 2019; Cerniauskas et al., 2020). Clinically, the compstatin platform has matured to produce both a PEGylated bivalent C3 inhibitor (pegcetacoplan/APL-2) approved for paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) and non-PEGylated Cp40-derived candidates such as AMY-101 in human trials, establishing class translatability and safety management paradigms for chronic C3 inhibition (Lamers et al., 2022; Mastellos et al., 2019).

For ophthalmic translation, medicinal chemistry on Cp40 has produced fourth-generation, non-PEGylated lysine-extended derivatives (Cp40-KK, Cp40-KKK) with markedly improved solubility and favorable intraocular retention after intravitreal (IVT) administration in NHPs; these scaffolds maintain subnanomolar C3 affinity and exhibit retinal tissue engagement and co-localization with C3, suggesting target-driven distribution in ocular compartments (Hughes et al., 2020). Within the compstatin clinical lineage, earlier intravitreal compstatin (POT-4) and the PEGylated derivative APL-2/pegcetacoplan provided clinical benchmarks in geographic atrophy (GA), albeit with formulation-dependent considerations (e.g., PEG-associated risks) that motivate PEG-free ocular candidates such as AMY-101 or next-generation Cp40 analogues tailored for ocular delivery (Cerniauskas et al., 2020; Hughes et al., 2020; Lamers et al., 2022).

Therapeutic History  
Compstatin peptides have undergone extensive preclinical characterization and early clinical translation across complement-driven indications. Systemically, Cp40/AMY-101 blocked intravascular hemolysis and C3b opsonization in primate models and attenuated alloimmune responses and downstream B/T cell activation in transplantation models, demonstrating potent complement control and immunomodulatory ripple effects in vivo (Lamers et al., 2022). AMY-101 progressed through a first-in-human phase 1 trial (single and multiple ascending doses; subcutaneous and intravenous) with acceptable safety, no serious adverse events, and pharmacodynamic evidence of complement pathway modulation in healthy volunteers (Amyndas Pharmaceuticals S.A., 2017), and subsequently achieved local-delivery efficacy in a randomized, double-blind, split-mouth Phase IIa study for gingival inflammation with durable anti-inflammatory responses and no drug-induced immunogenicity detected (Hasturk et al., 2021). In the periodontal trial, AMY-101 reduced clinical indices (modified gingival index, bleeding on probing) and decreased matrix metalloproteinases (MMP-8, MMP-9) in gingival crevicular fluid, with effects persisting up to 90 days after the three weekly local injections, supporting local tolerability, biomarker responsiveness, and sustained pharmacodynamic action (Hasturk et al., 2021).

Within ophthalmology and AMD, multiple lines of evidence support the clinical plausibility of C3-directed strategies. Early intravitreal compstatin (POT-4) dosing in a primate model was associated with disappearance of drusen deposits, and PEGylated C3 inhibition (APL-2/pegcetacoplan) reduced GA lesion growth in Phase II and has achieved efficacy signals in Phase III, representing the first successful complement intervention in GA and validating the target class for dry AMD (Cerniauskas et al., 2020; Lamers et al., 2022). Conversely, downstream or pathway-restricted modulation disappointed: anti-C5 agents (eculizumab, LFG316) and alternative pathway blockade at factor D (lampalizumab) failed to deliver clinical benefit in GA, bolstering the rationale for upstream C3 control that addresses both opsonization and effector generation (Cerniauskas et al., 2020). Although AMY-101 itself has not yet been reported in human ocular trials in the provided context, Cp40-class derivatives have been tailored for ocular use and evaluated in NHPs after single IVT injections, achieving prolonged C3-saturating vitreous concentrations and retinal/choroidal target engagement for over three months—exposure metrics compatible with infrequent intravitreal maintenance regimens (Hughes et al., 2020).

Critically, human iPSC-derived RPE models carrying the common CFH Y402H high-risk variant establish a mechanistic and phenotypic bridge to AMD and provide in vitro evidence that Cp40 reverses disease-relevant pathology, particularly lysosome/autophagy defects and complement-driven lysosomal overburden, thereby directly supporting the translational concept for dry AMD (Cerniauskas et al., 2020). The RPE findings are reinforced by the broader AMD complement literature: complement activation on photoreceptor outer segments drives monocyte-dependent retinal atrophy, factor H genetics (including Y402H) modulates ocular alternative pathway activity, and complement deposition in Bruch’s membrane/sub-RPE space contributes to drusen biogenesis and RPE stress—elements that are mechanistically addressable by C3 blockade (Mastellos et al., 2019).

In sum, compstatin-class agents have an extensive preclinical pedigree and emergent clinical validation. For AMD specifically, the class has ocular proof-of-concept (POT-4) and clinical validation of C3 inhibition (pegcetacoplan), and Cp40 analogues demonstrate prolonged ocular PK in NHP; AMY-101 contributes human safety and local-delivery experience that supports translational risk management but requires dedicated ocular development for dry AMD (Cerniauskas et al., 2020; Lamers et al., 2022; Hughes et al., 2020).

Mechanism of Action  
C3 sits at the convergence of the classical, lectin, and alternative complement pathways; its cleavage by C3 convertases (C4b2b for CP/LP and C3bBb for AP) produces the anaphylatoxin C3a and the opsonin C3b, which seeds an amplification loop by forming additional alternative pathway convertases on activating surfaces (Mastellos et al., 2019). Persistent generation and deposition of C3b (and downstream C5b-9) under dysregulated conditions fuels inflammatory tissue damage and membrane injury; thus, targeting C3 offers initiation-independent suppression of both opsonization and effector generation (C3a, C5a, MAC), addressing multiple pathogenic modalities concurrently (Lamers et al., 2022; Mastellos et al., 2019).

Compstatins, including Cp40/AMY-101, are substrate-protecting inhibitors that bind to a defined site on C3/C3b (between MG4 and MG5 of the β-chain) and prevent the binding of native C3 to the active convertase, thereby blocking C3 turnover and the AP amplification cycle regardless of initiating pathway (Lamers et al., 2022). This binding mode is supported by co-crystallographic and kinetic analyses, and SAR has established critical roles for N-methylated Trp and charged residues (Asp7, Arg12) in affinity and solubility (Lamers et al., 2022). Unlike some PEGylated bivalent constructs that display altered target preference and weaker affinity to soluble C3, monovalent PEG-free Cp40 binds strongly to both soluble and surface-bound C3/C3b, preserving potency where soluble target is abundant (e.g., in plasma and vitreous), a beneficial profile for local ocular delivery (Lamers et al., 2022).

In human iPSC-RPE carrying CFH Y402H, C3 overactivation and increased C3 turnover correlate with multiple autophagy–lysosome abnormalities: increased expression of autophagosome markers (LC3-II, p62, ATG12–ATG5), expansion and activation of late endosomal/lysosomal/exosomal compartments (LAMP1, CD63; increased Lysotracker), reduced lysosomal functional readouts (decreased Cathepsin D expression/activity), swollen lysosome-like vesicles, lysosomal membrane fragility (elevated galectin-8 recruitment), and extracellular deposition of Cathepsin D in ApoE-positive drusen-like material (Cerniauskas et al., 2020). Mechanistic links are twofold: (1) excessive extracellular C3 activation increases surface C5b-9 (MAC) formation, which is internalized to lysosomes and triggers lysosomal membrane permeabilization with cathepsin leakage; (2) intracellular C3–autophagy crosstalk via ATG16L1 and cathepsin-mediated C3 activation may perturb autophagy flux, compounding lysosomal stress (Cerniauskas et al., 2020). In this model, Cp40 reduces C3 turnover (biochemical ELISA/MSD readouts) and diminishes C3b and C5b-9 deposition on and within RPE, leading to restoration of lysosomal pH/function and autophagic flux (Cerniauskas et al., 2020). Specifically, Cp40 treatment increases Cathepsin D expression/activity, reduces lysosome size and swelling, stabilizes lysosomal membranes (reduced galectin-8/LAMP co-localization), normalizes melanosome content (increased mature type 4 melanosomes), and decreases the volume of drusen-like deposits on transwells—collectively, a reversal of RPE disease phenotypes (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).

Importantly, the RPE data underscore that “tuning” C3 is necessary: stable shRNA knockdown of C3 (~85% reduction) did not restore melanosome/lysosome number and increased stress vacuoles indicative of a detrimental effect, whereas pharmacologic Cp40-mediated control of C3 turnover (not wholesale depletion) was beneficial—consistent with C3’s homeostatic roles in tissue clearance and immune surveillance (Cerniauskas et al., 2020). Rapamycin-driven autophagy activation failed to correct lysosomal deficits in the same AMD-RPE model, underscoring that upstream removal of complement burden may be required to re-enable autophagic clearance in complement-dysregulated RPE (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).

For ophthalmic PK/PD, next-generation Cp40 derivatives (Cp40-KK, Cp40-KKK) demonstrate prolonged vitreous residence and target engagement after single IVT injection in cynomolgus NHPs, measured by a sensitive SPR-based competition assay that recovers peptide from C3 complexes by heat dissociation and quantifies active inhibitor; ocular C3 levels were assayed in parallel (Hughes et al., 2020). Following a 0.5 mg IVT dose, Cp40-KKK maintained C3-saturating vitreous concentrations over three months (~200 nM during month 1; ~100–120 nM by month 3) and co-localized with C3 in retinal/choroidal tissue, suggesting target-driven retention and possible depot release; intraocular C3 concentrations measured were ~0.1–2 nM, implying inhibitor:target ratios sufficient for sustained pharmacodynamic coverage (Hughes et al., 2020). Only ~0.2% of the initial dose remained unbound in vitreous by day 14, consistent with rapid clearance of free peptide and prolonged persistence of target-bound pools (Hughes et al., 2020). These PK relationships, combined with AMY-101’s subnanomolar C3 affinity and prolonged plasma half-life (>60 h), support the feasibility of durable local complement control via IVT compstatin analogues without PEGylation (Lamers et al., 2022; Hasturk et al., 2021; Lamers et al., 2022).

Expected Effect  
In the proposed iPSC-RPE assay, AMY-101/Cp40 is expected to bind native C3 in the extracellular milieu and block its convertase-mediated cleavage, thereby suppressing both C3a/C3b generation and downstream terminal-pathway activation, including formation and lysosomal internalization of C5b-9 complexes that exacerbate lysosomal membrane permeabilization and cathepsin leakage in complement-dysregulated RPE (Lamers et al., 2022; Cerniauskas et al., 2020). The RPE model provides direct biochemical and cell-biological readouts that respond to C3 inhibition: Cp40 reduced C3 turnover (C3b/iC3b/C3c in supernatants), decreased surface and lysosomal C3b and C5b-9, and restored lysosomal functional markers (Cathepsin D expression and activity), organelle morphology (reduced lysosomal swelling; increased mature melanosomes), and membrane integrity (decreased galectin-8 recruitment), with associated reductions in drusen-like deposit burden—outcomes that would be expected for AMY-101 under similar conditions (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).

Given the measured RPE complement activity and the presence of C3 in ocular compartments, PD monitoring can include extracellular C3 fragment profiling (e.g., MSD capture for C3a domain detecting C3b; ELISA for C3b/iC3b/C3c), surface and lysosomal C5b-9 immunostaining (e.g., co-localization with LAMP markers), and lysosomal/autophagy endpoints such as LAMP1/CD63 expression, LysoTracker signal, LC3-II and p62 accumulation, Cathepsin D protein/activity, and galectin-8–based lysosomal damage assays (Cerniauskas et al., 2020). In the referenced RPE studies, effective Cp40 exposure used 6 µM for seven days with media change mid-course—a regimen that achieved robust biochemical and morphological rescue; potency in cellular systems is governed by high target abundance and convertase dynamics and may not mirror biochemical IC₅₀ values, but AMY-101’s subnanomolar C3 affinity and the low-nanomolar ocular C3 concentrations measured in NHP vitreous suggest that pharmacologically relevant free drug levels in the assay will saturate target (Cerniauskas et al., 2020; Hughes et al., 2020; Lamers et al., 2022). We note that no direct IC₅₀ for Cp40/AMY-101 in RPE culture is provided in the cited work; therefore, the proposed IC₅₀ value (~0.5 µM) cannot be confirmed from the current context and dosing should be guided empirically by the established 6 µM exposure used to reverse phenotypes in the Y402H model (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).

Regarding photoreceptor outer segment (POS) processing, impaired lysosomal function in RPE is causally linked to defective POS degradation; complement activation on POS can drive monocyte-dependent retinal atrophy, providing disease-relevant context for expecting that restoration of lysosomal flux via C3 inhibition would secondarily improve POS handling (Mastellos et al., 2019; Cerniauskas et al., 2020). However, the provided RPE studies did not explicitly report POS uptake/degradation assays in Cp40-treated cells, so POS-specific rescue should be treated as a testable hypothesis rather than an established outcome; lysosomal/autophagy restoration and reduced complement burden provide a mechanistic basis for such improvement (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).

For translational extrapolation to IVT dosing paradigms, ocular PK from Cp40-KK/KKK in NHP indicates that non-PEGylated Cp40 analogues can achieve multi-month C3-saturating exposure (retinal/choroidal co-localization with C3), which, combined with AMY-101’s high-affinity binding and absence of PEG (avoiding anti-PEG immunogenicity and PEG-related tissue effects), supports feasibility of infrequent dosing with a favorable mechanistic risk–benefit profile (Hughes et al., 2020; Lamers et al., 2022).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
– Mechanistic centrality and breadth of action at C3: AMY-101 directly blocks the C3 convertase–substrate interaction, upstream of opsonization and effector generation, thereby intercepting multiple pathogenic drivers operative in dry AMD (C3b deposition, C3a/C5a-mediated inflammation, and MAC-mediated membrane injury), in contrast to pathway-restricted or terminal-pathway agents that have underperformed in GA (Lamers et al., 2022; Mastellos et al., 2019; Cerniauskas et al., 2020). By protecting C3 from cleavage rather than inhibiting a specific convertase assembly, AMY-101 exerts initiation-independent control, appropriate for the complex and mixed complement activation modes present in AMD (Hasturk et al., 2021).  
– Direct human RPE disease-model evidence: In Y402H iPSC-RPE, Cp40 suppressed C3 turnover and downstream C5b-9 deposition, relieved lysosomal overburden and membrane fragility, restored Cathepsin D expression/activity and melanosome maturation, and reduced drusen-like deposits—an unusually comprehensive, mechanistically coherent rescue of AMD-relevant cellular pathology that maps directly onto the hypothesized mode of action for AMY-101 (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).  
– Ocular PK/translational feasibility: Single IVT dosing of Cp40 lysine-extended derivatives in NHPs yielded >90 days of C3-saturating intraocular exposure with retinal/choroidal target engagement and co-localization, supporting achievable, infrequent dosing intervals for a PEG-free compstatin platform; methods to quantify ocular drug and C3 (SPR competition; ELISA) and retinal localization are validated and can be deployed for PD monitoring (Hughes et al., 2020).  
– Clinical precedent for the target class in GA and systemic safety: Pegcetacoplan’s efficacy in reducing GA growth validates C3 inhibition in dry AMD; prolonged systemic C3 inhibition has an acceptable safety profile with appropriate vaccination and monitoring, and AMY-101 itself showed favorable safety, lack of anti-drug antibodies, and durable local efficacy in a human local-delivery setting (periodontal disease) (Lamers et al., 2022; Hughes et al., 2020; Hasturk et al., 2021).  
– PEG-free design: Non-PEGylated Cp40 analogues avoid PEG-associated liabilities (anti-PEG antibodies, tissue vacuolation, and potential association with CNV conversion observed in a subset of GA patients on PEGylated C3 inhibitors), while maintaining strong affinity for soluble C3—attributes favorable for chronic ocular use (Hughes et al., 2020; Lamers et al., 2022).

Weaknesses and Gaps  
– No human ocular clinical data for AMY-101: Despite compelling RPE in vitro data and NHP ocular PK for related derivatives, AMY-101 lacks reported human ocular PK/safety and clinical efficacy data in AMD in the provided context; bridging studies will be required to confirm ocular tolerability, immunogenicity, and dosing intervals specific to AMY-101 (Lamers et al., 2022; Hughes et al., 2020).  
– Species specificity constrains preclinical breadth: Compstatins bind human/NHP C3 with high specificity, precluding meaningful small-animal efficacy studies beyond surrogate approaches; this increases reliance on NHP for ocular PK/PD and toxicity packages and complicates broader in vivo AMD model validation (Lamers et al., 2022).  
– POS degradation not yet demonstrated under Cp40 in RPE: Although lysosomal/autophagy restoration strongly predicts improved POS clearance, the provided RPE studies did not report POS uptake/degradation assays post-Cp40; this is an essential disease-relevant endpoint to include in the next-stage validation (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).  
– Need to balance complement suppression: Stable C3 knockdown in the RPE model exacerbated stress phenotypes, underscoring the necessity for controlled pharmacologic inhibition rather than depletion; clinical protocols must monitor for under/over-suppression and preserve homeostatic complement roles in the immunoprivileged ocular environment (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).  
– Potential safety/biology caveats with C3 blockade in the eye: Theoretical risks include modulation of macrophage polarization that might favor neovascularization and the necessity to maintain anti-infective surveillance locally; although systemic experience with vaccination mitigates infectious risk, ocular-specific risks and CNV conversion propensity must be assessed prospectively for PEG-free C3 inhibitors (Mastellos et al., 2019; Hughes et al., 2020). Patient stratification (“complotype”) and baseline complement activity vary with age/sex and genetics (e.g., CFH), necessitating biomarker-driven inclusion and response frameworks in trials (Mastellos et al., 2019).

Decision-grade Assessment and Development Plan  
– Scientific validity: Strong. The AMD-RPE disease biology is directly addressed by AMY-101’s mechanism, with robust iPSC-RPE rescue data for Cp40 spanning biochemical, organellar, and deposit readouts; the mechanism integrates with known complement–lysosome–autophagy crosstalk and MAC-induced lysosomal permeabilization (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).  
– Translational feasibility: Good, with defined tasks. Ocular PK/PD in NHPs for Cp40 derivatives is compelling and suggests IVT dosing intervals of 2–3 months; AMY-101 has favorable systemic/local safety credentials and is PEG-free (Hughes et al., 2020; Hasturk et al., 2021). The absence of human ocular data for AMY-101 is a remediable gap.  
– Comparative landscape: Favorable. Factor D and C5 failures in GA argue for upstream intervention; C3 inhibition has shown clinical benefit (pegcetacoplan), placing AMY-101 in a validated mechanistic class, with potential safety advantages from PEG-free design and strong affinity to soluble C3 (Cerniauskas et al., 2020; Lamers et al., 2022).  
– Key risks: Ocular safety (immunogenicity, inflammation, CNV conversion), pharmacodynamic over-suppression, and unknowns around long-term retinal tissue distribution with AMY-101 (as distinct from Cp40-KKK). These will be addressed with staged NHP toxicology/PK, dosing optimization, and clinical biomarker surveillance (Hughes et al., 2020; Mastellos et al., 2019).

Recommended studies before IND for dry AMD  
1) In vitro translational package in human iPSC-RPE (Y402H and non-risk): reproduce Cp40 rescue with AMY-101 under identical conditions (6 µM; 7-day dosing), including biochemical C3 turnover (C3b/iC3b/C3c), surface/lysosomal C5b-9, LAMP1/CD63, LysoTracker, LC3-II, p62, galectin-8, Cathepsin D expression and activity, melanosome quantitation, and drusen-like deposit volume (Cerniauskas et al., 2020). Add POS feeding assays (pulse–chase of isolated POS) to quantify phagosome–lysosome fusion kinetics and rhodopsin degradation as POS-specific endpoints (Cerniauskas et al., 2020).  
2) Ocular PK and PD in cynomolgus NHPs with AMY-101: single- and multiple-dose IVT, vitreous/tissue levels by SPR competition assay, vitreous C3 fragments and C3:drug ratios, retinal/choroidal co-localization, and depot behavior versus Cp40-KKK; include dose ranging (≥0.5 mg) and extended sampling to ≥90 days (Hughes et al., 2020).  
3) Ocular safety/toxicology: GLP IVT studies (single and repeat-dose) evaluating inflammation, IOP, ERG, OCT/FAF, histopathology (RPE, choroid, retina), and immunogenicity (anti-drug antibodies intraocular/systemic) with correlation to drug levels and C3 suppression; monitor for CNV risk signals (Mastellos et al., 2019; Hughes et al., 2020).  
4) Biomarker strategy: translate RPE/animal biomarkers to clinical sampling—aqueous/vitreous C3 fragments, C5a, sC5b-9; imaging biomarkers (FAF for lipofuscin; OCT for outer retinal integrity; autofluorescent deposit quantification) to track pharmacodynamic effects on deposit burden and RPE health (Cerniauskas et al., 2020; Hughes et al., 2020).  
5) Patient stratification plan: incorporate CFH Y402H and broader complotype profiling in trial design, with baseline complement activity and demographic modifiers (age, sex) to enrich for responders and appropriately power efficacy analyses (Mastellos et al., 2019).

Conclusion  
AMY-101 (Cp40) is a mechanistically precise, high-affinity C3 inhibitor whose action is well matched to the complement-driven lysosomal/autophagy pathology observed in dry AMD RPE biology; Cp40 has already demonstrated reversal of Y402H RPE disease phenotypes across orthogonal readouts, and Cp40-class ocular PK in NHPs supports infrequent IVT dosing without PEG, addressing practical treatment constraints (Cerniauskas et al., 2020). The class has clinical validation in GA (pegcetacoplan) and an increasingly reassuring safety dossier for chronic C3 inhibition, while AMY-101 itself exhibits favorable human safety and local-delivery performance in non-ocular indications (Lamers et al., 2022; Hasturk et al., 2021). The principal development gaps—human ocular PK/safety for AMY-101 and POS-specific efficacy readouts—are tractable with focused preclinical and early clinical work. Taken together, the weight of mechanistic, cellular, and translational evidence supports advancing AMY-101 into an ophthalmic development path for dry AMD with a high likelihood of mechanistic efficacy and a manageable risk profile under a biomarker-driven, stratified clinical strategy (Cerniauskas et al., 2020; Lamers et al., 2022; Mastellos et al., 2019).
