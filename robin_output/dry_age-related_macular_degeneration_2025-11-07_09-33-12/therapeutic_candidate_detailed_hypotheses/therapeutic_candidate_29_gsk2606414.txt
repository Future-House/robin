Proposal for GSK2606414 (PERK inhibitor; Tocris Cat. No. 5160)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
GSK2606414 is a small‐molecule, synthetic inhibitor of PERK (protein kinase R‐like endoplasmic reticulum kinase) originally developed by GlaxoSmithKline. It belongs to an indoline derivative class of compounds that were discovered through structure‐guided medicinal chemistry optimization to achieve potent ATP‐competitive inhibition of the PERK kinase domain (Jiang et al., 2017; Axten, 2017). The compound was designed to target the unfolded protein response (UPR) and integrated stress response (ISR) by binding in the ATP pocket of PERK, thereby blocking its autophosphorylation and subsequent phosphorylation of its downstream target, eIF2α. This family of inhibitors has historically been used as a research tool to dissect the role of PERK in diverse contexts, including cancer, neurodegeneration, and other stress‐related diseases, owing to their ability to modulate protein synthesis under conditions of endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress (Chu et al., 2021; Kalinin et al., 2025). The high biochemical potency (with reported PERK IC₅₀ values in the sub-nanomolar range in enzymatic assays) contrasts with the micromolar concentrations sometimes needed to achieve cellular effects, as demonstrated in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cell studies where IC₅₀ values in the low micromolar range have been observed (Jiang et al., 2017). Through its capacity to inhibit the PERK-eIF2α axis, GSK2606414 has become an important tool compound when exploring how ER stress contributes to cellular dysfunction and death, while its chemical scaffold has inspired the development of further optimized analogues for more selective targeting (Jiang et al., 2017; Axten, 2017).

Therapeutic History:  
GSK2606414 has been extensively studied across multiple preclinical disease models, most notably in neurodegeneration and cancer. In the field of neurodegenerative diseases, it has been shown to provide neuroprotection in models of prion disease and tauopathy by alleviating chronic translational repression and restoring synaptic protein synthesis (Hetz et al., 2019; Mercado et al., 2018). Its efficacy in alleviating ER stress was further demonstrated in high glucose-induced neurotoxicity models in neuroblastoma cells, where the inhibitor attenuated the PERK/p-eIF2α/ATF4/CHOP axis and ameliorated mitochondrial dysfunction (Gundu et al., 2022). In oncology, GSK2606414 has been used both as a monotherapy and in combination with other agents, where it demonstrated modest antitumor effects in pancreatic xenografts and synergized with chemotherapeutic approaches (Axten, 2017; Agalakova, 2025). Regarding ocular applications, several studies have explored its effects on RPE cells. In particular, investigations using ARPE-19 cells showed that treatment with GSK2606414 resulted in dose-dependent inhibition of RPE cell proliferation and was accompanied by reduced phosphorylation of eIF2α and downstream effects including decreased CHOP and VEGF mRNA expression under ER stress conditions induced by thapsigargin (Chu et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2017). However, it should be noted that the compound has also been associated with detrimental outcomes in certain ocular models; for instance, in the P23H transgenic rat model of rhodopsin retinitis pigmentosa, PERK inhibition by a closely related compound resulted in reduced photoreceptor survival and decreased retinal function (Athanasiou et al., 2017). Despite these mixed outcomes, the literature provides evidence for ocular tolerability in select contexts, and the compound’s use in RPE cells for dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) has been proposed based on its potential to correct protein synthesis deficits (Chu et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2017). To date, no clinical trials have been reported in dry AMD with GSK2606414; its use has remained largely in the domain of preclinical mechanistic studies, with the therapeutic history marking both potential benefits in acute ER stress contexts and significant toxicity concerns in chronic dosing paradigms (Hetz et al., 2019; Vieira et al., 2024).

Mechanism of Action:  
GSK2606414 acts by competitively binding to the ATP-binding pocket of PERK, thereby inhibiting its autophosphorylation and subsequent activation. Under conditions of ER stress, PERK becomes activated and phosphorylates the alpha subunit of eukaryotic initiation factor 2 (eIF2α), leading to a global reduction in protein synthesis while selectively promoting the translation of stress-responsive mRNAs such as ATF4 (Jiang et al., 2017; Axten, 2017). By inhibiting this phosphorylation event, GSK2606414 effectively blocks the downstream PERK-eIF2α-ATF4-CHOP signaling cascade. This results in the alleviation of stress-induced translational repression and is thought to restore the synthesis of essential proteins, including those involved in autophagy and lysosomal function, such as phagocytic receptors (e.g., MERTK) and lysosomal enzymes (e.g., cathepsin D) critical for photoreceptor outer segment (POS) ingestion and processing in RPE cells (Chu et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2017). Detailed biochemical studies have shown that GSK2606414 exhibits remarkable selectivity against other members of the EIF2AK (eIF2α kinase) family, demonstrating over 385-fold selectivity over kinases such as PKR, which supports its role as a targeted PERK inhibitor (Athanasiou et al., 2017). However, it should be noted that while the on-target mechanism is well delineated, multiple studies have also attributed off-target effects to this compound, including inhibition of KIT and RIPK1 kinases. For example, GSK2606414 has been reported to cause enhanced KIT endocytosis and degradation, as well as to inhibit RIPK1-mediated cell death pathways at concentrations frequently used for PERK inhibition, raising concerns about the specificity of the observed biological effects (Mahameed et al., 2019; Rojas-Rivera et al., 2017; Scarpellini, 2023). These off-target interactions could potentially confound interpretation of its effects in complex tissue models and must be carefully considered when extrapolating its mechanism to the treatment of dry AMD. In addition, some studies have shown that suppression of PERK signaling can result in paradoxical effects on autophagy and cellular stress responses, suggesting that the balance of PERK activity is crucial for maintaining cell homeostasis (Saptarshi et al., 2022). Thus, the molecular interactions of GSK2606414 involve both the intended blockade of PERK-mediated phosphorylation events and unintended modulation of other kinase-dependent pathways that may contribute either to the therapeutic effect or to toxicity (Gundu et al., 2022; Kalinin et al., 2025).

Expected Effect:  
The working hypothesis for employing GSK2606414 in dry AMD centers on its ability to reverse the chronic ER stress observed in retinal pigment epithelial cells. In dry AMD, prolonged ER stress is implicated in the downregulation of proteins essential for phagocytosis, such as MERTK, and enzymes involved in lysosomal function like cathepsin D. The ensuing disruption of photoreceptor outer segment (POS) ingestion and processing is thought to contribute to the accumulation of debris and the formation of drusen, hallmarks of AMD pathology (Saptarshi et al., 2022; Chu et al., 2021). By inhibiting PERK, GSK2606414 is expected to reduce eIF2α phosphorylation, thereby mitigating the translational shutdown that suppresses the synthesis of these critical proteins. Restoration of normal protein synthesis should, in theory, lead to improved translation of phagocytic receptors and lysosomal enzymes, reestablishing the autophagy–lysosome pathway and enhancing the clearance of POS, which is essential for maintaining retinal homeostasis (Jiang et al., 2017; Chu et al., 2021). Several studies in RPE cell lines have demonstrated that treatment with GSK2606414 can downregulate pro-apoptotic markers such as CHOP and reduce VEGF mRNA expression under conditions of ER stress, indicating that it can modulate stress responses without inducing overt apoptosis, at least within a defined concentration range (Jiang et al., 2017). These findings suggest that in a carefully controlled dosing regimen, GSK2606414 might restore a more balanced ER stress response in RPE cells, thereby normalizing autophagic flux—which is vital for POS clearance—and preserving essential cellular functions such as receptor recycling and enzyme activity. However, it is essential to note that while the hypothesis is supported by cellular data showing modulation of key UPR components, direct evidence linking PERK inhibition by GSK2606414 to improved MERTK expression or cathepsin D translation in the context of POS phagocytosis is limited and remains largely inferential at this stage (Jiang et al., 2017; Saptarshi et al., 2022). Additional mechanistic studies will be needed to validate that the restoration of protein synthesis indeed translates into improved phagocytic function and lysosomal processing in RPE cells afflicted by dry AMD.

Overall Evaluation:  
When evaluating GSK2606414 as a potential therapeutic candidate for dry AMD, several strengths and weaknesses become apparent. On the positive side, the compound is among the best-characterized PERK inhibitors available, with a well-documented mechanism of action that has been reproduced across multiple independent studies in various disease models (Jiang et al., 2017; Axten, 2017; Hetz et al., 2019). In vitro studies in RPE cells using ARPE-19 models have demonstrated that GSK2606414 can inhibit key components of the ER stress response, including the phosphorylation of eIF2α and the upregulation of downstream effectors such as CHOP and VEGF. These effects collectively suggest that the compound has the potential to restore protein synthesis in conditions where chronic ER stress has led to the loss of essential proteins required for phagocytosis and lysosome function (Chu et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2017). This is particularly relevant for dry AMD, where impaired degradation of photoreceptor outer segment debris due to reduced MERTK and cathepsin D activity may underlie disease progression (Saptarshi et al., 2022; Chu et al., 2021).

However, the therapeutic history of GSK2606414 also highlights significant challenges that must be addressed before it can be considered a viable drug candidate for dry AMD. One of the most critical issues is the compound's well-documented off-target toxicities. Studies have shown that GSK2606414 not only inhibits PERK but also has potent inhibitory activities against other kinases such as KIT and RIPK1. These off-target effects can lead to unanticipated consequences including pancreatic toxicity, weight loss, and other systemic adverse effects (Hetz et al., 2019; Mahameed et al., 2019; Rojas-Rivera et al., 2017; Scarpellini, 2023). Such toxicities are particularly concerning for a chronic condition like dry AMD, in which long-term treatment is envisioned. The narrow therapeutic window observed in many preclinical models raises doubts about the translational potential of GSK2606414 without further chemical refinement to improve its selectivity profile (Vieira et al., 2024).

Another notable weakness is the complexity of PERK’s role in RPE cell biology. While chronic PERK activation is associated with deleterious outcomes such as reduced protein synthesis, inappropriate PERK signaling may also be essential for adaptive responses, including autophagy and the antioxidant defense mediated by Nrf2. Several studies using PERK knockdown models in RPE cells have demonstrated that excessive suppression of PERK can impair autophagic flux and reduce Nrf2-dependent antioxidant responses, thereby exposing cells to heightened oxidative stress (Saptarshi et al., 2022). This dual-edged nature of PERK signaling implies that complete or excessive inhibition of this pathway might exacerbate rather than ameliorate the very dysfunctions it is intended to correct. In light of this, the hypothesis that GSK2606414 will restore the synthesis of critical proteins such as MERTK and cathepsin D hinges on achieving a delicate balance between mitigating the harmful effects of chronic ER stress and preserving necessary adaptive signaling (Chu et al., 2021; Gundu et al., 2022).

Furthermore, while the proposed mechanism of action is compelling, direct evidence linking GSK2606414-mediated PERK inhibition to enhanced expression of phagocytic receptors and lysosomal enzymes in RPE cells remains sparse. Most of the published literature reports effects on generic ER stress markers (p-eIF2α, CHOP, VEGF) or on cell proliferation rather than on the specific processes of photoreceptor outer segment ingestion, cathepsin D/MERTK translation, or lysosomal degradation (Saptarshi et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2017). Thus, while the biochemical rationale is sound, further mechanistic studies—preferably in animal models of dry AMD—are required to conclusively demonstrate that the normalization of protein synthesis via PERK inhibition translates into functional improvements in POS clearance and retinal homeostasis.

In summary, the overall evaluation of GSK2606414 as a candidate for repurposing in dry AMD is mixed. On one hand, its potent and selective inhibition of the PERK pathway (with documented effects on eIF2α phosphorylation and downstream transcription factors) positions it as a promising candidate to reverse maladaptive ER stress responses in RPE cells—a central feature in the pathogenesis of dry AMD (Jiang et al., 2017; Chu et al., 2021). On the other hand, the compound’s significant off-target activities and associated systemic toxicities, along with the complex role of PERK signaling in maintaining normal RPE function, temper enthusiasm for its immediate translation into a clinical setting (Hetz et al., 2019; Rojas-Rivera et al., 2017; Vieira et al., 2024). In addition, while the hypothesis that PERK inhibition could restore the translation of essential phagocytic and lysosomal proteins is attractive, the current literature does not provide direct evidence that this strategy improves POS ingestion and degradation. Therefore, before committing to further development of GSK2606414 for dry AMD, it would be critical to conduct in-depth mechanistic studies in models that recapitulate the hallmark features of dry AMD and to explore modifications of the chemical scaffold to reduce off-target effects while preserving the desired PERK inhibitory activity (Athanasiou et al., 2017; Saptarshi et al., 2022).

Overall, GSK2606414 remains a compelling tool for probing the role of PERK in retinal degeneration and ER stress-related pathologies. However, as a repurposed therapeutic candidate for dry AMD, its potential is tempered by concerns over toxicity and the nuanced biology of PERK in RPE cells. Moving forward, a focused preclinical development strategy that includes detailed pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic studies in AMD-relevant animal models, along with concurrent medicinal chemistry to improve selectivity, will be essential to determine whether this compound—or a closely related derivative—can be effectively translated into a safe and efficacious treatment for dry AMD (Chu et al., 2021; Jiang et al., 2017; Athanasiou et al., 2017).
