Proposal for Tauroursodeoxycholic Acid (TUDCA; Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. T0261)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Tauroursodeoxycholic acid (TUDCA) is the taurine-conjugated form of ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA), a hydrophilic secondary bile acid generated by microbial metabolism and hepatic conjugation, and long used in hepatobiliary disease pharmacotherapy as a cytoprotective bile salt scaffold (class: bile acids; subclass: hydrophilic ursane bile acids/taurine conjugates) (Cabrera et al., 2019). As a small amphipathic molecule, TUDCA exhibits chemical-chaperone activity that stabilizes nascent polypeptides, reduces protein misfolding/aggregation burden, and modulates unfolded protein response (UPR) signaling, thereby lowering endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress across cell types (e.g., hepatocytes, epithelial cells, neurons) (Kusaczuk, 2019). In vitro and in vivo ocular studies identify TUDCA as a broadly neuroprotective agent that rescues photoreceptors and retinal cells in multiple injury paradigms, consistent with multimodal actions that include mitigation of ER stress, inhibition of mitochondrial pathway apoptosis, reduction of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and anti-inflammatory effects (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Win et al., 2021). In retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) models, TUDCA directly promotes phagocytosis via Mer tyrosine kinase (MerTK) activation and restores phagocytic function under oxidative stress, implicating a direct action on the canonical RPE phagocytic machinery (Murase et al., 2015). Delivery to ocular tissues is feasible with systemic dosing and local routes; related UDCA crosses the human blood–retinal barrier, and TUDCA tissue distribution has been quantified after intravitreal, systemic, and suprachoroidal administration in large animals (Daruich et al., 2021; Olsen et al., 2020).

Therapeutic History  
Clinically, the UDCA/TUDCA class is established in hepatology; mechanistic reviews document antiapoptotic activity via inhibition of mitochondrial cytochrome c release, stabilization of mitochondrial membranes, attenuation of ER stress via chaperone actions, increases in glutathione pools, and activation of survival kinases (p38/ERK/MAPK/PI3K), with a translational safety track record that has motivated evaluation in neurodegenerative disorders (Cabrera et al., 2019). In ophthalmology, preclinical data repeatedly show that systemic or local TUDCA protects retina across models: it preserves photoreceptors in genetic (rd10, P23H, Rpgr knockout) and injury models (retinal detachment, light damage), decreases ER stress and apoptosis markers (e.g., caspase-3), and improves electroretinography (ERG) function (Win et al., 2021; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021). In diabetic retinopathy–like models and vascular stress paradigms, TUDCA or UDCA reduces vascular permeability, inflammation, and ER stress, and in retinal endothelial cells these effects require the bile acid receptor TGR5, supporting receptor-mediated protection in the retina (Lenin et al., 2023). In RPE models (ARPE-19 and primary human RPE), TUDCA protects against H₂O₂-induced oxidative injury and thapsigargin-induced ER stress, improves viability, suppresses apoptosis, and activates antioxidant defenses, while also reversing oxidative-stress–induced phagocytic dysfunction via MerTK/FAK signaling (Alhasani et al., 2020; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021). Although these mechanistic actions align with pathogenic axes in dry age-related macular degeneration (dAMD), no clinical trials in dAMD are referenced in the supplied context; the current evidence base is preclinical and translational (e.g., drug distribution and barrier penetration) rather than RPE/dAMD clinical efficacy (Olsen et al., 2020; Daruich et al., 2021).

Mechanism of Action  
ER proteostasis and unfolded protein response modulation  
- Chemical chaperoning: TUDCA stabilizes folding intermediates, reduces aberrant protein–protein interactions, promotes ATF6-driven folding capacity, and prevents UPR malfunction, thereby attenuating ER stress signaling (downregulating BiP/GRP78, ATF4/eIF2α, XBP1 splicing, and CHOP in multiple models) (Kusaczuk, 2019). In ARPE-19 cells, TUDCA reduces thapsigargin-induced CHOP expression and spliced XBP1, with parallel decreases in apoptosis and improved survival, linking chaperone action to UPR attenuation in RPE (Alhasani et al., 2020). Across retinal systems, TUDCA repeatedly lowers ER stress signatures, and the retina-relevant pathophysiology of UPR–mitochondrial crosstalk (IRE1/JNK pro-apoptotic routing, CHOP-mediated death, PERK’s protective vs. maladaptive bifurcation) underscores why ER stress relief is mechanistically central in RPE and photoreceptors (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Alhasani et al., 2020).

Mitochondrial membrane stabilization and anti-apoptotic signaling  
- Intrinsic apoptosis blockade: TUDCA inhibits Bax translocation, preserves mitochondrial outer membrane integrity, prevents cytochrome c release, and suppresses caspase activation (caspase-3/7), thereby interrupting the mitochondrial execution cascade and preserving oxidative phosphorylation capacity (Alhasani et al., 2020; Cabrera et al., 2019). In neuronal and isolated mitochondrial systems, co-incubation with TUDCA prevents amyloid-β–induced mitochondrial membrane perturbation, preserves redox state and protein conformation of the outer membrane, and limits cytochrome c release—convergent evidence for direct mitochondrial membrane stabilization and preservation of electron transport chain function under proteotoxic/oxidative stress (Khalaf et al., 2022). TUDCA upregulates survival signaling (PI3K/Akt → Bad phosphorylation; ERK/MAPK) and inhibits stress kinases (JNK/c-Jun), further shifting the balance away from mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP) and toward survival (Cabrera et al., 2019; Win et al., 2021).

Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory reprogramming  
- NRF2 axis and redox control: In RPE oxidative injury models, TUDCA reduces intracellular ROS and malondialdehyde (MDA), restores NRF2 expression, upregulates antioxidant enzymes (SOD1, catalase, GPX1, NQO1, GCLM), elevates reduced glutathione (GSH), and rescues SOD/catalase activities—features of a robust antioxidant response that stabilizes mitochondrial redox homeostasis (Alhasani et al., 2020). Suppression of inflammatory signaling (decreased IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α transcription and secretion) further reduces secondary mitochondrial injury and death receptor cross-talk (Alhasani et al., 2020). Such antioxidant/anti-inflammatory shifts are consistent with TUDCA-induced NRF2 pathway enhancement reported across tissues and with the known linkage between persistent UPR activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine induction in RPE (Alhasani et al., 2020; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).

RPE phagocytic machinery activation  
- MerTK/FAK signaling and POS uptake: TUDCA increases MerTK phosphorylation in a concentration-dependent manner, maintains FAK phosphorylation under oxidative stress, and restores H₂O₂-impaired RPE phagocytosis in both ARPE-19 and primary human RPE, supporting a direct effect on RPE’s canonical POS ingestion machinery (MerTK–FAK–integrin axis) (Murase et al., 2015). Functional assays demonstrate increased uptake and lysosomal digestion of photoreceptor outer segments (pHrodo® and FITC readouts), indicating rescue of both recognition/engulfment and downstream processing (Murase et al., 2015). Notably, in the MerTK rescue paradigm GRP78 was not altered by TUDCA, implying that the pro-phagocytic effect can occur independently of a measurable reduction in ER stress under those specific conditions and may involve upstream integrin/FAK/Src signaling that converges on MerTK (Murase et al., 2015).

Bile acid receptors and signaling in the retina  
- TGR5-dependent protection: In diabetic and inflammatory retinal models, TUDCA reduces ER stress markers (GRP78, IRE1α, sXBP1, CHOP), inflammation, and vascular permeability; these endothelial barrier and anti-inflammatory effects require TGR5 expression in retinal endothelial cells and correlate with functional rescue in vivo (ERG, optokinetic responses), suggesting a receptor-mediated component that is present in the retina (Lenin et al., 2023). Although FXR appears less relevant in retina, additional candidate receptors (TGR5, α5β1 integrin, S1PR2, PXR, GR/mineralocorticoid receptor) have been implicated at the transcript/protein level in ocular tissues, providing plausible upstream routes for TUDCA’s signaling (Daruich et al., 2022; Win et al., 2021).

Mechanistic integration in AMD biology  
- In RPE, ER stress and mitochondrial dysfunction reinforce each other: UPR activation (IRE1/JNK, ATF4–CHOP) promotes mitochondrial apoptosis and ATP decline; mitochondrial impairment (Δψm loss, ROS) feeds back to exacerbate ER stress and can drive epithelial–mesenchymal transition seen in early dAMD (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Alhasani et al., 2020). TUDCA’s combined actions—chemical chaperoning of ER proteostasis, MOMP blockade/cytochrome c retention, redox control via NRF2, and restoration of MerTK/FAK-dependent phagocytosis—directly map onto these intersecting failure modes and therefore represent a coherent, biologically grounded therapeutic hypothesis for dAMD (Alhasani et al., 2020; Cabrera et al., 2019; Murase et al., 2015).

Important mechanistic gaps for dAMD  
- Despite strong plausibility, direct RPE bioenergetics readouts (ATP levels, oxygen consumption rate, mitochondrial membrane potential) after TUDCA in dAMD-relevant stress paradigms are not reported in the provided RPE studies and remain a central evidentiary gap for this indication (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021). Likewise, while TUDCA rescues phagocytosis and activates MerTK, there are no data here on cathepsin transcription, maturation, or activity in RPE phagolysosomes after TUDCA, precluding claims about normalization of lysosomal proteases in dAMD RPE (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Murase et al., 2015).

Expected Effect  
In a dAMD-relevant RPE assay comprising oxidative and/or ER stress with phagocytosis readouts, the following effects are expected based on the compiled evidence:

- ER stress attenuation and reduced UPR-driven apoptosis: TUDCA should lower CHOP and spliced XBP1 levels and decrease TUNEL/caspase-3/7 activation, improving viability under thapsigargin or oxidative stress, consistent with chemical chaperone–mediated UPR modulation (Alhasani et al., 2020).  
- Mitochondrial preservation and anti-apoptosis: TUDCA should suppress Bax translocation/cytochrome c release and maintain mitochondrial membrane integrity, thereby reducing mitochondrial pathway apoptosis and preserving the proton motive force needed for oxidative phosphorylation; while these endpoints have not yet been quantified in RPE with Seahorse or JC-1 in the supplied reports, they are supported mechanistically in retina/neuronal systems and hepatocyte models (Cabrera et al., 2019; Khalaf et al., 2022).  
- Antioxidant reset via NRF2: TUDCA should reduce ROS/MDA, restore NRF2 expression, increase GSH, and upregulate antioxidant enzymes (SOD, CAT, GPX1, NQO1, GCLM), consistent with lower oxidative injury to mitochondrial lipids/proteins and stabilized respiratory capacity (Alhasani et al., 2020).  
- Restoration of RPE phagocytosis via MerTK/FAK: Under oxidative stress (H₂O₂), TUDCA is expected to restore POS ingestion/digestion as measured by pHrodo®/FITC assays, accompanied by increased MerTK phosphorylation and maintenance of FAK signaling, potentially via upstream integrin activation; this rescues both recognition/engulfment and lysosomal processing phases (Murase et al., 2015).  
- Anti-inflammatory effects: TUDCA should reduce IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α at mRNA/protein levels in RPE challenged by H₂O₂, limiting cytokine-driven mitochondrial impairment and secondary UPR activation (Alhasani et al., 2020).  
- Putative receptor mediation: In endothelial/retinal contexts, TUDCA’s ER-stress and barrier benefits require TGR5; although RPE-specific TGR5 dependency is not demonstrated in the supplied texts, receptor-mediated contributions in the retinal environment are plausible and testable (Lenin et al., 2023).  

Expression and pathway presence in RPE are well established for MerTK, integrins (e.g., αvβ5), and FAK—core components of the POS phagocytosis cascade targeted by TUDCA in RPE cells (Murase et al., 2015).  

Dosing and assay considerations:  
- In ARPE-19, 100 µM TUDCA improved survival under 750 µM H₂O₂; higher concentrations (200–500 µM) impaired viability, indicating a window for benefit (Alhasani et al., 2020).  
- In phagocytosis assays, TUDCA increased MerTK phosphorylation at high concentration (1 mM) and improved phagocytosis at lower concentrations (≥ 0.1 µM) under non-H₂O₂ conditions, suggesting multiple dose-dependent mechanisms (direct vs. upstream integrin-mediated pathways) (Murase et al., 2015).  

Endpoints to include for hypothesis validation:  
- ER stress/UPR: GRP78/BiP, sXBP1, CHOP, ATF4, eIF2α-P, ATF6 targets (Alhasani et al., 2020; Kusaczuk, 2019).  
- Mitochondrial bioenergetics: Δψm (JC-1/TMRM), oxygen consumption rate and extracellular acidification rate (Seahorse), cellular ATP (luminescent readout), cytochrome c release/Bax translocation (Cabrera et al., 2019; Khalaf et al., 2022).  
- Redox/inflammation: ROS (DCFH-DA), MDA (TBARS), GSH, NRF2 targets, cytokine ELISAs (Alhasani et al., 2020).  
- Phagocytosis: MerTK/FAK phosphorylation, POS binding/engulfment/digestion (pHrodo®/FITC), and key lysosomal proteolysis markers; importantly, add cathepsin D/B/L maturation/activity assays to address the specific lysosomal arm of the hypothesis, which remains untested in the provided literature (Murase et al., 2015; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).  

Overall Evaluation  
Summary judgment  
TUDCA is a mechanistically coherent, repurposable candidate for dAMD that targets central RPE failure modes—ER proteostasis breakdown, mitochondrial injury/apoptosis, oxidative and inflammatory stress—and functionally restores a defining RPE task (POS phagocytosis) via MerTK/FAK signaling under oxidative insult (strengths: convergent mechanistic breadth and direct RPE function rescue) (Alhasani et al., 2020; Cabrera et al., 2019; Murase et al., 2015). However, two critical evidence gaps must be addressed to de-risk the program for dAMD: (1) lack of direct demonstration that TUDCA preserves RPE mitochondrial bioenergetics and ATP production under dAMD-relevant stress, and (2) absence of data that TUDCA normalizes lysosomal cathepsin expression/maturation/activity downstream of phagosome–lysosome fusion (weaknesses: missing bioenergetic and lysosomal endpoints underpinning the hypothesis) (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).  

Strengths  
- Mechanistic fit to dAMD pathophysiology: TUDCA reduces ER stress/UPR effectors (CHOP, sXBP1), blocks mitochondrial pathway apoptosis (Bax/cytochrome c/caspase-3), induces NRF2-driven antioxidant programs, and suppresses inflammatory cytokines—all directly relevant to the ER–mitochondria redox loop that destabilizes RPE in early AMD (Alhasani et al., 2020; Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).  
- Direct RPE functional efficacy: Restoration of POS phagocytosis via MerTK activation and FAK stabilization under oxidative stress offers a clear, disease-relevant mechanism to preserve outer segment turnover and RPE homeostasis (Murase et al., 2015).  
- Mitochondrial stabilization plausibility: Strong cross-system evidence shows TUDCA preserves mitochondrial membrane structure, limits cytochrome c release, and blocks permeability transition–like events, which would support ATP maintenance under stress (albeit not yet measured in RPE) (Khalaf et al., 2022; Cabrera et al., 2019).  
- Retinal in vivo activity and barrier access: Systemic TUDCA protects photoreceptors and retinal function in multiple rodent disease models; related UDCA crosses the human blood–retinal barrier, and ocular delivery platforms achieve retinal distribution in large animals, supporting translational feasibility (Win et al., 2021; Daruich et al., 2021; Olsen et al., 2020).  
- Receptor-mediated tractability: TGR5 dependency for retinal endothelial barrier/inflammation effects offers a defined receptor axis for pharmacodynamic biomarkers in vivo, complementing MerTK/FAK functional readouts in RPE (Lenin et al., 2023).  

Weaknesses and risks  
- Mitochondrial bioenergetics unproven in RPE: No ATP, OCR/ECAR, or Δψm data in stressed RPE after TUDCA are provided; this is a pivotal gap because preservation of energy production is central to the proposed disease-modifying effect in dAMD (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).  
- Lysosomal protease (cathepsin) claims unsupported: While TUDCA rescues phagocytosis and MerTK signaling, there are no data here that TUDCA restores cathepsin expression/maturation/activity in RPE; the stated hypothesis reference to “alleviates ER stress–induced downregulation of MERTK and cathepsins” is not substantiated in the supplied sources and should be considered unverified for RPE/dAMD (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021; Murase et al., 2015).  
- Dose–mechanism tension: In RPE phagocytosis assays, MerTK phosphorylation was robust at 1 mM, whereas phagocytosis enhancement occurred at ≥ 0.1 µM, suggesting indirect upstream mechanisms at low doses and raising questions about clinically relevant concentrations for direct MerTK phosphorylation (Murase et al., 2015).  
- Heterogeneity of mechanistic readouts: In one RPE phagocytosis study, GRP78 was not changed by TUDCA during rescue, indicating ER stress reduction is not required for the pro-phagocytic effect in that setting; mechanism may differ across contexts (Murase et al., 2015).  
- Evidence quality and generalizability: Several RPE-focused studies are from lower-tier journals, and many mechanistic claims derive from non-RPE systems; rigorous replication with polarized primary human RPE and in dAMD-relevant in vivo models is needed (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).  

Decision framework and next experiments  
Given the favorable mechanistic alignment and preclinical retinal efficacy, TUDCA merits advancement to a focused preclinical validation plan tailored to dAMD, gated by resolution of the bioenergetics and lysosomal gaps:  

- Bioenergetics/mitochondria (must-have): In human primary, polarized RPE under defined oxidative and ER stress (e.g., low-dose H₂O₂, tunicamycin/thapsigargin), quantify Δψm (TMRM), OCR/ECAR (Seahorse), cellular ATP, cytochrome c release, and Bax translocation with and without TUDCA; include NRF2 dependency (siRNA) to map antioxidant mediation (Alhasani et al., 2020; Cabrera et al., 2019).  
- Phagolysosomal proteolysis (must-have): In POS ingestion assays, measure cathepsin D/B/L maturation (pro-/mature forms), activity (fluorogenic substrates), lysosomal pH and degradative capacity; test whether TUDCA normalizes these under ER/oxidative stress and whether effects require MerTK or integrin/FAK signaling (Murase et al., 2015).  
- ER–mitochondria crosstalk mapping (should-have): Track UPR arms (IRE1/JNK, PERK/eIF2α/ATF4, ATF6) alongside mitochondrial readouts to define causal sequence; test whether TUDCA biases PERK toward pro-survival signaling and reduces CHOP/TRB3 induction (Alhasani et al., 2020; Kusaczuk, 2019).  
- In vivo dAMD-relevant model (should-have): In RPE mitochondrial/oxidative stress models (e.g., sodium iodate low-dose; cigarette-smoke–derived oxidants), test systemic and local TUDCA dosing for RPE structure/function (autofluorescence, OCT, ERG) and RPE phagocytosis biomarkers, plus mitochondrial/UPR markers (Win et al., 2021; Olsen et al., 2020).  
- Pharmacology/PK/PD (must-have): Link ocular exposure (vitreous/choroid/RPE) to effect using delivery modalities with translational potential (oral UDCA as a proxy crossing BRB; suprachoroidal implant pharmacokinetics); incorporate PD markers (retinal ER stress proteins, NRF2 targets) (Daruich et al., 2021; Olsen et al., 2020; Lenin et al., 2023).  

Positioning in dAMD  
If the above gaps are closed positively, TUDCA would be positioned as a proteostasis–mitochondria stabilizer that preserves RPE phagocytic function and metabolic sufficiency—an upstream, disease-modifying approach complementary to complement modulation and to anti-angiogenic strategies (the latter relevant if geographic atrophy coexists with incipient neovascularization) (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021). The MerTK/FAK rescue of phagocytosis provides a strong functional anchor for mechanism-based biomarkers, while the antioxidant/UPR signatures support PD tracking in early-phase clinical translation (Murase et al., 2015; Alhasani et al., 2020).

Go/no-go conclusion  
- Go for preclinical dAMD validation with a clear focus on mitochondrial bioenergetics and lysosomal protease function in RPE, using polarized human RPE and in vivo oxidative/ER-stress models that recapitulate early dAMD (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).  
- No-go for cathepsin-centered claims until direct RPE evidence is generated; likewise, refrain from asserting ATP rescue in RPE until OCR/ATP/Δψm data are obtained (Bilbao-Malavé et al., 2021).  

On balance, TUDCA is a high-priority repurposing candidate with strong mechanistic plausibility and supportive retinal efficacy, but it requires targeted, RPE-centric bioenergetics and lysosomal validation to meet decision-grade standards for a dAMD program (Cabrera et al., 2019; Win et al., 2021; Murase et al., 2015; Alhasani et al., 2020).
