Proposal for D609 (Phosphatidylcholine-specific PLC inhibitor; Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. D0209)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
D609, chemically known as tricyclodecan-9-yl xanthogenate, is a synthetic small‐molecule compound belonging to the xanthate class. It was originally synthesized through classical organic‐chemical methods involving the reaction of diastereomeric tricyclic alcohols with carbon disulfide in the presence of a strong base such as potassium tert‐butoxide, yielding a mixture of eight stereoisomers due to its three asymmetric centers (Bhat et al., 2022; Eurtivong et al., 2023). As a member of the xanthate family, D609 has been extensively studied for its diverse biological activities that include antiviral, antitumoral, anti‐inflammatory, and neuroprotective effects (Bhat et al., 2022). Its mechanism of competitive inhibition of phosphatidylcholine-specific phospholipase C (PC-PLC) was discovered during the evaluation of lipid signaling pathways, and it is commercially available (for example, Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. D0209) as a research-grade compound (Wang et al., 2020). This chemical class has traditionally been investigated in various biomedical contexts, with early work focusing on its antiviral properties dating back to the 1980s and subsequent studies extending its uses into oncology and neurology (Bhat et al., 2022). Its water solubility and relatively low molecular weight make it an attractive candidate for repurposing into ocular applications where intracellular signaling and oxidative stress play key roles.

Therapeutic History:  
D609 has been the subject of more than 700 published reports over the last three decades and has been explored in preclinical studies ranging from antiviral and anticancer applications to neuroprotection and inflammation modulation (Bhat et al., 2022). One of the most compelling lines of evidence for repurposing D609 concerns its potential in treating ocular conditions, particularly dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD). In several studies, D609 has been directly evaluated for its ability to protect retinal pigmented epithelium (RPE) cells from oxidative damage using in vitro cell models such as the widely used ARPE-19 cell line and primary human RPE cells (Wang et al., 2020). For instance, one study employing a high-content screening strategy on 814 protective compounds identified D609 as a lead candidate that maintained RPE cell viability above 95% under severe oxidative stress induced by sodium iodate—a condition used to model dry AMD (Wang et al., 2020). Furthermore, in vivo animal models, including sodium iodate-induced AMD mouse models, have confirmed that D609 can protect the RPE layer, preserve retinal structure and function, and prevent geographic atrophy—findings that underscore its translational potential for dry AMD therapy (Wang et al., 2020). Outside the field of ophthalmology, D609 has been evaluated in oncology, where it affected cell proliferation and induced apoptosis in certain tumor cell lines, and in neuroscience for its neuroprotective effects against amyloid-beta toxicity and oxidative stress in neuronal cultures (Bhat et al., 2022). Although no clinical trials have yet been reported that specifically target dry AMD with D609, the preclinical data generated in ocular, cancer, and neurodegenerative studies provide a robust therapeutic history that supports depth of investigation into its ocular application (Wang et al., 2020).

Mechanism of Action:  
At its core, D609 is a competitive inhibitor of PC-PLC. PC-PLC is an enzyme that hydrolyzes phosphatidylcholine (PC) into phosphorylcholine and diacylglycerol (DAG). In RPE cells, overactivation of this enzyme under oxidative stress conditions may lead to excessive DAG production that, in turn, drives hyperactivation of protein kinase C (PKC) and downstream signaling events deleteriously affecting cytoskeletal dynamics and phagocytosis (Wang et al., 2020). D609 exerts its inhibitory activity by mimicking parts of the natural substrate, competitively binding to the active site of PC-PLC—in part through its dithiocarbonate group, which can chelate zinc ions that are critical for enzyme function (Wang et al., 2020; González-Roura et al., 2002). In addition to its direct inhibition of PC-PLC, D609 has been reported to exert antioxidative effects indirectly via upregulation of metallothionein proteins—an antioxidative protein family that plays a protective role against reactive oxygen species (ROS) and metal-induced damage (Wang et al., 2020). This dual mechanism—direct enzyme inhibition and indirect antioxidant protection—suggests that D609 may help stabilize the lipid signaling environment of the RPE, normalizing DAG levels and mitigating PKC-mediated perturbations in actin cytoskeletal dynamics that are essential for the phagocytosis of photoreceptor outer segments (POS) (Wang et al., 2020). Some studies even propose that the beneficial effects observed in RPE cells may derive more from the metallothionein upregulation than from pure PC-PLC inhibition, raising the possibility that D609’s mode of action is multifactorial rather than strictly enzyme-targeted (Wang et al., 2020). However, in the context of the current hypothesis—where the goal is to inhibit the DAG-PKC axis to restore cytoskeletal dynamics and phagocytosis—the competitive inhibition of PC-PLC by D609 remains a key molecular mechanism (Bhat et al., 2022). It should be noted that D609 is not entirely selective; it also inhibits other enzymes in lipid metabolism such as sphingomyelin synthase (SMS) and possibly phospholipase D (PLD), which could contribute to both its therapeutic efficacy and its off-target effects (Eurtivong et al., 2023; González-Roura et al., 2002). The compound’s broad-spectrum inhibition profile, while advantageous in some settings, raises concerns regarding precise modulation of the PC-PLC–DAG–PKC pathway in the specialized setting of RPE biology (Zhao et al., 2020; Bhat et al., 2022). Nevertheless, the downstream reduction in DAG levels is expected to temper PKC activation, which is crucial since PKC hyperactivation is known to impair actin polymerization and therefore phagocytosis—a critical function of RPE cells in maintaining photoreceptor health (Wang et al., 2020).

Expected Effect:  
The working hypothesis for repurposing D609 for dry AMD is that its inhibition of PC-PLC in RPE cells will lead to reduced DAG formation, thereby limiting DAG-driven PKC hyperactivation. This in turn is expected to stabilize cytoskeletal dynamics by restoring normal actin polymerization, thus improving the phagocytic capacity of the RPE to internalize and process POS. In experimental assays, ARPE-19 cells subjected to oxidative insult (e.g., hydroquinone treatment) typically display disrupted actin architecture and impaired POS uptake. Treatment with D609 is hypothesized to counteract these effects by balancing the intracellular signaling milieu—specifically by reducing the aberrant activation of the DAG-PKC axis—and by promoting the antioxidant metallothionein pathway, which further preserves cellular homeostasis (Wang et al., 2020). Expression of PC-PLC and related signaling components in RPE cells has been indirectly verified by the cellular responses observed upon oxidative challenge, and the reversal of these effects by D609 supports the notion that this enzyme is active and pathophysiologically relevant in RPE cells (Wang et al., 2020). The expected phenotypic outcomes in the assay include a restoration of actin polymerization, as evidenced by improved F-actin staining patterns, increased phagocytosis of labeled POS, and decreased levels of oxidative markers such as ROS. Indeed, previous studies demonstrated that when ARPE-19 cells were pretreated with D609, they exhibited markedly reduced necrotic and apoptotic markers, along with preserved mitochondrial mass and function under oxidative stress conditions induced by sodium iodate (Wang et al., 2020). These outcomes directly support the hypothesis that normalization of PC-PLC activity via D609 not only mitigates the immediate oxidative insult but also restores the critical cellular function of POS internalization—a process that, when dysfunctional, is a hallmark of dry AMD pathology (Wang et al., 2020).

Overall Evaluation:  
D609 presents as an intriguing and promising repurposed therapeutic candidate for dry age-related macular degeneration, particularly because of its ability to modulate lipid signaling pathways that are disrupted by oxidative stress and contribute to RPE dysfunction. One of the principal strengths of D609 is its well-documented protective effect on RPE cells both in vitro and in vivo. Studies have shown that D609 maintains high levels of cell viability in RPE models under conditions of severe oxidative stress, and its administration in animal models of AMD has led to significant preservation of retinal structure and function (Wang et al., 2020). Moreover, its water solubility and low molecular weight facilitate ocular delivery and reduce formulation challenges, making it suitable for local administration to the retina (Wang et al., 2020). The dual-action mechanism—combining inhibition of PC-PLC with upregulation of metallothionein—provides both a direct reduction in deleterious DAG-PKC signaling and an indirect bolster to the cell’s antioxidant defenses, which is crucial in the context of dry AMD where oxidative stress is a primary driver of pathology (Wang et al., 2020).

However, several weaknesses must be noted. First, D609 is a non-selective inhibitor; while it effectively targets PC-PLC, it also inhibits other enzymes such as sphingomyelin synthase and phospholipase D, which may introduce off-target effects that could complicate its clinical use (Eurtivong et al., 2023; González-Roura et al., 2002). This lack of selectivity may lead to unpredictable interactions in the intricate lipid signaling networks present in the retina. Second, the compound exists as a mixture of eight stereoisomers, and the differential activity among these isomers is not well understood. Variability in stereoisomeric composition could result in inconsistent pharmacodynamic outcomes and present challenges in the regulatory approval process (Eurtivong et al., 2023; Bhat et al., 2022). Third, the anticipated IC₅₀ of approximately 35 µM for PC-PLC inhibition in RPE cells is relatively high, suggesting that effective biological activity may require high local concentrations that may be difficult to achieve or maintain in vivo without risking toxicity (Wang et al., 2020; Bhat et al., 2022). Although ocular safety studies in rabbits have been favorable, further pharmacokinetic and toxicological investigations will be essential to confirm the therapeutic window for chronic treatment in dry AMD (Wang et al., 2020; Bhat et al., 2022).

From a mechanism-of-action standpoint, while the hypothesis that reducing DAG-mediated PKC hyperactivation will normalize actin dynamics and improve phagocytosis is biologically plausible, much of the existing literature on D609’s protective effects in RPE cells emphasizes its ability to upregulate metallothionein expression and confer antioxidant protection rather than solely modulating the DAG-PKC axis (Wang et al., 2020). Thus, it remains to be clarified whether the beneficial outcomes in RPE phagocytosis are driven primarily by alterations in the DAG-PKC pathway or by a broader restoration of cellular redox balance. This mechanistic ambiguity, while not diminishing the overall therapeutic promise, suggests that future studies should incorporate detailed pathway analyses to dissect the relative contributions of each mechanism.

In summary, D609 is well supported by preclinical evidence as a compound that can protect RPE cells from oxidative damage and has potential applications in dry AMD therapy (Wang et al., 2020). Its established safety profile in ocular studies, combined with a mechanistic basis that addresses both lipid signaling dysregulation and oxidative stress, offers significant advantages for repurposing in this context. Nonetheless, challenges related to off-target effects, stereochemical variability, and the need for high effective concentrations must be rigorously addressed in subsequent studies. Overall, D609 merits further investigative efforts in translational research focused on dry AMD, with particular emphasis on optimizing its pharmacodynamics, ensuring reproducible stereoisomeric composition, and validating its efficacy in restoring RPE phagocytic function under physiologically relevant oxidative stress conditions (Eurtivong et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2020).
