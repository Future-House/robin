Proposal for GSK0660 (PPARβ/δ antagonist; Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. SML0694)

Below is a detailed evaluation report of the repurposing potential of GSK0660 for the treatment of dry age‐related macular degeneration (dry AMD). The report is organized into five main sections: (I) Overview of the Therapeutic Candidate, (II) Therapeutic History, (III) Mechanism of Action, (IV) Expected Effect in RPE Models, and (V) Overall Evaluation. Each section draws on the available literature and data from the provided context.

────────────────────────────────────────  
I. OVERVIEW OF THE THERAPEUTIC CANDIDATE  

GSK0660 is a synthetic small‐molecule inhibitor that gained prominence as the very first PPARβ/δ (peroxisome proliferator‐activated receptor beta/delta)–selective inhibitory ligand reported in the scientific literature (Toth et al., 2012). Its design represents a significant breakthrough in the field of nuclear receptor modulation by demonstrating that subtype selectivity within the PPAR family can indeed be achieved with a low molecular weight organic compound. Structurally, GSK0660 belongs to the thiophenecarboxylate chemical class and features a uniquely tailored molecular architecture that includes several key pharmacophores: a methoxy‐substituted 1,4‐diaminobenzene core thought to be essential for receptor binding; a 4′‐aminophenyl substituent that not only contributes to high‐affinity interactions with the receptor’s ligand‐binding domain but also offers a promising site for subsequent chemical modification; and a methyl thiophene carboxylate moiety that helps orient the molecule within the hydrophobic pocket of PPARβ/δ (Toth et al., 2012). Extensive structure–activity relationship (SAR) studies have demonstrated that modifications to portions of the molecule—particularly the 4′‐aminophenyl substituent by introduction of medium‐length n‐alkyl chains—can enhance binding affinity by up to tenfold compared to the original structure (Toth et al., 2012).

GSK0660 was originally synthesized through a multistep organic process that began with commercially available precursors, followed by key transformation steps such as nucleophilic aromatic substitution to install appropriate groups on a benzene ring, catalytic reduction to generate the requisite amine functions, and finally sulfonamidation reactions to yield the final compound (Toth et al., 2012). In essence, this compound was developed primarily as a research tool to dissect the specific biological roles of PPARβ/δ and does not itself originate from a natural product or traditional antibiotic source. Instead, its design and synthesis reflect a targeted medicinal chemistry strategy aimed at obtaining a high degree of receptor selectivity.

Belonging to the broader class of PPAR modulators, GSK0660 occupies a niche that is distinct from the more commonly encountered PPAR agonists. Whereas most efforts historically have focused on the development of agonists for metabolic disorders and anti‐inflammatory indications, GSK0660 and its close analogs are used primarily to achieve receptor inhibition. This selective antagonism is critical for experimental setups in which the contribution of PPARβ/δ to downstream gene regulation and cellular homeostasis is being evaluated (Toth et al., 2012). Furthermore, the structure of GSK0660 aligns with the more general trends in nuclear receptor ligand development, capturing several “drug‐like” properties that, despite known limitations in bioavailability, maintain its status as a high‐quality research compound in proof‐of‐concept investigations.

II. THERAPEUTIC HISTORY  

GSK0660 has been widely used as a chemical probe to examine the biological functions of PPARβ/δ in various preclinical models. In early studies, it served as the first selective antagonist for this receptor subtype and was instrumental in clarifying the roles of PPARβ/δ in lipid metabolism, cell proliferation, and inflammation (Toth et al., 2012). Its experimental applications have spanned a number of research areas. For instance, several investigations in models of neurodegeneration—particularly in Parkinson’s disease—have utilized GSK0660 in both in vitro experiments (using human neuroblastoma cell lines such as SH‐SY5Y) and in vivo rodent models. In these studies, GSK0660 was able to negate the neuroprotective effects of PPARβ/δ agonists by blocking the receptor’s activity, thereby providing compelling evidence for a detrimental role of PPARβ/δ activation under neurodegenerative stress conditions (Antonosante et al., 2023; Boya et al., 2023). Such data have contributed to a nuanced understanding of PPAR signaling in the brain, implying that under certain conditions receptor inhibition may be more desirable than activation.

Moreover, GSK0660 has also been evaluated in dermatological applications. In recent studies focusing on psoriasis‐like skin disease, GSK0660—when formulated topically—was shown to mitigate epidermal hyperplasia and reduce inflammatory markers without systemic drug accumulation, thus proving useful for localized treatment strategies (Hack et al., 2012). These dermatological studies not only underscored the compound’s ability to counteract PPARβ/δ‐mediated signaling in inflamed tissues but also highlighted practical challenges associated with its formulation. For example, its inherent chemical instability, manifesting as a tendency to undergo oxidation (where solutions of GSK0660 may turn green upon light exposure at room temperature), underscores issues related to its shelf‐life and stability in clinical formulations (Hack et al., 2012; Lieber, 2015).

Despite its usefulness in these diverse experimental models—ranging from neurodegenerative to inflammatory skin diseases—the therapeutic history of GSK0660 reveals a critical gap: there is no record within the available literature of its application in ocular diseases or in the treatment of dry AMD. A systematic search of clinical trial databases has confirmed that no human clinical investigations have been registered evaluating GSK0660 for indications such as retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) dysfunction, macular degeneration, or related ocular conditions (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.). In other words, while the compound has been validated as a tool in non‐ocular systems, its repurposing for dry AMD is entirely uncharted territory. This lack of ocular‐specific data is a limitation that must be considered when assessing its potential for enhancing lysosomal function in RPE cells.

Furthermore, limitations inherent to GSK0660—most notably its poor systemic bioavailability and the requirement for relatively high concentrations to achieve effective antagonism—are recurring themes in its therapeutic history (Toth et al., 2012; Zhang et al., 2016; Lieber, 2015). Such pharmacokinetic drawbacks have spurred efforts to develop improved derivatives that possess enhanced potency and bioavailability. For instance, SAR studies have evaluated modifications to the 4′‐aminophenyl substituent, yielding analogs with substantially higher binding affinities (Toth et al., 2012). Nonetheless, all available records indicate that GSK0660 itself has remained primarily as a research tool rather than advancing to clinical or even advanced developmental stages in any therapeutic domain beyond the proof‐of‐concept experimental setting.

III. MECHANISM OF ACTION  

At the molecular level, GSK0660 exerts its action by binding selectively to the ligand‐binding domain (LBD) of PPARβ/δ, thereby inhibiting receptor activation. This interaction is competitive in nature; GSK0660 occupies the receptor pocket, effectively blocking binding of endogenous ligands or synthetic agonists (Toth et al., 2012). The molecular determinants of this selective interaction have been elucidated through SAR studies. The methoxy‐substituted 1,4‐diaminobenzene core forms critical hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic contacts with specific residues within the LBD, while the 4′‐aminophenyl group and the methyl thiophene carboxylate moiety contribute additional electrostatic and van der Waals interactions that stabilize the binding complex (Toth et al., 2012).

A notable aspect of GSK0660’s pharmacology is its dual mode of action. In addition to functioning as a competitive antagonist, GSK0660 has been reported in some experimental contexts to act as an inverse agonist. Under basal conditions—where PPARβ/δ exhibits a low level of constitutive transcriptional activity—GSK0660 can further suppress receptor activity beyond mere inhibition by promoting the recruitment of transcriptional corepressors. These corepressors (including members of the NCoR/SMRT family and class I histone deacetylases) bind to the receptor‐corepressor complex, modifying chromatin structure and decreasing transcription of PPARβ/δ target genes (Lieber, 2015; Monsalve et al., 2013). Through these mechanisms, GSK0660 effectively shifts the balance away from gene activation toward transcriptional repression.

The detailed molecular interactions responsible for these phenomena have been characterized using a variety of biochemical assays, including competitive TR‐FRET binding studies and reporter assays in cell‐based systems (Toth et al., 2012). The ability of GSK0660 to inhibit transcriptional activation in response to exogenous agonists has been well demonstrated in several cell models—including skeletal muscle myoblasts where PPARβ/δ activates genes such as carnitine palmitoyltransferase‐1A or pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase‐4 (Monsalve et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2016). This cellular pharmacology underpins its potential to serve as an experimental “off‐switch” for PPARβ/δ‐mediated transcription.

In the context of the proposed repurposing hypothesis for dry AMD, the mechanistic emphasis shifts toward the regulation of lysosomal biogenesis through transcription factor EB (TFEB). Although direct evidence linking PPARβ/δ antagonism with the modulation of TFEB activity in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells is not yet established, the working hypothesis posits that PPARβ/δ normally exerts a repressive influence on a suite of genes involved in lysosomal function—the coordinated lysosomal expression and regulation (CLEAR) network. TFEB is recognized as the master regulator of lysosomal and autophagic gene expression and, upon activation, translocates to the nucleus to drive expression of critical lysosomal proteins such as LAMP1 and proteases such as Cathepsin D (Abokyi & Tse, 2025). By antagonizing PPARβ/δ, GSK0660 is hypothesized to relieve this repressive effect, thereby “releasing the brake” on TFEB activity. This would allow for enhanced transcription of lysosomal genes that are vital for the degradation of photoreceptor outer segments (POS) and the clearance of toxic lipofuscin constituents like A2E.

It is important to note that while the biochemical rationale is compelling, the specific molecular details linking PPARβ/δ inhibition to enhanced TFEB function have yet to be firmly established in ocular models. Nonetheless, the ability of GSK0660 to modulate receptor conformation and corepressor recruitment provides a credible mechanistic basis for the hypothesis, even as further studies must elucidate the crosstalk between PPARβ/δ signaling and the lysosomal gene network in RPE cells (Toth et al., 2012; Abokyi & Tse, 2025; Lieber, 2015).

IV. EXPECTED EFFECT IN RPE MODELS  

Dry AMD is characterized by gradual dysfunction and atrophy of the RPE, which plays an essential role in the daily phagocytosis and degradation of photoreceptor outer segments (POS). A central pathogenic mechanism in dry AMD is the accumulation of undigested POS material and the toxic byproduct A2E, which, in turn, leads to lipofuscin deposition, oxidative stress, and eventual RPE cell death. Critical to the maintenance of RPE homeostasis is the proper function of the lysosomal machinery responsible for cellular clearance. Lysosomal biogenesis, autophagic flux, and the efficient degradation of internalized debris are centrally regulated by transcription factor EB (TFEB), which activates the CLEAR network of genes encoding proteins such as LAMP1 (a lysosomal‐associated membrane protein) and Cathepsin D (a key lysosomal protease) (Abokyi & Tse, 2025).

Under normal conditions, PPARβ/δ appears to play a role in regulating gene expression through the recruitment of coactivators and corepressors. The hypothesis driving the repurposing of GSK0660 for dry AMD is that activated PPARβ/δ imposes a repressive signal on the CLEAR network genes, thereby limiting TFEB‐mediated lysosomal biogenesis in RPE cells. In an experimental paradigm based on A2E‐loaded RPE cell cultures—a standard in vitro model that mimics the cellular environment of dry AMD—it is anticipated that treatment with GSK0660 at concentrations approximating its receptor inhibition IC₅₀ (~200 nM) will alleviate PPARβ/δ‐mediated repression. This derepression is expected to promote the nuclear translocation of TFEB and upregulate its target genes, culminating in an enhanced lysosomal degradative capacity (Abokyi & Tse, 2025).

The expected cellular responses following GSK0660 treatment in RPE models can be summarized as follows. First, by interrupting the ligand‐induced activation of PPARβ/δ, GSK0660 should shift the balance between transcriptional coactivators and corepressors in favor of a transcriptional environment that supports lysosomal gene expression. This would lead to a measurable increase in the mRNA and protein levels of LAMP1 and Cathepsin D, both of which are critical for lysosomal function and for the degradation of POS components (Abokyi & Tse, 2025). Second, the enhanced expression of these proteins should translate into an improved capacity for POS degradation—a phenotype that can be monitored using assays that track the clearance of fluorescently labeled POS or the reduction in A2E accumulation. Third, improved lysosomal biogenesis and autophagic clearance are expected to reduce the chronic accumulation of lipofuscin, thereby mitigating oxidative stress and preserving RPE function over time.

In addition to these molecular and cellular endpoints, there is also the expectation that successful derepression of TFEB activity will indirectly contribute to improved cell viability and resistance to stress. The RPE, being highly active in terms of metabolic turnover and phagocytic activity, is particularly vulnerable to the detrimental effects of lysosomal dysfunction. Thus, a treatment that effectively restores lysosomal function is likely to bolster the RPE’s ability to manage accumulated debris and maintain metabolic homeostasis.

It should be acknowledged that one of the primary challenges will be the optimization of the effective dose of GSK0660 in the context of ocular cells. While the compound exhibits potent receptor inhibition in vitro, previous studies have identified limitations with its bioavailability and chemical stability (Toth et al., 2012; Strosznajder et al., 2021). These issues necessitate careful attention to formulation and drug delivery strategies tailored to the ocular environment. For example, controlled‐release formulations or nanoparticle‐based delivery systems might be required to ensure that effective local concentrations of GSK0660 can be maintained in the retina while avoiding systemic exposure and off‐target effects.

Moreover, despite the attractive theoretical framework linking PPARβ/δ antagonism to TFEB activation, direct experimental evidence in RPE cells is currently lacking. As such, the expected effect remains a hypothesis that must be rigorously validated through a series of in vitro assays—including quantitative PCR for lysosomal gene expression, immunoblotting for protein markers, and functional assays to assess the clearance of photoreceptor debris. Additional studies might also involve monitoring autophagic flux using established markers such as LC3-II and p62 to provide a more comprehensive picture of the compound’s impact on RPE cell homeostasis.

V. OVERALL EVALUATION  

GSK0660 stands out as a highly characterized PPARβ/δ antagonist with a well‐documented profile in non‐ocular applications. The compound’s discovery as the first selective ligand for PPARβ/δ established a benchmark in the field and has since made it a prominent research tool in studies of neurodegeneration, inflammation, and even dermatological applications such as psoriasis (Toth et al., 2012; Antonosante et al., 2023; Hack et al., 2012). From a medicinal chemistry perspective, the structural features of GSK0660—the methoxy‐substituted 1,4‐diaminobenzene core, the 4′‐aminophenyl substituent, and the methyl thiophene carboxylate moiety—endow the molecule with high selectivity for the receptor. SAR studies have demonstrated that modifications to these groups can further enhance binding affinity, offering prospects for optimization if needed (Toth et al., 2012).

The principal strength of the compound lies in its receptor selectivity and in its demonstrated capacity to function as both a competitive antagonist and an inverse agonist. This duality is central to the proposed mechanism for reversing PPARβ/δ‐mediated repression of TFEB, which in turn may enhance lysosomal biogenesis and improve the degradative capacity of RPE cells. Considering that lysosomal dysfunction and the resultant accumulation of undegraded POS and toxic byproducts such as A2E are recognized pathological features of dry AMD, the hypothesis that GSK0660 can restore a more robust lysosomal function is innovative and scientifically compelling (Abokyi & Tse, 2025).

However, significant caveats temper this optimism. One major limitation is that, to date, GSK0660 has not been studied in the context of retinal diseases or any ocular applications. Although extensive preclinical research has been undertaken in models of Parkinson’s disease and psoriasis, no direct evidence supports its efficacy in modulating RPE cell lysosomal function or in mitigating dry AMD pathology (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Lieber, 2015). This represents a considerable translational gap that would require substantial preclinical work to bridge.

Another critical challenge is the compound’s pharmacokinetic profile. GSK0660 is known to suffer from poor bioavailability, and it frequently requires relatively high concentrations to achieve its intended effects in vitro. Furthermore, the chemical instability of GSK0660—demonstrated by its tendency to oxidize and change color under conditions of light exposure and room temperature storage—poses additional obstacles for formulation development, especially in a sensitive environment such as the eye where prolonged stability and controlled drug release are paramount (Toth et al., 2012; Hack et al., 2012; Strosznajder et al., 2021).

The translation of these findings to an application in dry AMD would also necessitate a careful reexamination of dosing strategies and delivery mechanisms. For instance, while topical formulations have been effective in dermatological models (Hack et al., 2012), the ocular setting will require alternative approaches to bypass obstacles such as the blood‐retinal barrier and to achieve sustained local drug concentrations without systemic spillover. Innovative drug delivery platforms—perhaps involving biodegradable implants, nanoparticles, or hydrogels—will likely need to be developed to turn the promising molecular mechanism of GSK0660 into a clinically viable treatment option.

Furthermore, although the mechanistic rationale linking PPARβ/δ antagonism with the activation of TFEB is theoretically sound, the direct evidence supporting this specific link in RPE cells is limited. Existing literature on TFEB clearly demonstrates its role as a master regulator of lysosomal genes in retinal cells (Abokyi & Tse, 2025), yet none of the studies directly establish that PPARβ/δ provides a repressive signal to TFEB in this context. Thus, the entire repurposing hypothesis, while attractive, remains speculative until dedicated studies in RPE models confirm that inhibition of PPARβ/δ by GSK0660 indeed results in derepression of TFEB and a subsequent upregulation of lysosomal markers such as LAMP1 and Cathepsin D.

Lastly, while GSK0660 has garnered a robust reputation as a research tool, its historical use in non‐ocular tissues suggests that extensive medicinal chemistry optimization may be required to enhance its physicochemical properties for ocular therapy. In essence, the compound may serve as an excellent lead structure, but modifications that improve its bioavailability, chemical stability, and potency in the ocular microenvironment will be essential steps before considering any clinical translation.

In summary, GSK0660 embodies both promise and challenges as a candidate for repurposing to treat dry AMD. On the one hand, its well‐defined receptor selectivity and dual mode of inhibition provide a solid mechanistic foundation to hypothesize that, by antagonizing PPARβ/δ, it may relieve repression of TFEB and thereby restore lysosomal function in RPE cells. On the other hand, the lack of any direct ocular data, coupled with severe limitations in bioavailability and chemical stability, underscores that significant preclinical validation and formulation optimization will be required before GSK0660 can be considered a viable therapeutic candidate for dry AMD (Toth et al., 2012; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Zhang et al., 2016; Hack et al., 2012).

────────────────────────────────────────  
VI. CONCLUDING REMARKS  

Repurposing GSK0660 for dry AMD is underpinned by an innovative hypothesis that bridges nuclear receptor biology with lysosomal regulation—a convergence that is highly relevant given the central role of lysosomal dysfunction in RPE degeneration. The expected therapeutic mechanism is clear: by blocking PPARβ/δ activity, GSK0660 may relieve a repressive influence on the CLEAR network, thereby enabling TFEB to drive lysosomal biogenesis and enhance the clearance of toxic photoreceptor debris. Such a therapeutic approach would address a key pathogenic process behind the progressive accumulation of lipofuscin and other deleterious cellular materials in the RPE, which are hallmarks of dry AMD (Abokyi & Tse, 2025).

Nonetheless, several hurdles must be overcome for this conceptual strategy to move forward. First, the absence of any preexisting data linking GSK0660 to ocular applications necessitates a comprehensive set of in vitro experiments targeting RPE cells—including assays for gene expression, lysosomal function, and POS degradation. Second, the known limitations of GSK0660—poor bioavailability, chemical instability, and the requirement for high concentrations—must be addressed via improved drug formulations or through the development of optimized analogs. Third, it will be critical to investigate whether the proposed crosstalk between PPARβ/δ signaling and TFEB‐mediated lysosomal regulation holds true in the RPE microenvironment, potentially involving studies using animal models of dry AMD. Finally, given the highly specialized nature of ocular drug delivery, translational efforts may need to incorporate modern delivery strategies such as implantable controlled‐release devices, nanoparticles, or hydrogels to ensure sustained local efficacy while minimizing systemic exposure.

In its current form, GSK0660 remains a highly informative research tool with excellent selectivity for PPARβ/δ, yet its direct translation into an ocular therapeutic faces significant challenges. The compound’s historical utility in preclinical models of Parkinson’s disease and psoriasis attests to its potential to modulate critical signaling pathways; however, the leap from these systems to effective treatment of dry AMD will require concerted efforts in medicinal chemistry, formulation science, and rigorous preclinical testing (Antonosante et al., 2023; Hack et al., 2012; Boya et al., 2023).

Overall, the repurposing hypothesis for GSK0660 offers a high‐risk, high‐reward strategy. The innovative concept of enhancing lysosomal function via the relief of PPARβ/δ‐mediated repression of TFEB is attractive and represents a novel intervention point for restoring RPE homeostasis. Should preclinical studies confirm that antagonism of PPARβ/δ indeed leads to significant upregulation of lysosomal genes and a measurable improvement in POS degradation in RPE cells, GSK0660 or its optimized derivatives could provide the basis for a new therapeutic class targeting the underlying pathophysiology of dry AMD.

Moving forward, the research team should prioritize:  
1. Rigorous in vitro validation in human RPE models (such as ARPE‐19 cells or primary RPE cultures) to examine TFEB activation and lysosomal gene expression changes (e.g., increases in LAMP1 and Cathepsin D levels) following treatment with GSK0660 at appropriate concentrations.  
2. Detailed pharmacokinetic and stability studies, coupled with efforts to modify the chemical structure (e.g., by optimizing the 4′‐aminophenyl substituent as suggested by SAR studies) to improve both the potency and bioavailability of the therapeutic agent.  
3. Development of ocular delivery strategies that overcome the challenges posed by the eye’s protective barriers—ensuring that effective drug concentrations can be delivered locally while minimizing systemic exposure.  
4. In vivo studies in suitable animal models of dry AMD to evaluate both the safety and efficacy profile of GSK0660, with endpoints that include assessment of RPE cell function, clearance of photoreceptor debris, and overall preservation of retinal structure and function.

In conclusion, while GSK0660 holds promise as a novel modality for enhancing lysosomal biogenesis via PPARβ/δ inhibition, its transition from a research tool in neurodegenerative and dermatological studies to an effective treatment for dry AMD is contingent on overcoming significant pharmacokinetic and formulation hurdles. The ultimate success of this repurposing effort will depend on rigorous preclinical validation that demonstrates not only the expected molecular effects in RPE cells but also the clinical relevance of these observations in ameliorating retinal degeneration.

VII. SUMMARY  

To summarize, the evaluation of GSK0660 for dry AMD repurposing can be encapsulated as follows:

• Overview: GSK0660 is a synthetically derived, thiophenecarboxylate‐class molecule designed as the first selective PPARβ/δ antagonist. Its structure, defined by a methoxy‐substituted 1,4‐diaminobenzene core, a 4′‐aminophenyl substituent, and a methyl thiophene carboxylate moiety, is supported by extensive SAR data that also suggest avenues for chemical optimization (Toth et al., 2012).  
• Therapeutic History: Historically, the compound has been employed as a research tool in models of neurodegeneration and inflammatory skin diseases. No literature presently documents its use in ocular applications or in treating conditions resembling dry AMD, a gap that underscores both the opportunity and the risk inherent to its repurposing (Antonosante et al., 2023; Hack et al., 2012; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).  
• Mechanism of Action: GSK0660 works by competitively binding to the PPARβ/δ ligand‐binding domain and, in certain contexts, acting as an inverse agonist via recruitment of transcriptional corepressors. The repurposing hypothesis contends that by blocking PPARβ/δ, the repressive influence on TFEB is alleviated, leading to enhanced lysosomal biogenesis—a mechanism that, while plausible, awaits definitive validation in RPE cells (Toth et al., 2012; Lieber, 2015; Monsalve et al., 2013).  
• Expected Effect: In A2E‐loaded RPE cells, treatment with GSK0660 is anticipated to upregulate the expression of TFEB target genes such as LAMP1 and Cathepsin D. This should result in improved POS degradation and lysosomal clearance, which are crucial for countering the pathogenesis of dry AMD. Nevertheless, dose optimization and adaptation to the ocular milieu remain key challenges (Abokyi & Tse, 2025).  
• Overall Evaluation: While GSK0660 is conceptually compelling because of its selective PPARβ/δ antagonism and its innovative potential to unlock TFEB‐mediated lysosomal pathways, its poor bioavailability, chemical instability, and lack of ocular‐specific preclinical data temper enthusiasm. The candidate is best viewed as a valuable starting point that will require considerable further optimization—both chemically and in terms of delivery methods—before it can be advanced as a viable therapeutic for dry AMD (Toth et al., 2012; Hack et al., 2012; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).

The repurposing of GSK0660 for dry AMD thus presents a visionary, albeit risky, strategy that could address an unmet clinical need if the central hypothesis is confirmed and associated pharmacokinetic challenges can be overcome.

────────────────────────────────────────  
VIII. FINAL CONSIDERATIONS  

Given the current state of evidence, it is clear that while GSK0660 boasts a strong mechanistic rationale due to its precise inhibition of PPARβ/δ and well‐characterized molecular interactions, significant gaps remain with respect to its application in ocular therapeutics. For instance, elucidating whether PPARβ/δ indeed represses the CLEAR network in RPE cells is critical to substantiate the therapeutic hypothesis. Equally important is formulating strategies to mitigate the compound’s bioavailability and stability issues in the highly sensitive ocular environment. Successful resolution of these challenges would not only validate the approach but might also allow GSK0660 or its improved derivatives to emerge as a pioneering therapy targeting lysosomal dysfunction—a central driver in the progression of dry AMD.

In light of these considerations, the repurposing strategy for GSK0660 should be pursued with a dual focus on mechanistic validation in relevant cellular models and on the parallel development of advanced ocular delivery systems. Such a multidisciplinary research effort would be essential to transform a promising preclinical compound into a candidate that can safely and effectively restore RPE lysosomal function in patients with dry AMD.

To conclude, while GSK0660 holds promise as a novel modality for enhancing lysosomal biogenesis via PPARβ/δ inhibition, its transition from a research tool in neurodegenerative and dermatological studies to an effective treatment for dry AMD is contingent on overcoming significant pharmacokinetic and formulation hurdles. The ultimate success of this repurposing effort will depend on rigorous preclinical validation that demonstrates not only the expected molecular effects in RPE cells but also the clinical relevance of these observations in ameliorating retinal degeneration.

This comprehensive evaluation thereby underscores that GSK0660 provides a scientifically intriguing starting point for a new therapeutic strategy against dry AMD. However, its progression will demand substantial additional research—spanning mechanistic studies, medicinal chemistry optimization, and innovative ocular formulation development—to fully assess and harness its potential in this context (Toth et al., 2012; ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Zhang et al., 2016; Hack et al., 2012).
