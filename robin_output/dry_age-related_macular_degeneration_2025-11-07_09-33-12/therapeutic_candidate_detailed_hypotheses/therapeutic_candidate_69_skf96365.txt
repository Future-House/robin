Proposal for SKF96365 (TRPC channel inhibitor; Tocris Cat. No. 1261)

Below is a comprehensive evaluation of SKF96365 as a repurposed therapeutic candidate for dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD). The following report is organized into five sections: (I) Overview of Therapeutic Candidate, (II) Therapeutic History, (III) Mechanism of Action, (IV) Expected Effect in Dry AMD, and (V) Overall Evaluation. Each section cites multiple studies from the available literature context.

─────────────────────────────  
I. OVERVIEW OF THERAPEUTIC CANDIDATE

SKF96365 (1-{[3-(4-methoxyphenyl)propoxy]-4-methoxyphenethyl}-1H-imidazole hydrochloride) is a fully synthetic small molecule discovered by medicinal chemists interested in modulating receptor-mediated calcium entry pathways. Originally introduced as an inhibitor of receptor-induced Ca²⁺ influx in non‐excitable cells such as platelets and neutrophils (Singh et al., 2010), the compound later gained attention as a modulator of transient receptor potential (TRP) channels. As a member of the imidazole derivative class, SKF96365 shares structural features with phenylalkylamine compounds and is characterized by its alkylated imidazole core embellished with substituted methoxyphenyl moieties; these structural modifications were purposefully introduced to confer the molecule with broad activity on Ca²⁺ permeation mechanisms (Harteneck & Gollasch, 2011; Singh et al., 2010; Harteneck, Klose, & Krautwurst, 2011).

The chemical synthesis of SKF96365 involves multi-step organic transformations, whereby the imidazole ring is first derivatized and subsequently appended with both aliphatic and aromatic substituents. These modifications are believed to be critical in endowing SKF96365 with the ability to interact with various ion channel proteins across the plasma membrane. Although initially developed for its capacity to block receptor-mediated Ca²⁺ entry, further pharmacological investigations revealed that SKF96365 exhibits broad-spectrum activity by inhibiting not only store-operated channels (SOCE) and receptor-operated channels (ROC) but also interfering with key subtypes of the transient receptor potential canonical (TRPC) family (Singh et al., 2010).

The compound’s broad inhibitory spectrum, however, comes at the cost of selectivity. While low micromolar concentrations (typically in the range of 2–30 μM) are required to inhibit TRPC channels, SKF96365 has also been shown to block other calcium channels such as low‐voltage‐activated T‐type channels at submicromolar concentrations (IC₅₀ ~560 nM) (Singh et al., 2010). This dual and even overlapping activity pattern reflects its classification as a non‐selective TRP channel blocker and places it within the larger group of broad-spectrum calcium channel modulators. Its widespread use across a multitude of experimental systems—from studies of cellular Ca²⁺ entry in endothelial cells to investigations in neuronal and glial systems—further underscores its utility as a pharmacological tool, despite the inherent drawbacks associated with low target selectivity (Singh et al., 2010; Harteneck, Klose, & Krautwurst, 2011; Harteneck & Gollasch, 2011).

In summary, SKF96365 is a synthetic compound belonging to the imidazole derivative family, originally designed to inhibit receptor‐mediated Ca²⁺ influx. It has been repurposed as a TRPC channel inhibitor, although its inhibitory profile extends well beyond TRPC channels to include several classes of voltage‐gated and store-operated channels. This broad mechanism of action, while advantageous for experimental dissection of Ca²⁺ signaling pathways, poses potential challenges for its development as a selective therapeutic agent for dry AMD (Singh et al., 2010).

II. THERAPEUTIC HISTORY

The therapeutic history of SKF96365 reflects its evolution from a research tool into a potential candidate for repurposing in diverse pathological states. Initially, SKF96365 was introduced in the early 1990s as an inhibitor of receptor-mediated calcium entry. Early studies demonstrated that it could effectively block Ca²⁺ influx in non‐excitable cells such as platelets, neutrophils, and endothelial cells, with reported half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC₅₀’s) of approximately 10 μM (Singh et al., 2010; Harteneck, Klose, & Krautwurst, 2011). These early findings spurred interest in its utility as a diagnostic blocker for various Ca²⁺ entry mechanisms, particularly before the molecular identification of TRP channels.

Following the molecular cloning of TRPC family members, SKF96365 was quickly repurposed to study these channels in both heterologous expression systems and native tissues. Its ability to inhibit TRPC channels in multiple cell types solidified its reputation as a key pharmacological tool. For example, studies in cultured cells such as PC-12 and HL-60 cells provided evidence for its capacity to differentiate between ATP- and bradykinin-induced Ca²⁺ entry and offered insights into receptor-regulated calcium entry pathways (Harteneck & Gollasch, 2011). In addition to non-ocular systems, SKF96365 has been widely used in research involving the cardiovascular and nervous systems, highlighting its diverse biological applications.

In the context relevant to ocular research, several investigations have applied SKF96365 to retinal models. For instance, studies in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells indicated that basal calcium entry in these cells is mediated, at least in part, by TRPC channels. One particular study demonstrated that in both cultured RPE cells and primary human RPE cells, SKF96365 reduced intracellular Ca²⁺ concentration in a dose-dependent fashion, implicating TRPC channels in the maintenance of basal Ca²⁺ levels necessary for proper RPE function (Wimmers & Strauss, 2007). Moreover, ocular safety data from experiments using SKF96365 in lamina cribrosa cells—cells that contribute to the optic nerve head microenvironment implicated in glaucoma—have shown that at concentrations around 10 μM to 100 μM, the compound can modulate Ca²⁺-dependent signaling without overt cytotoxicity (Irnaten et al., 2020).

Additional preclinical studies have not only examined SKF96365 in the context of RPE cellular physiology but also explored its potential in anti-proliferative and anti-fibrotic applications. In malignant glioma cells, for example, concentrations in the 25 μM range were shown to suppress proliferation, an effect that was attributed to its combined blockade of TRPC channels and low‐voltage‐activated T‐type channels (Singh et al., 2010). Despite these encouraging preclinical findings, to date there have been no clinical or veterinary applications of SKF96365 specifically for the treatment of dry AMD or similar retinal degenerative conditions.

Collectively, the therapeutic history of SKF96365 demonstrates that while it has been a valuable experimental tool for elucidating Ca²⁺ dynamics in a wide array of tissues—including ocular cells—it has not yet been translated into a clinical therapeutic for conditions such as dry AMD. Its continued use in preclinical studies helps to support the strategy of targeting calcium dysregulation; however, concerns over its off-target activity underline the challenges inherent in repurposing a broadly acting ion channel modulator for chronic degenerative diseases (Singh et al., 2010; Wimmers & Strauss, 2007; Irnaten et al., 2020).

─────────────────────────────  
III. MECHANISM OF ACTION

A detailed understanding of the mechanism of action of SKF96365 provides important insights into both its potential utility and its limitations as a therapeutic candidate. SKF96365 was originally characterized as an inhibitor of receptor-mediated calcium entry, a function that was later expanded upon with the identification of TRP channels. Mechanistically, SKF96365 has been shown to inhibit receptor-operated and store-operated calcium entry pathways, acting as a noncompetitive, state-independent, and voltage-insensitive blocker—that is, it interferes with Ca²⁺ entry regardless of whether channels are in the closed, open, or inactivated state (Singh et al., 2010; Harteneck & Gollasch, 2011).

Electrophysiological studies in heterologous systems reveal that SKF96365 inhibits recombinant TRPC channel currents at IC₅₀ values generally in the low micromolar range (approximately 5–30 μM). However, a striking aspect of its pharmacological profile is its potent inhibition of low‐voltage‐activated T‐type calcium channels. For example, investigations using human CaV3.1 channels have determined an IC₅₀ of approximately 560 nM for SKF96365—a potency that is nearly an order of magnitude higher than that observed for TRPC channels (Singh et al., 2010). This inhibitory action on T‐type channels is consistent with findings in native tissue models such as rat cerebellar Purkinje cells, where SKF96365 produced profound and rapid blockade of T-type Ca²⁺ currents.

The molecular basis for SKF96365’s broad channel blockade likely involves a direct interaction with the ion permeation pores of these channels. While the detailed binding site has not been unequivocally defined, evidence suggests that SKF96365 interacts allosterically with channel structures, leading to pore block without significantly altering the kinetics of channel activation or inactivation. This mode of inhibition appears to be state-independent, meaning that channel blockade occurs irrespective of the channel’s conformational state (Singh et al., 2010). In addition to its effects on TRPC and T-type channels, SKF96365 also impairs the function of high‐voltage‐activated calcium channels (including L‐type, P/Q‐type, and N‐type channels) when used at typical concentrations employed in TRPC channel studies (Singh et al., 2010).

Beyond calcium channels, the compound has been reported to exhibit off-target activities against other ion channels and cellular components. For instance, it has been shown to inhibit certain potassium channels, interfere with sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺-ATPase activity, and even affect nicotinic receptor function in some cellular contexts (Harteneck & Gollasch, 2011; Singh et al., 2010). This promiscuity in channel blockade is a double-edged sword: on one hand, it increases the overall capacity of SKF96365 to alter intracellular Ca²⁺ dynamics; on the other hand, it complicates the attribution of specific physiological or pathological outcomes solely to TRPC inhibition.

From a molecular perspective, TRPC channels in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) are thought to contribute to the maintenance of basal calcium levels that are requisite for key cellular processes such as phagocytosis and endolysosomal trafficking. Although SKF96365 was not originally designed with ocular applications in mind, its ability to inhibit these channels makes it a candidate for modulating disrupted Ca²⁺ signaling in RPE cells—a critical factor in the pathogenesis of dry AMD (Wimmers & Strauss, 2007; Zhao et al., 2015). However, the breadth of SKF96365’s mechanism of action means that at concentrations required to modulate TRPC channel activity within the RPE, the compound is very likely to concurrently block other calcium channel subtypes. This scenario is corroborated by data demonstrating that at 10 μM, SKF96365 can block recombinant channels from all three major voltage-gated calcium channel classes, including CaV1 (L-type), CaV2 (P/Q-, N-type), and CaV3 (T-type) channels (Singh et al., 2010).

The molecular consequences of such broad calcium blockade include an overall reduction in Ca²⁺ influx, which could normalize cytosolic Ca²⁺ transients if they are aberrantly elevated but might also disrupt other calcium-dependent signaling cascades necessary for healthy cellular function. In the context of RPE cells, where tightly regulated Ca²⁺ oscillations are critical for proper phagosome–lysosome fusion and photoreceptor outer segment (POS) degradation, this balance is particularly delicate. The potential therapeutic benefit of SKF96365 in dry AMD thus hinges on its ability to recalibrate Ca²⁺ homeostasis without triggering deleterious off-target effects (Singh et al., 2010).

─────────────────────────────  
IV. EXPECTED EFFECT IN DRY AMD

Dry age-related macular degeneration is characterized by progressive RPE dysfunction, abnormal calcium signaling, impaired phagocytosis of POS, and subsequent accumulation of toxic deposits such as lipofuscin. In healthy RPE cells, calcium regulation is essential for several cellular processes, including the fusion of phagosomes with lysosomes—a step that is critical for the degradation of shed photoreceptor outer segments. Dysfunction in these calcium-dependent events is believed to contribute to the pathogenesis of dry AMD (Zhao et al., 2015; Wimmers & Strauss, 2007).

The central hypothesis under consideration is that SKF96365 will modulate Ca²⁺ influx through TRPC channels in RPE cells, with an approximate IC₅₀ near 5 μM, thereby normalizing cytosolic Ca²⁺ transients. This normalization is expected to restore proper Ca²⁺ dynamics that drive efficient phagosome trafficking and lysosome fusion. In vitro studies using ARPE-19 cells—the human cell line commonly employed as a model for RPE physiology—demonstrate that disrupting abnormal Ca²⁺ entry can lead to improved co-localization of lysosomal markers such as LAMP1 with intracellular phagosomes. The increased co-localization is taken as an indicator of enhanced phagosome–lysosome fusion and, consequently, more effective degradation of POS (Wimmers & Strauss, 2007).

In practical terms, if SKF96365 is able to restore near-physiological Ca²⁺ transients in the RPE, the process of phagosome maturation would likely proceed more efficiently. Under normal conditions, following the ingestion of photoreceptor outer segments, phagosomes are trafficked through a maturation process involving early endosomal (e.g., Rab5-positive) and late endosomal/lysosomal (e.g., Rab7-, LAMP1-positive) steps before complete degradation occurs (Zhao et al., 2015; Keeling et al., 2018). By counteracting the dysregulation in Ca²⁺ entry, SKF96365 could theoretically help re-establish the kinetics of phagosome–lysosome fusion and POS clearance, which might slow or prevent the accumulation of deleterious deposits in RPE cells—a hallmark of dry AMD progression.

Moreover, evidence from ocular research suggests that SKF96365 has been applied in experimental settings beyond RPE studies. For instance, in lamina cribrosa cells—a cell type implicated in glaucomatous pathology—SKF96365 at concentrations around 10 μM was able to mitigate oxidative stress–induced profibrotic gene expression. While this study was not focused on AMD, the fact that the compound could safely modulate Ca²⁺-dependent signaling pathways in an ocular context lends indirect support to the idea that similar mechanisms might be harnessed in RPE cells (Irnaten et al., 2020).

However, a critical aspect of the expected effect is the balance between beneficial TRPC channel inhibition and detrimental off-target blockade. Because SKF96365 potently blocks T-type calcium channels (with an IC₅₀ near 560 nM) and also affects high-voltage-activated channels, there is a substantial risk that, at concentrations effective for TRPC modulation, the compound will inhibit additional calcium channels necessary for other vital RPE functions. As a result, while the anticipated normalization of Ca²⁺ transients may promote phagosome maturation and enhance LAMP1 co-localization with phagosomes, the broad inhibitory profile could also lead to unintended suppression of other calcium-dependent processes that maintain RPE cell homeostasis (Singh et al., 2010).

In summary, the expected effect of SKF96365 in dry AMD is twofold. First, it is anticipated to reduce pathological Ca²⁺ overload in RPE cells by inhibiting aberrant TRPC channel activity, thereby restoring the intracellular Ca²⁺ signaling required for phagosome–lysosome fusion. Second, this normalization is expected to improve the degradation of photoreceptor outer segments, which, in turn, would reduce the accumulation of cellular debris that contributes to RPE dysfunction in dry AMD. Nonetheless, these effects must be interpreted with caution given the compound’s propensity for off-target interactions, which may complicate its overall benefit-risk profile (Wimmers & Strauss, 2007; Singh et al., 2010; Zhao et al., 2015).

V. OVERALL EVALUATION

Evaluating SKF96365 for repurposing in the treatment of dry AMD requires a careful weighing of its mechanistic rationale against the limitations imposed by its pharmacological profile. On the strength side, SKF96365 offers a compelling mechanism based on decades of research linking Ca²⁺ dysregulation to RPE dysfunction. Its ability to inhibit TRPC channels—a family of ion channels that contribute to the maintenance of basal Ca²⁺ levels and are critical for processes such as phagosome trafficking and lysosome fusion in RPE cells—directly addresses a known pathological mechanism in dry AMD (Singh et al., 2010; Wimmers & Strauss, 2007). The hypothesis that normalization of aberrant Ca²⁺ transients can enhance the clearance of photoreceptor outer segments is well supported by experimental evidence demonstrating improved lysosomal marker co-localization in cultured RPE cells when Ca²⁺ entry is moderated (Wimmers & Strauss, 2007).

Additionally, SKF96365 has an established track record as an ocular research tool. Studies in lamina cribrosa cells in glaucoma models have shown that the compound can be administered at ocularly relevant concentrations with observable functional effects on Ca²⁺-dependent gene expression without immediate cytotoxicity (Irnaten et al., 2020). This observed ocular safety in short-term studies lends some support to its potential translation into an ocular therapeutic context. Moreover, the broader utility of SKF96365 in modulating intracellular Ca²⁺ has been confirmed in a variety of preclinical models, ranging from glioma cell lines to endothelial cells, highlighting its robustness as a Ca²⁺ channel inhibitor (Singh et al., 2010).

However, several significant weaknesses temper the overall enthusiasm for direct repurposing of SKF96365 as a treatment for dry AMD. The foremost limitation is its lack of selectivity. Although SKF96365 inhibits TRPC channels in the micromolar range, it concurrently blocks other calcium channels—most notably T-type channels—with a potency that is approximately ninefold greater than that for TRPC channels (Singh et al., 2010). Such potent off-target blockade is problematic because T-type calcium channels, along with L-type and other voltage-gated channels, are essential for a multitude of cellular functions within the retina and the central nervous system. Their inadvertent inhibition could lead to compromised cellular viability and dysregulation of signaling cascades that extend beyond phagosome clearance in the RPE (Singh et al., 2010).

Another critical concern is the compound’s broad spectrum of activity. In addition to its effects on TRPC and T-type channels, SKF96365 has been shown to inhibit high-voltage-activated calcium channels, interfere with potassium channel conductance, inhibit sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺-ATPase activity, and even modulate nicotinic receptor function (Harteneck & Gollasch, 2011; Pena & Ordaz, 2008). The concomitant blockade of these various ion channel systems increases the risk that systemic or even localized ocular administration of the compound will produce off-target adverse effects that may compromise both retinal and extra-retinal cell functions. In the context of dry AMD—a chronic degenerative condition that would likely require long-term therapy—the risk of cumulative toxicity or dysregulation of fundamental Ca²⁺-dependent processes is particularly concerning.

Moreover, while mechanistic studies in cell culture models (such as ARPE-19 cells) have provided valuable insights into SKF96365’s role in modulating Ca²⁺ signaling, there is a distinct lack of in vivo data demonstrating its efficacy in models of dry AMD. The retinal environment in vivo is complex, with contributions from multiple cell types—including RPE, photoreceptors, microglia, and vascular endothelial cells—and is subject to intricate immune and inflammatory interactions. The translation of in vitro findings to in vivo efficacy, especially for a compound with a narrow therapeutic index resulting from overlapping inhibitory concentrations for TRPC versus T-type channels, remains a formidable challenge (Wimmers & Strauss, 2007; Irnaten et al., 2020; Singh et al., 2010).

A further limitation is that the expected benefit—namely, the normalization of Ca²⁺ transients to restore phagolysosomal fusion—relies on a very specific modulation of intracellular signals. RPE cells exhibit highly regulated calcium oscillations that are critical not only for phagocytosis but also for maintaining cellular metabolism, gene expression, and autophagic processes. Given that SKF96365 is a nonselective inhibitor, it is likely to perturb these tightly controlled Ca²⁺ signaling networks in ways that are not entirely predictable. Such disturbances might lead to unintended alterations in RPE function, potentially exacerbating rather than ameliorating the compromised homeostasis seen in dry AMD (Zhao et al., 2015; Singh et al., 2010).

Finally, while there is some evidence in ocular contexts from glaucoma models, the chronic administration required for a degenerative disease such as dry AMD has not been explored. Long-term safety studies in animal models, coupled with detailed pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic analyses, are necessary to fully assess whether SKF96365 could be safely repurposed for ocular use. The risk of systemic absorption and off-target effects in the central nervous system or cardiovascular tissues cannot be ignored, given the compound’s known actions on multiple ion channel types (Irnaten et al., 2020; Maier et al., 2015).

In weighing these strengths and weaknesses, SKF96365 appears to offer valuable proof-of-concept evidence that modulating Ca²⁺ influx through TRPC channels can impact processes relevant to RPE phagosome trafficking and POS clearance. However, the compound’s broad-spectrum inhibition, particularly its potent T-type calcium channel blockade, undermines its candidacy as a safe, selective, and effective therapeutic agent for dry AMD. Consequently, while SKF96365 remains an important pharmacological tool in dissecting the role of Ca²⁺ signaling in RPE physiology, its direct repurposing for clinical treatment of dry AMD would require significant chemical refinement—or the development of derivative compounds with improved selectivity—to narrow its inhibitory spectrum and thus enhance its therapeutic index (Singh et al., 2010; Wimmers & Strauss, 2007).

─────────────────────────────  
VI. CONCLUSION

In conclusion, the comprehensive literature review indicates that SKF96365, a synthetic imidazole derivative initially developed as an inhibitor of receptor-mediated calcium entry, demonstrates robust activity against a variety of calcium channels—including TRPC channels that contribute to basal Ca²⁺ influx in retinal pigment epithelial cells. Its ability to modulate Ca²⁺ transients provides a mechanistically attractive hypothesis for restoring proper phagosome–lysosome fusion and enhancing photoreceptor outer segment clearance—which are central to the pathogenesis of dry age-related macular degeneration (Singh et al., 2010; Wimmers & Strauss, 2007).

Nonetheless, significant challenges temper its clinical potential. The compound’s nonselectivity, evidenced by its potent blockade of low-voltage-activated T-type channels (IC₅₀ ~560 nM) and its concurrent inhibition of high-voltage-activated calcium channels and other ion channel systems, severely narrows the feasible therapeutic window (Singh et al., 2010; Harteneck & Gollasch, 2011). The risk that, at effective doses intended to normalize RPE Ca²⁺ signaling, SKF96365 might inadvertently suppress essential calcium-dependent processes is nontrivial, especially given the delicate balance of Ca²⁺ homeostasis required in the RPE for photoreceptor maintenance and survival (Singh et al., 2010; Maier et al., 2015).

Furthermore, although preclinical in vitro evidence in ARPE-19 cells and similar models is promising, there remains a critical gap in in vivo data specific to dry AMD. The complex interplay of cellular, molecular, and environmental factors in the human retina poses considerable hurdles for the translation of cell culture findings to clinically relevant outcomes. In addition, the chronic nature of AMD necessitates long-term safety and efficacy data—information that is currently lacking for SKF96365 in the context of ocular therapeutics (Irnaten et al., 2020; Maier et al., 2015).

Given these considerations, SKF96365 may best be regarded at this stage not as a final drug candidate for dry AMD therapy, but rather as a lead compound that validates the strategy of targeting aberrant Ca²⁺ signaling within RPE cells. Future research should focus on chemically optimizing SKF96365 to enhance its selectivity for TRPC channels while minimizing off-target interactions with T-type, L-type, and other voltage-gated channels. Parallel investigations into alternative TRPC modulators with a narrower pharmacological profile may also prove fruitful. In any case, rigorous preclinical studies—including in vivo models of dry AMD—are imperative before clinical translation can be contemplated (Singh et al., 2010; Wimmers & Strauss, 2007).

Ultimately, the therapeutic strategy of modulating Ca²⁺ influx to restore phagosome–lysosome fusion and improve POS clearance in RPE cells remains a compelling avenue for treating dry AMD. SKF96365 offers a valuable starting point and proof-of-concept, but its broad-spectrum ion channel inhibition necessitates careful re-engineering to achieve the necessary balance between efficacy and safety. Only with further chemical refinement, targeted in vivo validation, and comprehensive toxicity evaluation can such a compound be advanced toward clinical trials for dry AMD (Singh et al., 2010; Wimmers & Strauss, 2007; Zhao et al., 2015).

OVERALL EVALUATION SUMMARY

• Overview of the Candidate:  
SKF96365 is a synthetic imidazole derivative belonging to the broad-spectrum TRP channel blockers, originally developed to inhibit receptor-mediated Ca²⁺ entry. Its chemical structure and modifications enable it to interact with multiple Ca²⁺ channels, making it a useful tool in experimental Ca²⁺ signaling studies (Singh et al., 2010; Harteneck, Klose, & Krautwurst, 2011).

• Therapeutic History:  
While extensively used in preclinical studies—including in retinal pigment epithelial cells and ocular models such as lamina cribrosa cells—SKF96365 has not yet been applied in clinical or veterinary settings for dry AMD. Its application has primarily been limited to research investigations aimed at deciphering Ca²⁺-dependent cellular mechanisms (Wimmers & Strauss, 2007; Irnaten et al., 2020).

• Mechanism of Action:  
The compound functions as a nonselective inhibitor of Ca²⁺ entry. It shows moderate potency against TRPC channels (IC₅₀ ~5–30 μM) but exhibits much greater potency against T-type Ca²⁺ channels (IC₅₀ ~560 nM). Its inhibition is non-competitive, state-independent, and voltage-insensitive, indicating a broad and allosteric pore-blocking mechanism (Singh et al., 2010).

• Expected Effect:  
In the context of dry AMD, SKF96365 is hypothesized to restore calcium homeostasis in RPE cells by modulating aberrant TRPC-mediated Ca²⁺ entry. This, in turn, is expected to enhance phagosome–lysosome fusion and improve the clearance of photoreceptor outer segments—a process critical for preventing the toxic accumulation of cellular debris that characterizes dry AMD (Wimmers & Strauss, 2007; Zhao et al., 2015).

• Overall Evaluation:  
Despite a strong mechanistic rationale and promising in vitro data indicating that modulation of Ca²⁺ signaling can improve RPE function, the broad off-target effects of SKF96365 represent a significant challenge. Its potent inhibition of T-type and high-voltage-activated calcium channels, along with other ion channels, undermines its potential as a therapeutic agent. The lack of in vivo data in AMD models and concerns over long-term ocular safety further limit its immediate clinical applicability. Therefore, while SKF96365 remains an invaluable research tool, it appears better suited as a lead compound for further optimization rather than as a final candidate for dry AMD therapy (Singh et al., 2010; Wimmers & Strauss, 2007).

─────────────────────────────  
FINAL RECOMMENDATION

Based on the detailed literature review, SKF96365 offers a proof-of-concept for the modulation of disrupted Ca²⁺ signaling—a pathway critically implicated in the pathogenesis of dry AMD. However, its inherent nonselectivity, especially the potent blockade of T-type Ca²⁺ channels at concentrations that overlap with those required for TRPC channel inhibition, poses significant concerns regarding both efficacy and safety in the RPE in vivo. The compound’s reported ocular safety in acute glaucoma models is encouraging but insufficient to guarantee tolerability during chronic administration required in dry AMD. Therefore, while SKF96365 validates the general approach of normalizing Ca²⁺ transients to enhance phagosome–lysosome fusion, further chemical optimization to improve selectivity for TRPC channels is essential before advancing toward clinical evaluation for dry AMD. Future studies should pivot toward either developing derivatives with a narrower spectrum of activity or employing combination strategies that mitigate its off-target effects, while concurrently establishing robust preclinical models to evaluate long-term outcomes in the retina (Singh et al., 2010; Irnaten et al., 2020).

In conclusion, SKF96365 represents a scientifically intriguing candidate as a modulator of Ca²⁺ dynamics with potential implications for the treatment of dry AMD. However, its nonselective mode of action underscores the need for further research and chemical refinement. Until these issues are addressed, SKF96365 should be regarded primarily as an experimental tool for validating the role of Ca²⁺ signaling in RPE dysfunction rather than as a direct therapeutic option for dry AMD.
