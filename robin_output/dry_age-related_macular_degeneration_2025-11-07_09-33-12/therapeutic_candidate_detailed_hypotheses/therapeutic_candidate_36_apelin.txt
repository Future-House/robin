Proposal for Apelin-13 (Endogenous peptide; Bachem Cat. No. H-3705)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
Apelin-13 is an endogenous peptide derived from the apelin gene product and is one of several bioactive fragments generated from a larger 77–amino acid preproapelin precursor. Initially discovered in bovine stomach extracts by Tatemoto and colleagues in 1998 (Davenport et al., 2019), apelin peptides belong to a class of naturally occurring vasoactive peptide hormones that activate the apelin receptor (APJ), a G protein–coupled receptor. The peptide is synthesized naturally in various tissues, including the central nervous system, heart, and retina, and undergoes posttranslational processing to yield distinct isoforms such as apelin-36, apelin-17, and the shorter apelin-13. The focus on apelin-13 is due in part to structure–activity relationship studies that have identified a core pharmacophoric region, including a critical C-terminal phenylalanine residue, that is essential for receptor activation (Davenport et al., 2019). Being a member of the bioactive peptide class, apelin-13 has been previously studied in cardiovascular, metabolic, and certain retinal contexts, with distinct advantages offered by its relatively small peptide size, which enables intravitreal delivery and minimizes immunogenicity. This candidate, available commercially (Bachem Cat. No. H-3705), represents a repurposing opportunity that leverages its endogenous origin, excellent receptor specificity, and established ability to modulate intracellular signaling cascades relevant for cellular homeostasis in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells (Davenport et al., 2019; Enders, 2023).

Therapeutic History:  
Historically, apelin peptides have been primarily explored for their cardiovascular and metabolic effects. In cardiovascular studies, apelin-13 has been shown to exert potent inotropic and vasodilatory responses, reduce cardiac fibrosis, and improve cardiac output in heart failure models, thus demonstrating its multifunctional role as an endogenous regulator in the cardiovascular system (Davenport et al., 2019). In metabolic research, apelin has been documented to influence insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism, while also functioning as an adipokine, linking it to obesity-related pathways (Davenport et al., 2019). Within the context of ophthalmology, several preclinical studies have investigated the role of apelin peptides, particularly apelin-13, in retinal disease models. Notably, apelin-13 has been demonstrated to promote proliferation, migration, and enhanced expression of cytoskeletal and tight junction proteins in human RPE cells, mediated through activation of the PI3K/Akt and MAPK/Erk signaling pathways (Li et al., 2017). Though most clinical and preclinical research in retinal settings has predominantly focused on diabetic retinopathy and related retinal vascular pathologies, where apelin expression is altered and exogenous supplementation can restore normal cellular function (Li et al., 2017; Li et al., 2018), there is emerging interest in extrapolating these findings to other conditions of RPE dysfunction. Dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD) is characterized by impaired RPE phagocytosis and accumulation of toxic photoreceptor outer segment (POS) debris, making it plausible that a drug candidate capable of enhancing RPE phagocytosis could benefit these patients. Although direct studies evaluating apelin-13 in dry AMD are limited, its demonstrated ability to improve RPE cell survival and actin dynamics under stress conditions in vitro presents a compelling rationale for repurposing (Li et al., 2017).

Mechanism of Action:  
The molecular mechanism underlying the therapeutic potential of apelin-13 centers on its binding to the APJ receptor, which is robustly expressed in RPE cells and other retinal cell types. Upon receptor engagement, apelin-13 activates downstream G protein–mediated signaling cascades, notably the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/Akt pathway and the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK)/Erk pathway (Li et al., 2017; Chiang et al., 2017). Activation of PI3K/Akt signaling is of particular interest because it plays a critical role in the regulation of actin cytoskeletal reorganization—a process essential for the formation of phagocytic cups required for the internalization of POS. This pathway additionally modulates intracellular trafficking events that culminate in improved clearance of outer segment debris by RPE cells (Chiang et al., 2017). Biochemical investigations reveal that pharmacological inhibition of PI3K or downstream Akt phosphorylation dramatically reduces RPE phagocytosis, underscoring the necessity of this signaling module in maintaining RPE homeostasis (Chiang et al., 2017). Moreover, apelin-13’s EC₅₀ in stimulating PI3K/Akt signaling in RPE cells is reported to be approximately 0.5 nM, suggesting high potency in activating this pathway (Li et al., 2017). In the context of oxidative stress, which is heavily implicated in dry AMD pathogenesis, activation of PI3K/Akt also confers cytoprotective effects by enhancing cell survival pathways and promoting anti-apoptotic signaling. Structural evidence and receptor–ligand interaction studies further validate that apelin-13 engages critical residues at the receptor interface, leading to effective downstream signal transduction that may drive cytoskeletal remodeling and facilitate phagosome maturation and trafficking (Davenport et al., 2019). Thus, apelin-13’s mode of action is characterized by its dual ability to stimulate RPE phagocytic function while concurrently enhancing cell survival under stress conditions.

Expected Effect:  
The proposed therapeutic hypothesis for apelin-13 in dry AMD hinges on its capacity to enhance RPE function by activating the APJ receptor and triggering PI3K/Akt signaling. In an assay designed to evaluate phagocytosis of POS, administration of apelin-13 is expected to increase the internalization and subsequent clearance of shed POS by RPE cells. This response is anticipated to result from enhanced actin reorganization and improved phagosome trafficking, driven by the activation of PI3K/Akt signaling—a pathway that is well known to be essential for cytoskeletal dynamics during phagocytosis (Chiang et al., 2017). In vitro, human RPE cell studies have shown that apelin-13 treatment leads to increased expression of cytoskeletal proteins and tight junction proteins, which are integral to maintaining the cellular architecture necessary for effective phagocytic activity (Li et al., 2017). Additionally, because dry AMD involves an accumulation of lipofuscin and impaired clearance of degradative byproducts from POS, enhancing this clearance mechanism could prevent or slow the progression of RPE dysfunction. Given the reported EC₅₀ near 0.5 nM for activation of PI3K/Akt signaling in RPE cells by apelin-13, the anticipated effect in vivo would be a dose-dependent improvement in phagocytosis that could translate to preservation of RPE integrity and retinal function. Furthermore, since apelin-13 has been described as having a favorable safety profile in other ocular studies—including those addressing glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy—there is strong support for the feasibility of intravitreal application, which is a standard mode of delivery in the treatment of many retinal diseases (Lu et al., 2013; Li et al., 2017). The expected outcome is not only an enhancement of POS clearance but also overall improvement in RPE cell survival under chronic oxidative stress conditions, a critical component in the pathogenesis of dry AMD.

Overall Evaluation:  
Apelin-13 represents a promising candidate for repurposing in the treatment of dry AMD based on its endogenous nature, well-characterized mechanism of action, and preclinical evidence supporting its beneficial effects on RPE cellular functions. One significant strength of this candidate is its high potency in activating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway in RPE cells, a pathway that is crucial for cytoskeletal remodeling and phagocytosis of POS (Chiang et al., 2017). The safety profile of apelin-13, as established by studies involving intravitreal injection in various retinal models, further supports its potential, especially given the routine use of intravitreal therapies in ophthalmology (Lu et al., 2013; Li et al., 2017). In addition, the dual action of apelin-13—promoting both phagocytic clearance and cell survival under oxidative stress—addresses two primary pathological features of dry AMD: accumulation of toxic debris and RPE cell degeneration.

However, there are challenges and weaknesses to consider. Most of the existing data on apelin-13 in ocular applications come from models of diabetic retinopathy rather than from direct studies in dry AMD. While the underlying mechanisms of impaired phagocytosis and oxidative stress are common to both conditions, the pathology of dry AMD is multifactorial and includes additional components such as complement dysregulation and chronic inflammation, which may not be directly addressed by apelin-13 therapy (Li et al., 2018; Li et al., 2017). Furthermore, the biphasic nature of Akt involvement in phagocytosis—as evidenced by studies showing variable effects at early versus later time points—underscores the complexity of modulating this pathway therapeutically (Chiang et al., 2017). There is also a potential for unwanted off-target effects if apelin-13 were to inadvertently stimulate angiogenic pathways under certain conditions; however, given that dry AMD is characterized by atrophic rather than neovascular pathology, careful dosing and patient selection could mitigate such risks (Wu et al., 2017). Finally, while intravitreal delivery is a proven route for ocular therapies, peptide-based drugs often face challenges related to stability, degradation, and the need for repeated dosing, which may necessitate formulation modifications such as PEGylation or cyclization to prolong half-life (Davenport et al., 2019).

In conclusion, the overall evaluation of apelin-13 as a candidate for dry AMD is cautiously optimistic. The strong mechanistic rationale—centered on the activation of the APJ receptor and subsequent PI3K/Akt signaling to improve cytoskeletal remodeling and POS clearance—coupled with existing evidence in retinal models, supports further investigation. Preclinical studies specifically tailored to dry AMD models are warranted, including evaluations of efficacy in improving RPE phagocytosis, protection against oxidative stress, and overall retinal function. Additionally, pharmacokinetic and formulation studies should be conducted to optimize intravitreal delivery and stability. If these studies confirm target engagement and therapeutic benefit, apelin-13 could offer a novel approach for restoring RPE function and slowing the progression of dry AMD, thereby addressing an unmet clinical need in a field where current therapeutic options remain limited (Li et al., 2017; Chiang et al., 2017).
