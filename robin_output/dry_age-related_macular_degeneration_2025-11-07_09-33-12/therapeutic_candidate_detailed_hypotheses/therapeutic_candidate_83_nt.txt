Proposal for NT-219 (Dual IGF1R/SHP2 inhibitor; Nanosyn research compound)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
NT-219 is a small-molecule inhibitor originally characterized as a novel attenuator of insulin/IGF signaling (IIS) that blocks the IGF1R→AKT axis via a dual mechanism: rapid reduction of IGF1 receptor autophosphorylation to prevent downstream AKT activation, and promotion of inhibitory Ser phosphorylation and degradation of IRS1 and IRS2 to sustain IIS suppression in cells (dual-step mechanism) (El-Ami et al., 2014). In the earliest in vitro characterizations, NT-219 inhibited IGF1-induced AKT phosphorylation, induced IRS1/2 Ser phosphorylation and degradation, and elevated expression of FOXO target genes such as p27 and MnSOD in human A375 and N2a cells, thereby functionally mimicking reduced IIS in mammalian cells (El-Ami et al., 2014). In vivo, NT-219 increased stress resistance and protected Caenorhabditis elegans from proteotoxic stress induced by human Aβ or polyQ models, without extending lifespan, consistent with partial IIS attenuation and downstream activation of DAF-16/FOXO and HSF-1 programs (El-Ami et al., 2014).

Beyond IIS, several oncology reports and abstracts describe NT-219 as suppressing IRS1/2 and phosphorylated STAT3, framing the compound as a dual inhibitor of IRS and STAT3 signaling with activity in murine melanoma and colorectal cancer (CRC) systems (e.g., B16 melanoma variants, CRC brain metastasis models) (Azevedo et al., 2023). In CRC work, NT-219 is presented as a first-in-class IRS2-targeting agent that triggers IRS2 Ser phosphorylation and degradation, reducing oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) gene expression and mitochondrial respiration in IRS2-expressing CRC cells, with antitumor synergy when combined with 5-FU in intracranial CRC brain metastasis mouse models (Greenberg et al., 2022a, 2022b).

With respect to provenance, author affiliations and disclosures indicate NT-219’s development by NovoTyr/TyrNovo; one source states that a company developing NT-219 positions it as a novel IRS1/2 inhibitor, noting it is “already being tested in clinical trials,” although details of those trials are not provided in the excerpt (El-Ami et al., 2014; Greenberg et al., 2022a). Available reports do not supply chemical synthesis routes, structural class information, formulation beyond use of 2-hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin for solubilization in some experiments, or pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic details (El-Ami et al., 2014).

Therapeutic History  
The preclinical history of NT-219 spans two principal areas: (1) aging/neurodegeneration model systems in C. elegans and cell culture; and (2) oncology models in mice and human cancer cell lines. In nematodes, NT-219 reduced late-onset proteotoxicity in Aβ and polyQ models, increased stress resistance (heat shock, UV), induced DAF-16 nuclear translocation, and required DAF-16 and HSF-1 for its protective effects; the compound did not extend lifespan under the reported paradigms, distinguishing stress/proteostasis benefits from longevity effects (El-Ami et al., 2014). The authors explicitly called for testing NT-219 in vertebrate Alzheimer’s or Huntington’s disease models, emphasizing that efficient administration routes would need to be developed; the excerpt provides no evidence that such mammalian neurodegeneration studies were completed (El-Ami et al., 2014).

In oncology, multiple independent preclinical lines report NT-219’s ability to destabilize IRS2 protein, suppress OXPHOS genes, reduce mitochondrial respiration in IRS2-positive CRC cells, and synergize with 5-FU to inhibit CRC brain metastasis growth and extend survival in mice (Greenberg et al., 2022a, 2022b). Additional immuno-oncology abstracts describe NT-219 as a dual IRS/STAT3 inhibitor that increases tumor PD-L1 and improves the efficacy of anti–PD-1 or anti–CTLA-4 by reprogramming the tumor microenvironment toward higher CD8+ T-cell activation and reduced myeloid suppressor populations in murine melanoma models (Azevedo et al., 2023). One CRC preprint notes TyrNovo develops NT-219 and states it is “already being tested in clinical trials,” but the materials provided do not contain peer-reviewed clinical data, detailed safety/tolerability, or ocular exposure information (El-Ami et al., 2014; Greenberg et al., 2022a).

Critically, there is no report in the provided literature of NT-219 being used to treat dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD), retinal degenerations, or any ocular disease; likewise, there is no published application to retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) biology, MerTK signaling, or photoreceptor outer segment (POS) phagocytosis (El-Ami et al., 2014; Greenberg et al., 2022a; Azevedo et al., 2023). Within the RPE field, the candidate is untested, and the extrapolation from cancer and nematode proteostasis contexts to RPE phagocytosis is speculative in the absence of ocular data.

Mechanism of Action  
Primary mechanistic characterization places NT-219 as an IIS/IGF1R pathway inhibitor acting through a dual-step process: early partial inhibition of IGF1R autophosphorylation that prevents AKT phosphorylation (Thr308, Ser473), followed by induction of inhibitory Ser phosphorylation of IRS1/2 and their proteasomal degradation, which produces a sustained IIS blockade (El-Ami et al., 2014). In human cells, this culminates in reduced AKT activity and a shift toward FOXO-dependent transcription (e.g., increased p27, MnSOD), indicative of stress-responsive and anti-proliferative programs typical of IIS attenuation (El-Ami et al., 2014). In C. elegans, IIS attenuation by NT-219 triggers DAF-16/FOXO nuclear translocation and HSF-1 activation to enhance proteostasis and stress resistance; protection from proteotoxicity requires these effectors, as daf-16 or hsf-1 RNAi abrogates benefits (El-Ami et al., 2014).

Mechanistic distinctions versus canonical IIS genetic knockdown are noted. For Aβ models, NT-219 promotes accumulation of higher molecular weight Abeta species (hyperaggregation) similar to IIS reduction by daf-2 RNAi, consistent with detoxification by sequestration of oligomers; for polyQ models, NT-219 reduces SDS-resistant aggregates but does not increase high-MW species to the same extent as daf-2 RNAi, implying compound-specific modulation of protein aggregation dynamics (El-Ami et al., 2014). Collectively, NT-219 acts upstream (IGF1R and IRS1/2) to suppress AKT and activate FOXO/HSF-1/NRF-like programs, with context-dependent modulation of aggregation states.

Oncology abstracts expand NT-219’s mechanism to include suppression of phosphorylated STAT3 and dual targeting of IRS1/2 and STAT3, which increases PD-L1, reduces suppressive myeloid cells, and synergizes with checkpoint inhibitors in melanoma models, and also anchors the agent’s proximal action to IRS2 degradation with downstream suppression of OXPHOS and β-catenin in CRC brain metastasis models (Azevedo et al., 2023; Greenberg et al., 2022a, 2022b). These oncology data reinforce IRS1/2 as validated proximal targets of NT-219 and suggest broader transcriptional/immunologic impacts via STAT3 in tumor contexts (Azevedo et al., 2023).

No evidence in the provided literature indicates that NT-219 directly inhibits SHP2 phosphatase activity; the compound is repeatedly characterized by its actions on IGF1R autophosphorylation and IRS1/2 degradation, with some reports adding STAT3 inhibition, but there is no description of direct SHP2 binding or enzymatic inhibition (El-Ami et al., 2014; Azevedo et al., 2023). Likewise, there is no evidence presented that NT-219 modulates MerTK phosphorylation or its dephosphorylation by any phosphatase.

Disease Biology Context and Relevance to Hypothesized Mechanism in RPE  
RPE clearance phagocytosis of POS is a receptor-integrated, actomyosin-driven process requiring both αvβ5 integrin and MerTK, with a choreography that separates POS recognition/binding from internalization and processing (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Müller & Finnemann, 2020). MFG-E8 bridges phosphatidylserine on POS to αvβ5 integrin, triggering FAK activation, which in turn promotes MerTK phosphorylation at light onset in vivo; RPE lacking αvβ5 fail to exhibit the coordinated FAK/MerTK phosphorylation cycle and display desynchronized phagocytosis (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Müller & Finnemann, 2020). MerTK activation then drives essential engulfment signaling toward the actin cytoskeleton, with protrusive actin assembly and myosin-II–based contractility required to form and close the phagocytic cup (Zihni, 2025).

Downstream of MerTK, multiple mechanistic routes engage the Rho family GTPases and PI3K signaling. Mer recruits PI3K and PLC-γ to generate PIP2/PIP3 and activate PKCs, while PIP3 species bind DOCK180 family GEFs (as complexes with ELMO) to activate Rac1 at the membrane; independently, Gas6–Mer signaling can phosphorylate Vav1 (a Dbl-family GEF), further promoting Rac1 activation (Wu et al., 2006). In parallel to this Mer-dependent axis, POS–MFG-E8–αvβ5 engagement activates Rac1 via a pathway that is independent of FAK and MerTK, indicating that Rac1 activation is integrin-driven and converges with MerTK-dependent cytoskeletal programs during cup formation (Lieffrig et al., 2023). Functional studies demonstrate that Rac1 activation is necessary, and that a coordinated increase of Rac1 activity with suppression of RhoA/ROCK signaling is independently required to assemble the F-actin phagocytic cup; perturbing either node (e.g., sema4D/plexin-B1 reducing Rac1, RhoA activation) impairs internalization (Lieffrig et al., 2023). Notably, in MERTK-deficient RPE, Rac1 activation upon POS challenge persists but F-actin phagocytic cups fail to form due to failure to downregulate the RhoA–ROCK pathway; pharmacological ROCK inhibition is sufficient to restore engulfment, acidification, and digestion in MERTK-lacking RPE, highlighting the primacy of the Rac1/RhoA balance for internalization and processing (Lieffrig et al., 2023).

PI3K–AKT signaling participates in RPE cytoskeletal regulation, but with nuance: AKT1/2 are expressed and activated in RPE by POS challenge; AKT inhibition increases POS binding while leaving internalization largely unaffected, and AKT controls F-actin recruitment by regulating myosin-II localization to phagocytic cups—myosin-II activity is required for internalization (Lieffrig et al., 2023). This suggests that complete blockade of AKT can alter cortical cytoskeletal dynamics in ways that may not improve (and might complicate) internalization, even if binding is increased (Lieffrig et al., 2023). More broadly in the retina and RPE, IGF-1/insulin receptors activate PI3K/AKT and are linked to cytoprotection; IGF-1R is tyrosine-phosphorylated by light stress in ROS membranes with associated PI3K binding, and PI3K–AKT activation protects RPE from oxidative injury, a key pathogenic driver in AMD (Rajala, 2010). Thus, systemically blocking IGF1R→AKT may deprive RPE of a cytoprotective pathway critical under oxidative stress conditions relevant to dry AMD (Rajala, 2010).

The hypothesis under consideration asserts that IGF1R activation recruits SHP2 to dephosphorylate MerTK, and that dual IGF1R/SHP2 inhibition by NT-219 would sustain MerTK phosphorylation and enhance PI3K/Rac1 signaling to improve POS internalization. The provided literature does not support this cross-talk model. While MerTK possesses domains that can recruit SH2-containing adapters and there is a plausible framework for SH2-mediated anti-inflammatory signaling, the specific involvement of SHP2 in dephosphorylation/regulation of MerTK in RPE is not demonstrated in the provided sources; indeed, multiple reviews explicitly note the absence of direct evidence for MerTK dephosphorylation or SHP2 activity in RPE/retina controlling phagocytosis or AMD-relevant outcomes (Ghosh et al., 2024; Albert et al., 2015; Ghosh et al., 2024; Lieffrig et al., 2023; Ghosh et al., 2024). Furthermore, none of the NT-219 sources implicate SHP2 as a target; rather, they define the compound’s proximal activity against IGF1R autophosphorylation and IRS1/2 degradation (and STAT3 in oncology abstracts), with no SHP2 pharmacology reported (El-Ami et al., 2014; Azevedo et al., 2023). Absent evidence that IGF1R activation suppresses MerTK via SHP2 in RPE, or that NT-219 inhibits SHP2, the central premise—to sustain MERTK phosphorylation by dual IGF1R/SHP2 inhibition—remains unsubstantiated in the current literature set.

Expected Effect  
Proposed assay: ARPE-19 (or primary human RPE) POS clearance phagocytosis with readouts for binding, internalization, Rac1-GTP, RhoA activity, F-actin cup formation, MERTK phosphorylation status, and myosin-II localization.

Mechanistic expectation from the literature:
- NT-219 will inhibit IGF1R autophosphorylation and reduce AKT phosphorylation; it will also induce IRS1/2 Ser phosphorylation and degradation, producing a sustained IIS blockade in RPE if the compound engages its canonical targets in this cell type (El-Ami et al., 2014).
- In RPE phagocytosis, AKT inhibition increases POS binding while leaving internalization largely unaffected; AKT controls myosin-II localization to cups and myosin-II activity is required for internalization; therefore, IIS blockade by NT-219 could alter binding and myosin-II dynamics without enhancing internalization, and might even risk impairing the coordinated contractility required for cup closure depending on the extent and timing of AKT suppression (Lieffrig et al., 2023).
- There is no evidence that NT-219 directly increases MERTK phosphorylation or sustains it by inhibiting a MerTK-directed phosphatase; FAK is the upstream effector known to promote MERTK activation after αvβ5 engagement, and Mer-driven PI3K/PIP3 production recruits DOCK180/ELMO to activate Rac1, while Vav1 provides a parallel route to Rac activation (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2006).
- Rac1 activation required for cup formation is driven by αvβ5 ligation via a pathway independent of FAK and MerTK and is modulated by semaphorin/plexin signaling; there is no indication that IGF1R/IIS inhibition increases Rac1 activation or overrides antagonistic RhoA/ROCK signaling (Lieffrig et al., 2023). Indeed, cup formation requires both increased Rac1 and decreased RhoA activity; manipulating only IIS/AKT is unlikely to rebalance this axis favorably (Lieffrig et al., 2023).
- In MERTK-deficient RPE, failure to suppress RhoA/ROCK—not failure to activate Rac1—explains defective cup formation; pharmacological ROCK inhibition rescues engulfment and downstream maturation, underscoring that targeting ROCK can bypass some MerTK-dependent defects (Lieffrig et al., 2023).

Taken together, the most defensible expectation is that NT-219 will reduce IIS/AKT signaling in ARPE-19, potentially increase POS binding, and alter myosin-II localization to cups, without a clear mechanism to enhance internalization; effects on Rac1-GTP are unlikely to be directly augmented, and there is no sound mechanistic basis in the provided literature to expect sustained MerTK phosphorylation through SHP2 inhibition by NT-219 (El-Ami et al., 2014; Lieffrig et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2006). Given evidence that PI3K–AKT activation protects RPE from oxidative damage—a central feature of dry AMD pathophysiology—sustained IIS blockade could be deleterious outside of a narrow, carefully controlled window (Rajala, 2010).

Contextualizing with dry AMD biology and MerTK function:
- MerTK is essential for RPE internalization of POS; genetic loss causes failure to engulf POS and retinal degeneration in RCS rats and some mouse models, and MERTK/TYRO3 signaling redundantly maintains an anti-inflammatory RPE gene expression program that influences retinal viability (Müller & Finnemann, 2020; Albert et al., 2015; Ghosh et al., 2024).
- After ingestion, internalized POS phagosomes enter LC3-associated phagocytosis (LAP) and autophagy pathways required for efficient degradation; MerTK promotes autophagy and inhibits NLRP3 inflammasomes, constraining retinal inflammation (Ghosh et al., 2024). These functions are distinct from IIS modulation and point to MerTK-proximal strategies (e.g., enhancing MerTK activation, targeting RhoA/ROCK to restore cup formation) as more rational levers to improve phagocytosis than IGF1R inhibition (Lieffrig et al., 2023).
- In RPE lacking MerTK ligation, phagocytic cup formation is impaired even when Rac1 activation is intact, and ROCK inhibition restores engulfment and maturation—an actionable, pathway-proximal intervention that has empirical support in RPE systems (Lieffrig et al., 2023).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths  
- Pharmacologic definition and tractability: NT-219’s mechanism is well supported in mammalian cells by decreased IGF1R autophosphorylation, suppressed AKT phosphorylation, and sustained IIS blockade via IRS1/2 Ser phosphorylation and degradation, with downstream activation of FOXO-regulated gene expression (El-Ami et al., 2014).
- In vivo activity in worms and robust oncology preclinical signals indicate that NT-219 is bioactive and can produce meaningful phenotypes via IIS attenuation and IRS1/2 suppression; preclinical efficacy includes synergy with 5-FU in CRC brain metastasis and potentiation of checkpoint blockade in melanoma (El-Ami et al., 2014; Greenberg et al., 2022a; Azevedo et al., 2023).
- The compound’s formulation has precedent (2-HP-β-cyclodextrin) and dose ranges have been explored in cell and worm systems, offering starting points for exploratory dosing in vitro (El-Ami et al., 2014; Greenberg et al., 2022b).

Weaknesses and key risks for dry AMD  
- Mechanistic disconnect with the hypothesis: There is no evidence in the provided literature that IGF1R activation recruits SHP2 to dephosphorylate MerTK in RPE, nor that NT-219 inhibits SHP2; MerTK’s downstream anti-inflammatory signaling may involve SH2-adapter recruitment, but SHP2-mediated dephosphorylation of MerTK in RPE has not been demonstrated (Ghosh et al., 2024; Albert et al., 2015; Wu et al., 2006; El-Ami et al., 2014; Ghosh et al., 2024). The central premise—that dual IGF1R/SHP2 inhibition by NT-219 will sustain MerTK phosphorylation—is unsupported by current evidence (El-Ami et al., 2014; Ghosh et al., 2024).
- Risk of antagonizing protective signaling: IGF1R/IR→PI3K/AKT signaling protects RPE from oxidative stress, a driver of dry AMD; broad IIS/AKT inhibition could be cytotoxic or reduce resilience to oxidative injury in RPE (Rajala, 2010).
- RPE phagocytosis data argue against the expected pro-internalization effect: In RPE, AKT inhibition increases binding but leaves internalization largely unaffected, and AKT controls myosin-II localization required for internalization; sustained IIS/AKT blockade is not a rationale to boost internalization and may perturb the force-generation needed for cup closure (Lieffrig et al., 2023).
- Lack of ocular/RPE data: There are no reports of NT-219 in ocular tissues, RPE cells, or phagocytosis assays; the compound’s tolerability, pharmacology, and target engagement in RPE are unknown (El-Ami et al., 2014).
- More mechanistically proximal and validated levers exist: Cup formation requires increased Rac1 and decreased RhoA/ROCK; in MERTK deficiency, ROCK inhibition rescues engulfment and downstream events, whereas Rac1 activation cannot overcome high RhoA activity; targeting ROCK or MerTK activation itself is more directly supported to enhance POS internalization (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2006).
- The candidate’s oncology-specific mechanistic breadth (IRS/STAT3 dual inhibition, β-catenin/OXPHOS modulation) introduces off-target immunologic and metabolic effects that could be undesirable in retina and RPE, where mitochondrial function and immune quiescence are tightly regulated (Greenberg et al., 2022a, 2022b; Azevedo et al., 2023).

Assessment of the specific mechanistic claims in the proposal:  
- “IGF1R activation recruits SHP2 to dephosphorylate MerTK”: The provided literature does not support this assertion. While MerTK contains domains that can engage SH2-containing proteins and may suppress NF-κB through SHP-related mechanisms in other phagocytes, specific demonstration of SHP2-mediated MerTK dephosphorylation or suppression in RPE is absent (Wu et al., 2006; Ghosh et al., 2024). No source shows that IGF1R recruits SHP2 to target MerTK in RPE.  
- “NT-219 is a dual IGF1R/SHP2 inhibitor”: None of the documents identify NT-219 as a SHP2 inhibitor. The compound acts on IGF1R autophosphorylation and IRS1/2 degradation, and some oncology abstracts add STAT3 inhibition; SHP2 is not listed among NT-219’s targets (El-Ami et al., 2014; Azevedo et al., 2023).  
- “Sustained MerTK phosphorylation and Rac1-GTP by NT-219”: MerTK activation is promoted by the αvβ5→FAK axis and by its ligation through GAS6/PROS1; downstream, Mer activates PI3K, DOCK180/ELMO, and Vav1 to elevate Rac1. IIS suppression by NT-219 does not obviously reinforce this cascade and, importantly, Rac1 activation required for cup formation is driven by αvβ5 signaling independent of FAK/MerTK, and inadequate suppression of RhoA/ROCK—not Rac1 deficiency—limits cup formation in MERTK-deficient settings (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2006).

Recommendation  
Given the absence of supporting evidence for the proposal’s key mechanistic link (IGF1R/SHP2→MerTK dephosphorylation) and potential risks of IIS/AKT suppression in RPE cells under oxidative stress, NT-219 should not be prioritized as a repurposed therapeutic for dry AMD to enhance RPE phagocytosis. If the team wishes to explore this axis despite these concerns, initial work should be stringently gated to low-risk, mechanistically focused in vitro experiments in human RPE with explicit go/no-go criteria:

- Biochemical target engagement in RPE: confirm IGF1R autophosphorylation and AKT suppression, and assess IRS1/2 degradation at NT-219 exposures spanning reported active ranges in human cells, with parallel viability and oxidative stress-challenge assays (El-Ami et al., 2014; Rajala, 2010).  
- MerTK signaling: test whether NT-219 alters MerTK tyrosine phosphorylation after POS challenge or GAS6/PROS1 addition, with FAK-dependent activation as positive control; due to the lack of any SHP2 data for NT-219, include SHP2 activity readouts only as exploratory corollaries, not as assumed mechanisms (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Müller & Finnemann, 2020; Ghosh et al., 2024).  
- Phagocytosis function: quantify POS binding versus internalization; F-actin cup counts; myosin-II localization; Rac1-GTP and RhoA-GTP; include ROCK inhibition as a positive internalization control and sema4D as a Rac1-downregulating negative control (Lieffrig et al., 2023).  
- Autophagy/LAP and inflammatory readouts: measure LC3-II, p62, and NLRP3-related markers following POS feeding with/without NT-219 to detect adverse effects on LAP or pro-inflammatory signaling, as MERTK’s anti-inflammatory function via autophagy is integral to retinal homeostasis (Ghosh et al., 2024).

Absent clear, positive results across these mechanistic checkpoints, further development should be halted. Parallel investment is better directed toward pathway-proximal strategies with published efficacy signals in RPE:

- Rho kinase inhibition to restore cup formation and downstream processing in MerTK-compromised contexts (Lieffrig et al., 2023).  
- Direct MerTK pathway activation (optimized GAS6/PROS1 engagement, integrin→FAK reinforcement), guided by the well-defined αvβ5→FAK→MerTK signaling axis (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Müller & Finnemann, 2020).  
- Modulators that enhance LAP/autophagy to promote POS digestion and inhibit NLRP3, aligning with MERTK’s anti-inflammatory role (Ghosh et al., 2024).

Conclusion  
On balance, NT-219’s pharmacology is incompatible with the proposal’s mechanistic rationale and presents non-trivial risks in the RPE context. The evidence base does not support SHP2 involvement in MerTK dephosphorylation in RPE or NT-219 activity at SHP2, and IIS/AKT inhibition is unlikely to enhance POS internalization while potentially undermining RPE resilience to oxidative stress relevant to dry AMD (Ghosh et al., 2024; Albert et al., 2015; Ghosh et al., 2024; El-Ami et al., 2014; Rajala, 2010). The candidate lacks ocular data, and superior, pathway-proximal approaches exist with direct evidence for improving phagocytosis or downstream processing. Deprioritize NT-219 for dry AMD and redirect efforts to ROCK pathway modulation, MerTK pathway agonism, and LAP/autophagy enhancement strategies that align with established RPE phagocytosis biology (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Müller & Finnemann, 2020; Ghosh et al., 2024).
