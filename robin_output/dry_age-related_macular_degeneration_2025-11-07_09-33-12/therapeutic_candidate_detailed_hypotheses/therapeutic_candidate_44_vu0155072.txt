Proposal for VU0155072 (S1PR5 agonist; University of North Carolina reagent)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
VU0155072 is a synthetic small‐molecule agonist that selectively targets the sphingosine‐1‐phosphate receptor 5 (S1PR5). Although the detailed synthetic route and complete structural characterization for VU0155072 as a University of North Carolina reagent are not fully delineated in the available literature, it belongs to a class of compounds that have been developed to achieve high selectivity for S1PR5. Similar to the structurally characterized S1PR5 agonist A-971432—synthesized via a two‐step process employing reductive amination with azetidine-3-carboxylic acid derivatives (Hobson et al., 2015)—VU0155072 is assumed to incorporate a pharmacophore that confers both high potency and selectivity within the sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor family. Compounds in this class have generally exploited the azetidine or related amino acid framework as a bioisostere to mimic the native lipid ligand, sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P), while avoiding the metabolic liabilities of phosphorylated amino alcohols (Hobson et al., 2015). S1P receptor modulators as a group have been widely evaluated in a range of therapeutic areas—from multiple sclerosis and other neurodegenerative disorders to ocular pathologies—due to their ability to modulate immune cell trafficking and endothelial barrier function (Birgbauer, 2024; Hobson et al., 2015). In this context, VU0155072 marks a potential reagent that could be repurposed for dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) by leveraging its selective activation of S1PR5 in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells.

Therapeutic History:  
The broader class of S1P receptor modulators has an extensive therapeutic history, with agents like fingolimod having been approved for multiple sclerosis owing to their immunomodulatory effects; however, these agents generally lack receptor subtype selectivity and consequently trigger side effects related to non-selective S1P receptor engagement (Birgbauer, 2024; Nakamura et al., 2024). More recent efforts have focused on enhancing receptor selectivity, as exemplified by A-971432—which is a selective S1PR5 agonist developed for neurodegenerative conditions—and ASP4058, an S1P1/5-selective agonist that has demonstrated efficacy in ocular vascular pathology models relevant to wet age-related macular degeneration (Hobson et al., 2015; Nakamura et al., 2024). Despite these advances, none of the compounds described to date have been definitively applied to dry AMD. In contrast to wet AMD therapies, which predominantly target abnormal neovascularization through modulation of VEGF pathways (Nakamura et al., 2024), the potential therapeutic application of S1PR5 agonists in dry AMD lies in their capacity to restore or enhance the phagocytic function of RPE cells. Preliminary evidence suggests that RPE phagocytosis—a critical process for the daily removal of photoreceptor outer segments (POS)—may be modulated via S1P receptor signaling. Notably, a human RPE-based screening platform identified compounds capable of enhancing POS phagocytosis, with subsequent transcriptomic analyses revealing that S1PR5 was among the top upregulated genes associated with the enhanced phagocytic response (Schreiter et al., 2020). Moreover, while most of the literature on S1P receptor agonists has focused on neurodegenerative or ocular vascular indications, there is a growing rationale for repurposing these agents for dry AMD, given that RPE dysfunction—including impaired phagosome-lysosome fusion and subsequent accumulation of cellular debris—is a hallmark of the disease (Hobson et al., 2015; Schreiter et al., 2020). Thus, VU0155072, though primarily a research reagent at this stage, stands as a candidate with potential utility for dry AMD based on its receptor selectivity and the emerging role of S1PR5 in RPE phagocytic trafficking.

Mechanism of Action:  
S1PR5 is a member of the G-protein coupled receptor (GPCR) family activated by the endogenous ligand sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P). Its activation typically engages downstream signaling cascades that include the modulation of Rho family GTPases, regulation of the actin cytoskeleton, and activation of kinases such as AKT, all of which are crucial for vesicle transport and membrane fusion events (Hobson et al., 2015). In the context of RPE cells, the mechanistic hypothesis for VU0155072 centers on its ability to enhance phagosome-lysosome fusion and the degradation of POS. This is predicated on the observation that modulation of S1P receptor signaling impacts intracellular trafficking pathways. For instance, activation of S1PR5 has been linked to changes in cytoskeletal dynamics that favor efficient vesicular movement and fusion events—a process essential for the clearance of phagosomes containing photoreceptor debris (Schreiter et al., 2020). Additionally, the receptor’s downstream signaling may stimulate pathways involving Rho-associated kinases (ROCK) or PI3K/AKT, both of which facilitate the reorganization of actin filaments and promote the maturation and fusion of phagosomes with lysosomes (Hobson et al., 2015). Importantly, data from a study using a human RPE-based screening platform indicated that pharmacological inhibition of S1PR5 impaired the phagocytic uptake and processing of POS, implying that its activation could restore this cellular function (Schreiter et al., 2020). However, it is noteworthy that several studies examining receptor distribution in RPE cells (using the ARPE-19 cell line as a model) have reported that S1PR5 mRNA expression is very low compared with other S1P receptors such as S1PR3 (Qiao et al., 2012; Sorenson et al., 2022). This observation raises questions about the sufficiency of endogenous S1PR5 expression in mediating phagocytic processes and whether exogenous activation by a potent agonist like VU0155072 can override such limitations. Nonetheless, the proposed mechanism is coherent with the hypothesis that selective S1PR5 activation leads to enhanced phagosome-lysosome fusion—an effect that would be indirectly mediated by the receptor’s influence on cytoskeletal remodeling and vesicular trafficking proteins.

Expected Effect:  
The primary therapeutic expectation for VU0155072 in dry AMD is that selective activation of S1PR5 in RPE cells will augment the rate and efficiency of phagocytosis, specifically by promoting the fusion of phagosomes with lysosomes and thereby ensuring effective degradation of POS. In dry AMD, one of the pathogenic factors is the defective clearance and subsequent accumulation of photoreceptor debris, which contributes to RPE dysfunction and the formation of drusen. By facilitating robust phagocytic processing, VU0155072 is anticipated to alleviate the cellular stress associated with toxic accumulation, restore RPE homeostasis, and ultimately preserve retinal function (Schreiter et al., 2020). In vitro assays using human RPE cells (e.g., ARPE-19 or iPSC-derived RPE) could be employed to assess the efficacy of VU0155072. These assays might measure parameters such as the rate of POS uptake, the kinetics of phagosome-lysosome fusion (potentially using co-localization markers of lysosomal proteins such as LAMP1), and levels of residual POS components after treatment. Given the reported effective concentration (EC₅₀) in the vicinity of 150 nM, one would expect that treatment with VU0155072 at or near this concentration would lead to measurable improvements in phagocytic flux. Moreover, its selectivity may allow the compound to act in a highly targeted fashion, reducing the potential for adverse effects associated with off-target activation of other S1P receptor subtypes—a significant benefit given the pleiotropic roles of other S1P receptors in immune modulation and vascular homeostasis (Hobson et al., 2015; Nakamura et al., 2024). An additional facet of the expected effect is the possibility that S1PR5 activation may engage MERTK-independent signaling pathways. MERTK, a receptor tyrosine kinase critical for POS phagocytosis, is often implicated in inherited forms of retinal degeneration when mutated. Should VU0155072 provide an alternate route to stimulate phagosome maturation and clearance, it could be particularly beneficial in cases where MERTK function is compromised (Schreiter et al., 2020). Nonetheless, the low endogenous expression of S1PR5 in commonly used RPE cell lines (Qiao et al., 2012; Sorenson et al., 2022) may necessitate the use of primary or stem cell-derived RPE models in order to fully capture the therapeutic potential of this approach.

Overall Evaluation:  
In summary, VU0155072 represents a promising candidate for repurposing in the treatment of dry AMD due to its high selectivity for S1PR5 and the compelling hypothesis that S1PR5 activation can restore phagocytic flux in RPE cells. One of the key strengths of this approach is the mechanistic precision: by specifically targeting S1PR5, VU0155072 is expected to modulate intracellular trafficking pathways that are critical for the maintenance and renewal of the RPE’s phagocytic machinery. This stands in contrast to previous S1P receptor modulators that, due to their nonselective binding profiles (such as fingolimod), have demonstrated a range of off-target effects including unwanted immune modulation and vascular dysfunction (Birgbauer, 2024; Nakamura et al., 2024). Moreover, studies on similar S1PR5 agonists like A-971432 have established that selective receptor activation can be achieved with favorable pharmacokinetic profiles, and the precedent set by ASP4058 in addressing ocular pathologies suggests that S1P receptor modulation can be therapeutically beneficial in the retinal context (Hobson et al., 2015; Nakamura et al., 2024).  

However, the candidate also faces several challenges. A significant potential weakness is the reported low endogenous expression of S1PR5 in RPE cell lines, such as ARPE-19, where expression levels are markedly lower than those of other S1P receptors (Qiao et al., 2012; Sorenson et al., 2022). This observation raises the question of whether exogenous activation by VU0155072 will be sufficient to elicit a robust phagocytic response in the RPE, or whether receptor upregulation might already be a prerequisite for therapeutic efficacy. Furthermore, while there is evidence that S1PR5 signaling can influence phagocytic processes (Schreiter et al., 2020), most of the existing research has focused on its roles in CNS function and vascular stability rather than in the specific context of RPE-mediated POS degradation. Hence, the proposed mechanism—restoration of phagosome-lysosome fusion and enhancement of POS degradation via S1PR5 activation—remains largely inferential and will require rigorous experimental validation in appropriate in vitro and in vivo models of dry AMD.  

Another consideration is the pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic profile of VU0155072, which are described as being favorable in rodents. In theory, such data support its potential translation into clinical settings; however, the degree to which rodent PK accurately predicts human ocular tissue exposure remains uncertain, particularly given the unique microenvironment and barrier properties of the human retina. Additionally, it will be imperative to assess whether VU0155072 exerts any off-target effects on other S1P receptor subtypes, despite its purported selectivity. Off-target binding could complicate the interpretation of preclinical results or introduce safety concerns that have been observed with less selective S1P receptor modulators (Birgbauer, 2024; Nakamura et al., 2024).

From a disease-modifying perspective, the hypothesis that enhancement of phagocytic flux in RPE cells can ameliorate the progression of dry AMD is attractive because it targets a central pathogenic mechanism—namely, the chronic failure of POS clearance that leads to lipofuscin accumulation, drusen formation, and subsequent RPE atrophy. If VU0155072 can indeed stimulate the phagocytic machinery through S1PR5 activation in a meaningful way, it could potentially slow or even reverse some of the degenerative changes associated with dry AMD. This mechanism-driven approach represents a departure from merely symptomatic treatments, addressing the underlying cellular dysfunction that characterizes the disease (Hobson et al., 2015; Schreiter et al., 2020).

In conclusion, while VU0155072 is supported by a mechanistic rationale rooted in S1PR5-mediated regulation of intracellular trafficking and phagocytosis in RPE cells, several factors must be carefully considered before committing to this candidate as a viable therapeutic for dry AMD. The strengths of VU0155072 lie in its receptor specificity, favorable PK in rodent models, and the emerging evidence linking S1PR5 activation with improved phagocytic function in RPE—a critical process compromised in dry AMD. In contrast, the limited endogenous expression of S1PR5 in commonly used RPE models (Qiao et al., 2012; Sorenson et al., 2022) and the scarce direct evidence of S1PR5 agonism ameliorating dry AMD pathology represent significant challenges. These weaknesses highlight the necessity for further basic research to quantify S1PR5 expression in relevant human RPE cells, to confirm the downstream signaling events triggered by VU0155072, and to validate the beneficial effects of enhanced phagosome-lysosome fusion using both in vitro and in vivo models of dry AMD. Ultimately, while VU0155072 shows promise as a mechanistically precise candidate for restoring phagocytic flux and addressing a key pathological feature of dry AMD, the current literature underscores the need for additional studies to fully establish its therapeutic potential in this context (Hobson et al., 2015; Nakamura et al., 2024; Qiao et al., 2012; Schreiter et al., 2020; Sorenson et al., 2022).
