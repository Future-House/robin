Proposal for Axitinib (VEGFR/PDGFR inhibitor; Pfizer)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
Axitinib is a synthetic, indazole-based, small-molecule receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor originally discovered via medicinal chemistry optimization for anti-angiogenic oncology indications and subsequently approved as an oral therapy (Inlyta) for advanced renal cell carcinoma, reflecting high potency and selectivity for vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFR1–3) with additional activity against PDGFR-β and c-KIT (Fei et al., 2024; Tosi et al., 2017). In biochemical and cellular assays relevant to ocular angiogenesis, axitinib demonstrates pan-VEGFR inhibition with substantially greater sensitivity than anti-VEGF monoclonal antibodies in endothelial cells, and it also inhibits PDGFR-β–mediated signaling in pericytes at low nanomolar concentrations, consistent with dual-pathway blockade within the VEGF/PDGF axis (Giddabasappa et al., 2016). Pharmacologically, oral axitinib exhibits a short plasma half-life (~2.5–6.1 h) requiring twice-daily systemic dosing in oncology, and class-typical adverse events are dominated by hypertension; these PK/AE features motivated ocular delivery strategies to achieve high intraocular exposure with minimal systemic spillover (Siedlecki et al., 2017). As a class, multi-targeted VEGFR/PDGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have long been deployed systemically for cancer and are being repurposed for ocular neovascular diseases with the goals of broader VEGF-pathway suppression and reduced injection burden via sustained intraocular delivery platforms (suprachoroidal suspensions, intravitreal depot implants, and bioresorbable hydrogel systems) (Fabre et al., 2022; Fei et al., 2024). Axitinib is notable among TKIs for very high VEGFR affinity and measurable inhibition of other angiogenesis-related kinases, including TIE2, which has implications for both efficacy breadth and potential effects on vascular stability in the retina and choroid (Leitch et al., 2024; Bakri et al., 2024).

Therapeutic History  
Preclinical ocular data consistently show axitinib suppresses choroidal and retinal neovascularization across models, with oral dosing at 5 mg/kg reducing laser-induced choroidal neovascularization (L-CNV) in mice, and continuous low-dose systemic infusion (0.875 mg/day for 7 days) decreasing fluorescein leakage and CNV lesion area in rats; in vitro, axitinib suppresses 3D sprouting and inhibits both endothelial and pericyte proliferation/migration at non-toxic concentrations, aligning with dual VEGFR/PDGFR blockade (Fei et al., 2024; Giddabasappa et al., 2016). In a rat L-CNV model, axitinib reduced FA leakage scores and CNV lesion area by approximately one-third versus vehicle, and in co-culture sprouting assays axitinib alone outperformed anti-VEGF and anti-PDGF combinations, reinforcing the mechanistic rationale for dual-pathway inhibition in pathological ocular angiogenesis (Giddabasappa et al., 2016). Combinatorial approaches show synergy: co-administration with anti-VEGF antibodies enhanced anti-angiogenic effects in nonclinical studies, consistent with the broader concept that inhibiting pericyte/PDGFR-β support can potentiate VEGF-directed therapy (Giddabasappa et al., 2016; Siedlecki et al., 2017).

Multiple ocular delivery strategies for axitinib have entered human investigation in neovascular AMD (nAMD) to address the treatment burden and durability limitations of intravitreal biologics, including a suprachoroidal injectable suspension (CLS-AX) and an intravitreal bioresorbable hydrogel implant (OTX-TKI) designed for 6–9 months of sustained release (Fei et al., 2024). Early clinical and translational signals indicate that suprachoroidal delivery achieves high posterior segment exposure with minimal systemic levels, and initial phase 1/2a experience with CLS-AX in nAMD reported acceptable tolerability and pharmacologic activity at low doses, while OTX-TKI demonstrated favorable safety and durability in early cohorts (Wolf et al., 2022; Kaiser et al., 2020). Across the TKI class, parallel depot strategies (e.g., vorolanib in Durasert, sunitinib depots) have shown biologic activity and reduced treatment burden in nAMD, although some programs reported visual acuity tradeoffs or required rescue therapy at non-negligible rates, highlighting the need for careful dose, exposure, and target-selectivity optimization (Fei et al., 2024; Leitch et al., 2024).

Topical/eye-drop TKI programs (pazopanib, regorafenib, PAN-90806, acrizanib) underscore translational challenges: although preclinical topical dosing can reduce leakage and CNV thickness, multiple phase II programs failed to meet efficacy endpoints or were terminated due to insufficient posterior segment exposure and ocular safety issues such as reversible corneal haze with acrizanib, indicating that noninvasive delivery often cannot reproducibly attain therapeutic concentrations at the choroid/RPE in humans (Fei et al., 2024). Importantly, the available literature contains no reports of axitinib being tested clinically for dry AMD (geographic atrophy) or for enhancing RPE phagocytosis; the development history for axitinib and related VEGFR/PDGFR TKIs is focused on nAMD and other retinal vascular diseases, and reviews repeatedly note a lack of evidence for dry-AMD applications in this drug class to date (Fei et al., 2024). Furthermore, while localized ocular delivery is being pursued to mitigate systemic exposure, case reports of retinal adverse events during systemic axitinib therapy—including impaired retinal circulation, reversible retinal toxicity, and central retinal vein occlusion—require vigilance and thoughtful PK/PD design in ocular programs (Bakri et al., 2024). More broadly, despite active investigation of small molecules for AMD, reviews emphasize that no clinical success stories for small-molecule drugs in AMD (outside recently approved complement inhibitors for GA) have been established, underscoring translational risk for new mechanisms and reformulations (Fei et al., 2024).

Mechanism of Action  
Axitinib is a potent ATP-competitive inhibitor of VEGFR1 (FLT1), VEGFR2 (KDR), and VEGFR3 (FLT4), with additional inhibitory activity against PDGFR-β and c-KIT, thereby interrupting intracellular tyrosine kinase signaling downstream of multiple pro-angiogenic ligands, including VEGF-A/B/C/D and PDGF-B (Tosi et al., 2017; Giddabasappa et al., 2016). In relevant ocular cell types, axitinib inhibits VEGF-driven endothelial proliferation with markedly greater potency than anti-VEGF antibodies and exerts nanomolar inhibition of PDGF-driven pericyte proliferation, supporting the mechanistic premise that simultaneous disruption of endothelial and pericyte signaling can destabilize nascent pathological vessels and reduce neovascular growth (Giddabasappa et al., 2016). In 3D co-culture sprouting models that recapitulate endothelial–pericyte interactions, axitinib alone achieves robust inhibition of sprout number and length across low-nanomolar concentrations, matching or exceeding the effect of anti-VEGF plus anti-PDGF combinations, a pattern consistent with multi-receptor inhibition at the level of VEGFR/PDGFR RTKs (Giddabasappa et al., 2016). Preclinical studies in retinal endothelial cells further demonstrate axitinib suppression of downstream MAPK/ERK signaling and oxidative stress markers, aligning with direct VEGFR pathway inhibition; limited in vitro reports also suggest reduction of VEGFR1/VEGFR2 and PDGFR-β phosphorylation in RPE or mixed cell models, though these do not extend to functional phagocytosis endpoints (Lazzara et al., 2024).

Mechanistically, axitinib’s kinase profile extends beyond VEGFR/PDGFR to other angiogenesis-modulating targets; notably, among three pan-VEGFR TKIs profiled preclinically (vorolanib, sunitinib, axitinib), only axitinib exhibited strong TIE2 inhibition, a pathway essential for vascular quiescence and stability that could influence choroidal and retinal vascular homeostasis under chronic intraocular exposure (Bakri et al., 2024). In comparative kinase panels and endothelial functional assays, all three TKIs displayed robust pan-VEGFR inhibition and abolished endothelial sprouting at high concentrations, but the TIE2-inhibitory signal was disproportionately strong for axitinib, suggesting type-specific off-target consequences that warrant consideration during dose selection for chronic ocular use (Bakri et al., 2024). While intravitreal biologics neutralize extracellular VEGF ligands, axitinib acts intracellularly to block receptor signaling, potentially addressing compensatory upregulation of VEGF-C/D observed with VEGF-A-only inhibition; however, the breadth of intracellular TKI targeting can also propagate off-target effects in non-endothelial cells if exposures are not carefully controlled (Leitch et al., 2024).

Delivery-route pharmacology is central to ocular mechanism realization: suprachoroidal injection of axitinib has demonstrated posterior-segment targeting with markedly elevated drug levels in the posterior eye cup versus intravitreal injection in rabbits and sustained RPE/choroid exposure with minimal systemic levels, thereby achieving compartmentalized receptor inhibition where angiogenic lesions and the RPE reside (Wolf et al., 2022; Kaiser et al., 2020). Complementary intravitreal hydrogel implant strategies (OTX-TKI) provide long-acting axitinib delivery (6–9 months), maintaining pharmacologically relevant intraocular concentrations to chronically suppress VEGFR/PDGFR signaling in nAMD; these platforms are designed to mitigate systemic adverse events while sustaining on-target ocular pharmacodynamics (Fei et al., 2024). Importantly, chronic VEGF pathway suppression carries theoretical risks for neurosensory retina and RPE health, and reviews emphasize the need to balance durable angiogenic control with potential adverse effects of continuous VEGF blockade in ocular tissues, particularly for non-neovascular indications (Wolf et al., 2022).

Therapeutic targets in the RPE relevant to the present hypothesis—MerTK and αvβ5 integrin—are core components of the photoreceptor outer segment (POS) phagocytic machinery, with αvβ5 required for synchronized diurnal phagocytosis and MerTK essential for engulfment and internalization; however, the provided literature contains no direct experimental evidence that axitinib upregulates MerTK or αvβ5 expression or function in RPE, nor that PDGFR-β signaling tonically suppresses RPE phagocytic differentiation in vivo (Tosi et al., 2017). Reviews and experimental summaries repeatedly state that available axitinib data focus on endothelial/pericyte targets and CNV models, and that no studies directly link axitinib to enhanced RPE POS uptake, MerTK activation, or αvβ5 integrin upregulation in dry AMD models or RPE cell assays (Fei et al., 2024; Siedlecki et al., 2017). Therefore, while axitinib’s biochemical capacity to inhibit PDGFR-β could, in principle, modulate RPE phenotypes if PDGFR-β were a suppressive node in RPE phagocytic signaling, this mechanistic leap is not supported by direct evidence in the provided literature (Bakri et al., 2024; Fei et al., 2024).

Expected Effect  
The proposed assay hypothesis is that low-dose axitinib will modulate PDGFR-β signaling in RPE to promote a pro-phagocytic differentiation state, enhancing MerTK and αvβ5 expression and POS uptake; however, the body of evidence provided does not include direct demonstrations of axitinib increasing RPE phagocytosis, MerTK activation, or αvβ5 expression in vitro, ex vivo, or in vivo (Fei et al., 2024; Siedlecki et al., 2017). Available data show that axitinib reduces VEGFR1/VEGFR2 and PDGFR-β phosphorylation in retinal endothelial systems and in limited contexts citing RPE/HUVEC cells, but these findings stop short of connecting PDGFR-β inhibition to increased RPE phagocytic function, and no POS ingestion assays, MerTK phosphorylation readouts, or αvβ5 functional binding assays are reported for axitinib in RPE (Lazzara et al., 2024). By contrast, the canonical RPE phagocytic apparatus depends on αvβ5-mediated POS binding and MerTK-mediated engulfment, with genetic ablation models establishing necessity; the literature here confirms this machinery but does not implicate PDGFR-β overactivation as a hindrance to RPE phagocytic differentiation nor demonstrate that TKIs restore phagocytosis in RPE (Tosi et al., 2017).

If the assay system relies on differentiating human RPE and measures POS uptake alongside MerTK and αvβ5 expression, axitinib’s expected on-target effects would be limited to PDGFR-β kinase inhibition in RPE if PDGFR-β is expressed and active under the assay conditions; nevertheless, there is no provided evidence that such inhibition increases MerTK or αvβ5 levels or POS ingestion, and the hypothesis that PDGFR-β tone suppresses RPE phagocytosis remains unsubstantiated here (Fei et al., 2024; Siedlecki et al., 2017). It is conceivable that low-dose axitinib could indirectly affect RPE phenotype via modulation of growth-factor signaling crosstalk, extracellular matrix deposition, or oxidative stress pathways observed in retinal endothelial cells; however, the provided literature does not extend these effects to RPE phagocytic function, and any expectation of enhanced MerTK/αvβ5 expression is speculative in the absence of direct data (Siedlecki et al., 2017; Lazzara et al., 2024).

Translational pharmacology considerations highlight both opportunity and risk: suprachoroidal axitinib enriches drug at the choroid/RPE with minimal systemic exposure, offering a delivery solution for RPE-targeted modulation if the mechanism were validated; at the same time, axitinib’s TIE2 inhibition and class-level concerns about chronic VEGF pathway suppression in the neurosensory retina argue for caution in non-neovascular indications such as dry AMD (Kaiser et al., 2020; Bakri et al., 2024). Moreover, systemic axitinib has been associated with retinal vascular adverse events in case reports, indicating that even low systemic exposures can affect retinal physiology, so RPE-targeted development would necessitate rigorous intraocular PK/PD mapping and safety margins that preserve vascular stability while testing RPE functional endpoints (Bakri et al., 2024). Given these constraints, the expected effect in an RPE phagocytosis assay is, at present, not evidence-based in the provided materials and should be framed as a hypothesis requiring first-principles validation rather than an anticipated outcome derived from prior literature (Fei et al., 2024).

Overall Evaluation  

Strengths  
- Pharmacologic potency and dual-pathway coverage: Axitinib’s high-affinity pan-VEGFR inhibition with demonstrated PDGFR-β activity enables simultaneous suppression of endothelial and pericyte signaling, a combination that underpins robust anti-angiogenic efficacy in CNV models and may reduce fibrosis-linked extracellular matrix deposition in neovascular settings (Giddabasappa et al., 2016).  
- Validated ocular delivery options: Suprachoroidal suspension (CLS-AX) and intravitreal hydrogel implant (OTX-TKI) platforms have progressed into clinical testing for nAMD with encouraging ocular safety/PK signals, providing translationally relevant routes to expose the RPE/choroid compartment while minimizing systemic exposure (Wolf et al., 2022; Fei et al., 2024).  
- Clinical-stage experience in nAMD: Early-phase trials indicate biological activity and tolerability of axitinib-based intraocular approaches, suggesting a viable path to long-acting ocular therapy development if a new indication such as RPE phagocytosis enhancement is supported by mechanistic data (Leitch et al., 2024).  
- Class learnings and comparator programs: The broader TKI field in nAMD (vorolanib, sunitinib derivatives) offers benchmarks for durability, rescue-free rates, and safety considerations, facilitating rational dose and exposure setting for axitinib formulations (Fei et al., 2024; Leitch et al., 2024).  

Limitations and Critical Gaps  
- No direct evidence for RPE phagocytosis enhancement: Across the provided literature, there are no RPE-specific data showing axitinib increases MerTK or αvβ5 expression, augments POS binding or internalization, or improves phagocytic capacity in differentiating RPE; multiple summaries explicitly state the absence of such evidence and confine axitinib’s demonstrated actions to anti-angiogenic endpoints (Fei et al., 2024; Siedlecki et al., 2017).  
- Unvalidated mechanistic premise in RPE: While PDGFR-β is central to pericyte biology and vessel stabilization, the assertion that PDGFR-β overactivation hinders RPE differentiation and that its inhibition promotes a pro-phagocytic state is not supported in the provided texts, and the core RPE phagocytic machinery (MerTK, αvβ5) is not linked here to PDGFR-β modulation by TKIs (Tosi et al., 2017; Bakri et al., 2024).  
- Safety liabilities and target selectivity: Axitinib’s strong TIE2 inhibition could perturb vascular stability with chronic intraocular exposure, and class-level concerns exist for continuous VEGF pathway suppression affecting neurosensory retina and RPE; rare but notable retinal vascular AEs reported with systemic axitinib further mandate conservative exposure design and careful safety pharmacology tailored to non-neovascular indications (Bakri et al., 2024; Wolf et al., 2022).  
- Delivery constraints for non-neovascular endpoints: Topical TKI programs frequently failed due to inadequate posterior segment exposure and/or ocular surface safety findings, suggesting that any RPE-targeted application of axitinib would require invasive delivery (suprachoroidal/IVT depot) with chronic exposure, elevating the bar for safety and benefit-risk justification in a dry AMD population (Fei et al., 2024).  
- Field precedent: Reviews underscore that, to date, small-molecule success in AMD has been limited, and there are no approved ocular TKIs for either wet or dry AMD, reinforcing translational uncertainty for new mechanism-of-use proposals lacking direct preclinical support (Fei et al., 2024; Bakri et al., 2024).  

Development Recommendations and Go/No-Go Criteria  
- Mechanistic validation in RPE: Before proceeding, perform a staged in vitro program in human iPSC-RPE and primary RPE to test whether axitinib modulates PDGFR-β phosphorylation and secondarily increases MerTK expression, MerTK phosphorylation upon POS challenge, αvβ5 expression/localization, and POS binding/internalization; include dose-response spanning sub-nanomolar to low-nanomolar exposures aligned with ocular PK projections from suprachoroidal or depot IVT delivery (Wolf et al., 2022; Kaiser et al., 2020).  
- Specificity controls and orthogonal readouts: Use chemical and genetic controls to isolate PDGFR-β dependence (e.g., PDGFR-β ligand titrations, selective PDGFR-β inhibitors distinct from axitinib, and PDGFR-β knockdown), and include neutralizing antibodies and MerTK/αvβ5 functional blocking to map pathway causality for any observed phagocytosis changes (Tosi et al., 2017; Siedlecki et al., 2017).  
- Safety pharmacology in pigmented models: Given potential TIE2-related and VEGF-suppression liabilities, include long-term RPE morphology, barrier function (TEER), transcriptomic stress signatures, and photoreceptor/RPE co-culture viability endpoints at projected clinical exposures; extend to pigmented rabbit or NHP pharmacology to define posterior-segment exposure, ocular distribution (RPE/sclera/choroid), and a NOAEL for chronic dosing (Bakri et al., 2024; Kaiser et al., 2020).  
- Translational gating: Advance only if axitinib reproducibly increases POS uptake and MerTK/αvβ5 functional markers in RPE with a therapeutic window that avoids endothelial destabilization signals and preserves neuronal/RPE integrity in chronic exposure models; otherwise, deprioritize axitinib for RPE-targeted dry AMD and refocus efforts on mechanisms directly validated to enhance RPE phagocytosis or survival (Fei et al., 2024; Wolf et al., 2022).  

Conclusion  
On the strength of available evidence, axitinib is a validated, potent anti-angiogenic TKI with multiple ocular delivery platforms in clinical development for nAMD and robust preclinical efficacy in CNV models; however, the central hypothesis that low-dose axitinib enhances RPE phagocytosis via PDGFR-β modulation and upregulates MerTK/αvβ5 lacks direct support in the provided literature, and multiple sources explicitly state that no such RPE-centric data exist for axitinib at present (Giddabasappa et al., 2016; Fei et al., 2024). The translational and safety profiles suggest that invasive ocular delivery could achieve RPE-adjacent exposure, but off-target TIE2 inhibition and class-level VEGF-suppression risks must be weighed carefully in a non-neovascular indication, particularly absent clear preclinical efficacy on RPE phagocytic endpoints (Bakri et al., 2024; Wolf et al., 2022). Accordingly, while axitinib remains attractive for anti-angiogenic applications, it should not be advanced for dry AMD on a phagocytosis-enhancement rationale without first producing compelling, direct RPE mechanistic data; in the absence of such data, development resources would be better allocated to candidates with demonstrated effects on MerTK/αvβ5 function or on complement-mediated RPE pathology more proximal to dry AMD pathophysiology (Giddabasappa et al., 2016; Fei et al., 2024). Pending rigorous in vitro and ex vivo validation as outlined, the current evidence base supports a cautious “mechanism validation required” stance rather than progression to in vivo dry AMD studies or clinical translation for this proposed use (Fei et al., 2024; Bakri et al., 2024).
