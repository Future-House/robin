Proposal for SB202190 (p38 MAPK inhibitor; Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. SML0687)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
SB202190 is a synthetic small‐molecule inhibitor belonging to the pyridinyl imidazole class of compounds. Originally discovered through rigorous cell‐based phenotypic screens aimed at identifying agents with anti‐inflammatory properties, SB202190 was developed to selectively inhibit p38 mitogen‐activated protein kinase (MAPK), particularly the α and β isoforms. The molecule was first characterized using radiolabeled ligand binding studies, which confirmed its ability to bind the ATP‐binding pocket of p38 MAPK and suppress its catalytic function (Young, 2013; Hirosawa et al., 2009). Being available commercially (e.g., Sigma‐Aldrich Cat. No. SML0687), SB202190 has been widely used as a biochemical tool to dissect the role of p38 in cellular stress and inflammatory responses. This class of compounds, including SB203580 as a closely related analogue, is recognized for its ability to modulate intracellular signaling cascades under various stress conditions. They have been broadly used in research settings to probe the molecular basis of cytokine production, cell differentiation, apoptosis, and cytoskeletal regulation (Young, 2013; Maugeri et al., 2021). The candidate under discussion is being repurposed with the hypothesis that its high potency against p38α/β—exhibiting an IC₅₀ of approximately 40 nM—can be leveraged to correct stress‐induced cytoskeletal remodeling defects and improve the phagocytic uptake of photoreceptor outer segments (POS) in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells, particularly under oxidative stress conditions that mimic the pathophysiology of dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) (Liu et al., 2025).

Therapeutic History:  
Historically, SB202190 has been predominantly employed as a research reagent to interrogate the role of p38 MAPK signaling in various cell types and disease models. In the context of ocular studies, SB202190, similar to its analogue SB203580, has been used to examine fibrotic responses, cell migration, and cytokine production in RPE cells. For instance, several in vitro studies have demonstrated that inhibition of p38 MAPK by SB202190 effectively reduces TGF‐β2–induced extracellular matrix (ECM) production (specifically collagen type I and fibronectin synthesis), which serves as an experimental surrogate for fibrotic remodeling observed in retinal degenerative conditions (Saika et al., 2005). Moreover, experiments using ARPE‐19 human RPE cells have revealed that SB202190 can reverse abnormal cell migration and reduce the transformation of RPE cells into fibroblast‐like cells in models of proliferative vitreoretinopathy (PVR) (Saika et al., 2005). Although these models do not directly replicate dry AMD, they capture key aspects of RPE dysfunction, such as cytoskeletal disorganization and aberrant ECM deposition, which are also implicated in the progression of AMD. Beyond ocular research, p38 MAPK inhibitors have been investigated in other pathologies characterized by chronic inflammation and stress‐induced cellular dysfunction, including rheumatoid arthritis and muscular dystrophies (Brennan et al., 2021; Hirosawa et al., 2009). However, to date, clinical applications of SB202190 itself have not been pursued, and its use has remained restricted to preclinical experimental studies, thereby providing both a strong mechanistic rationale and a cautionary note regarding its translational potential (Federspiel et al., 2023; Young, 2013). Importantly, while numerous studies have elucidated p38 MAPK’s involvement in retinal injury and oxidative stress responses, there is limited direct clinical evidence supporting the use of SB202190 for treating dry AMD, and its application in this indication remains strictly hypothetical at this stage (Moustardas et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025).

Mechanism of Action:  
At the molecular level, SB202190 is known to selectively target the ATP‐binding pocket of p38 MAPK, thereby inhibiting its enzymatic activity and preventing the phosphorylation of downstream substrates that are pivotal to stress‐induced signaling cascades. Under normal conditions, p38 MAPK becomes activated through dual phosphorylation on specific threonine and tyrosine residues within its activation loop. Once activated, p38 MAPK stimulates various substrates, including transcription factors (e.g., cFos) and regulatory proteins involved in the control of cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, and inflammatory mediator production (Young, 2013; Hirosawa et al., 2009). In RPE cells, activation of p38 MAPK has been linked to cytoskeletal rearrangements that lead to stress‐induced cellular defects; these include formation of abnormal actin structures and impaired formation of phagocytic cups necessary for the internalization of POS (Pocrnich et al., 2009; Wang et al., 2024). In the experimental setting, SB202190 has been shown to effectively block the TGF‐β2 mediated phosphorylation of p38, thereby reducing the transcriptional activity of Smad3/4 complexes without impacting the initial receptor‐mediated phosphorylation events of Smads2/3. This mechanism suggests that SB202190 acts downstream of TGF‐β receptor activation, interfering with p38‐dependent enhancement of transcription factor complex formation and nuclear translocation, which in turn can dampen pathological ECM production and abnormal cell migration (Saika et al., 2005). Additionally, literature indicates that stress‐activated p38 signaling contributes to the senescence of RPE cells by regulating the senescence‐associated secretory phenotype (SASP) through modulation of p53 and NF‐κB pathways (Liu et al., 2025). By inhibiting p38 MAPK, SB202190 is hypothesized to replicate the protective effects of endogenous negative regulators such as DUSP4, which dephosphorylate and inactivate p38 MAPK, ultimately reducing RPE cell senescence and maintaining cellular homeostasis (Liu et al., 2025). It is noteworthy that p38 MAPK signaling is intricately intertwined with other MAPK family members, and some studies have reported that SB202190, particularly at higher doses, exhibits a biphasic response where alternative signaling pathways (such as MEK/ERK) may become upregulated, emphasizing the importance of careful dose optimization in any therapeutic application (Wang et al., 2024; Hirosawa et al., 2009). Thus, the mechanism of action of SB202190 involves a highly specific inhibition of p38α/β kinase activity, which is expected to restore normal actin dynamics and cytoskeletal organization in RPE cells, thereby facilitating proper phagosome formation and efficient POS uptake under oxidative stress conditions akin to those found in dry AMD.

Expected Effect:  
The primary hypothesis driving the repurposing of SB202190 for dry AMD is that inhibition of p38α/β in RPE cells will reduce stress‐induced cytoskeletal remodeling defects and thereby improve the phagocytic internalization of photoreceptor outer segments. Under oxidative stress conditions, such as exposure to hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) in in vitro models using ARPE‐19 cells, activation of p38 MAPK is known to mediate cytoskeletal disorganization and promote aberrant cell migration, processes that are deleterious to the RPE’s phagocytic function (Pocrnich et al., 2009; Wang et al., 2024). By inhibiting p38 MAPK with SB202190 at a low nanomolar concentration (approximately 40 nM), it is anticipated that the compound will reduce the phosphorylation of p38, thereby preventing the downstream activation of stress‐responsive transcription factors and proteins responsible for actin remodeling (Young, 2013; Hirosawa et al., 2009). In doing so, SB202190 is expected to “normalize” the actin cytoskeletal dynamics in H₂O₂‐treated ARPE‐19 cells, thus facilitating the re‐establishment of properly organized phagocytic cups required for POS ingestion—a process critical for photoreceptor maintenance and overall retinal homeostasis (Liu et al., 2025; Saika et al., 2005). In parallel, inhibition of p38 MAPK may downregulate pro‐fibrotic and pro‐senescent signaling pathways (e.g., those mediated by TGF‐β2, p53, and NF‐κB), therefore limiting the aberrant deposition of ECM components and improving the overall cellular microenvironment (Liu et al., 2025; Saika et al., 2005). As a result, the expected effect of SB202190 in the assay proposed would be a significant improvement in phagosome internalization of POS under oxidative conditions, combined with a reduction in the markers of cytoskeletal stress and cell senescence. Moreover, enhanced phagocytic activity by RPE cells could mitigate pathological features of dry AMD, such as drusen formation and eventual RPE degeneration, thereby potentially slowing disease progression (Moustardas et al., 2023; Du et al., 2010). The compound’s effect is further anticipated to extend to modulation of inflammatory cytokine release, given the well‐established role of p38 MAPK in regulating cytokine production, thus providing a dual benefit by both preserving cytoskeletal integrity and reducing chronic, low‐grade inflammation in the retina (Liu et al., 2025; Kyosseva, 2016).

Overall Evaluation:  
Evaluating the potential of SB202190 as a repurposed drug candidate for dry AMD involves weighing both the strengths and the inherent challenges associated with targeting the p38 MAPK pathway in the RPE. One of the major strengths of SB202190 is its well‐characterized mechanism of action as a selective inhibitor of p38α/β, which is pivotal in mediating stress‐induced cytoskeletal remodeling, inflammation, and cellular senescence in RPE cells. The compound has demonstrated robust in vitro anti‐fibrotic and anti‐migratory effects in ARPE‐19 cell models that mirror key features of RPE dysfunction observed in dry AMD (Saika et al., 2005). Furthermore, the concept of normalizing actin dynamics to enhance phagocytic cup formation and subsequent POS uptake is highly attractive, given that impaired phagocytosis is a core feature driving the degeneration observed in dry AMD (Pocrnich et al., 2009; Wang et al., 2024). The protective role played by endogenous regulators like DUSP4, which deactivates p38 MAPK and preserves RPE integrity, supports the rationale for a pharmacological approach that mimics this effect (Liu et al., 2025). Additionally, numerous studies have shown that p38 MAPK inhibitors can modulate inflammatory responses and reduce ECM deposition; such effects are promising in the context of retinal diseases where chronic inflammation and fibrosis exacerbate degenerative processes (Moustardas et al., 2023; Saika et al., 2005).

However, several weaknesses and potential caveats must be thoroughly considered. First, the p38 MAPK pathway exhibits complex, context‐dependent behavior in RPE cells. There is evidence suggesting that, under certain circumstances, p38 MAPK activation can be cytoprotective by facilitating adaptive stress responses, such as the upregulation of antioxidant defenses (Pocrnich et al., 2009). This duality indicates that complete or prolonged inhibition of p38 MAPK may inadvertently dampen protective signaling, potentially leading to adverse outcomes. Additionally, published studies have documented that SB202190, when used at higher concentrations, can show biphasic responses or off‐target effects that might include the unintended activation of alternative MAPK pathways (Wang et al., 2024; Hirosawa et al., 2009). This underscores the critical need for precise dose optimization and comprehensive pharmacodynamic studies to define a therapeutic window where the beneficial effects on cytoskeletal remodeling and phagocytosis are maximized while the risks of impairing physiological stress responses are minimized.

Furthermore, while the preclinical literature provides substantial mechanistic insights and supportive data from retinal injury models—particularly in contexts such as PVR—the clinical translation of these findings to dry AMD remains unproven. SB202190 has not been advanced into clinical trials for retinal degenerative diseases, and its application has thus far been confined to experimental paradigms (Kim et al., 2021; Kyosseva, 2016). Therefore, any proposal to repurpose SB202190 for dry AMD must be accompanied by robust in vivo efficacy studies using animal models that faithfully recapitulate the chronic, multifactorial nature of the disease. Moreover, the impact of SB202190 on other critical RPE functions, such as phagocytosis of POS under normal conditions and cytoskeletal dynamics related to cell adhesion and migration, needs to be extensively characterized to avoid unintended disruption of essential homeostatic processes (Kim et al., 2021; Kyosseva, 2016).

Another consideration is the limitation inherent to the use of SB202190 as a research tool rather than a clinical‐grade pharmaceutical compound. Its pharmacokinetic and safety profiles have been characterized in experimental contexts, but there is a lack of clinical data regarding potential ocular toxicity, bioavailability, and long‐term impact on retinal structure and function (Federspiel et al., 2023; Young, 2013). The multifaceted role of p38 MAPK in the retina—spanning inflammatory cytokine production, regulation of apoptosis, and cytoskeletal dynamics—means that a fine balance must be maintained; excessive suppression could lead to undesired consequences such as impaired wound healing or compromised cell survival under stress. As such, a detailed understanding of the dose‐response relationship and the temporal dynamics of p38 inhibition in specific RPE subpopulations will be essential.

In summary, SB202190 presents as a promising candidate for repurposing in the treatment of dry AMD based on strong mechanistic rationale and preclinical evidence supporting its ability to modulate pathological pathways implicated in RPE dysfunction. Its capacity to inhibit p38α/β with high potency, restore actin dynamics under oxidative conditions, and improve phagocytic function aligns well with the therapeutic goals of preserving RPE integrity and retinal homeostasis (Liu et al., 2025). Nonetheless, the dualistic nature of p38 MAPK signaling—with its capacity to mediate both deleterious and protective responses—poses a significant challenge that mandates careful and comprehensive preclinical evaluation. The candidate’s strengths reside in its well‐characterized inhibitory activity, established use in modulating fibrotic and inflammatory responses in ocular models, and its potential to replicate the endogenous protective functions mediated by regulators such as DUSP4 (Liu et al., 2025). Conversely, the weaknesses center on potential off‐target effects, the risk of inadvertently suppressing adaptive stress responses, and the absence of clinical studies directly addressing dry AMD.

Overall, while SB202190 holds promise based on its molecular mode of action and supportive in vitro data regarding cytoskeletal normalization and phagocytosis, the translational viability for dry AMD will ultimately depend on rigorous dosing studies, long‐term safety assessments, and demonstration of efficacy in animal models that capture the chronic oxidative and inflammatory milieu of the human retina (Moustardas et al., 2023; Saika et al., 2005; Wang et al., 2024). Only through such comprehensive studies can we adequately determine whether SB202190’s benefits outweigh its potential risks and if it can be successfully repurposed as a therapeutic intervention to mitigate the progression of dry AMD.
