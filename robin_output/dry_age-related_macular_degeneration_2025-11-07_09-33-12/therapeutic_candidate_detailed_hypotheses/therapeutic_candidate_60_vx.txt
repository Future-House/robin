Proposal for VX-765 (Caspase-1 inhibitor; Vertex research compound)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
VX-765, also known as Belnacasan, is a synthetic, orally available caspase-1 inhibitor originally developed by Vertex Pharmaceuticals as part of their research into anti-inflammatory therapeutics (Dhani et al., 2021; Kallinich, 2015). This drug is a peptidomimetic compound designed to mimic the substrate recognition sequence of caspase-1 – a feature that traces its molecular design to early peptide-based inhibitors based on the tetrapeptide motif YVAD (Kudelova et al., 2015; Doyle et al., 2015). VX-765 is administered as an inactive prodrug that is rapidly cleaved by plasma and liver esterases into its active metabolite, VRT-043198, which then exerts inhibitory effects on caspase-1 (Liang et al., 2022; Kallinich, 2015). As a member of the caspase inhibitor class, VX-765 represents a second‐generation approach compared to its predecessor pralnacasan (VX-740), seeking improved oral bioavailability, enhanced selectivity, and a more favorable pharmacokinetic profile (Dhani et al., 2021; Kudelova et al., 2015). The drug belongs to the broader class of inflammasome inhibitors that target IL-1β and IL-18 releasing cascades, which have been extensively explored for inflammatory, autoimmune, and neurological disorders (Kallinich, 2015; Kudelova et al., 2015). The synthetic origin and deliberate design emphasizes a precision targeting of caspase-1’s catalytic cysteine, a critical residue in the enzyme’s active site, thereby inhibiting its proteolytic function; this is particularly important given the role of caspase-1 in mediating pyroptosis and inflammatory cytokine maturation (Kallinich, 2015; Kudelova et al., 2015).

Therapeutic History:  
Historically, VX-765 has been evaluated in preclinical studies and early-phase human trials mainly for systemic inflammatory and neurological conditions. Early investigations demonstrated that VX-765 robustly reduced plasma cytokine levels in dose-dependent manners in Phase I studies, and subsequent Phase II trials in psoriasis provided evidence of both safety and clinical activity (Dhani et al., 2021; Kudelova et al., 2015; Maroso et al., 2011). Its predecessor pralnacasan showed efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis clinical trials, although long-term use in animal models eventually raised concerns about potential hepatotoxicity, leading to its withdrawal despite promising initial results (Dhani et al., 2021; Kudelova et al., 2015). VX-765 was later repurposed for neurological conditions; it has been tested in partial epilepsy where, despite a good safety profile, its efficacy was less than anticipated (Dhani et al., 2021; Kudelova et al., 2015; Kallinich, 2015). In addition, VX-765 has demonstrated anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective effects in preclinical models of Alzheimer’s disease by reducing IL-1β production in the brain and preserving synaptic function (Liang et al., 2022; Flores et al., 2018). Importantly, although the clinical and preclinical development history of VX-765 spans a variety of inflammatory and neurodegenerative diseases, it has not been extensively evaluated for dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD) or retinal diseases per se (Dhani et al., 2021; Rasoulinejad et al., 2020; Kallinich, 2015). Nevertheless, a search in clinical trial databases has uncovered a limited number of studies investigating caspase-1 inhibitors in the context of macular degeneration, reinforcing the notion that the therapeutic paradigm may be extended to ocular disorders (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.).

Mechanism of Action:  
Caspase-1 is a cysteine-dependent aspartate-directed protease that plays a central role in the activation of inflammatory cytokines by cleaving pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18 into their mature, biologically active forms; these cytokines are major mediators of inflammatory cascades and pyroptotic cell death (Kallinich, 2015; Kudelova et al., 2015). VX-765 functions by inhibiting caspase-1 through a competitive mechanism that involves binding to the catalytic cysteine residue; structural studies have indicated that its peptidomimetic scaffold effectively occupies the substrate-binding pocket, thereby preventing substrate access (Kudelova et al., 2015; Kallinich, 2015). Although the hypothesis for repurposing VX-765 in dry AMD posits an irreversible inhibition of caspase-1, the bulk of the literature supports that VX-765 is, in fact, a reversible inhibitor (Dhani et al., 2021; Kallinich, 2015). Activation of VX-765 in vivo produces VRT-043198, which exhibits low nanomolar inhibitory potency against human caspase-1 (IC₅₀ ~3.68–9.91 nM) and comparable activity in murine models (IC₅₀ ~18–52.1 nM), rendering it suitable for precise modulation of caspase-1 activity (Flores et al., 2018; Rasoulinejad et al., 2020). By inhibiting caspase-1, VX-765 effectively prevents the maturation and secretion of IL-1β and IL-18, thereby disrupting the propagation of inflammatory signals that can lead to dysfunctional cellular responses such as pyroptosis—a lytic form of programmed cell death triggered by inflammasome activation (Kallinich, 2015; Younus & Reddy, 2018). In RPE cells, where caspase-1 expression has been documented as a component of inflammasome signaling especially during stress conditions, inhibition of caspase-1 is anticipated to preserve lysosomal integrity by reducing cytokine-induced lysosomal destabilization and preventing the cleavage of substrates that contribute to cell death pathways (Gao et al., 2019; Kudelova et al., 2015). Moreover, evidence suggests that caspase-1 activation leads to gasdermin D cleavage, which forms membrane pores and facilitates pyroptotic cell death; therefore, blockade by VX-765 could mitigate this deleterious cascade (Kallinich, 2015; Flores et al., 2018).

Expected Effect:  
In the context of dry AMD, the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) is a critical cell type responsible for maintaining photoreceptor health through the daily phagocytosis of shed photoreceptor outer segments (POS). Under pathological conditions, chronic low-grade inflammation driven by the inflammasome can lead to caspase-1 activation in RPE cells, resulting in IL-1β maturation and subsequent pyroptotic signaling that compromises lysosomal integrity (Gao et al., 2019; Marneros, 2023). The proposed therapeutic hypothesis is that VX-765, by inhibiting caspase-1 at an IC₅₀ value of approximately 10 nM in RPE cell assays (for example, in the ARPE-19 cell line), will significantly reduce IL-1β production and attenuate subsequent inflammatory cascades (Kallinich, 2015; Rasoulinejad et al., 2020). This preservation of lysosomal function is clinically significant because it is expected to stabilize LAMP1-positive lysosomal structures within the RPE, thereby maintaining efficient clearance of POS and preventing the accumulation of toxic debris—a hallmark of dry AMD pathogenesis (Kudelova et al., 2015). Preclinical evidence from studies using caspase-1 inhibitors indicates that such intervention leads to a reduction in inflammatory cytokine release and improves cellular homeostasis in contexts similar to those observed in AMD (Kudelova et al., 2015). Although the majority of previous studies have focused on systemic inflammatory or neurological disorders, the underlying mechanisms—namely, the inhibition of the caspase-1/IL-1β axis—are directly relevant to RPE dysfunction and can feasibly be repurposed for the treatment of dry AMD (Marneros, 2023). The ultimate expected outcome would be the preservation of RPE phagocytic pathways, ensuring proper degradation of POS, reducing chronic inflammation, and thereby potentially slowing or halting the progression of geographic atrophy in dry AMD (Gao et al., 2019; Marneros, 2023).

Overall Evaluation:  
The repurposing of VX-765 for dry AMD represents a compelling strategy, driven by a strong mechanistic rationale and bolstered by its demonstrated efficacy in other inflammatory models. One of the primary strengths of VX-765 is its potent and selective inhibition of caspase-1, with low nanomolar IC₅₀ values ensuring that only minimal concentrations are required to achieve therapeutic effects (Flores et al., 2018; Rasoulinejad et al., 2020). This level of potency is crucial given the sensitivity of RPE cells to inflammatory mediators and the need for high selectivity in ocular tissues where off-target effects could be particularly damaging (Dhani et al., 2021; Kallinich, 2015). Moreover, the fact that VX-765 has already undergone Phase II clinical evaluation for indications such as psoriasis and epilepsy provides an established safety profile and foundational pharmacokinetic data that may be extrapolated to ocular applications, albeit with necessary adjustments for local delivery (Dhani et al., 2021; Kudelova et al., 2015).

Despite these strengths, several challenges persist. A critical point of concern is the discrepancy between the working hypothesis, which posits irreversible inhibition of caspase-1, and the well-documented reversible mode of action of VX-765 (Dhani et al., 2021; Kallinich, 2015). This difference in mechanism may influence both the duration of drug action and the frequency of dosing required to maintain therapeutic inhibition in vivo. Furthermore, while the anti-inflammatory effects have been established in systemic and neuronal contexts, direct evidence supporting the efficacy of VX-765 in preserving RPE lysosomal integrity and phagocytic function in dry AMD remains limited. Specific in vitro studies using ARPE-19 cells have indicated a reduction in IL-1β release and preservation of LAMP1-positive lysosomes upon caspase-1 inhibition (Kudelova et al., 2015), yet these findings need to be replicated and further validated in dry AMD models to ascertain translational potential.

Another potential weakness arises from safety considerations observed in the development history of related compounds. Pralnacasan (VX-740), a predecessor of VX-765, was associated with dose-limiting hepatotoxicity in long-term animal studies despite initial safety in human trials (Dhani et al., 2021; Kudelova et al., 2015). Although VX-765 has demonstrated a better safety profile in early clinical testing, caution must be exercised when repurposing the drug for chronic ocular use, particularly given the possibility of systemic absorption from an ocular delivery formulation (Dhani et al., 2021; Rasoulinejad et al., 2020). Additionally, while systemic administration of VX-765 has been explored, the optimal method for targeting the retina—whether through intravitreal injection, sustained-release implants, or other localized delivery systems—remains to be determined. Such considerations are paramount given the unique anatomy of the eye and the necessity of achieving pharmacologically active concentrations in the retina while minimizing exposure to off-target tissues (Kallinich, 2015; Gao et al., 2019).

From a biochemical perspective, the proposed mechanism of action offers a robust framework for the therapeutic intervention in dry AMD. Inhibition of caspase-1 directly limits the maturation of IL-1β and IL-18, inflammatory cytokines that have been implicated in the disruption of lysosomal function and phagocytic degradation of POS in RPE cells (Kallinich, 2015; Flores et al., 2018). Given that chronic inflammation and impaired degradation of photoreceptor debris are central to the progression of geographic atrophy in dry AMD, targeting this pathway could have a disease-modifying effect (Gao et al., 2019; Marneros, 2023). Furthermore, preventing the activation of gasdermin D by caspase-1 could reduce pyroptotic cell death, thereby preserving RPE cell viability—a critical factor in maintaining the outer blood-retina barrier and overall retinal function (Kallinich, 2015; Rasoulinejad et al., 2020).

Another area of promise lies in the potential for repurposing VX-765 based on emerging evidence that links inflammasome activity to ocular disease pathogenesis. Several recent studies have highlighted the role of caspase-1 and its downstream effects in models of retinal degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, and even neovascular AMD (Gao et al., 2019; Bian et al., 2011). Although much of the focus has been on the neovascular form of AMD—with interventions targeting VEGF signaling—the underlying inflammatory processes are shared with the dry form, particularly in the progression of geographic atrophy. The fact that caspase-1 activation has been demonstrated in activated macrophages and retinal microglia in the context of choroidal neovascularization (Marneros, 2023) further supports the notion that modulating this pathway could yield benefits in dry AMD in which inflammatory stress on the RPE is a prominent feature.

On the translational front, the existing clinical data for VX-765 suggest that its safety and tolerability profile is adequate for further investigation in ocular indications (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Marneros, 2023). The challenges associated with systemic toxicity observed in some preclinical contexts must be carefully balanced against the potential benefits of localized ocular delivery, which may allow lower doses and reduced systemic exposure. In the event that future preclinical work confirms the preservation of lysosomal function and improved POS clearance in RPE models, VX-765 could represent a viable candidate to slow the progression of dry AMD—a condition for which there are currently no approved disease-modifying treatments (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Marneros, 2023).

Overall, the strategy of repurposing VX-765 for dry AMD is supported by a coherent mechanistic rationale: by attenuating the caspase-1/IL-1β axis, the compound is expected to reduce inflammasome-mediated lysosomal damage, a key pathological event in RPE dysfunction and photoreceptor degeneration (Kallinich, 2015; Gao et al., 2019). Preclinical studies in other models have demonstrated that caspase-1 inhibition can reduce inflammatory cytokine secretion and preserve cell viability; extending these findings to RPE cells is a logical progression that could address unmet needs in dry AMD therapy (Kallinich, 2015; Rasoulinejad et al., 2020). The extensive development history of VX-765 in other indications provides valuable insights into the dosing, safety, and pharmacodynamics of the compound, thereby reducing some of the risks traditionally associated with early-stage drug development (Dhani et al., 2021; Kudelova et al., 2015).

Nevertheless, several critical hurdles remain. First, the discrepancy between the hypothesized irreversible inhibition and the documented reversible inhibition necessitates a re-evaluation of dosing strategies to achieve sustained caspase-1 suppression in the retinal environment (Dhani et al., 2021; Kallinich, 2015). Second, the translation of systemic safety data to ocular applications is not straightforward; the unique microenvironment of the eye, coupled with the challenges of achieving adequate intraocular concentrations, calls for rigorous preclinical studies using relevant animal models of dry AMD (Gao et al., 2019; Marneros, 2023). Additionally, given the potential for off-target effects and the historical context of hepatotoxicity observed with related compounds, investigators must prioritize the development of formulations that maximize local efficacy while minimizing systemic exposure (Dhani et al., 2021; Kudelova et al., 2015).

Finally, it is important to recognize that while the anti-inflammatory approach targeting the caspase-1 pathway is scientifically sound, dry AMD is a multifactorial disease with a complex pathogenesis that includes oxidative stress, genetic predispositions, and metabolic dysregulation. Therefore, combination therapies that target multiple pathways may ultimately prove more effective than monotherapy with a single agent (Gao et al., 2019; Marneros, 2023). In this light, VX-765 could be considered as a component of a broader therapeutic strategy, potentially in conjunction with agents that address other aspects of RPE dysfunction and retinal degeneration.

In conclusion, the repurposing of VX-765 for dry AMD is supported by a robust body of preclinical and clinical data that demonstrate its ability to inhibit caspase-1 and attenuate inflammatory cascades. The compound’s synthetic origins, its established role as a reversible peptidomimetic caspase-1 inhibitor, and its demonstrated efficacy in reducing IL-1β maturation collectively form a strong foundation for its potential use in preserving lysosomal integrity and sustaining POS clearance in RPE cells (Dhani et al., 2021; Kallinich, 2015). However, further targeted studies in ocular models are imperative to validate these effects in the context of dry AMD, to optimize dosing regimens that account for its reversible mechanism, and to develop delivery strategies that ensure localized efficacy with minimal systemic risk. The candidate’s prior clinical evaluation in other indications offers a promising starting point, yet the leap to treating a chronic ocular disease will require careful, systematic investigation. Overall, VX-765 holds promise as a therapeutic candidate for dry AMD, but its ultimate success will depend on overcoming formulation challenges, confirming its mechanism in RPE cells, and integrating its use into a multifaceted therapeutic regimen tailored to the complex pathogenesis of dry AMD (ClinicalTrials.gov, n.d.; Marneros, 2023; Rasoulinejad et al., 2020).
