Proposal for SBI-0206965 (ULK1 inhibitor; Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. SML1783)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate:  
SBI-0206965 is a synthetic small molecule inhibitor that belongs to the class of pyrimidine analogs specifically designed to target the ULK1 (Unc-51-like autophagy-activating kinase 1) enzyme and, to a lesser extent, its paralog ULK2. This compound was developed via a targeted, medicinal chemistry approach using principles of structure–activity relationship (SAR) optimization that originally emerged from modifications of focal adhesion kinase (FAK) inhibitor scaffolds, eventually identifying a pyrimidine core that confers selectivity toward ULK1 kinase activity (Kocak et al., 2022). Commercially available from Sigma-Aldrich (Cat. No. SML1783), SBI-0206965 has been primarily utilized as a tool compound to dissect the roles of ULK1 in autophagy regulation. Compounds of this class have generally been employed in preclinical studies to probe autophagy pathways—in cancer models, for example—to determine the role of autophagy in cellular survival and stress responses. The chemical probe is further characterized by its ability to interfere with autophagy initiation processes, placing it within the emerging category of autophagy modulators intended to refine cellular clearance mechanisms rather than completely abrogate them (Kocak et al., 2022).

Therapeutic History:  
The pharmacological activity of SBI-0206965 has primarily been established in cancer research and neurodegenerative models, where its ability to inhibit ULK1-mediated autophagy is used to sensitize tumor cells to chemotherapy and to protect neurons from acute degenerative stress. For instance, in non–small cell lung cancer cells, SBI-0206965 has been shown to reduce autophagic flux and promote apoptotic cell death when combined with other chemotherapeutic agents (Limpert et al., 2018). Moreover, preclinical studies using retinal ganglion cell models have demonstrated that intravitreal injection of higher concentrations (e.g., 50 µM) of SBI-0206965 can reduce acute axonal degeneration and modify autophagy markers, suggesting that autophagy modulation might translate to neuroprotective outcomes in ocular tissues (Sundermeyer, 2022). Despite these promising results in models of cancer and neuronal injury, there have been no dedicated studies or clinical trials assessing the use of SBI-0206965 specifically for dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) or related retinal diseases. Nonetheless, foundational evidence supports the concept that autophagy dysregulation, particularly in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), contributes to the pathogenesis of dry AMD, providing a rationale for exploring autophagy regulators like SBI-0206965 in this context (Blasiak et al., 2025; Falcão et al., 2025).

Mechanism of Action:  
At the molecular level, SBI-0206965 functions by competitively inhibiting ULK1 kinase activity, which is a critical initiating event in the canonical autophagy pathway. Under physiological conditions, ULK1 forms a complex with adaptor proteins ATG13, FIP200, and ATG101 to trigger autophagosome formation by promoting the phosphorylation of downstream targets such as Beclin-1 and VPS34; these events are essential for membrane nucleation and the subsequent formation of autophagic vesicles (Intartaglia et al., 2022). SBI-0206965 achieves an inhibitory effect on ULK1 with a biochemical IC₅₀ in the low nanomolar range (approximately 108 nM for ULK1 and 711 nM for ULK2), thereby impairing the phosphorylation cascade that would normally lead to the formation of autophagosomes (Kocak et al., 2022). It is noteworthy that the inhibition of ULK1 by SBI-0206965 has been reported to result in decreased phosphorylation of key autophagy regulators, alongside a reduction in LC3-positive puncta and concomitant increases in the autophagic adaptor molecule p62, confirming the compound’s ability to attenuate canonical autophagy (Pinelli et al., 2025; Limpert et al., 2018). Importantly, emerging evidence in RPE cells suggests that, while canonical autophagy is ULK1-dependent, the noncanonical process known as LC3-associated phagocytosis (LAP) is largely independent of ULK1. This mechanistic divergence forms the basis of the hypothesis that low-dose ULK1 inhibition with SBI-0206965 could selectively modulate the balance between bulk autophagy and LAP, favoring the latter—a process known to be integral for the efficient degradation of photoreceptor outer segments (POS) in RPE cells (Liton et al., 2023; Jain et al., 2023). However, it is also crucial to mention that the compound exhibits off-target inhibition on kinases such as FAK and AMPK, which could potentially modify additional cellular signaling pathways involved in autophagy and cell survival (Karmacharya & Jung, 2023).

Expected Effect:  
The central hypothesis posits that low-dose administration of SBI-0206965 in RPE cells will induce a selective modulation in autophagy initiation. Specifically, the drug is expected to downregulate the canonical autophagy pathway sufficiently to reduce potential overactivation, which may lead to cell stress or death, while simultaneously sparing or even indirectly promoting LC3-associated phagocytosis (LAP)—a noncanonical autophagy process crucial for phagolysosomal fusion and efficient POS degradation. RPE cells naturally exhibit high rates of autophagy due to the daily phagocytosis of shed photoreceptor outer segments; indeed, impaired autophagy in these cells has been directly linked to the pathogenesis of dry AMD, characterized by the accumulation of undigested cellular material and subsequent drusen formation (Pinelli et al., 2023; Blasiak et al., 2025). By partially inhibiting ULK1 activity, SBI-0206965 at submicromolar concentrations is hypothesized to reduce excessive bulk autophagy without compromising the LC3 recruitment to phagosomes—a hallmark of LAP—which is essential for maintaining the continuous renewal of photoreceptor components (Blasiak et al., 2024; Liton et al., 2023). In practical assay systems using cultured RPE cells, one would anticipate observing an increase in markers associated with LAP (such as enhanced LC3 lipidation on phagosomes and improved lysosomal fusion) alongside a controlled, rather than complete, suppression of canonical autophagy markers. Such an outcome would support enhanced POS degradation, ultimately alleviating one of the critical pathogenic processes in dry AMD. It is also expected that preserved or enhanced LAP activity would diminish the accumulation of toxic metabolites and lipofuscin, thereby contributing to improved retinal function and slowed disease progression (Limpert et al., 2018; Pinelli et al., 2025).

Overall Evaluation:  
SBI-0206965 presents several compelling advantages as a candidate for modulating autophagy in the context of dry age‐related macular degeneration. Its well-characterized mechanism of action as a ULK1 inhibitor allows for targeted interference with a key early step in canonical autophagy—an approach that has proven valuable in preclinical cancer models and, more recently, in studies addressing neuronal injury in the retina (Kocak et al., 2022; Sundermeyer, 2022). The hypothesis that low-dose SBI-0206965 can shift the cellular autophagy balance toward favoring LC3-associated phagocytosis (LAP) is particularly innovative, given that LAP is instrumental in the efficient degradation of photoreceptor outer segments by RPE cells. This mechanistic nuance, wherein canonical autophagy is dampened while LAP remains active due to its ULK1 independence, holds significant promise for restoring proper phagocytic and degradative functions in RPE cells—a process that is critically disrupted in dry AMD (Pinelli et al., 2023; Liton et al., 2023).

Despite these strengths, several challenges must be acknowledged. First, the off-target effects of SBI-0206965 on kinases such as FAK, AMPK, NUAK1, and MLK1 raise concerns regarding the potential for unintended modulation of other signaling pathways. Such off-target activities could compromise RPE cell viability or alter cellular stress responses that are vital for maintaining retinal homeostasis (Karmacharya & Jung, 2023). Second, while its potency as a ULK1 inhibitor is adequate for investigational use (with an IC₅₀ for ULK1 around 108 nM), newer and more selective ULK1 inhibitors have since been developed, and SBI-0206965 may not represent the most optimal chemical probe in terms of specificity and potency (Martin et al., 2018). Third, most of the existing data on SBI-0206965 derives from cancer cells and neuronal models, and there is a notable lack of direct experimental evidence that confirms its efficacy or safety in RPE cells or animal models of dry AMD. This gap underscores the necessity for carefully designed in vitro studies using RPE lines and subsequent in vivo AMD models to determine the appropriate dosing window that achieves the desired modulation of autophagy without detrimental suppression of essential bulk autophagy processes (Blasiak et al., 2025; Falcão et al., 2025). Additionally, the balance between inhibiting canonical autophagy and preserving LAP is delicately poised; over-inhibition of ULK1 might inadvertently impair necessary degradation pathways, potentially exacerbating cellular dysfunction rather than mitigating it (Pinelli et al., 2025; Si et al., 2023).

Nevertheless, the underlying rationale for repurposing SBI-0206965 in dry AMD is scientifically grounded. Autophagy dysfunction in RPE cells is a well-documented contributor to the progression of dry AMD, and therapeutically modulating this process represents a novel strategy with the potential to alleviate the accumulation of toxic cellular debris and drusen formation. Given that RPE cells face an immense phagocytic burden and are highly sensitive to disruptions in autophagic flux, agents that can fine-tune autophagy—as opposed to completely inhibiting it—are highly desirable. SBI-0206965, when employed at carefully titrated low doses, might provide the necessary modulation to reduce excessive canonical autophagy, thereby preventing the overaccumulation of autophagic vacuoles, while simultaneously preserving or even enhancing LC3-associated phagocytosis that is crucial for processing endogenous waste material (Pinelli et al., 2023; Sundermeyer, 2022; Blasiak et al., 2025).

In summary, SBI-0206965 exhibits considerable promise as an autophagy modulator with potential applications in the treatment of dry AMD due to its targeted inhibition of ULK1, a pathway central to canonical autophagy regulation in RPE cells. The hypothesis that low-dose administration can favorably shift the autophagic balance toward LAP, thereby improving POS degradation while preventing deleterious overactivation of bulk autophagy, is mechanistically sound and aligns with current understandings of RPE cell biology in AMD pathogenesis. However, the candidate’s moderate potency, documented off-target effects, and the paucity of data in ocular models warrant cautious optimism. Further investigation is required, including optimization of dosing strategies and rigorous assessment in preclinical RPE and AMD models, to fully ascertain the therapeutic window and clinical translatability of SBI-0206965 for dry AMD. Overall, while the candidate is not without its limitations, the innovative therapeutic approach it represents—targeted modulation of autophagy to enhance noncanonical phagocytic processes in RPE cells—merits continued exploration and could ultimately contribute to the development of a novel treatment strategy for dry age‐related macular degeneration (Kocak et al., 2022; Karmacharya & Jung, 2023; Sundermeyer, 2022; Blasiak et al., 2025).
