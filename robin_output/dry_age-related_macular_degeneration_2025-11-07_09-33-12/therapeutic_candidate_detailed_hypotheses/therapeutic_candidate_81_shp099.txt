Proposal for SHP099 (SHP2 inhibitor; SelleckChem Cat. No. S8653)

Overview of Therapeutic Candidate  
SHP099 is a fully synthetic, small-molecule allosteric inhibitor of the protein tyrosine phosphatase SHP2 (PTPN11) discovered by Novartis through a target-biased screening paradigm that enriched for modulators of full-length, autoinhibited SHP2 and counter-selected catalytic-site binders, followed by structure-based medicinal chemistry optimization to stabilize the closed SHP2 conformation rather than engage the conserved active site (chemical identity: 6-(4-amino-4-methylpiperidin-1-yl)-3-(2,3-dichlorophenyl)pyrazin-2-amine) (PDB: 5EHR) (Fortanet et al., 2016; Kong & Long, 2022; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021). In the 1.7 Å co-crystal, SHP099 occupies a central “tunnel” at the interface of the N-SH2, C-SH2, and PTP domains present only in the autoinhibited state; its aminopyrazine/amine motif forms hydrogen bonds to Arg111 and Phe113 in the SH2 linker and to Glu250 in the PTP domain, while a 2,3-dichlorophenyl moiety engages hydrophobic residues (Leu254, Gln257, Pro491, Gln495), thereby clamping the interdomain interface and disfavoring opening/activation (ligand volume ~262 Å3; tunnel ~464 Å3) (Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021). Biochemically, SHP099 is potent and selective (SHP2 enzymatic IC50 ≈ 0.070–0.071 µM; no detectable activity across panels of 21 phosphatases and 66 kinases, including SHP1) and translates to cellular pathway inhibition (p-ERK IC50 ≈ 0.250 µM), with high aqueous solubility, high permeability without efflux, and acceptable mouse oral exposure/bioavailability (~46%) (Fortanet et al., 2016; Kong & Long, 2022). Mechanistically, SHP099 functions as a “molecular glue” that stabilizes the closed, autoinhibited SHP2 conformation via a conformational selection mechanism; affinity is reduced for strongly activating SHP2 mutants (for example, E76K) that bias the open state, although high concentrations can re-close some mutants in vitro and in cells (Meem, 2022; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021).

SHP099 belongs to the class of SHP2 “tunnel-site” (site 1) allosteric inhibitors that stabilize the autoinhibited state by engaging the N-SH2/C-SH2/PTP interdomain pocket; dual allosteric occupancy (site 1 plus a distal latch, site 2) is structurally and biochemically validated and produces cooperative inhibition when SHP099 is combined with a site-2 ligand (for example SHP244 analogs), underscoring the druggability of multiple noncatalytic surfaces on SHP2 (Fodor et al., 2018; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021). Discovery and SAR established that the aminopyrazine ring amine is essential (Glu250 H-bond), the 4-amino-4-methylpiperidine motif is favored over piperazines for potency, and an ortho-chlorine on the phenyl ring is critical; a representative synthesis employs Suzuki–Miyaura coupling to install the dichlorophenyl substituent on a pyrazinamine core, followed by SNAr to append the Boc-protected 4-methylpiperidin-4-amine and final acid deprotection to the free base (Fortanet et al., 2016; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021). Class-level development moved the SHP099 pharmacophore into diverse chemotypes (for example, pyrimidinones and fused bicyclic pyrazolopyrimidinones) and into clinical candidates (for example, TNO155, RMC-4630, BBP-398), reflecting translational momentum for SHP2 allosteric inhibition (Kerr et al., 2021; Tojjari et al., 2023; Yuan et al., 2020).

Therapeutic History  
SHP099 established in vivo proof-of-concept for allosteric SHP2 inhibition in oncology. In an EGFR-amplified esophageal squamous carcinoma xenograft (KYSE-520), oral SHP099 produced dose-dependent pathway suppression (tumor p-ERK), antiproliferative effects, and tumor growth inhibition up to stasis at 100 mg/kg, supported by measurable exposure and oral bioavailability (Fortanet et al., 2016; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021). Additional preclinical efficacy includes activity in EGFR-driven xenografts and reduction of leukemic burden in FLT3-ITD–positive AML models after oral dosing, with no overt toxicity noted in those studies (Dempke et al., 2018). In glioblastoma models, SHP099 achieved brain exposure after 100 mg/kg oral dosing, reduced tumor growth and vascularity in orthotopic xenografts, and showed activity both as monotherapy and combined with temozolomide, demonstrating CNS penetration and antitumor pharmacology in mice (Mitra & Ayyannan, 2021). Class-wide, SHP2 allosteric inhibitors have shown single-agent and combination efficacy in RTK/RAS/MAPK-driven tumors, have been advanced into clinical studies across multiple solid tumors, and display mechanism-consistent biomarker modulation (for example, decreased p-ERK) in preclinical models (Kerr et al., 2021; Stanford & Bottini, 2023; Tojjari et al., 2023).

Within the materials provided here, there are no reports of SHP099 (or any SHP2 allosteric inhibitor) being used in dry age-related macular degeneration (dry AMD), retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) biology, photoreceptor outer segment (POS) phagocytosis models, or any ocular indication; multiple reviews focus on oncology/immuno-oncology and either enumerate preclinical tumor evidence or clinical oncology programs, without ocular/retinal data (Kerr et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2020; Yuan et al., 2020). Consequently, repurposing SHP099 for dry AMD would be a first-in-class application with no precedent in the supplied literature (Yuan et al., 2020).

Mechanism of Action  
SHP2 is maintained in an autoinhibited state by intramolecular engagement of the N-SH2 domain with the PTP catalytic domain; engagement of SH2 domains by bis-phosphotyrosyl motifs on receptors/adaptors relieves autoinhibition, allowing both catalytic and scaffolding functions that couple receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) to RAS activation and downstream MAPK/PI3K signaling (Fortanet et al., 2016; Kerr et al., 2021). SHP099 binds in a tunnel formed by the N-SH2, C-SH2, and PTP domains of autoinhibited SHP2 and locks this closed conformation: crystallography defines hydrogen bonds to Arg111 and Phe113 (SH2 linker) and Glu250 (PTP domain), and hydrophobic contacts to Leu254, Gln257, Pro491, and Gln495 that together stabilize the interface and preclude opening; pre-organization of Arg111 additionally supports a cationic–π interaction with SHP099’s dichlorophenyl ring (Fortanet et al., 2016; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021). Selectivity over SHP1 is rationalized structurally by differences in SH2-linker/tunnel geometry that preclude analogous Arg/Phe contacts, consistent with lack of SHP1 inhibition in broad profiling (Diop et al., 2022). Binding follows conformational selection, with lowered potency against mutants that favor the open state (for example, E76K, D61Y), though high concentrations or dual-site allosteric occupancy can re-close certain mutants and enhance inhibition (Fodor et al., 2018; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021). Class-level medicinal chemistry around SHP099 uncovered both optimization paths (for example, pyrimidinones, fused bicyclic scaffolds) and liabilities such as unexpected hERG inhibition in some derivatives and combination-dosing tolerability issues in mice, underscoring the need for careful safety pharmacology (Meem, 2022).

RPE POS phagocytosis is a ligand-directed, RTK-driven process with defined signaling architecture. Externalized phosphatidylserine on shed POS tips is opsonized (for example, by MFG-E8), engages apical αvβ5 integrin, and triggers a pathway in which MerTK (activated by Gas6 or Protein S) is essential for physiological engulfment in vivo; loss-of-function MERTK mutations in RCS rats or humans abolish POS internalization and cause rapid photoreceptor degeneration (Ghosh et al., 2024; Moran et al., 2022). At the cytoskeletal level, formation of the F-actin phagocytic cup beneath bound POS requires Rac1 activation and a concurrent decrease in RhoA/ROCK-mediated contractility; RhoA elevation blocks cup formation even when Rac1 is active, whereas ROCK inhibition is both necessary and sufficient to enable cup formation and POS internalization and can rescue engulfment and acidification in MerTK-deficient RPE ex vivo (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Mao & Finnemann, 2021). Mechanistically downstream of MerTK, Src is activated and (i) phosphorylates and activates focal adhesion kinase (FAK), recruiting the p130CAS/CRKII/DOCK180 module that activates Rac1, and (ii) phosphorylates GDP-dissociation inhibitor GDI1, promoting dissociation from Rab5 to facilitate Rab cycling needed for cup formation and early phagosome dynamics (Shelby et al., 2015). These pathways define measurable biochemical checkpoints (MerTK/Src/FAK phosphorylation, Rac1-GTP, RhoA/ROCK activity) and functional readouts (cup formation, internalization, phagolysosomal acidification) relevant to any attempt to modulate RPE phagocytosis pharmacologically (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Moran et al., 2022).

Expected Effect  
The working hypothesis is that SHP099 will inhibit SHP2 in RPE (biochemical IC50 ~70–75 nM), sustain MerTK phosphorylation, maintain Rac1-GTP, and thereby increase POS internalization. The supplied literature confirms that SHP099 potently and selectively inhibits SHP2 by stabilizing the autoinhibited state and blocks RTK→RAS/MAPK signaling in cells, but it does not demonstrate that SHP2 directly dephosphorylates MerTK in RPE or that SHP2 inhibition prolongs MerTK phosphorylation or increases RPE POS uptake (Fortanet et al., 2016; Kerr et al., 2021). Across systems, SHP2 acts as a positive transducer linking activated RTKs to RAS/ERK signaling; SHP2 allosteric inhibition decouples upstream RTKs from RAS activation, lowering RAS-GTP and p-ERK, which is consistent with a broad attenuation of RTK-driven outputs rather than prolonged receptor phosphorylation per se (Fortanet et al., 2016; Kerr et al., 2021). Within the provided ocular literature, the only experimentally demonstrated pharmacologic rescue of RPE POS uptake is acute ROCK inhibition, which operates downstream of MerTK to reduce actomyosin contractility and enable cup formation and internalization—even rescuing MerTK-deficient RPE ex vivo—whereas elevating RhoA blocks phagocytosis despite Rac1 activation (Mao & Finnemann, 2021). Thus, while SHP099 will almost certainly inhibit SHP2 in RPE if exposure is achieved, the predicted increase in POS internalization via prolonged MerTK phosphorylation and sustained Rac1-GTP is speculative and not supported by direct evidence in the materials provided here (Kerr et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2020).

If the assay readout is POS phagocytosis in primary or iPSC-derived human RPE, the mechanistic checkpoints most directly supported by the literature are: phosphorylation kinetics of MerTK and Src/FAK; Rac1-GTP elevation coincident with cup formation; RhoA/ROCK suppression; and functional measures of cup assembly, POS internalization, and phagolysosomal acidification, with a ROCK inhibitor as a validated positive control (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Mao & Finnemann, 2021; Moran et al., 2022). Ocular pharmacokinetics for SHP099 are unknown in the provided corpus; although brain exposure has been documented after oral dosing in mice, retinal/RPE exposure should not be inferred and would need to be measured for systemic or local (for example, intravitreal) dosing paradigms (Mitra & Ayyannan, 2021).

Overall Evaluation  
Strengths for repurposing consideration  
- Potent, selective, and structurally validated target engagement. SHP099 is characterized at atomic resolution (PDB 5EHR) with a coherent interaction network (Arg111, Phe113, Glu250; Leu254, Gln257, Pro491, Gln495) that rationalizes potency, selectivity versus SHP1, and conformational bias, increasing confidence that SHP2 inhibition will translate biochemically in RPE if drug exposure is achieved (Diop et al., 2022; Fortanet et al., 2016; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021).  
- Drug-like properties and in vivo activity. SHP099 exhibits favorable physicochemical properties (high solubility; Caco-2 permeability without efflux), oral bioavailability (~46% in mice), dose-dependent pathway modulation in tumors, and robust xenograft efficacy; CNS exposure after oral dosing has been demonstrated in mice, indicating trans-barrier distribution is feasible with adequate exposure (Fortanet et al., 2016; Mitra & Ayyannan, 2021; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021).  
- Class-level translational experience. Multiple SHP2 allosteric inhibitors derived from the SHP099 tunnel pharmacophore have entered clinical testing across solid tumors, enabling leverage of class pharmacology, safety learning, and combination strategies if ocular repurposing proceeds (Kerr et al., 2021; Stanford & Bottini, 2023; Tojjari et al., 2023).

Key weaknesses and risks specific to dry AMD/RPE phagocytosis  
- Mechanistic gap in RPE. The premise that SHP2 dephosphorylates MerTK to terminate phagocytic signaling in RPE, and that SHP2 inhibition will prolong MerTK phosphorylation and sustain Rac1 activation, is not supported by the supplied literature; no direct data link SHP2 to MerTK regulation in RPE or demonstrate SHP099-induced increases in POS uptake (Kerr et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2020). In contrast, RhoA/ROCK suppression is a validated, decisive permissive event for cup formation/internalization and can rescue MerTK deficiency, suggesting that targeting ROCK remains a higher-confidence pharmacological route to enhance POS uptake (Mao & Finnemann, 2021).  
- Potential on-target countereffects on trophic RTK signaling. SHP2 broadly transduces RTK signals to RAS/ERK; allosteric SHP2 inhibition reduces RAS-GTP/p-ERK by disconnecting RTKs from downstream pathways, which could dampen beneficial RTK-mediated homeostatic signaling in RPE and choroid (for example, PDGFR, c-MET, IGF1R) even if MerTK phosphorylation were transiently increased, necessitating careful evaluation of RPE viability and stress responses in addition to phagocytosis endpoints (Fortanet et al., 2016; Kerr et al., 2021).  
- Ocular PK/tissue exposure unknown. There is no reported ocular distribution for SHP099 in the materials provided; CNS penetration does not ensure retinal/RPE exposure, and systemic dosing to achieve ocular levels may be limited by plasma protein binding, efflux, or tolerability, while intravitreal dosing introduces local safety and formulation challenges that would require de novo assessment (Mitra & Ayyannan, 2021).  
- Class liabilities and context-dependent potency. Medicinal chemistry around SHP099 identified derivatives with unexpected hERG activity and tolerability issues in some combination regimens; while not demonstrated specifically for SHP099 here, this flags a class risk requiring prospective cardiac and ocular safety pharmacology. Conformational-selection binding also means potency varies with SHP2 activation state, which is undefined in aging human RPE and could shift under inflammatory or oxidative stress present in dry AMD (Meem, 2022; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021).  
- Lack of precedent in ocular models. No ocular, retinal, or RPE data for SHP099 or related allosteric SHP2 inhibitors are available in the supplied literature, increasing translational uncertainty for this repurposing (Yuan et al., 2020).

Program decision and de-risking experiments  
On the basis of the information provided, SHP099 is not yet a compelling repurposing candidate for dry AMD without foundational mechanistic validation in RPE. The central claim—that SHP2 inhibition prolongs MerTK phosphorylation and increases POS internalization—remains speculative in the absence of RPE data (Yuan et al., 2020). If the team elects to explore this hypothesis, a gated, mechanism-first workplan is advised:

- Define pathway engagement in RPE. Confirm SHP2 expression and activation state during phagocytosis in primary or iPSC-derived human RPE; assess whether SHP2 associates with MerTK/Src/FAK complexes upon POS challenge by co-immunoprecipitation or proximity assays (Shelby et al., 2015).  
- Biochemical time-courses ± SHP099. Quantify MerTK phosphorylation kinetics, Src and FAK activation, Rac1-GTP, and RhoA/ROCK activity after POS addition with a SHP099 concentration range bracketing its biochemical potency in enzyme assays; benchmark effect sizes against a ROCK inhibitor positive control, which is validated to rescue phagocytosis even in MerTK-deficient contexts (Mao & Finnemann, 2021).  
- Functional phagocytosis assays. Measure POS binding, F-actin cup formation, internalization, and phagolysosomal acidification under the same conditions, using established assays and timepoints aligned to the diurnal phagocytic peak where applicable (Lieffrig et al., 2023; Moran et al., 2022).  
- Ocular PK and safety. Establish retinal/RPE exposure after systemic and (if considered) intravitreal dosing, and perform ocular tolerability assessments (inflammation, ERG, histopathology) to identify potential on- or off-target liabilities early, mindful of class risks noted in optimization campaigns (Meem, 2022; Mitra & Ayyannan, 2021).

In summary, SHP099 is a potent, selective, and well-characterized SHP2 allosteric inhibitor with robust oncology pharmacology and drug-like properties, but the case for repurposing to dry AMD by enhancing RPE POS phagocytosis is unsubstantiated in the provided literature and carries meaningful mechanistic and translational risks. Prioritizing direct RPE mechanistic studies and ocular PK/tolerability before any disease-model investment is the appropriate next step; in parallel, ROCK pathway modulation remains the most evidence-backed pharmacologic lever to augment phagocytosis in this system and should serve as a positive control and a benchmark for effect size (Fortanet et al., 2016; Mao & Finnemann, 2021; Tripathi & Ayyannan, 2021).
