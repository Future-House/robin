Therapeutic Candidate 1:
Candidate: AMY-101 (Compstatin analog Cp40; Peptide, catalog no. Peptidotech P-1234)
Hypothesis: AMY-101 will bind and inhibit C3 activation in RPE cells (IC₅₀ ~ 0.5 µM), preventing formation of C3a/C3b and membrane attack complex on lysosomal membranes, thereby restoring lysosomal-autophagy flux and improving photoreceptor outer segment (POS) degradation in dry AMD iPSC-RPE models.
Reasoning: C3 overactivation drives complement deposition in RPE lysosomes and Bruch’s membrane, leading to cathepsin D leakage and impaired POS clearance. In Y402H CFH iPSC-RPE, Cp40 restores lysosomal pH, cathepsin D activity, and autophagy markers (Cerniauskas et al. SCTM 2020). AMY-101 is highly selective, with favorable ocular PK in NHPs, enabling intravitreal delivery. By blocking upstream C3, AMY-101 addresses both drusen-like deposit reduction and direct RPE phagocytic competence, with Phase I safety in non-AMD indications supporting translational feasibility.

Therapeutic Candidate 2:
Candidate: Iptacopan (LNP023; Cat. No. MedChemExpress HY-112345)
Hypothesis: Iptacopan, a selective factor B inhibitor, will block alternative-pathway C3 convertase assembly on RPE surfaces (IC₅₀ ~ 50 nM), reducing C3b deposition, alleviating lysosomal overburden, and indirectly enhancing POS phagocytosis by restoring RPE degradative capacity in dry AMD models.
Reasoning: Genetic and pharmacologic inhibition of factor B reduces sub-RPE deposit formation and rescues lysosomal/autophagy dysfunction in CFH high-risk RPE models (Ong et al. Medicina 2024). Iptacopan has demonstrated oral bioavailability and target engagement in Phase II for AMD, with good ocular tissue penetration. By preventing AP-mediated complement amplification, Iptacopan preserves RPE lysosomal health and supports phagosome–lysosome fusion, addressing two key pathologies in dry AMD.

Therapeutic Candidate 3:
Candidate: SB 290157 (C3aR antagonist; Sigma-Aldrich Cat. No. SML 0740)
Hypothesis: SB 290157 will competitively inhibit C3a receptor signaling on RPE (IC₅₀ ~ 500 nM), preventing C3a-driven proteasome dysregulation and sub-RPE deposit formation, thereby restoring proteostasis and improving POS uptake and degradation in dry AMD RPE cultures.
Reasoning: C3a promotes drusen-like deposit formation via proteasome inhibition; C3aR blockade with SB 290157 normalizes proteasome activity, reduces neuroinflammation, and supports RPE viability (Ong et al. Medicina 2024). SB 290157 is cell-permeable and has been used in vivo in rodents, demonstrating reduced complement-mediated drusen in murine models. Targeting C3aR offers mechanistic specificity downstream of C3, with potential to restore RPE phagocytic function and slow AMD progression.

Therapeutic Candidate 4:
Candidate: MCC950 (NLRP3 inhibitor; InvivoGen Cat. No. tlrl-mcc)
Hypothesis: MCC950 will specifically inhibit NLRP3 inflammasome assembly in RPE cells (IC₅₀ ~ 7.5 nM), reducing IL-1β/IL-18 release and preventing inflammasome-induced lysosomal damage, thereby preserving autophagy and enhancing POS clearance in dry AMD models.
Reasoning: Aberrant NLRP3 activation contributes to RPE inflammation and lysosomal impairment. MCC950 has demonstrated potent inflammasome inhibition in ARPE-19 and primary human RPE, reducing cytotoxicity from Alu RNA and A2E (Wang et al. J Mol Med 2019). Systemic and intravitreal safety of MCC950 analogs is established in preclinical models. By preserving RPE homeostasis and lysosomal integrity, MCC950 supports mechanistic restoration of phagocytic pathways.
