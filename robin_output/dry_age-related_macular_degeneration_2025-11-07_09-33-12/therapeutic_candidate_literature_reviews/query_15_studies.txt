Query: Studies elucidating how complement dysregulation in the subretinal space impairs RPE phagocytosis and contributes to drusen formation, with target validation in dry AMD specimens

Complement dysregulation in the subretinal space disrupts RPE phagocytosis by impairing the normal processing of cellular debris. Loss or reduced function of key regulatory proteins such as CFH, CD46, and CD55—often exacerbated by genetic variants like CFH Y402H—compromises Factor I–mediated inactivation of C3b, leading to increased opsonization that misdirects clearance away from RPE-mediated phagocytosis (behnke2022ifnβimmunomodulationand pages 31-33, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 10-11). Enhanced C3 convertase activity results in elevated C3a and C5a production, which recruits and activates microglia and macrophages; these immune cells, in turn, sequester debris and contribute to chronic inflammation at the RPE–Bruch’s membrane interface (biber2023thecomplementsystem pages 34-35, jiao2018theroleof pages 35-40). Oxidative stress and lipofuscin accumulation further exacerbate the situation by downregulating local complement inhibitors and promoting membrane attack complex deposition, thereby impairing lysosomal degradation within RPE cells (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 20-21, jiao2018theroleof pages 40-46). Proteomic and immunohistochemical studies from dry AMD specimens have validated these mechanisms by demonstrating co-localization of complement components and altered regulator expression within drusen deposits (lin2018theroleof pages 75-78, jiao2018theroleof pages 187-191).

Complement dysregulation impairs the RPE’s ability to clear debris, which promotes inflammation and drusen formation in dry AMD. These findings validate targets such as CFH dysfunction and excessive complement activation as central to disease pathology.
