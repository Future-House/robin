Query: Literature on complement pathway inhibitors or modulators (e.g., C3, C5, factor H) that indirectly improve RPE phagocytosis and reduce drusen burden in dry AMD preclinical models

Multiple preclinical lines of evidence indicate that complement modulation can indirectly restore RPE degradative capacity (lysosome/autophagy) and reduce drusen-like burden. In an iPSC-RPE model from AMD patients carrying CFH Y402H, unchecked C3 turnover led to lysosomal C5b‑9 accrual, membrane fragility, cathepsin D leakage, and impaired lysosomal function; pharmacologic C3 blockade with the compstatin analogue Cp40 relieved lysosomal overburden and restored lysosomal/autophagy function, consistent with improved intracellular degradative capacity and reduced drusen-like material (i.e., indirect enhancement of RPE phagocytosis) (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 7-9, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 24-25). In human RPE cultures, compstatin-family C3 inhibitors blocked complement activation driven by drusen-like deposits, and in nonhuman primates with early-onset macular degeneration a compstatin C3 inhibitor suppressed or reversed drusen formation, providing direct preclinical support for C3-targeting to reduce sub-RPE deposits (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 25-27, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 9-10). Complement downstream mediators reinforce this mechanism: C3a drives sub‑RPE deposit formation via proteasome dysregulation, whereas C3aR blockade prevents these deposits, increases proteasome activity, and alleviates neuroinflammation with functional rescue in models, aligning with improved proteostasis that supports RPE phagocytic competence (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 7-9, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13).

Targeting the alternative pathway also affects deposit biology. Factor B deletion or pharmacologic inhibition normalizes dysregulated complement signaling and reduces sub‑RPE deposits (noting sex-dependent effects), and TNF‑α is identified as a regulator of factor B production, pointing to upstream AP control as drusen‑modifying (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 10-11, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13). By contrast, C5/MAC pathway modulation reduces inflammasome priming and protects retinal neurons, but genetic C5 ablation does not consistently prevent sub‑RPE deposits, indicating less direct impact on drusen burden across models (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 9-10, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 27-28). Mechanistically, complement overactivation impairs RPE lysosomal/autophagic flux and MAC degradation, linking complement control to restoration of RPE clearance pathways (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 12-13). Animal data underscore AP involvement: C3 and factor B are essential for drusen formation in certain murine models, supporting upstream targeting to limit deposits (hammadi2023bruch’smembranea pages 10-12).

Collectively, the strongest preclinical evidence for indirect improvement of RPE phagocytosis and drusen reduction centers on C3-directed inhibition (compstatins) and factor B blockade, with supportive mechanistic data for C3a/C3aR antagonism. C5-directed strategies mainly attenuate inflammation but show inconsistent effects on drusen formation across models (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 25-27, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 9-10, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 7-9, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 10-11, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 27-28, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 12-13, hammadi2023bruch’smembranea pages 10-12).

In preclinical dry-AMD–relevant systems, C3 inhibition (compstatins) restores RPE lysosomal/autophagic function and suppresses drusen-like pathology, and in primates reduces or reverses drusen formation. Alternative pathway targeting via factor B inhibition reduces sub‑RPE deposits, whereas C5/MAC blockade primarily dampens inflammatory signaling with variable effects on deposits (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 25-27, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 9-10, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 10-11, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 27-28). These data support upstream complement modulation as the more reliable route to indirectly enhance RPE phagocytic competence and limit drusen burden (ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 7-9, ong2024thecomplementsystem pages 11-13, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 12-13, hammadi2023bruch’smembranea pages 10-12).
