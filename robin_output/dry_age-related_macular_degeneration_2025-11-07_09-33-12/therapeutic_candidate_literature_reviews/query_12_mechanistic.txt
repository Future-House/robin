Query: Mechanistic studies dissecting how oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, or lipofuscin accumulation in RPE cells lead to defective outer segment clearance in dry AMD models

Mechanistic studies in dry AMD models demonstrate that oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and lipofuscin accumulation converge to disrupt the efficient clearance of photoreceptor outer segments (POS) by retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12). Excess ROS generated during phagocytosis and mitochondrial leakage oxidatively damages macromolecules, impairs proteostasis, and undermines the autophagy–lysosome system needed for POS degradation (egbejimi2022decipheringunderlyingmechanisms pages 26-32, toma2021oxidativeandnitrosative pages 4-5). Mitochondrial dysfunction characterized by mtDNA damage, ATP depletion, and imbalanced fission/fusion further compromises energy-dependent processes such as phagosome trafficking and lysosomal fusion (ruan2021agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 16-17, lenin2023dysfunctionalautophagyproteostasis pages 2-4). In parallel, lipofuscin accumulation, particularly its component A2E, acts as a photosensitizer that both amplifies ROS production and directly impairs lysosomal enzyme activity by raising lysosomal pH and interfering with lipid metabolism (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12, wang2019autophagydysfunctioncellular pages 2-4). These interrelated events create a feed‐forward loop: increased oxidative stress exacerbates mitochondrial and lysosomal dysfunction, leading to further accumulation of lipofuscin, and ultimately culminating in defective POS clearance and RPE degeneration (ruan2021agerelatedmaculardegeneration pages 6-7, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 9-10).

Defective outer segment clearance in dry AMD is driven by a network of oxidative, mitochondrial, and lipofuscin-mediated insults that compromise RPE degradative capacity. This cascade of events ultimately leads to energy deficits, impaired autophagy, and RPE dysfunction.
