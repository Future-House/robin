Query: Studies detailing high-throughput small-molecule screening strategies using the photoreceptor outer segment phagocytosis assay in RPE cells, focusing on assay design, reproducibility metrics, hit validation workflows, and adaptation for drug discovery

High-throughput screens based on photoreceptor outer segment (POS) phagocytosis in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells are designed by culturing physiologically relevant human embryonic stem cell–derived RPE in a miniaturized 384-well format, typically seeding 45,000 cells per well and maturing them for 13 days before challenging with sonicated POS at approximately 10^6/mL for 3 hours to capture receptor–ligand–dependent uptake (karlUnknownyearsvenschreiterkaterina pages 1-2, schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 1-2). Positive controls using MFGE8 and GAS6 (combined at 2.5 mg/mL) as well as DMSO vehicle controls define assay windows and modulate external ligand conditions to bias hit discovery toward modulators independent of MERTK (karlUnknownyearsvenschreiterkaterina pages 1-2).

Reproducibility is achieved through empirical optimization of cell seeding density and POS preparation, coupled with automated multi-field imaging and image analysis that generate quantitative metrics including POS per cell counts, z-scores, and coefficients of variation—with reported intraplate and interplate variations of around 10% and 20%, respectively (karlUnknownyearsvenschreiterkaterina pages 3-5, karlUnknownyearsvenschreiterkaterina pages 5-8, karlUnknownyearsvenschreiterkaterina pages 27-28). Hits from the primary screen are revalidated by repurchasing compounds as powders and retesting them in dose–response experiments in both the 384-well and orthogonal transwell formats, while corroborative assays such as EDTA washes, FACS, and western blotting are deployed to distinguish internalized from surface-bound POS and to confirm activity in disease-relevant settings such as MERTK mutant RPE (schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 11-13, schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 13-14, schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 2-3, schreiter2020ahumanretinal pages 1-2).

This integrated phenotypic, target-agnostic strategy is well suited for drug discovery and repurposing, as it combines biologically relevant screening conditions with robust reproducibility metrics and multi-tiered orthogonal validation workflows (karlUnknownyearsvenschreiterkaterina pages 1-2).

This screening platform combines high-content imaging with rigorous controls and validation strategies to ensure robust hit identification and reproducibility. It provides a scalable, clinically relevant tool for drug discovery in retinal degenerative diseases.
