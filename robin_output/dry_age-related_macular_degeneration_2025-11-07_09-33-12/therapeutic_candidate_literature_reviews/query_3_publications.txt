Query: Publications describing small-molecule modulators of autophagy or lysosomal biogenesis (e.g., TFEB activators) that specifically enhance RPE outer segment degradation, with efficacy demonstrated in patient-derived or animal models of dry AMD

Trehalose, a small‐molecule TFEB activator, has been shown to rescue autophagy deficits in RPE cells exposed to oxidative stress and to modulate photoreceptor outer segment (POS) recycling in vitro (abokyi2025agerelateddrivingmechanisms pages 8-9, abokyi2025agerelateddrivingmechanisms pages 7-8). In these studies, trehalose restored lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy‐dependent defenses, yet the efficacy was demonstrated using hydroquinone‐induced injury models and an animal model of inherited macular degeneration rather than in classical, patient-derived dry AMD models. Similarly, Ghosh et al. employed both AAV2-TFEB gene therapy and trehalose treatment in CFH mutant iPSC-RPE and mouse models with lysosomal deficits, demonstrating restoration of CLEAR network gene expression and lysosomal function; however, direct assays of enhanced RPE outer segment degradation were not described (ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 6-6, ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 6-8). Additional mechanistic studies using the AKT2 inhibitor CCT128930 showed a reduction in TFEB phosphorylation—a surrogate marker for improved lysosomal competence—with implications for POS degradation, yet these data stop short of providing a direct functional readout in relevant dry AMD models (ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 1-2, ghosh2024theakt2sirt5tfebpathway pages 4-6).

These publications collectively support that small-molecule activators of TFEB can restore lysosomal and autophagy function in RPE cells. However, direct evidence demonstrating enhanced RPE outer segment degradation in patient-derived or well-established animal models of dry AMD remains insufficient.
