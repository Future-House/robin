Query: Publications on cross-talk between Bruch’s membrane alterations and RPE phagocytosis dynamics in dry AMD, focusing on extracellular matrix influences on receptor-mediated uptake

Bruch’s membrane (BrM) undergoes significant extracellular matrix alterations in dry AMD that influence RPE receptor‐mediated uptake and phagocytosis. ECM remodeling—manifested as age‐related thickening, altered collagen cross-linking, and changes in glycosaminoglycan composition (notably heparan sulfate and its proteoglycans)—impairs diffusion of nutrients and the clearance of oxidized photoreceptor outer segment (POS) debris, thereby increasing the phagocytic load on RPE cells (betts2024molecularmechanismsof pages 23-27, betts2024molecularmechanismsof pages 19-23, somasundaran2020retinalpigmentepithelium pages 1-2). These compositional changes also modify the availability and presentation of bridging molecules such as MFG-E8, Gas6, and Protein S that are essential for linking POS phosphatidylserine to RPE surface receptors like MerTK and integrin αvβ5 (betts2024molecularmechanismsof pages 27-30, kwon2020phagocytosisbythe pages 3-5).

Furthermore, the basal adhesion of RPE cells to BrM via integrins (α3β1, α6β1, and αvβ3) is critical for maintaining proper cell polarity and receptor localization; disrupted ECM signals may lead to altered receptor trafficking and compromised phagocytic efficiency (sorrentino2025stemcelltherapy pages 2-4, sorrentino2025stemcelltherapy pages 4-5). In addition, extracellular vesicle dynamics influenced by ECM alterations may further modulate receptor recycling and downstream signaling, exacerbating RPE dysfunction (martins2024contributionofextracellular pages 7-8, martins2024contributionofextracellular pages 9-11).

Alterations in BrM ECM thereby directly affect ligand availability, receptor localization, and signaling cascades that govern RPE phagocytosis. This cross-talk between ECM remodeling and receptor-mediated uptake represents a critical mechanism underlying dry AMD pathogenesis.
