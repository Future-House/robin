Query: Investigations into lysosomal enzyme deficiencies (e.g., cathepsins D and L) or acidification defects in RPE cells from dry AMD patients, and attempts to restore phagocytic function

Investigations in dry AMD models have revealed lysosomal defects in RPE cells, most notably a decrease in cathepsin D protein levels and enzymatic activity, along with its mislocalization into drusen‐like deposits, which compromise autophagosome maturation and degradative capacity (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 4-7, cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2). In contrast, evidence for cathepsin L deficiencies is mainly derived from in vitro models such as AGE‐exposed RPE and aging studies, where reductions in cathepsin L protein and activity correlate with impaired photoreceptor outer segment (POS) degradation (sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 199-202, sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 213-216). Lysosomal acidification defects have been reported in models of dry AMD; A2E accumulation in RPE cells induces a rise in lysosomal pH that reduces the activity of lysosomal enzymes, thereby impairing phagocytic turnover (choi2024protectiveeffectof pages 29-36, si2023theroleof pages 5-7). 

Several experimental interventions have been pursued to restore phagocytic function. Pharmacologic complement modulation with agents such as the compstatin analogue Cp40 has been shown to relieve lysosomal overburden and restore lysosomal function in high-risk RPE cells (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2, boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 13-14). In addition, reacidification strategies employing zinc ionophores have successfully restored lysosomal pH and improved degradative capacity in RPE models (choi2024protectiveeffectof pages 29-36). Although gene therapy targeting cathepsin D deficiency has demonstrated efficacy in animal models (boya2023lysosomesinretinal pages 13-14), translational efforts in patient-derived RPE remain to be clearly established (mesquita2021purificationandcharacterization pages 24-27, voisin2019cathepsinbphdependent pages 9-10).

These findings indicate that deficiencies in cathepsin D and acidification defects contribute significantly to phagocytic impairment in dry AMD. Restorative approaches such as complement modulation, reacidification, and gene therapy offer promising avenues to rescue lysosomal dysfunction and normalize phagocytosis in affected RPE cells.
