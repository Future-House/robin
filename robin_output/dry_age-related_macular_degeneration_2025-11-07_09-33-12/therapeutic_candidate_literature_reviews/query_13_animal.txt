Query: Animal model papers (e.g., MerTK−/− rats/mice, Ccl2−/−/Cx3cr1−/− mice) that recapitulate RPE phagocytosis deficits and deposit accumulation resembling human dry AMD

Multiple animal models reproduce RPE phagocytosis failure and deposit accumulation pertinent to dry AMD pathogenesis. The RCS rat, carrying a Mertk loss-of-function, shows a cell-autonomous RPE engulfment defect that leads to early subretinal outer segment (OS) debris accumulation and rapid photoreceptor loss; mechanistically, TAM signaling links MerTK to αvβ5-dependent binding and to the ligands Gas6 and Protein S that stimulate OS uptake (ghosh2024intheeyes pages 2-3, nandrot2014animalmodelsin pages 6-7, nandrot2014animalmodelsin pages 3-6). MerTK-deficient mouse lines exhibit a dramatic loss of the diurnal phagocytic burst, increased bisretinoids (A2E), and early RPE inflammation, with severity modulated by genetic background and partial compensation by TYRO3 (ghosh2024intheeyes pages 8-9, lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 19-20). Disrupting apical OS binding and synchronization recapitulates age-linked deposits: Itgb5−/− mice lack the phagocytic peak and develop lipofuscin accumulation and vision loss, while MFG-E8−/− phenocopies rhythm loss without the aging phenotype; ANXA2−/− delays downstream signaling and traps phagosomes apically (nandrot2014animalmodelsin pages 1-3, lieffrig2023clearancephagocytosisby pages 17-19, nandrot2014animalmodelsin pages 3-6).

Models of phagosome processing/lysosomal failure generate basal deposits and lipofuscin/A2E: Cathepsin D mutants (mcd/mcd, mcd2/mcd2) accrue indigestible OS material with basal deposits; βA3/A1-crystallin and Mreg knockouts reduce CatD activity and delay POS degradation with age-associated lipofuscin/A2E buildup; CD36−/− mice accumulate oxLDL and thicken Bruch’s membrane; neprilysin−/− and oxidative-stress models (Sod1−/−, RPE Sod2 knockdown) produce subretinal deposits, BlamD/BlinD, Bruch’s membrane thickening, and photoreceptor degeneration (ramkumar2010retinalultrastructureof pages 10-11, nandrot2014animalmodelsin pages 3-6, ramkumar2010retinalultrastructureof pages 4-5, ramkumar2010retinalultrastructureof pages 8-10).

Immune-chemokine models reinforce the deposit phenotype. Ccl2−/−/Cx3cr1−/− mice develop early, high-penetrance drusen-like lesions, Bruch’s membrane thickening, RPE hypopigmentation/vacuolation, photoreceptor OS disorganization/atrophy, elevated A2E, and complement deposition, with subretinal microglia/macrophage “foam cells” implicated in deposit genesis; phenotype is strain-dependent (tuo2007murineccl2cx3cr1deficiency pages 2-4, tuo2007murineccl2cx3cr1deficiency pages 4-5, ramkumar2010retinalultrastructureof pages 7-8, raoul2010ccl2ccr2andcx3cl1cx3cr1 pages 2-4, ramkumar2010retinalultrastructureof pages 8-10, hagbilevi2016retinalphenotypefollowing pages 7-8). Aged Ccl2−/− and Ccr2−/− mice show autofluorescent subretinal macrophages and RPE/photoreceptor atrophy without CNV, consistent with para-inflammatory clearance deficits (chen2011dysregulationinretinal pages 8-9). Species constraints mean murine deposits can be apical/subretinal or intracellular rather than classic basal drusen, underscoring the need for EM-based adjudication (ramkumar2010retinalultrastructureof pages 3-4, ramkumar2010retinalultrastructureof pages 1-2).

These papers demonstrate that impairing RPE OS binding/engulfment (MerTK/αvβ5 axis) or phagosome-lysosome processing is sufficient to drive lipofuscin/bisretinoid accumulation and subretinal/basal deposits with RPE and photoreceptor degeneration. Chemokine-deficient models add a complementary route via impaired phagocyte trafficking/clearance to produce drusen-like lesions and Bruch’s membrane pathology (nandrot2014animalmodelsin pages 6-7, nandrot2014animalmodelsin pages 3-6, tuo2007murineccl2cx3cr1deficiency pages 2-4).
