Query: Single-cell sequencing or spatial transcriptomics papers mapping RPE cellular heterogeneity and identifying subpopulations with compromised phagocytic gene expression signatures in dry AMD

Multiple single‐cell transcriptomic studies employing iPSC‐derived RPE from geographic atrophy (GA) patients versus controls have robustly mapped cellular heterogeneity, identifying discrete subpopulations that range from progenitor to mature RPE states (senabouth2021transcriptomicandproteomic pages 4-8, senabouth2022transcriptomicandproteomic pages 3-4). These studies profile canonical RPE functions—including phagocytosis—with several reporting expression of phagocytosis‐associated genes (e.g., GULP1, MFGE8, CD36) and noting differential expression between GA and control samples (senabouth2021transcriptomicandproteomic pages 48-54, senabouth2021transcriptomicandproteomic pages 8-11). However, despite these extensive analyses, none of the provided excerpts explicitly demonstrate that any specific RPE subpopulation exhibits a compromised or reduced phagocytic gene expression signature in dry AMD (senabouth2021transcriptomicandproteomic pages 4-8, senabouth2021transcriptomicandproteomic pages 14-17). Spatial transcriptomics data, as illustrated in large-scale atlas approaches of RPE and choroid, offer high-resolution mapping that could potentially resolve functionally impaired subpopulations, but the current evidence does not include explicit findings of impaired phagocytosis (huang2023dynamichumanretinal pages 1-2).

The literature shows strong evidence for RPE heterogeneity and differential expression of RPE functional genes in dry AMD, yet explicit identification of a subpopulation with compromised phagocytic gene expression remains unreported. Future studies combining spatial transcriptomics with functional validation are needed to address this gap.
