Query: Transcriptomic and proteomic studies profiling RPE cells from dry AMD donors versus healthy controls, highlighting dysregulated pathways directly linked to impaired outer segment phagocytosis

Multiple studies using both transcriptomic and proteomic profiling of RPE cells from dry AMD donors versus healthy controls have documented dysregulation of pathways essential for outer segment (POS) phagocytosis. Transcriptomic analyses reveal significant alterations in key signaling cascades, such as PI3K/Akt, mTOR, and MEK/ERK, which are crucial for coordinating cytoskeletal remodeling, membrane trafficking, and GTPase regulation during phagocytosis (voisin2022proteinsassociatedwith pages 7-10, voisin2022proteinsassociatedwith pages 1-2). In parallel, proteomic studies indicate mitochondrial dysfunction characterized by altered oxidative phosphorylation, reduced ATP production, and imbalances in retinoid and lipid metabolic enzymes, thereby compromising the energy supply required for efficient POS processing (beranovagiorgianni2018proteomicsofhuman pages 6-8, senabouth2022transcriptomicandproteomic pages 6-8). Additionally, changes in extracellular matrix components and adhesion molecules suggest impaired interactions between RPE cells and photoreceptor outer segments, while dysregulated autophagy and complement pathway components may further inhibit proper phagosome maturation (senabouth2021transcriptomicandproteomic pages 17-20, beranovagiorgianni2018proteomicsofhuman pages 5-6). 

These integrated molecular disturbances provide a mechanistic underpinning for the impaired phagocytic clearance observed in dry AMD. Defects in signaling, energy metabolism, and cellular adhesion collectively contribute to reduced outer segment phagocytosis by the RPE.
