Assay Overview: This assay employs RPE cell models—such as ARPE-19, primary RPE, or iPSC‐derived RPE from AMD patients—to mimic key aspects of dry AMD pathogenesis. Cells are exposed to an acute oxidative challenge using either hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) or A2E combined with light to induce ROS generation. Fluorescent probes like CellROX quantify intracellular ROS levels, while viability assays (e.g., MTT or CellTiter-Glo) assess cell survival, providing a rapid, quantitative measure of antioxidant efficacy (chang2014thegenerationof pages 1-2, chang2014thegenerationof pages 5-7).

Biomedical Evidence: Oxidative injury is a central mechanism in RPE degeneration during dry AMD. Clinically, AMD is associated with reduced antioxidant defenses, increased lipid peroxidation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and elevated oxidative biomarkers. The assay’s induction of ROS with H₂O₂ or A2E+light directly parallels the photo-oxidative stress and chronic oxidative burden observed in patients (kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 7-8, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 13-14).

Previous Use: Similar oxidative stress assays have been leveraged in drug discovery to identify compounds that protect RPE cells from oxidative damage. Notably, high-throughput screenings using these models identified curcumin and ciclopirox olamine as agents that enhance viability and reduce ROS levels in RPE cultures (chang2014thegenerationof pages 1-2, cai2019highthroughputscreeningidentifies pages 12-13).

Overall Evaluation: The assay’s strength lies in its biological relevance and quantitative, high-throughput potential for initial screening. However, acute oxidative challenges may not fully capture the chronic, multifactorial nature of dry AMD pathology, and additional models that incorporate prolonged stress and tissue complexity could be needed (chang2014thegenerationof pages 5-7, kushwah2023oxidativestressand pages 7-8).

This assay is a valuable preliminary tool for evaluating antioxidant therapeutics in AMD. Complementary models addressing chronic oxidative stress and retinal microenvironment interactions would further strengthen drug discovery efforts.
