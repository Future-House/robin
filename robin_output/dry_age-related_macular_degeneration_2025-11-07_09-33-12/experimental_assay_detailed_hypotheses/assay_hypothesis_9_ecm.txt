Assay Overview: An ECM stiffness barrier assay would culture human RPE as a polarized monolayer on substrates with defined mechanics and ligand presentation—either tunable hydrogels (e.g., polyacrylamide/PEGDA) spanning the aged Bruch’s membrane (BrM)-relevant range or nitrite‑crosslinked/aged ECM—to quantify barrier integrity (TEER, fluorescent‑dextran paracellular flux), junctional organization (ZO‑1 immunolocalization), and mechanotransduction endpoints (actin stress fibers, focal adhesion/integrin clustering), alongside RPE functions (POS phagocytosis, polarized VEGF/PEDF) and viability/inflammatory output (IL‑6/MCP‑1) (schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 5-6, nguyen2025conjugationofadhesion pages 13-17, dujardin2024developmentofa pages 271-271, dujardin2024developmentofaa pages 271-271, white2017theinfluenceof pages 1-6). Cell sources should prioritize post‑mitotic hiPSC‑RPE or hfRPE matured 2–3 months to achieve high TER, with ARPE‑19 reserved for mechanistic screening and integrin‑ligand optimization (dujardin2024developmentofa pages 271-271, dujardin2024developmentofaa pages 271-271, nguyen2025conjugationofadhesion pages 13-17). Laminin isoform/density should be specified because LM‑511/551 versus LM‑332 differentially tunes contractility/viscoelasticity and downstream function (schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 5-6, schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 1-5).

Biomedical Evidence: Aging/AMD remodel BrM—thickening, compositional shifts, crosslinking, drusen, and loss of elasticity—leading to increased stiffness and altered diffusion at the RPE–ECM interface that engages integrin‑based focal adhesions and actomyosin tension to regulate junctional permeability (TEER/flux) (schimmel2024theinfluenceofa pages 5-6, schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 5-6). Substrate stiffness modulates RPE spread, stiffness, traction, focal adhesion number/size via molecular clutch dynamics, with non‑optimal rigidity destabilizing force transmission and impairing barrier homeostasis (schimmel2024theinfluenceofa pages 5-6, schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 5-6). ECM biochemistry is not neutral: laminin isoforms and ligand density alter RPE contractility and reduce POS internalization at higher tension, directly linking matrix cues to disease‑relevant dysfunction (schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 5-6). Aged/nitrite‑modified ECM diminishes RPE attachment/viability and aged BrM lowers phagocytosis, recapitulating AMD phenotypes; ECM protein coatings can rescue these deficits, underscoring assay pharmacologic tractability (dujardin2024developmentofa pages 267-268, dujardin2024developmentofaa pages 267-268). Clinically and mechanistically, outer BRB barrier failure coexists with choroidal/BrM aging; vascular literature further shows basement membrane stiffening elevates endothelial activation/permeability and that Rho‑dependent mechanics modulate complement injury—validating stiffness as a causal axis at the RPE/CC interface (cabrera2018roleofendothelial pages 29-34, cabrera2018roleofendotheliala pages 29-34, cabrera2018roleofendotheliala pages 65-71).

Previous Use: Tunable hydrogels and defined ECM ligands have been used to show stiffness‑dependent changes in RPE adhesion, metabolism, inflammatory cytokines (IL‑6, MCP‑1) and cytoskeletal mechanics, and to dissect laminin‑specific mechanoresponses in hiPSC‑RPE; however, many stiffness studies did not report TEER/flux (white2017theinfluenceof pages 1-6, schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 1-5). Barrier assays (TEER, flux) are established in RPE BRB chips and Transwells and respond to disease‑relevant perturbations (e.g., hypoxia, TNF‑α), and drug effects on TEER/permeability have been measured in related models (e.g., melatonin improving TEER under metabolic stress), supporting assay sensitivity to therapeutic modulation (jahagirdar2021degenerativedisease‐on‐a‐chipdeveloping pages 16-19, dujardin2024developmentofa pages 268-268). ECM aging models (aged BM, nitrite‑modified ECM) have been used to test rescue via ECM coatings and demonstrate small‑molecule improvement of disease phenotypes (nicotinamide), indicating feasibility for drug evaluation, though explicit stiffness‑TEER drug screens are limited (dujardin2024developmentofa pages 267-268, dujardin2024developmentofaa pages 267-268).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths—Mechanistic alignment with AMD: BrM stiffening/compositional change, integrin/focal adhesion signaling, and junctional mechanosensitivity are central and quantifiable via TEER/flux plus cytoskeletal/adhesion readouts; stiffness and ligand identity can be precisely controlled; functional RPE endpoints (phagocytosis, polarized secretion) and inflammatory outputs add translational depth (schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 5-6, schimmel2024theinfluenceofa pages 5-6, nguyen2025conjugationofadhesion pages 13-17, schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 1-5, white2017theinfluenceof pages 1-6). Weaknesses—Stiffness alone will not capture drusen biogenesis, chronic inflammation/complement, or choroidal interactions; many scaffold systems risk diffusion limits or degradation toxicity; maturation time to high TER is long; cell characterization and material reporting are often inadequate; inclusion of appropriate ECM biochemistry and viscoelasticity is essential (nguyen2025conjugationofadhesion pages 13-17, dujardin2024developmentofa pages 284-284, dujardin2024developmentofa pages 268-268, dujardin2024developmentofa pages 271-271, dujardin2024developmentofaa pages 271-271). Net, this assay is well suited to evaluate therapeutics targeting ECM–RPE mechanotransduction or junctional integrity, but should be complemented by models incorporating drusen/complement and RPE–choroid co‑culture for comprehensive translational assessment (schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 1-5, cabrera2018roleofendothelial pages 29-34, cabrera2018roleofendotheliala pages 65-71).

An ECM stiffness barrier assay models a core AMD mechanism—age‑related BrM stiffening and altered ECM signaling—and provides quantitative barrier and mechanotransduction readouts sensitive to pharmacologic modulation. It is useful for testing agents that normalize RPE tension, integrin/FAK/Rho signaling, or tight‑junction integrity, provided ECM composition and RPE maturation are controlled. Use alongside models capturing drusen/complement and vascular components to cover the broader dry AMD pathophysiology (schimmel2024theinfluenceof pages 5-6, nguyen2025conjugationofadhesion pages 13-17, dujardin2024developmentofa pages 267-268, cabrera2018roleofendothelial pages 29-34).
