Assay Overview: This assay employs Seahorse bioenergetics profiling to measure oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) monolayers. It models the mitochondrial dysfunction and glycolytic alterations associated with dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD). These functional endpoints—basal respiration, maximal respiration, spare respiratory capacity, and ATP production—are derived from sequential injections of compounds in cultured primary human RPE, including donor‐derived AMD and non‐AMD cells (ebeling2020improvingretinalmitochondrial pages 9-10, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 49-52).

Biomedical Evidence: Mitochondrial dysfunction is central to AMD pathogenesis. Multiple studies have documented that AMD RPE exhibit reduced oxidative phosphorylation and altered bioenergetics that correlate with disease severity, including increased oxidative damage and compensatory glycolytic shifts. This evidence establishes that impaired mitochondrial function contributes to the “bioenergetic crisis” in AMD and underlines the importance of targeting mitochondrial pathways in therapeutic development (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 32-36, fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 36-40).

Previous Use: Seahorse profiling has been used to assess the impact of candidate drugs—such as NAC, rapamycin, PQQ, and NMN—on mitochondrial function in AMD RPE. These studies demonstrated disease-dependent drug responses and inter-donor variability while providing mechanistic insight into drug-induced rescue of mitochondrial defects (ebeling2020improvingretinalmitochondrial pages 9-10, ebeling2020improvingretinalmitochondrial pages 1-2).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths of this assay include its real-time, quantitative measurement of key bioenergetic parameters and its strong mechanistic linkage to mitochondrial function in AMD. Limitations include variability inherent to primary cell cultures, potential discrepancies between in vitro and in vivo responses, and the need for rigorous standardization in assay conditions (fisher2022mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 61-67, louie2022assessmentofposterior pages 49-56).

This assay is a valuable tool for early-stage drug screening in dry AMD, offering direct functional readouts that are central to disease pathology. It enables precise evaluation of therapeutic candidates aimed at restoring mitochondrial health despite the challenges of in vitro variability.
