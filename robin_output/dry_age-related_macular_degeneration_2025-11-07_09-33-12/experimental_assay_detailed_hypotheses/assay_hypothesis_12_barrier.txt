Disruption of RPE tight junctions is a central early event in dry AMD pathogenesis and is linked to subsequent complement activation, drusen formation, and neovascular events. Previous Use:  
FITC‑dextran flux assays in Transwells have been extensively used to model barrier breakdown following oxidative and inflammatory insults. These assays have been pivotal in demonstrating the efficacy of junction‑stabilizing therapeutics such as αCT1 and in correlating permeability changes with endothelial sprouting and complement deposition (김지호2025developmentofa pages 81-88, 정민환2017eyeona pages 89-100, obert2017targetingthetight pages 10-11).

Overall Evaluation:  
This assay is a robust and rapid method to evaluate early barrier dysfunction in dry AMD models. Its strengths include sensitivity to changes in tight junction integrity and adaptability for multi‑parametric readouts, whereas its weaknesses relate to the inherent limitations of certain cell lines (e.g., ARPE‑19) and the lack of full recapitulation of RPE–choroid interplay (dujardin2024developmentofa pages 266-267, hellinen2020retracteddrugflux pages 8-9).

The assay provides a valuable platform for early drug screening in dry AMD by quantifying rapid functional changes in RPE barrier integrity. It offers robust, multi‑readout data, though care must be taken in choosing the appropriate RPE model to enhance in vivo relevance.
