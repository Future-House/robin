Assay Overview: The proposed assay involves culturing polarized retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells on Transwell membranes to recreate an in vivo–like barrier and secretory polarity. Exogenous LDL/ApoB100 supplementation combined with an AGE-modified extracellular matrix is intended to mimic systemic lipid overload and ECM alterations seen in age-related macular degeneration (AMD). The assay quantifies basal deposit formation using Oil Red O to stain and measure lipid-rich deposits, thereby modeling a central aspect of drusen biogenesis (mesch2021towardsanin pages 77-79, mesch2021…anin pages 77-79).

Biomedical Evidence: Drusen formation is a hallmark of dry AMD and involves the accumulation of lipoproteins, advanced glycation end products, and extracellular matrix components beneath the RPE. The deposition of LDL/ApoB100–rich material in conjunction with AGE-modified ECM reflects key pathogenic mechanisms observed in human AMD, including altered lipid metabolism, complement activation, and local inflammation (pilgrim2017subretinalpigmentepithelial pages 10-11, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 24-27). These pathogenic insights are supported by both clinical and biochemical studies linking lipid dysregulation to progression of dry AMD (mesch2021…foragerelated pages 92-95).

Previous Use: Although polarized RPE cultures on Transwell inserts have been shown to produce drusen-like deposits and recapitulate aspects of Bruch’s membrane pathology, the specific combination of LDL/ApoB100 supplementation with AGE-modified ECM and Oil Red O staining has not been widely validated. Previous studies have used analogous approaches to detect neutral lipid accumulation and apolipoprotein expression in deposit formation (chen2020unstimulatedserumfreecultures pages 5-6, chen2020unstimulatedserumfreecultures pages 6-6), indicating that the proposed approach is building on established methodologies.

Overall Evaluation: This assay is promising because it targets central processes in dry AMD pathogenesis and offers clear, quantifiable endpoints. Strengths include physiological relevance and the opportunity for high-throughput screening, while limitations center on standardization of exogenous supplement concentrations and deposit reproducibility. 

The proposed assay effectively mimics key features of drusen biogenesis in dry AMD and offers a useful platform for preliminary drug screening. Further validation is needed to optimize conditions and ensure reproducible outcomes.
