Overall Evaluation: Strengths and weaknesses of this assay for testing therapeutics for dry age-related macular degeneration.

Assay Overview: Quantify mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) lesion frequency in RPE using long-amplicon qPCR (LA-qPCR) after oxidative stress with or without test agents. The assay models the ROS–mtDNA–mitochondrial dysfunction axis implicated in dry AMD by measuring polymerase-blocking lesions across the mitochondrial genome (e.g., long vs short amplicons in D-loop, rRNA/tRNA, and OXPHOS-encoding regions) from primary human RPE, iPSC-RPE, or ARPE-19 exposed to H2O2 or blue light; readouts are lesion frequency per 10 kb and region-specific damage, optionally paired to common deletion assays and copy number (mtDNA:nDNA) (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 9-11, mehrzadi2020mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 4-5, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 16-18).

Biomedical Evidence: RPE mitochondria in AMD show reduced density, cristae loss, decreased ATP, proteomic disruption, and functional respiratory deficits; mtDNA accumulates several-fold more damage than nuclear DNA, correlates with AMD stage, and lesions occur in control regions and OXPHOS genes central to bioenergetics (chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3, fisher2018perspectiveonamd pages 2-2, mehrzadi2020mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 4-5, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 9-11). Blue light and H2O2 induce mtROS and mtDNA damage in RPE, supporting causality and relevance of ROS-driven lesions; mtDNA damage can also amplify inflammation via DAMP signaling (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 9-11, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 16-18, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 7-8). AMD RPE exhibits metabolic reprogramming and elevated PGC-1α despite unchanged mtDNA content in some reports, arguing for direct lesion assays over copy-number alone (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 3-5).

Previous Use: LA-qPCR has documented increased mtDNA lesions in AMD donor RPE versus controls, age- and grade-associated increases, and region-specific vulnerability; oxidative paradigms in RPE have repeatedly used mtDNA damage as a primary endpoint (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 9-11, mehrzadi2020mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 4-5, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 16-18). Transmitochondrial cybrids demonstrate mtDNA-driven differences in energetics and gene programs pertinent to inflammation and mtDNA metabolism, underscoring pharmacologic tractability of mitochondrial integrity (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 9-11, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 7-8).

Overall Evaluation: Strengths: mechanism-proximal, quantitative, human-relevant, and sensitive to oxidative injury; correlates with disease severity and targets sequences essential for respiration—ideal as a pharmacodynamic readout in RPE models (mehrzadi2020mitochondrialdysfunctionin pages 4-5, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 9-11). Limitations: detects only polymerase-blocking lesions (misses 8-oxoG), confounded by copy number/heteroplasmy/mitophagy, and cross-compartment comparisons are fraught; clinical qualification as a standalone biomarker is lacking (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 9-11, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 7-9, chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3). Best used in a panel with bioenergetics (OCR/ATP), mtROS, membrane potential, proteomics, and quality-control markers (e.g., PGC-1α) (tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 7-8, lenin2023dysfunctionalautophagyproteostasis pages 2-4, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 3-5).

Direct mtDNA lesion quantification by LA-qPCR is a valid, mechanism-aligned assay for testing dry AMD therapeutics in RPE models, particularly as a pharmacodynamic marker of mitochondrial protection. It should be interpreted with its methodological constraints and complemented by functional mitochondrial endpoints to ensure translational relevance (kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 9-11, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 7-8).
