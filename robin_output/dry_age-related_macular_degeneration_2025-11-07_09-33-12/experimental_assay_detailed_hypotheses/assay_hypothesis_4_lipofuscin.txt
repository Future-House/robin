Assay Overview: This assay uses cultured RPE cells (e.g., ARPE-19) that are repeatedly loaded with photoreceptor outer segments to induce lipofuscin/A2E accumulation. It quantitatively measures RPE autofluorescence or A2E-specific emission using fluorescence microscopy or plate readers. This readout reflects lysosomal load and impaired autophagic flux, key events in dry AMD pathogenesis (jin2016anovelfluorescencebased pages 1-2, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 1-6).

Biomedical Evidence: Lipofuscin buildup, particularly A2E accumulation, has been linked to oxidative stress, lysosomal dysfunction, and defective autophagy in RPE cells, all of which contribute directly to RPE degeneration in dry AMD. Clinical imaging with fundus autofluorescence further validates this mechanism by correlating increased or altered autofluorescent signals with RPE pathology and atrophic changes (chen2021stagesofdrusenassociated pages 1-3, nociari2017lipofuscinaccumulationinto pages 14-17).

Previous Use: In vitro, assays based on A2E or lipofuscin-like autofluorescence have been used to monitor RPE functional status and assess therapeutic interventions such as lysosome acidifiers, mTOR inhibitors, and agents enhancing TFEB activity. These studies have demonstrated that modulating lysosomal parameters can reduce autofluorescence, providing proof-of-concept for drug discovery (falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 13-18, falcao2024enhancinglysosomefunction pages 21-26).

Overall Evaluation: The assay’s strength lies in its disease relevance and quantitative nature. Its ability to model key pathogenic mechanisms in RPE dysfunction makes it useful for early-stage drug screening. However, its interpretation may be confounded by factors such as photoreceptor screening effects and requires pairing with functional endpoints like cell viability and lysosomal activity to fully capture therapeutic potential (jin2016anovelfluorescencebased pages 1-2, nociari2017lipofuscinaccumulationinto pages 14-17).

This lipofuscin accumulation assay is a promising tool for evaluating therapeutics targeting dry AMD pathogenesis. It provides a quantitative, disease-relevant readout that, when integrated with complementary endpoints, can enhance the screening of candidate interventions.
