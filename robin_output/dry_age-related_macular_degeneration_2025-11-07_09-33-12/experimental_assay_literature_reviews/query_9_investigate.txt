Query: Investigate alterations in the extracellular matrix of Bruch’s membrane in dry age-related macular degeneration, including changes in collagen cross-linking, elastin degradation, matrix metalloproteinase and tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase expression in RPE cultures, and methods to recapitulate ECM stiffening or compositional changes in vitro.

Bruch’s membrane (BrM) in dry AMD exhibits pronounced ECM remodeling: non-enzymatic collagen cross-linking and accumulation of AGEs, lipids, GAGs, collagen, and elastin increase thickness and stiffness while reducing porosity and elasticity, with elevated nitrotyrosine as a marker of nitrosative modification and weakened collagen IV signal in donor tissue (biasella2023theextracellularmicroenvironment pages 7-8, eamegdool2020extracellularmatrixand pages 10-11, eamegdool2020extracellularmatrixand pages 8-10, molins2024biomaterialengineeringstrategies pages 3-4). Aging is further associated with decreased collagen solubility, loss of elastin, and reduced integrity/thickness of the macular elastic layer, changes linked to impaired transport and altered immunoregulation at the RPE–BrM interface (biasella2023theextracellularmicroenvironment pages 7-8, tan2019biofabricationofchoroidretina pages 153-156, candiello2009investigationintochanges pages 133-134). Aged BrM sequesters extracellular MMPs, diminishing proteolytic capacity and perturbing normal turnover that is normally balanced by TIMP3 (biasella2023theextracellularmicroenvironment pages 7-8).

Direct RPE culture data show complement attack elevates MMP-2/9 expression and activity, shifting the VEGF/PEDF protein balance toward pro-angiogenic signaling without changing their transcripts (bandyopadhyay2012matrixmetalloproteinaseactivity pages 1-2). Oxidative stress in RPE induces MMP-1 and MMP-3 and skews the MMP-1/3:TIMP-1 ratio toward type I collagen degradation; MMP-14/TIMP-2 interplay helps maintain MMP-2 activity and protect from oxidant injury (caban2022theroleof pages 5-7, caban2022theroleof pages 22-23). Angiotensin II increases MMP-2 activity and MMP-14 in RPE, enhancing type IV collagen degradation via ERK/p38 MAPK, and VEGF/TNF further regulate gelatinases (caban2022theroleof pages 7-8). Pathogenic TIMP3 variants resist turnover and accumulate in BrM, disrupting MMP/ADAM/ADAMTS control and inflammatory signaling (biasella2023theextracellularmicroenvironment pages 9-11).

In vitro, BrM stiffening/compositional change is recapitulated by nitrite modification of RPE-deposited ECM, which cross-links collagen I/IV, increases nitrotyrosine, weakens collagen detection, impairs RPE adhesion/spreading, reduces CD46, and increases bioactive C3a; coating with collagen IV rescues adhesion (eamegdool2020extracellularmatrixand pages 10-11, eamegdool2020extracellularmatrixand pages 8-10, biasella2023theextracellularmicroenvironment pages 9-11). EFEMP1-R345W–modified ECM models disease-associated ECM that drives complement deposition and basal deposit formation (biasella2023theextracellularmicroenvironment pages 9-11). Additional strategies include culturing RPE on AMD versus control donor BrM, which reveals collagen IV disorganization with elevated MMP-2 and C3a, and using ECM ligands (fibronectin, vitronectin) or rigid matrices to modulate cell–matrix signaling and immune activation; biomaterial BrM-like substrates and RPE–endothelial co-culture further reproduce oBRB mechanics and composition (caban2022theroleof pages 7-8, biasella2023theextracellularmicroenvironment pages 7-8, eamegdool2020extracellularmatrixand pages 14-14, molins2024biomaterialengineeringstrategies pages 3-4).

Dry AMD BrM shows cross-linked, stiffened collagen with nitrosative signatures, and compromised elastin architecture. RPE cultures demonstrate context-dependent increases in MMPs (MMP-2/9, MMP-1/3, MMP-14) and altered MMP/TIMP balance, with TIMP3 dysregulation implicated in ECM accumulation. Nitrite cross-linking, EFEMP1-mutant ECM, diseased donor BrM substrates, defined ECM ligands, and engineered BrM-like co-cultures are tractable in vitro methods to model ECM stiffening and compositional change (biasella2023theextracellularmicroenvironment pages 7-8, eamegdool2020extracellularmatrixand pages 10-11, bandyopadhyay2012matrixmetalloproteinaseactivity pages 1-2, biasella2023theextracellularmicroenvironment pages 9-11, caban2022theroleof pages 7-8, molins2024biomaterialengineeringstrategies pages 3-4).
