Query: Identify best practices for designing robust, high-content phenotypic assays in retinal pigment epithelial cell cultures to measure key dry age-related macular degeneration endpoints such as tight junction integrity, oxidative stress quantification, mitochondrial bioenergetic profiling, lipofuscin accumulation, and complement component deposition.

Robust phenotypic assays begin with selecting physiologically relevant RPE models (hiPSC‐derived or primary) cultured under defined, reproducible conditions that promote polarization and maturation, including the use of extracellular matrix coatings (e.g., Matrigel) and defined medium formulations (webster2023nrf2associatedantioxidantstress pages 77-79, webster2023nrf2associatedantioxidantstress pages 32-35, lugano2023bioengineeringanoptimised pages 302-304). For tight junction integrity, combine functional assessments such as transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) and paracellular flux assays with high‐content imaging of junctional proteins (e.g., ZO‑1) to ensure both quantitative and structural validation of barrier formation (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 15-16, asatryan2022newretinalpigment pages 1-2, lugano2023bioengineeringanoptimised pages 309-311, webster2023nrf2associatedantioxidantstress pages 100-102).

Oxidative stress quantification should integrate ROS‐sensitive fluorescent probes and determination of NRF2‐dependent target expression (HMOX1, NQO1) by qPCR and immunoblotting, with appropriate positive/negative controls to account for donor and treatment variability (webster2023nrf2associatedantioxidantstress pages 77-79, webster2023nrf2associatedantioxidantstress pages 92-95). Mitochondrial bioenergetic profiling is best achieved using metabolic flux analyses (e.g., Seahorse assays) together with normalization to cell count and verification of cellular maturity, possibly modulated by nicotinamide supplementation (lugano2023bioengineeringanoptimised pages 309-311, lugano2023bioengineeringanoptimised pages 302-304).

For lipofuscin accumulation, standardized autofluorescence imaging with defined excitation/emission settings and confocal microscopy—complemented by TEM for ultrastructural confirmation—ensures robust detection (mesch2021…foragerelated pages 43-47, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 74-77). Complement deposition should be measured by combining high-content immunofluorescence with sensitive immunoassays (e.g., ELISA, MSD) targeting markers such as C3, C3b, and C5b-9, using pharmacological agents like Cp40 as internal controls (cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 15-16, cerniauskas2020complementmodulationreverses pages 1-2, falcao2025targetinglysosomaldysfunction pages 2-4).

These integrated approaches, supported by standardized culture conditions and quantitative image/signal analysis, enable reliable measurement of dry AMD endpoints.

The best practices involve using well-defined RPE models, combining functional and imaging assessments, and integrating multiplexed readouts for oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, lipofuscin accumulation, and complement deposition. This multifaceted strategy ensures robust and reproducible high-content phenotypic assays for dry AMD research.
