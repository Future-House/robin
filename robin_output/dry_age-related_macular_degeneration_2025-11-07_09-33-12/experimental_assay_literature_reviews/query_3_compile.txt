Query: Compile detailed information on the biochemical composition and mechanisms of drusen formation in dry age-related macular degeneration, focusing on lipid accumulation, cholesterol ester pathways, lipofuscin and A2E deposition, extracellular matrix remodeling by retinal pigment epithelial cells, Bruch’s membrane interactions, and potential in vitro assay recapitulation strategies.

Biochemical composition of drusen. Drusen are extracellular deposits at the RPE–Bruch’s membrane (BrM) interface in which lipids constitute ≥40% of mass, dominated by esterified cholesterol (EC), unesterified cholesterol (UC), and phosphatidylcholine, together with apolipoproteins, complement proteins/regulators, carbohydrates, glycosaminoglycans, AGEs, metals (e.g., zinc), and intracellular/exosomal proteins (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 5-6, algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 2-3, meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 1-2, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 8-10, sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 72-77). BrM lipoprotein-like particles (BM‑LLPs) of intraocular (RPE) origin resemble LDL, are EC‑enriched, and accumulate to form a “lipid wall,” with EC particularly enriched in macula versus periphery (~7-fold) (meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 1-2).

Lipid accumulation and cholesterol ester pathways. The RPE secretes ApoB100-containing, cholesterol‑rich lipoproteins into BrM, where age‑related retention/oxidative modification (“response to retention,” “oil spill”) yields BLinD/soft drusen; transporters and remodeling factors implicated include ApoE/A‑I, ABCA1/G1, SR‑BI, LCAT, and CETP (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 5-6, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 6-7, brown2018metabolomicsandagerelated pages 3-5). Aging BrM reduces permeability and heparan sulfate proteoglycans, while CFH Y402H less effectively binds GAGs and oxidized lipids, diminishing competition with lipoproteins and favoring EC‑rich retention and complement deposition (sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 44-48, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 6-7, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 8-10).

Lipofuscin/A2E and RPE dysfunction. Repeated POS phagocytosis drives lipofuscin accumulation; A2E is the major bisretinoid fluorophore, is phototoxic, perturbs cholesterol metabolism, inhibits COX, and promotes mitochondrial/lysosomal dysfunction, ROS generation, complement activation, and RPE apoptosis, thereby increasing extracellular debris available to form drusen (algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 2-3, brown2018metabolomicsandagerelated pages 26-27, brown2018metabolomicsandagerelated pages 3-5, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 10-11). Lipofuscin/bisretinoids also sequester cholesterol in endo‑lysosomes, activate ASMase/ceramide, impair autophagy, and amplify complement by downregulating CFH and increasing CFB (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 11-12).

ECM remodeling and BrM interactions. With age/AMD, BrM thickens, collagen becomes cross‑linked/insoluble, elastin fragments, proteoglycans enlarge, and AGEs/oxidative adducts accumulate, lowering hydraulic permeability and trapping lipoproteins (sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 44-48). RPE-driven fibronectin matrix assembly scaffolds collagen IV and basal deposits; MMP/TIMP dysregulation and integrin signaling further modify ECM and favor deposit persistence (meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 4-5, meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 2-4, sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 37-41). Drusen impede metabolite diffusion and exacerbate RPE stress (algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 3-4).

In vitro recapitulation strategies. Use polarized primary/hiPSC‑RPE on BrM‑mimetic scaffolds (Transwells; engineered thin films via BF‑LS/LB) to recreate basal retention (meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 5-7, sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 227-230). Supply ApoB/E‑lipoproteins with CE/FC and oxPLs; modulate HSPG content and CFH status (Y402H) to drive “response to retention”; include complement to model deposition (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 6-7, meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 2-4). Induce lipofuscin by POS challenge ± A2E plus blue light; AGE‑modify ECM (methylglyoxal/glycoaldehyde) to crosslink proteins and reduce permeability (meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 4-5, sharif2015expressionandregulation pages 227-230). Readouts: Oil Red O and CE/FC quantification, drusen‑like EC/UC deposition, autofluorescence, ECM (fibronectin/collagen IV) accumulation, and complement/MAC deposition (brown2018metabolomicsandagerelated pages 3-5, meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 4-5, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 5-6).

Drusen in dry AMD are lipid-rich, EC‑dominated deposits seeded by RPE‑derived lipoproteins retained and oxidized within an aging, cross‑linked BrM, and amplified by lipofuscin/A2E‑driven RPE dysfunction and complement activity (tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 5-6, meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 1-2, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 10-11). RPE‑mediated ECM remodeling, decreased BrM permeability, and defective clearance create a self‑reinforcing cycle of lipid retention and inflammation (meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 2-4, algvere2016drusenmaculopathya pages 1-2). Polarized RPE on BrM‑mimetic scaffolds with defined lipoprotein/oxidative/AGE/complement inputs provides a tractable in vitro system to reproduce drusen biogenesis and test interventions (meng2021lipidaccumulationand pages 5-7, tang2024aberrantlipidmetabolism pages 6-7).
