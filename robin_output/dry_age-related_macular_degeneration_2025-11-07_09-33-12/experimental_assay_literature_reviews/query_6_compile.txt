Query: Compile a comprehensive list and evaluation of quantitative biochemical and imaging-based readouts suitable for high-throughput cell culture assays in dry age-related macular degeneration research, including reactive oxygen species detection, mitochondrial membrane potential assays, transepithelial electrical resistance measurements, tight junction immunofluorescence, and complement deposition immunoassays.

High-throughput, plate-compatible pipelines are established for RPE/barrier assays and support in-plate viability imaging, ATP-based proliferation/metabolism, and confocal immunofluorescence without sample transfer, which improves consistency and parallelization; microphysiological PMMA devices further reduce small-molecule absorption compared with PDMS, aiding translational readouts (lopezbernardoUnknownyear1403dhydrogelsand pages 16-18). Medium-throughput 96-well implementations for iPSC-RPE/retinal organoids have been demonstrated, enabling screening-scale imaging and immunoassays (watson2023unravellingstargardtdisease pages 215-218).

Transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) is a quantitative electrical readout of barrier tightness that correlates with tight-junction formation; values >500 Ω·cm2 are reported in vitro, but TEER requires polarized monolayers (transwells or organ-on-chip), limiting direct use in 3D RPE organoids where dextran permeability assays provide an alternative barrier metric (mesch2021…foragerelated pages 74-77). TEER has been operationalized in plate-compatible MPS and used to confirm confluence within a week; functional permeability readouts such as albumin clearance are also quantifiable in chip contexts (lopezbernardoUnknownyear1403dhydrogelsand pages 16-18).

Tight-junction immunofluorescence (e.g., ZO-1) is a core imaging readout that correlates with high TEER and confirms epithelial identity/polarity; confocal IF workflows and analysis pipelines are documented and compatible with transwell-grown polarized RPE (mesch2021…foragerelated pages 70-74, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 43-47). TEM provides ultrastructural confirmation of junctions and polarity, while organoid autofluorescence/pigmentation can necessitate protocol adaptations and complicate quantification (mesch2021…foragerelated pages 52-59, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 74-77).

Complement deposition immunoassays are directly applicable: iPSC-RPE show immunodetectable C3 and MAC deposition after challenge, and complement factors are measurable at protein and mRNA levels; human serum exposure modulates C3, though variability is noted and quantitative performance metrics are not provided (watson2023unravellingstargardtdisease pages 215-218, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 70-74, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 77-79).

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) and mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) assays are biologically motivated—RPE generate high mitochondrial ROS and polarization increases stress resistance—but the excerpts lack specific probe/kit details; available imaging hardware and filter sets support such live-cell readouts, while fixation-heavy workflows and intrinsic autofluorescence in aged/organoid RPE are practical constraints (parikh2023singlecelltranscriptomicsreveals pages 8-8, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 43-47, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 52-59, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 31-34, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 95-98).

High-throughput AMD-relevant readouts with the strongest methodological support here are TEER (monolayers/chips), dextran permeability (organoids), tight-junction IF with confocal/TEM validation, and complement deposition immunodetection in 96-well–compatible workflows. ROS and MMP assays are appropriate but require protocols beyond these excerpts; instrumentation and biological context are in place, with autofluorescence and live-cell compatibility as key caveats (lopezbernardoUnknownyear1403dhydrogelsand pages 16-18, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 74-77, watson2023unravellingstargardtdisease pages 215-218, mesch2021…foragerelated pages 43-47, parikh2023singlecelltranscriptomicsreveals pages 8-8).
