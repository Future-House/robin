Query: Perform a comprehensive review of the molecular mechanisms underlying RPE cell oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction in dry age-related macular degeneration, including sources of reactive oxygen species, compromised antioxidant defenses, pathways leading to mitochondrial DNA damage, apoptotic signaling cascades, and potential molecular biomarkers to measure.

Dry age‐related macular degeneration (AMD) is characterized by progressive RPE degeneration driven by chronic oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 7-9). Primary sources of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in RPE cells include iron-catalyzed Fenton chemistry, photoinduced ROS from lipofuscin and its vitamin A–derived fluorophore A2E, mitochondrial electron transport chain leakage, and NADPH oxidase activity during the processing of photoreceptor outer segments (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12, buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 7-9). External environmental factors such as blue/UV light exposure and smoking further exacerbate ROS generation (shu2023roleofoxidative pages 31-32).

Compromised antioxidant defenses occur as a result of impaired NRF2 signaling, reduced expression of superoxide dismutases and other antioxidant enzymes, diminished autophagy/mitophagy leading to lipofuscin accumulation, and age-associated NAD⁺ depletion (chen2023oxidativestressin pages 2-3, tong2022roleofmitochondria pages 14-16). Excess ROS oxidizes mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) – which is inherently vulnerable due to its proximity to ROS sources and limited repair capacity – thereby perpetuating mitochondrial dysfunction (toma2021oxidativeandnitrosative pages 4-5, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 16-18). This mitochondrial impairment initiates intrinsic apoptotic cascades marked by cytochrome c release and may also engage necroptosis and ferroptosis pathways (buonfiglio2024recentadvancesin pages 10-12, kilic2025oxidativestressand pages 48-51).

Potential molecular biomarkers include lipid peroxidation products (4-HNE, MDA, CEP), oxidized nucleotides, mtDNA damage and copy number, altered NRF2 and PGC-1α levels, NAD⁺ status, mitochondrial dynamics regulators (Drp1, mff), and cytochrome c release, with supplementary markers being inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β (shu2023roleofoxidative pages 13-14, terao2022oxidativestressinducedcellular pages 2-4, kaarniranta2019roleofmitochondrial pages 18-18).

RPE oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction are central mechanisms in dry AMD pathogenesis. Monitoring these molecular biomarkers can aid in early diagnosis and evaluation of therapeutic strategies.
